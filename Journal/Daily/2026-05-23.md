## 🙏 Daily Prayer

*Supplication — Asking for help for yourself and others*

Heavenly Father,

I come before You today asking for Your guidance and strength. Grant me the wisdom to see clearly what truly matters — not the noise of the world, but Your still, quiet voice leading me forward. Where I feel overwhelmed or scattered, calm my mind and sharpen my focus so I can show up fully in everything I do today.

Lord, I pray for the people in my life who are carrying burdens I may not even know about. Give them peace that surpasses understanding, and let me be a source of encouragement to them rather than another demand on their energy.

I ask that You help me stay true to the habits and commitments I've made — not out of rigid discipline alone, but because I genuinely want to become the person You created me to be. Give me grace when I fall short, and remind me that growth is a process, not a single moment.

Thank You for this day, for the gift of another chance to do better and be better.

Amen.

## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**From: Atomic Habits** — *James Clear*
Identity is the foundation of lasting change: instead of focusing on what you want to achieve, ask yourself who you want to *become*. Every small habit you keep is a vote cast for that identity — the goal is not to run a marathon, but to become someone who runs. Two steps: decide what kind of person you want to be, then prove it to yourself with small wins. **Action:** Apply the Never-Miss-Twice rule — one bad day is an accident, two in a row is the start of a new habit.

**From: The Psychology of Money** — *Morgan Housel*
The hardest financial skill is knowing when you have *enough*. The goalpost of wealth has a dangerous tendency to keep moving, and comparing yourself to others will always keep you feeling behind. Identify your personal "Untouchables" — reputation, freedom, family, happiness — and never risk them for money, because these things are almost certainly worth more to you than any dollar amount ever will be.

**From: Steve Jobs** — *Walter Isaacson*
Jobs's most enduring contribution wasn't any single product — it was his belief that the greatest innovations live at the *intersection of technology and the liberal arts*. His calligraphy class at Reed College became the Mac's beautiful typography; his love of the Beatles showed him that art and engineering could be inseparable. The lesson: deliberately cross disciplines. Whatever you're building, ask what the humanities would add to it.
