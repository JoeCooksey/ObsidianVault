# Circadian Rhythm Alignment

The circadian rhythm is the body's ~24-hour internal clock, entrained primarily by light. Aligning sleep with the day/night cycle — rather than just hitting a total hour count at arbitrary times — improves sleep quality and downstream health.

## Sleep With the Sun

A schedule anchored to darkness (e.g. 9-to-5, 10-to-6) keeps sleep in phase with the sun. Being asleep at night frees the daytime for **morning and afternoon sun exposure**, which regulates circadian rhythm (and has independent benefits). Morning sunlight is the strongest anchor; red light is a weaker fallback if sun isn't available.

## Consistent Wake Time

A fixed wake time is the primary anchor of the rhythm. On weekends, sleeping in is allowed only up to **~30 minutes** beyond the normal wake time — beyond that, the rhythm shifts and the next night's sleep onset becomes harder (a mini "social jet lag").

> [!example] The ~30-minute rule
> If you wake at 5:00 on weekdays, allow yourself until ~5:30 on weekends — no later, or you start dragging your clock forward.

## Why It Matters for Performance

Anecdotally, people on consistent reasonable schedules find it easier to stick to diets, train hard, and avoid injury. Poor sleep is associated with eating ~350 extra calories the next day through increased cravings.

Related levers: managing evening light ([[Pre-Bed Light Management]]) and front-loading caffeine ([[Caffeine Timing Protocol]]) both protect the rhythm. Part of the [[Sleepmaxxing Protocol]].
