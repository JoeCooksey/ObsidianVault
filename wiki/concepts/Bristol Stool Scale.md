# Bristol Stool Scale

The standard 7-type clinical classification of stool form, developed at the University of Bristol. The free, zero-equipment readout of gut function — target is **type 3–4**.

## The Seven Types

| Type | Form | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Separate hard lumps, like nuts | Severe constipation — slow transit |
| 2 | Lumpy, sausage-shaped | Mild constipation |
| **3** | **Sausage with surface cracks** | **Healthy** |
| **4** | **Smooth, soft sausage/snake** | **Ideal** — easiest to pass |
| 5 | Soft blobs with clear edges | Borderline — lacking fiber or fast transit |
| 6 | Mushy, ragged edges | Mild diarrhea |
| 7 | Entirely liquid | Diarrhea — very fast transit |

## What It Tracks (and What It Doesn't)

- Form correlates strongly with **transit time** — a pediatric study found r = 0.84 between Bristol score and total gut transit time (constipated children ~63 h vs healthy ~30 h).
- **Frequency barely correlates with transit time** — consistency and ease of passage are the better everyday signals, not count-per-week.
- Direct transit measurement still beats it: the blue-dye method explained more microbiome variation than Bristol form (classification AUC 0.72 vs 0.65) (Source: [[Blue Poo Transit Time Study (PREDICT 1)]]).

## Practical Use

- Persistent type 1–2 → more water, more fiber (gel-forming first: [[Fiber Supplement Tier List]]), more movement.
- Persistent type 6–7 → review recent antibiotics, alcohol, sweeteners, stress ([[Gut-Harmful Foods and Actions Tier List]]); >2 weeks or with blood → [[GI Red Flag Symptoms]].
