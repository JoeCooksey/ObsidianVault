# Non-Nutritive Sweeteners and Diet Soda Merit Verdict

The one-page answer to "how much merit do the sweeteners in diet sodas actually carry?" (Full evidence: [[Research - Artificial Sweeteners (Studies and Merit in Diet Sodas)]].)

## The Verdict in One Line
> **Diet soda is clearly better than sugar soda, and clearly worse than water/unsweetened drinks.** The sweeteners are a legitimate harm-reduction tool, not a health food — and not the poison the headlines imply.

## Merit Scorecard
| Claim about diet-soda sweeteners | Merit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Cause cancer" | **Low** | IARC 2B = *possible*, limited evidence; JECFA keeps generous ADI; you'd need ~9–14 cans/day |
| "Better than sugary soda" | **High** | RCTs: modest weight loss / neutral, avoids ~140 kcal + sugar spike per can |
| "Help you lose weight vs water" | **Low–moderate** | RCTs roughly a tie ([[Harrold 2023 - NNS Beverages vs Water RCT|Harrold]] slight edge); WHO says no long-term fat-loss benefit |
| "Harmless / totally inert" | **Low** | [[Suez 2022 - Sweeteners Alter Human Glucose Tolerance (Cell)|Suez 2022]]: sucralose/saccharin shift microbiome & glucose in some people |
| "Cause heart disease / diabetes" | **Weak–uncertain** | Only cohort associations, small absolute risk, ~70% explained by pre-existing diabetes ([[Reverse Causation in Sweetener Cohort Studies|reverse causation]]) |

## Practical Guidance
1. **Replacing sugar soda with diet soda:** solid net win. Do it.
2. **Diet soda vs water as your default:** water wins; treat diet soda as an occasional bridge, not the baseline.
3. **Molecule matters:** aspartame and stevia look cleanest metabolically; **sucralose/saccharin** carry the clearest glycemic signal; **erythritol** (not in most sodas) has a separate clot concern.
4. **Personalize:** if weight and glucose markers are fine, you're likely not a high responder; if they drift, cut sweeteners as a test.
