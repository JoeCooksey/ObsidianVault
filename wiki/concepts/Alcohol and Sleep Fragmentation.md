# Alcohol and Sleep Fragmentation

A nightcap helps people fall asleep faster but reliably **breaks up the second half of the night**, making it a leading lifestyle cause of [[Sleep Maintenance Insomnia|midnight wakeups]] (confidence: high — consistent across studies).

## Mechanism

- **REM suppression then rebound**: alcohol suppresses REM early in the night; as blood alcohol falls, the brain compensates with intensified REM later, causing frequent awakenings in the second half of the night (Source: [[Jackson Heart Sleep Study — Evening Substances 2019]]).
- **Neurotransmitter disruption**: metabolizing alcohol disturbs the sleep/wake neurotransmitter balance, producing lighter, more fragmented sleep.
- **Autonomic shift**: alcohol increases sympathetic ("fight or flight") activation and suppresses parasympathetic tone during sleep.

## Practical guidance

- **Stop drinking ≥4 hours before bed** to let blood alcohol clear before the vulnerable second half of the night.
- Even moderate evening intake shows day-to-day disruptive effects in real-world settings.
- Caffeine compounds the problem; evening alcohol can mask but not cancel daytime caffeine's stimulant effect — reduce both. Cut caffeine after ~1–2 p.m. (Source: [[Harvard Health — Awake at 3 a.m.]]).

> [!tip] Of all single changes, removing the evening drink is one of the highest-yield fixes for 3 a.m. wakeups.
