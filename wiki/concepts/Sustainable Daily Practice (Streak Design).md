# Sustainable Daily Practice (Streak Design)

A compounding project only pays off if you actually return to it for years. The failure mode is almost never ambition — it's a **daily bar set too high**, which collapses the streak on the first bad day. This page is the operating system for keeping a daily project alive. (Source: anti-burnout / streak-design analyses, 2025–2026)

## Rules that keep a streak alive

1. **Set a 5–15 minute floor, and count rough/unfinished work.** "If your rule is 'finished piece every day,' you're betting against your calendar, your energy, and your attention span." A 5-minute floor protects the chain. (Connects to [[Two-Minute Rule]].)
2. **Flexible output by energy.** Don't demand the same kind of work daily. On low days, *edit* instead of write, *review code* instead of writing new logic. The streak survives even when intensity drops.
3. **Identity over perfection.** Be "someone who returns to the work regularly," not "someone who performs perfectly." That identity survives bad sessions and messy output — and produces more over time *because* you stay in motion. (Connects to [[Identity-Based vs Goal-Based Habits]].)
4. **Kill start-up friction.** At the end of each session, write 1–2 bullets: what tomorrow's real work is, and the tiny fallback task if you feel stuck. Removes morning decision fatigue. (Connects to [[Implementation Intention]].)
5. **Respect ultradian rhythms.** Work in 90–120 min cycles of high then low alertness; schedule the hard creative block in a high window.
6. **Recovery is part of consistency.** Never compensate for a missed day with a punishing session — that boom-and-bust cycle is what causes burnout. Steady consistency beats short bursts of intensity.

## The one line to remember

> Lower the bar so far that quitting feels harder than continuing. Compounding rewards *time in motion*, not peak days.
