# Fundamental Analysis Valuation Metrics

> The "is this business worth the price?" layer. Fundamental analysis values a company by its actual economics — earnings, cash flow, assets, growth, debt — not its price chart.

## The Core Question

A stock is a fractional ownership claim on a business. Fundamental analysis asks two things: **(1) what is the business worth?** and **(2) is the current price below that?** The gap between the two is your [[Margin of Safety (Finance)]].

## Valuation Ratios

| Metric | What it measures | Rule of thumb |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| **P/E** (price ÷ earnings per share) | $ paid per $1 of annual profit | No universal "good" value; compare to the company's *own history*, *sector peers*, and the *market*. High = priced for growth (or overvalued); low = cheap (or in decline). |
| **PEG** (P/E ÷ growth rate) | P/E adjusted for growth | < 1.0 may be undervalued for its growth; > 2.0 may be over-optimistic. |
| **P/B** (price ÷ book value) | Price vs. net asset value | Most meaningful for asset-heavy businesses — banks, insurers, REITs. |
| **P/S** (price ÷ sales) | Price vs. revenue | Used when earnings are negative/volatile (early-growth, cyclical). |
| **FCF yield** (free cash flow ÷ market cap) | Real cash the business throws off | Harder to manipulate than accounting earnings. |

(Source: [[Fundamental Analysis Valuation Metrics]] — Schwab, Fidelity, Bankrate, Britannica)

## What Drives Value Beyond Ratios

1. **Earnings quality & growth** — are profits growing, and are they real cash or accounting artifacts?
2. **Competitive moat** — pricing power, network effects, switching costs, scale. A moat protects future earnings.
3. **Balance sheet / debt** — leverage amplifies both returns and bankruptcy risk; check debt-to-equity and interest coverage.
4. **Management & capital allocation** — do they reinvest well, buy back shares at sane prices, avoid value-destroying acquisitions?
5. **Industry & macro position** — interest rates, sector cycle, regulation, competitive dynamics.

## Pitfalls

- **P/E is distorted** by buybacks, one-time items, leverage, and the economic cycle — never use one ratio alone. (confidence: high)
- A "cheap" stock can be a **value trap** — cheap *because* the business is dying.
- Ratios are backward/snapshot-looking; the price reflects the market's view of the *future*.

> [!gap] Valuation is judgment, not arithmetic. Two analysts with the same data routinely reach different fair values. This is precisely why [[Index Fund Investing]] beats ~90% of people who try (see [[SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index]]).
