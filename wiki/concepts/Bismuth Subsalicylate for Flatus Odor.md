# Bismuth Subsalicylate for Flatus Odor

**The single most effective odor-specific intervention.** Bismuth subsalicylate is the active ingredient in Pepto-Bismol and Kaopectate; bismuth subgallate is the related compound in Devrom ("the internal deodorant").

## How it works
Bismuth binds sulfide ions to form insoluble, non-volatile **bismuth sulfide**. With the H₂S locked up as a solid, far less escapes as gas. (This same reaction harmlessly blackens stool and sometimes the tongue.)

## Evidence
- **>95% reduction** in fecal H₂S release at **524 mg four times daily** for 3–7 days; effect is dose-dependent. (Source: [[Suarez 1998 - Bismuth Subsalicylate Decreases Hydrogen Sulfide]])
- AAFP rates it **very effective** for odor (binds >95% of sulfide gases). (Source: [[AAFP 2009 - Effective Management of Flatulence]])

## How to use it
- **Episodic, not chronic** — for known social situations (event, travel, date).
- Standard OTC dosing per label; the studied antiflatulence dose was 524 mg up to 4×/day.

> [!warning] Safety. Contains **salicylate** (avoid with aspirin allergy, in children/teens with viral illness → Reye's risk, and combine cautiously with blood thinners). Chronic/high-dose **bismuth** can cause reversible neurotoxicity (tremor, confusion, memory issues). Do not use daily long-term.

## Contrast with charcoal
**Oral** activated charcoal does *not* work — it saturates before reaching the colon (Source: [[Suarez 1999 - Failure of Activated Charcoal to Reduce Gas]]). **External** charcoal garments do work (briefs ~100%, pads 55–77%). So: bismuth internally, charcoal externally.
