# Psychology of Guilt

The true subject of *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]*: the punishment is not the legal sentence but the **crushing internal torment** that begins the instant the crime is done. The murder occupies only a few pages; the rest of the novel is [[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov's]] conscience destroying him from within — fever, delirium, paranoia, a compulsion to confess warring with a compulsion to hide.

Dostoevsky's insight is that guilt operates beneath and against the rational mind. Raskolnikov's intellect fully authorized the murder via [[The Extraordinary Man Theory]], yet some deeper faculty refuses to ratify it — he faints at the police station, cannot spend the stolen money, and is driven toward [[Sonya Marmeladov|Sonya]] and confession as the only relief. The eventual confession is experienced as release, not defeat: the intolerable thing was carrying the [[Alienation and Isolation|isolation]], not facing the consequences. Guilt here is an inescapable inner tribunal, more relentless than any court.

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
