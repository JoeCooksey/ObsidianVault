# Fragmentation-Tolerant Activities

The selection filter for what to do in interruption-heavy time: a **fragmentation-tolerant** activity is one whose value barely drops when it's chopped into pieces by interruptions. It's the practical inverse of [[Interruption Resumption Cost]].

## The three tests

An activity is fragmentation-tolerant if it passes these:

1. **Low resumption cost** — you can stop mid-stream and restart without rebuilding a big mental model. (A flashcard passes; a derivation fails.)
2. **The medium holds the state, not your head** — audio/video can rewind; a book holds your page; an app remembers progress. You don't pay to re-load context.
3. **Self-contained chunks** — the unit of work is small and *completable* (one card, one rep, one easy problem), so an interruption lands *between* units, not inside one.

## Why some "productive" activities fail the filter

Genuinely deep work is **anti**-fragmentation by nature: a math proof, debugging firmware, drafting an essay, or an edge-of-ability [[Deliberate Practice|deliberate-practice]] rep all hold a large, fragile representation in working memory. Doing them in interrupted gaps gives you the stress of deep work with none of the depth. **Save them for continuous blocks**; don't spend them on call gaps.

## The happy overlap with learning science

The activities that survive fragmentation are mostly the *same* ones the learning-science cluster already endorses for other reasons:

- [[Microlearning]] — short spaced sessions *are* distributed practice by construction.
- [[Spaced Repetition]] + [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]] — a flashcard is the canonical fragmentation-tolerant unit.
- Passive-input learning (audiobooks, lectures, podcasts) — high tolerance, lower density; good for *exposure*, weak for *skill* unless paired with retrieval.

So the interruption-heavy job isn't just survivable — its dead time is a near-perfect host for a daily-learning habit ([[Daily Learning Habit (Operating System)]]), provided you add the retrieval step that passive listening lacks.

## The catch

Fragmentation-tolerant ≠ high-value automatically. Passive consumption tolerates interruption *because* it demands little — which also makes it low-density ([[Active vs Passive Leisure]]). The sweet spot is the activity that is **both** chunk-sized **and** effortful: a flashcard you have to *retrieve*, a problem you have to *solve*, a sentence you have to *produce*.
