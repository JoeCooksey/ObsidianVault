# Vegetable Soup Meal Prep Guide

A complete week-long meal prep guide for blended vegetable soup — all vegetables available at Costco, boiled and blended with top-tier seasonings, portioned into individual containers for the full week.

---

## Why Blended Vegetable Soup is the Perfect Meal Prep

- **Easy to batch cook**: one large pot = 7–10 individual servings
- **Scales infinitely**: double the batch, same effort
- **No texture degradation**: blended soups hold their quality for 4–5 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen
- **Nutritionally dense**: delivers multiple vegetable servings per portion
- **Cheap per serving**: Costco bulk pricing makes this $1.50–$2.50/serving
- **Zero prep at mealtime**: grab container, microwave 2–3 min, done

## The Master Blended Vegetable Soup Formula

**Yield**: ~10 portions (16–20 oz each)
**Total active time**: 25 minutes
**Total cook time**: 40–50 minutes

### The Ratio Formula

Follow this structure for any combination of Costco vegetables:

```
1 part aromatic base (onion + celery + garlic)
2 parts starchy thickener (potato / sweet potato / squash / cauliflower)
2 parts flavor vegetables (broccoli / zucchini / carrot / bell pepper)
1 part finishing vegetables (spinach / kale — added last)
Broth to cover (roughly 6–8 cups for a large pot)
```

### The Master Recipe (Step-by-Step)

**Ingredients** (one large batch):
- 2 large yellow onions, roughly chopped
- 4 stalks celery, roughly chopped
- 8 cloves garlic, smashed (no need to mince — blender handles it)
- 4 large carrots, roughly chopped
- 2 russet potatoes (or 1 large sweet potato), peeled and cubed
- 1 head broccoli, broken into florets
- 2 zucchini, roughly chopped
- 2 cups butternut squash cubes (Costco pre-cut)
- 2–3 cups fresh spinach or kale (add at end)
- 6–8 cups vegetable or chicken broth
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- **Seasoning blend** (choose one profile below)

**Equipment needed**:
- Large pot (8 qt or larger — essential for batch cooking)
- Immersion blender (fastest method) OR countertop blender (blend in batches, let steam escape)
- Ladle
- 10 × 16–20 oz containers with tight lids

---

### Step 1: Build the Aromatic Base (5 min)
Heat olive oil in the pot over medium-high heat. Add onions, celery, and garlic. Cook 4–5 minutes until softened and translucent. Season with a pinch of salt — this unlocks flavor from the vegetables.

### Step 2: Add Starchy Vegetables (3 min)
Add potatoes, sweet potatoes, or butternut squash (whichever you are using). Stir and cook 3 minutes — this step begins softening them so they absorb flavor.

### Step 3: Add Remaining Vegetables (2 min)
Add carrots, broccoli, zucchini, bell peppers, mushrooms, or whichever flavor vegetables you chose. Stir everything together.

### Step 4: Add Broth and Seasoning (2 min)
Pour in 6–8 cups broth. The broth should cover vegetables by about 1 inch. Add your chosen spice blend (see Seasoning Profiles below). Bring to a boil.

### Step 5: Simmer Until Tender (25–35 min)
Reduce heat to medium-low, cover, and simmer 25–35 minutes until all vegetables are fork-tender — especially the potatoes and squash. The longer you cook, the more flavors meld.

### Step 6: Add Finishing Greens (2 min)
Turn off heat. Stir in spinach or kale. It will wilt from residual heat in 1–2 minutes — do not boil greens or they lose color and nutrients.

### Step 7: Blend (5 min)
- **Immersion blender**: blend directly in the pot until completely smooth. The immersion blender is the fastest and safest tool for hot soups.
- **Countertop blender**: blend in 2–3 batches. Fill no more than half full. Remove the lid's center cap and cover with a folded kitchen towel to let steam escape safely.
- **Texture preference**: blend 100% for silky soup, or blend 70–80% for a slightly chunky texture.

### Step 8: Taste and Adjust
Taste the soup. Add:
- More salt → enhances all flavors
- Squeeze of lemon juice → brightens and lifts the whole profile
- Black pepper → warmth
- A splash of broth if too thick

### Step 9: Cool and Portion
**Do NOT immediately seal hot soup.** Let cool 15–20 minutes, then ladle into containers. Sealing while hot traps steam and creates bacterial conditions.

Portion into 10 × 16–20 oz individual containers.

### Step 10: Store
- **Refrigerator**: 4–5 days (consume by Thursday if you prepped Sunday)
- **Freezer**: up to 3 months. Freeze the portions you won't eat by Day 4.

## Weekly Rotation Plan

Batch cook twice per week (Sunday + Wednesday) to keep soup fresh without relying on freezer.

| Day | Eat | Notes |
|-----|-----|-------|
| Sunday | Batch 1 — fresh | Cook + store 5 servings |
| Monday | Batch 1 — refrigerator | Microwave 2–3 min |
| Tuesday | Batch 1 — refrigerator | Last day for optimal freshness |
| Wednesday | Batch 2 — fresh | Mid-week cook, store 4 more |
| Thursday | Batch 2 — refrigerator | |
| Friday | Batch 2 — refrigerator | |
| Saturday | Freezer reserve OR eat out | Freeze any Day 4+ portions |

**Alternatively**: Make one double batch Sunday → freeze 5 portions, refrigerate 5 → pull frozen portions out night before.

## Container Selection Guide

### Best Options
| Container | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|-----------|------|------|----------|
| **Glass (16–20 oz mason jars or Pyrex)** | No odor/stain, microwave + oven safe, airtight | Heavy, can crack if overfilled and frozen | Refrigerator storage, reheating in microwave |
| **BPA-free plastic (meal prep containers)** | Lightweight, stackable, affordable | Stains over time, absorb odors | Short-term refrigerator use |
| **Freezer-safe plastic bags (gallon)** | Space-efficient in freezer | Cannot microwave directly, single-use | Freezer storage |

### Recommended Setup
- **5 × glass 20 oz containers** for refrigerator portions (reheat directly in microwave)
- **5 × freezer-safe containers or bags** for frozen reserve
- Always leave ½ inch headspace in containers — soup expands when frozen

### Airtight seal is critical
Reduces oxidation, prevents flavor absorption from fridge odors, and minimizes freezer burn.

## Reheating

| Method | Time | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| Microwave (glass/plastic container) | 2–3 min on high, stir halfway | Fastest; check temp before eating |
| Stovetop | 5 min over medium heat | Best for quality; stir frequently |
| From frozen (microwave) | 4–5 min, stir at 2-min mark | Defrost setting first if very solid |

**Tip**: Soups thicken in the fridge as starch gels. Add a splash of water or broth when reheating and stir — it returns to normal consistency instantly.

## Key Rules

1. **Rough chop is fine** — everything gets blended; you're not competing in a presentation competition
2. **Sauté aromatics first** — the mirepoix base (onion + celery + garlic) is non-negotiable for flavor depth; do not skip
3. **Potatoes = your thickener** — more potato = thicker soup; less = thinner; adjust every batch
4. **Add greens last, off-heat** — boiling spinach/kale destroys color and B vitamins; residual heat is enough to wilt
5. **Cool before sealing** — 15–20 min cool time; hot food sealed in containers grows bacteria faster
6. **Label every container** — date + soup name + variant; this matters when you have multiple in the freezer
7. **The lemon juice rule** — every blended vegetable soup benefits from acid at the end; one squeeze can transform a flat soup into a bright one
8. **Season at every stage** — a pinch of salt when sautéing, more in the broth, final adjustment after blending
