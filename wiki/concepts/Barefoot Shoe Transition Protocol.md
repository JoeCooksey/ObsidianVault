# Barefoot Shoe Transition Protocol

The single most important rule of barefoot shoes: **transition slowly.** The most common cause of injury is switching too fast. Decades of cushioning leave the foot and calf muscles, Achilles tendon, and plantar fascia deconditioned; they need weeks to months to adapt to the new load. (Source: [[Research - Barefoot Shoes Benefits and Best Brands]])

## Protocol

- **Start small:** 30–60 minutes per day in the barefoot shoes (casual walking, not running).
- **Ramp gradually:** add ~15–30 minutes every few days.
- **Full transition:** typically **2–3 months**, varying per person.
- **Expect** foot and calf soreness — that is adaptation. **Stop / scale back** if you feel actual pain (sore heels, Achilles, knees, hips).
- **Form:** light steps, upright posture, avoid heavy heel strikes (there is no cushion to absorb them).
- **For running:** the RCT-backed protocol increased time in minimalist shoes by ~5% per week; one review recommends most runners cap minimalist-shoe running at **~35% of training time** and reserve it for performance situations, because running-related pain rose with mileage. (Source: [[Knapik 2021 - Update on Minimalist Running Shoes]])
- **Pair with gait retraining** for the impact-reduction benefits — minimalist shoes alone change loading only modestly. (Source: [[Yang 2020 - Gait Retraining Minimalist Shoes]])

## Cautions

- Injury risk in minimalist shoes **rises with body weight.** (Source: [[Knapik 2021 - Update on Minimalist Running Shoes]])
- With **active plantar fasciitis**, walking barefoot on hard surfaces can worsen symptoms; barefoot footwear is better framed as *prevention* (foot strengthening) than as *treatment* of an acute flare. (Source: [[Research - Barefoot Shoes Benefits and Best Brands]])
- Choose a higher-stack "gateway" model (e.g. [[Lems Shoes]] ~11 mm) if a 0–4 mm sole feels too aggressive at first.
