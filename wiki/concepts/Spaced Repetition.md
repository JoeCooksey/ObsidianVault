# Spaced Repetition

**Review at increasing intervals, just before you would forget.** Spreading the same total study time across days beats cramming it into one session — this is *distributed practice*, the second of the two **high-utility** techniques in the Dunlosky meta-analysis. (Source: [[Dunlosky 2013 — Improving Students' Learning]])

## The forgetting curve

Hermann Ebbinghaus (1885) measured how fast we forget: within ~20 minutes he had lost ~42% of memorized material, ~56% within an hour, and ~two-thirds within a day. Memory decays exponentially **unless reinforced**.

Each well-timed review flattens the curve: forgetting slows down and the memory lasts longer before the next review is needed.

## Why spacing works

- Letting a memory partly decay before retrieving it makes the retrieval *effortful* — a [[Desirable Difficulties|desirable difficulty]] that strengthens it more than an easy review would.
- The gaps between sessions let memories **consolidate**; sleep does much of this consolidation work. (Source: [[Make It Stick (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel)]])
- Massed practice (cramming) produces fast short-term gains that evaporate; spacing produces slower-feeling but durable learning.

## The intervals

Piotr Woźniak formalized optimal intervals in the **SM-2 algorithm** (SuperMemo, 1980s), still the basis of most flashcard apps. After each *successful* recall the interval expands:

```
1 day → 6 days → ~15 days → ~37 days → ~90 days → ...
```

Each card carries an **easiness factor** that lengthens or shortens its schedule based on how well you recalled it — so hard cards come back sooner, easy cards drift further out.

## How to do it

1. **Pair it with retrieval.** Spacing only works on *recall*, not re-reading — every repetition must be an [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)|active recall]] attempt.
2. **Use a system that schedules for you.** [[Anki]]-style apps run SM-2/FSRS automatically; you grade each answer and the app picks the next date.
3. **For non-flashcard skills:** revisit a topic after 1 day, then a few days, then a week+ — re-derive, re-solve, re-explain rather than re-read.
4. **Front-load when stakes are near**, but never collapse everything into one session.

## Where it fits

Spacing is the **retention** pillar of evidence-based learning. The minimal high-leverage stack is **[[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]] + Spaced Repetition + [[Interleaving (Learning)]]**. (Source: [[Research: How to Learn Anything (The Science of Learning)]])

> [!tip] One-line rule
> Same hours, spread out, learns more than the same hours crammed together — and it isn't close.
