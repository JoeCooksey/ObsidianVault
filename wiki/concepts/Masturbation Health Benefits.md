# Masturbation Health Benefits

Masturbation is a normal, common, and physically safe activity. Mainstream sexual medicine recognizes several benefits and **no severe physical side effects**. (Source: [[Masturbation Facts and Benefits — Cleveland Clinic]])

## Documented benefits (medium–high confidence)
- **Stress and mood**: orgasm releases dopamine and oxytocin, counteracting cortisol → reduced stress, improved mood, better sleep, mild pain relief (including menstrual cramps).
- **Sexual self-knowledge**: increases likelihood of orgasm with partners; associated with higher self-esteem and sexual empowerment (especially in women).
- **Genitourinary**: reduces vaginal dryness in older adults; ejaculation may relieve prostatic congestion.
- **Total ejaculation frequency** is associated with lower prostate cancer risk, though this tracks *ejaculation*, not masturbation specifically → [[Ejaculation Frequency and Prostate Cancer]].

## Debunked myths
Masturbation does **not** cause vision loss, mental illness, infertility, decreased sperm count, erectile dysfunction, penis shrinkage, or lowered libido. There is no evidence it "drains" energy or testosterone in any lasting way.

## When it becomes a problem
Only when **compulsive** — interfering with work, relationships, or responsibilities (see [[Compulsive Sexual Behavior Disorder (CSBD)]]) — or when distress arises from **shame/guilt** rooted in personal or religious beliefs (see [[Moral Incongruence (Pornography)]]). Rough technique can cause temporary chafing or reduced sensitivity.

## Bottom line
For most people, masturbation is benign-to-beneficial. Harm is tied to *compulsivity* or *value conflict*, not the act itself.
