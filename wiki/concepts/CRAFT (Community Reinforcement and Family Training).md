# CRAFT (Community Reinforcement and Family Training)

The **evidence-based method for helping a drinking/using loved one who has NOT asked for help** — and who resists it. Developed by [[Robert J. Meyers]]. It replaces confrontation and ultimatums with **invitation, positive reinforcement, and calm boundaries**.

> [!key] Core stance: you cannot nag, plead, or threaten someone into changing. You can change *your own* behavior so that being sober and being close to you becomes more rewarding than the drinking.

## Why it beats the alternatives
Across **4 randomized clinical trials**, CRAFT got treatment-refusing drinkers into care **~64–67% of the time vs ~30%** for confrontation-based interventions or Al-Anon/detachment (high confidence). (Source: [[CRAFT — Advaita Integrated Medicine]], [[Robert J. Meyers — CRAFT Evidence]].)

## The core tactics
1. **Reinforce sober time.** Give praise, attention, warmth, and quality time *when he is sober*. Make connection contingent on sobriety, not on the drinking.
2. **Withdraw warmth (not love) during use.** When he's drinking, calmly disengage from positive interaction — no fight, no lecture, just step back. (Reward sobriety, ignore intoxication.)
3. **Allow natural consequences.** Stop shielding him from the results of drinking (don't cover, clean up, or smooth it over). The consequence teaches; the rescue enables.
4. **Communicate without confrontation.** No nagging, pleading, or threatening (these trigger [[Psychological Reactance]]). Use [[Motivational Interviewing]] — open questions that let *him* voice reasons to change.
5. **Wait for windows.** Don't raise it when he's drunk or defensive. Pick a calm, sober, private moment.
6. **Take care of yourself.** CRAFT family members get less depressed, anxious, and angry **even when the loved one never changes** — because they reclaim their own life. This is the point, not a consolation prize.

## The communication formula
A CRAFT "I-statement" request: **be brief, be positive, name a specific behavior, name your feeling, offer to help, take partial responsibility.** Example: *"I'd love to watch the game with you Saturday morning before anything else — it's my favorite time with you."* (invitation) beats *"You drink too much, you should stop."* (attack).

*Sources: [[CRAFT — Advaita Integrated Medicine]], [[Robert J. Meyers — CRAFT Evidence]].*
