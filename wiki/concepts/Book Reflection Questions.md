# Book Reflection Questions

The **guiding-question bank** for book journaling. These prompts turn a blank page into a finished entry — answer the relevant ones and you have written your notes. Use during reading (to stay active) and after (to reflect). Pairs with [[Book Note-Taking System]].

## The 4 Universal Questions ([[Mortimer Adler|Adler]])

For any serious book, [[Mortimer Adler|Adler's]] analytical-reading questions (Source: [[Reading Retention Methods]]):

1. **What is the book about as a whole?** (the single thesis)
2. **What is being said in detail, and how?** (the main arguments and how they're built)
3. **Is the book true, in whole or part?** (your critical judgment)
4. **What of it?** (so what — what does this mean for *me*?)

If you answer only four questions, answer these.

## Comprehension — "Did I get it?"

- In one sentence, what is the author's central argument?
- What are the 3 most important ideas?
- What knowledge did I gain that I didn't have before? (Source: litlovers nonfiction discussion questions)
- Could I explain the core idea to a 12-year-old? (the Feynman test — Source: [[Reading Retention Methods]])
- What was memorable — which specific passages struck me as significant, profound, or disturbing?

## Critical / Analytical — "Is it true?"

- What central issue does the author explore, and what evidence supports it? Is it convincing? (Source: litlovers)
- Did the author provide enough research, and address counterarguments fairly?
- **What biases might the author have, and how could they have shaped the book?** (Source: search summary)
- How would I characterize the author's tone and style?
- What solutions does the author propose? What are the implications?
- How controversial are the issues raised — and where do I stand?

## Personal / Connection — "What does it mean to me?"

- Do these issues affect my life, directly or indirectly?
- Has this broadened my perspective on a hard personal or societal question?
- What did this book change my mind about, or confirm?
- What does this connect to — other books, my own experience, notes already in the vault?

## Application — "What will I do?" (the most important set)

- **What is the ONE thing I will do differently this week?** (Source: [[Reading Application Framework]])
- Where in my life does this apply *right now*?
- What are my next steps — what do I want to read, learn, or try next?
- Who should I share this with or teach this to?

## Fiction-Specific

- What are the major themes? How do they develop?
- Whose arc changed most, and why?
- What did the book make me feel or see differently?
- Which passage would I underline and return to?

## How to Use This Page

1. **During reading**: keep questions 1, "what's memorable," and "where does this apply" in mind — they keep reading active.
2. **After each chapter / the book**: answer the relevant block in writing — that *is* your [[Book Note-Taking System|journal entry]].
3. **On review**: re-answer "what is the thesis / one action" from memory before looking — active recall (Source: [[Reading Retention Methods]]).
