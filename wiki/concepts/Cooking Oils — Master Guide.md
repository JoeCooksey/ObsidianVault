# Cooking Oils — Master Guide

Which oil to use for **each type of cooking** plus **the best oils for raw/finishing use** — built on the actual science of oil stability, not just smoke-point folklore. Companion to the [[Healthiest Cooking Methods — Master Guide]].

## The One Idea That Fixes Most Mistakes
> [!tip] **Smoke point is a weak guide. Oxidative stability is the real one.** An oil's resistance to breaking down under heat depends on its **fat type** (saturated + monounsaturated = stable; polyunsaturated = fragile) and its **antioxidants** — *not* the temperature at which it visibly smokes. (Source: [[De Alzaa 2018 — Chemical and Physical Changes in Oils During Heating]]; [[Smoke Point (Cooking Oils)]])

The headline surprise: **extra-virgin olive oil is one of the *most* stable cooking oils** despite a modest smoke point, because it is low in polyunsaturated fat and rich in polyphenol antioxidants. In controlled heating tests it produced **lower** harmful polar compounds and trans fats than canola, grapeseed, or rice bran oil. (Source: [[De Alzaa 2018 — Chemical and Physical Changes in Oils During Heating]])

## Quick Pick — Best Oil by Cooking Method

| Cooking method | Temp zone | Best oils | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deep-frying** | 350–375°F | High-oleic sunflower/safflower, peanut, refined avocado; **EVOO** if cost is no object | EVOO outlasted canola & peanut in frying-life tests (Source: [[De Alzaa 2018 — Chemical and Physical Changes in Oils During Heating]]). Peanut is the restaurant default. |
| **Searing / stir-fry / wok** | 400°F+ | **Refined avocado** (480–520°F), refined peanut, high-oleic sunflower | High smoke point *and* stable. Refined avocado is the all-purpose high-heat champ. |
| **Roasting / baking** | 375–425°F | **Extra-virgin olive oil**, refined avocado, ghee | EVOO is fine here — its antioxidants protect it well past its 320–375°F nominal smoke point. |
| **Everyday sauté (medium)** | ~325–375°F | **Extra-virgin olive oil** (the default) | The single best all-rounder for home cooking: stable + healthy + flavorful. |
| **Low/gentle sauté, eggs** | <325°F | EVOO, butter, ghee | Low heat keeps butter from burning and minimizes [[Heat-Formed Food Toxins (HCAs, PAHs, AGEs, Acrylamide)\|AGEs]]. |
| **Baking (cakes/quick breads)** | n/a (in batter) | Neutral: light olive, refined avocado, canola; or butter for flavor | Flavor + texture matter more than stability since oil is shielded inside the batter. |
| **No heat — dressings & finishing** | raw | **EVOO**, flaxseed, walnut, toasted sesame | See [[Finishing Oils (Cold-Use Oils)]]. |

> [!tip] **If you keep two oils:** a bottle of **extra-virgin olive oil** (everyday cooking + finishing) and **refined avocado oil** (high-heat searing/frying) covers ~95% of a home kitchen.

## Smoke Point Reference (approximate °F)
Refined versions are higher than unrefined because refining strips the free fatty acids and impurities that smoke first. (Source: [[Smoke Point (Cooking Oils)]])

| Oil / fat | Smoke point | Best role |
|---|---|---|
| Refined avocado | 480–520°F | High-heat sear/fry |
| Safflower (refined) | 475–500°F | Deep-fry |
| Canola (refined) | 400–475°F | Neutral all-purpose |
| Light/refined olive | 390–470°F | High-heat neutral |
| Peanut (refined) | 450°F | Deep-fry |
| Sunflower (refined) | 450°F | Fry (choose high-oleic) |
| Soybean ("vegetable oil") | ~450°F | Neutral all-purpose |
| Corn | 400–450°F | Neutral |
| **Ghee** | ~485°F | High-heat, flavor |
| Grapeseed | 420°F | Sauté (high PUFA — less stable) |
| Sesame (refined) | 410°F | Stir-fry |
| Refined coconut | ~400°F | Medium heat |
| **Butter** | 302–350°F | Low-heat sauté, flavor |
| **Extra-virgin olive oil** | 320–375°F | Everyday cooking + finishing |
| Unrefined coconut / sesame / peanut | ~350°F | Medium-low |
| Beef tallow | ~420°F | High-heat, flavor |
| Lard | ~370°F | Medium-high |
| **Flaxseed** | ~225°F | **No-heat only** |
| Walnut | ~320°F | Finishing (don't heat hard) |

(Sources: WebstaurantStore smoke-point list; Colorado State KRNC; [[Smoke Point (Cooking Oils)]])

## Best Oils for Toppings & Finishing (Raw)
Use these *off the heat* — heat destroys their delicate fats and aromas. Full detail in [[Finishing Oils (Cold-Use Oils)]].

- **Extra-virgin olive oil** — the universal finisher: drizzle on salads, soups, pasta, bread, roasted veg. Polyphenols + flavor survive when not cooked.
- **Flaxseed oil** — highest plant omega-3 (ALA); smoke point ~225°F so **never cook it**. Drizzle on salads, grain bowls, yogurt, smoothies. Keep refrigerated; goes rancid fast.
- **Walnut oil** — nutty finisher for salads, roasted veg, desserts; also omega-3-rich and heat-fragile.
- **Toasted sesame oil** — a *seasoning*, not a cooking oil; add at the end of stir-fries and to dressings/noodles.
- **Cold-pressed / unrefined** versions are best for raw use — they keep the antioxidants and flavor that refining removes.

## The Saturated-Fat Oils (Coconut, Butter, Ghee, Tallow)
Very stable to heat (mostly saturated), but the **health** picture is mixed, not settled.

- **Coconut oil** ~90% saturated; **ghee** ~50%; **butter** ~63%; **beef tallow** ~50%. (Source: Levels; Parker County Beef Co.)
- A review of >635,000 people found butter did **not** raise heart-disease risk and was tied to slightly lower type-2-diabetes odds — but most guidance still favors **olive oil** as the everyday default over tropical/animal saturated fats. (Source: Levels)
- **Practical:** ghee and tallow are excellent *high-heat, high-flavor* fats; use saturated fats as flavor accents, keep EVOO as the workhorse.

## The Seed-Oil Question (short version)
Despite online alarm, major health bodies (AHA, Harvard, Johns Hopkins) find **no good evidence that seed oils harm health**; higher linoleic-acid (omega-6) intake is linked to **lower** all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. The real issue is that seed oils are abundant in **ultra-processed foods** — the problem is the junk, not the oil. Full treatment: [[Seed Oils Health Debate]]. (Source: [[AHA 2024 — No Reason to Avoid Seed Oils]])

## Hard Rules (do these regardless of oil)
> [!warning] **Never reuse frying oil**, and **discard any oil that has started smoking** — both mean oxidation products and harmful aldehydes are forming. (Source: Colorado State KRNC)

- Buy oil in **dark glass / tin**, store **cool and sealed**, away from light — light and heat oxidize oil on the shelf.
- Don't preheat oil until it smokes; heat to shimmering, not smoking.
- Match the bottle to the job: cheap refined oil for high-heat frying, good EVOO for everyday cooking and finishing.
- The more fragile the oil (high PUFA: grapeseed, sunflower, flax, walnut), the less heat it should ever see.

## One-Glance Cheat Sheet
- **Default everyday oil:** extra-virgin olive oil (cook *and* finish).
- **High-heat/frying:** refined avocado or peanut (or EVOO if you don't mind the cost).
- **Flavor fats:** ghee, butter, tallow — use as accents.
- **Finishing/raw only:** flax, walnut, toasted sesame, EVOO.
- **Never:** reuse oil, heat flax/walnut, or cook past the smoke point.
