# Food Health Tier List — Macronutrients

Ranks carbohydrate, protein, and fat sources by health value within each macro category. Criteria: nutrient density, fiber/micronutrient payload, glycemic and inflammatory impact, and degree of processing. The biggest lever is **whole vs. processed** — an S-tier carb beats a D-tier carb by more than carbs differ from fats as categories.

---

## 🍞 Carbohydrates

| Tier | Foods | Why |
|------|-------|-----|
| **S** | Non-starchy vegetables (broccoli, spinach, peppers), berries, lentils, beans, chickpeas | High fiber, low glycemic impact, dense in micronutrients and polyphenols; hard to overeat |
| **A** | Oats, quinoa, sweet potato, whole fruit (apples, oranges), barley, farro | Whole-food complex carbs + fiber; steady energy, minimal processing |
| **B** | Brown/wild rice, whole-grain bread and pasta, potatoes with skin | Decent nutrients but more starch-dense; portion-sensitive |
| **C** | White rice, white bread, most cereals, fruit juice | Refined, fiber stripped, faster blood-sugar spike; acceptable around exercise |
| **D** | Soda, candy, pastries, added sugar | Pure rapid glucose/fructose, no nutrients; drives inflammation and insulin dysregulation |

---

## 🥩 Protein

| Tier | Foods | Why |
|------|-------|-----|
| **S** | Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel), eggs, lentils/legumes | Complete (or fiber-rich) protein + omega-3s, choline, micronutrients |
| **A** | Skinless poultry, Greek yogurt, tofu/tempeh, shellfish, cottage cheese | Lean, high-quality, minimally processed |
| **B** | Lean beef, pork loin, cheese, whey/plant protein powder | Good protein; saturated fat or processing nudges them down; meat quality matters |
| **C** | Fattier cuts of red meat, conventional dairy in excess | Fine in moderation; saturated-fat load accumulates |
| **D** | Processed meats — bacon, hot dogs, deli meats, sausages | WHO Group 1/2 carcinogens; nitrates, sodium, linked to colorectal cancer risk |

---

## 🥑 Fats

| Tier | Foods | Why |
|------|-------|-----|
| **S** | Extra-virgin olive oil, avocado, nuts (walnuts, almonds), fatty fish, seeds (chia, flax) | Mono/polyunsaturated + omega-3s; anti-inflammatory, cardioprotective |
| **A** | Nut butters (no added oil), olives, whole eggs | Whole-food fats with extra nutrients |
| **B** | Coconut oil, butter, full-fat dairy, grass-fed animal fat | Saturated fat — neutral-to-fine in moderation; evidence still debated |
| **C** | Refined seed/vegetable oils (soybean, corn) used heavily, palm oil | High omega-6, often in processed/fried food; balance matters |
| **D** | Industrial trans fats / partially hydrogenated oils, deep-fried fast food | The one fat with near-universal agreement: actively harmful, raises heart disease risk |

---

## Key Principles

1. **Whole vs. processed is the dominant variable** — it matters more than which macro a food belongs to.
2. **Fiber is the through-line of every S/A-tier carb.** If a carb has had its fiber stripped, it drops tiers.
3. **Omega-3 content carries the fat list** and is also what elevates the top protein sources (fatty fish).
4. **Minimal processing carries protein** — quality of the source (wild fish, pastured eggs) separates tiers.
5. **Two near-universal "avoid" verdicts:** industrial trans fats and processed meats.
6. Context adjusts the list — athletes can use C-tier fast carbs around training; sedentary individuals should lean S/A.
