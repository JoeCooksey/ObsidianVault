# Cortisol Complete Guide

Cortisol is the body's primary **glucocorticoid** stress hormone, made in the adrenal cortex and released under control of the **HPA axis** (hypothalamus → pituitary ACTH → adrenal). The "cortisol masterclass" framing comes from [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]] (Aug 2025): the central reframe is that **cortisol is not "bad" — most cortisol-related problems come from a disrupted *rhythm*, not from cortisol itself** (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]).

The whole game is **timing**: you *want* cortisol high in the morning and low at night. So the two practical questions split cleanly into:
- **Raise / properly time morning cortisol** → [[Ways to Raise Morning Cortisol (Tier List)]]
- **Reduce evening / chronic cortisol** → [[Ways to Reduce Cortisol (Tier List)]]

---

## What Cortisol Does

(Source: [[Cortisol — Cleveland Clinic]])
- **Mobilizes energy** — raises blood glucose, directs glucose/fat metabolism (liver, muscle, fat, pancreas).
- **Runs the stress response** — released after the fast adrenaline "fight-or-flight" wave to sustain the response.
- **Controls inflammation** — in short bursts it is anti-inflammatory and immune-supportive; chronically elevated it suppresses immunity.
- **Sets blood pressure** — high cortisol → high BP; low cortisol → low BP.
- **Anchors the sleep-wake cycle** — peaks at waking, bottoms at bedtime.

---

## The Cortisol Rhythm (the core mental model)

Cortisol follows a strong **circadian rhythm** set by the brain's master clock (suprachiasmatic nucleus, SCN): it rises in the last hours of sleep, **peaks ~30–45 min after waking**, then declines all day to a nadir in the late evening (Source: [[Stalder et al 2025 — Cortisol Awakening Response (Endocrine Reviews)]]).

```
Cortisol
  high │      ╱╲  ← Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR): +50–156% in 30–45 min
       │     ╱  ╲___
       │    ╱       ╲____
       │   ╱             ╲_____
   low │__╱                    ╲______ ← nadir (late evening / early sleep)
       └──────────────────────────────
        wake          midday        bed
```

### Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR)
The CAR is a distinct burst on top of the rhythm — the first big cortisol pulse of the day, triggered by the **sleep→wake transition itself**, not by stressors (Source: [[Stalder et al 2025 — Cortisol Awakening Response (Endocrine Reviews)]]).
- **Magnitude:** cortisol rises **50–156%** in the first 30–45 minutes after waking (healthy adults).
- **Dual control:** HPA axis pathway + direct sympathetic nerve input to the adrenal gland; modulated by light, sleep timing, and anticipation of the day.
- **A robust CAR** = healthy, well-calibrated HPA axis → better focus, memory, mood regulation, immune modulation.
- **A blunted CAR** = chronic stress, burnout, PTSD, and is linked to cardiometabolic, autoimmune, and psychiatric conditions.

> [!note] A flat/blunted rhythm is the real enemy — not "high cortisol." The goal is a **steep morning peak and a low evening trough.**

## Burnout: two opposite cortisol signatures

Huberman frames burnout as two phases that need **opposite** interventions (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]):

| Phase | Cortisol pattern | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Early-phase burnout** | Elevated morning cortisol, "wired and tired" | Lower the spike: NSDR/Yoga Nidra, manage stress, keep morning routine but don't over-stimulate |
| **Late-phase burnout** | Flattened / depleted cortisol, no morning peak | Rebuild the peak: morning sunlight, exercise, timed caffeine; protect the evening |

This is why "lower your cortisol" is incomplete advice — if your rhythm is *flat*, you need to **raise** the morning end, not crush it further.

## When Cortisol Is Genuinely Too High or Too Low (clinical)

These are medical conditions — distinct from everyday rhythm tuning (Source: [[Cortisol — Cleveland Clinic]]).

**Hypercortisolism (Cushing syndrome)** — chronically high cortisol.
- *Causes:* long-term high-dose corticosteroid meds (prednisone, dexamethasone); pituitary or adrenal tumors (excess ACTH).
- *Signs:* central/facial weight gain ("moon face"), purple stretch marks, thin limbs with muscle weakness, high blood sugar → type 2 diabetes, high BP, weakened bones.

**Hypocortisolism (adrenal insufficiency / Addison's disease)** — chronically low cortisol.
- *Causes:* autoimmune destruction of adrenals (primary/Addison's), underactive pituitary (secondary), or **abrupt stopping of long-term steroids**.
- *Signs:* fatigue, unintended weight loss, loss of appetite, low blood pressure, dizziness on standing, salt craving, skin darkening.

> [!warning] Addison's disease is **life-threatening without hormone (corticosteroid) replacement.** Natural methods do not treat true adrenal insufficiency. Persistent symptoms of either extreme = see a doctor; do not self-diagnose from a single cortisol reading.

## How It's Measured
Cortisol can be tested in **blood, saliva, urine (24-hr), or hair** (hair reflects chronic exposure over months). Because of the steep rhythm, *timing of the sample matters* — a morning and an evening value together say more than one number. The CAR is measured as the rise across the first 30–45 min after waking.
