# Shoulder Instability Conservative Rehab

Non-surgical management of a loose/popping shoulder. It is the **first-line** option for many people and the **definitive** answer for some (especially atraumatic/multidirectional instability and lower-demand patients), but it has limits for a structurally torn labrum.

## What the program targets
- **Rotator cuff strengthening** — the cuff dynamically centers the ball in the socket (the "dynamic stabilizers" compensating for damaged static stabilizers).
- **Scapular stabilization** (periscapular: serratus anterior, lower trapezius, rhomboids) — a well-positioned shoulder blade keeps the socket under the ball.
- **Proprioception / neuromuscular control** — retrain the shoulder to react before it slips.
- **Activity modification** — temporarily avoid the provocative position (abduction + external rotation) and apprehension-triggering loads.
- **NSAIDs** for pain as needed.

## What it can and can't do
- **Can:** reduce symptoms, improve control and function, and is often enough for atraumatic/multidirectional instability and for people who can avoid at-risk activities.
- **Can't:** re-attach a torn labrum or replace lost bone. In **young, active patients with a traumatic [[Bankart Lesion]]**, conservative care leaves a **~53–67% re-dislocation rate** vs ~8–10% with surgery — so PT alone frequently fails to stop the popping in that subgroup. (Source: [[Research - Rotator Cuff Tear and Recurrent Shoulder Instability]])

## Evidence nuance for rotator cuff (not instability)
For ordinary degenerative cuff **pain**, the large GRASP RCT found a structured supervised exercise course was **no better than a single good advice session**, and steroid injections gave no long-term benefit — i.e., for cuff tendinopathy, simple home exercise + advice is reasonable. This does **not** apply to traumatic instability. (Source: [[GRASP Trial 2021 — Exercise vs Advice for Rotator Cuff Disorders]])

> [!note] A reasonable plan for many: a focused ~6–12 week PT block (cuff + scapular + proprioception). If the shoulder keeps subluxing/dislocating despite it — especially if young/active with a labral tear on MRI — that's the signal to discuss stabilization surgery rather than repeating PT indefinitely.
