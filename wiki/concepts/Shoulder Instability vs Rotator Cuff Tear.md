# Shoulder Instability vs Rotator Cuff Tear

These are two **different problems** in the same joint, and the distinction matters because the fixes differ. A shoulder that **"pops out" or "gives way" is the signature of instability — not a rotator cuff tear.**

## Two different structures

| | Rotator cuff | Labrum / capsule (instability) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | 4 muscles + tendons (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) that wrap the ball and keep it centered | A rim of cartilage (labrum) + ligamentous capsule that deepen the socket and act as a "doorstop" |
| Typical symptom | **Pain, weakness, loss of range** (esp. overhead, reaching, lying on it) | **Popping/slipping out, apprehension, a feeling of the joint shifting**, dead-arm |
| Classic cause | Degeneration with age, or a single overload | A traumatic **dislocation/subluxation** tearing the labrum (a [[Bankart Lesion]]) |

## Why "tore it once and it pops out ever since" = instability

When a shoulder dislocates the first time, it usually tears the **anterior-inferior labrum (Bankart lesion), present in ~90% of traumatic anterior dislocations**, and stretches the capsule. That damaged "doorstop" never fully reseals on its own, so the ball keeps sliding out — recurrent instability. The phrase "pops out" points here, even if it was called a "rotator cuff" injury at the time. (Source: [[Bankart Lesion]])

## They can coexist
Rotator cuff tears **do** occur alongside traumatic instability — about **25.7% incidence**, rising sharply in patients **over 40–60**, where a dislocation tears the cuff instead of (or as well as) the labrum. So both can be true at once. In a person who keeps dislocating, the labrum/capsule is the usual culprit; in an older patient with one dislocation then weakness, suspect a cuff tear. (Source: [[Research - Rotator Cuff Tear and Recurrent Shoulder Instability]])

> [!note] Practical upshot: the *right* test is an **MRI (often MR-arthrogram)** to see whether the labrum, the capsule, the cuff, or bone (glenoid/Hill-Sachs) is the problem — because PT-only vs surgery depends entirely on which one it is.

## Why it changes the answer
- **Instability from a labral tear** → strengthening helps symptoms but does **not** re-attach the labrum; recurrent dislocators often need a surgical repair to actually stop the popping. (Source: [[Arthroscopic Bankart Repair]])
- **Degenerative cuff tear / tendinopathy** → conservative care (exercise/advice) is often first-line and frequently sufficient. (Source: [[GRASP Trial 2021 — Exercise vs Advice for Rotator Cuff Disorders]])
