# Saladino Grass-Fed Meat Argument

Why [[Paul Saladino]] calls grass-fed ruminant meat "the best food for humans" — his five-part case, with an evidence check on each part.

## His Case

**1. Bioavailable nutrient density.** Red meat delivers complete protein, heme iron, zinc, B12, creatine, carnosine, choline in forms humans absorb far better than plant equivalents — with zero antinutrients blocking uptake. Organs multiply this: liver is "nature's multivitamin" (Source: [[Saladino Animal-Based Diet Official Guide]]).

**2. Grass-fed beats grain-fed on micronutrients.** More vitamin E, beta-carotene, CoQ10, vitamin C (his figure: ~11.3 vs ~7.3 mg/lb), better omega-6:3 ratio, more CLA (Source: [[Saladino Is Grass-Fed Meat Really Worth It]]).

**3. The contaminant argument (his real centerpiece).** Feedlot grain carries glyphosate, 2,4-D, atrazine (xenoestrogen), zearalenone, and dioxins into grain-fed animals; grass-fed bypasses the grain pathway entirely.

**4. Evolutionary fit.** Humans ate ruminants for ~2 million years; brain growth tracked hunting; meat is the food we're adapted to, while agricultural plants are recent.

**5. No plant defense chemicals.** Animals defend by running, plants by chemistry — meat carries none of the [[Antinutrients-Plant-Defense-Compounds]] he objects to.

## Evidence Check

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Meat is highly nutrient-dense and bioavailable | **True** — uncontroversial; heme iron/zinc/B12 bioavailability is real |
| Liver/organs extremely nutrient-dense | **True** — though "0.5 oz/day" guards against the real vitamin-A ceiling |
| Grass-fed micronutrient edge | **True but tiny** — differences are real, absolute amounts small (his own vitamin C delta is ~4 mg/lb; an orange has ~70 mg). Omega-3 edge = ~0.015 g vs 0.003 g per 3 oz — need ~100 servings to match one salmon serving (Source: [[Grass-Fed vs Grain-Fed Beef (Healthline + PMC Review)]]) |
| Contaminant avoidance | **Plausible, poorly quantified** — mechanistically reasonable ("likely end up in the muscle meat" is his own hedge); no measured-residue comparison showing health-relevant doses in grain-fed beef |
| Evolutionary fit | **Half-true** — meat mattered in human evolution, but every well-studied forager population also ate substantial plants; "adapted to eat meat" ≠ "adapted to eat mostly meat" |
| "Best single food for humans" | **Not supported** — nutrient-dense ≠ sufficient (no vitamin C at scale, no fiber, no folate depth); the populations with best measured outcomes eat meat as a component, not a base |

## Bottom Line

The defensible core: **ruminant meat is a genuinely excellent protein/micronutrient source, and grass-fed is a modest quality upgrade bought mainly for what it avoids, not what it adds** — consistent with the vault's earlier sourcing verdict that grass-fed is "over-hyped for health, fine for taste/ethics" ([[Animal Product Sourcing (Grass-Fed, Pasture-Raised, Wild-Caught)]]). The leap from "excellent food" to "best food, and plants are toxic" is where the evidence stops following him.
