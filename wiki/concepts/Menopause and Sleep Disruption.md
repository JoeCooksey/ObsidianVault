# Menopause and Sleep Disruption

The peri- and post-menopausal transition is a major hormonal driver of frequent nocturnal and early-morning awakenings in women (confidence: high — multiple peer-reviewed reviews).

## Mechanisms

- **Declining estradiol and progesterone** disrupt CNS pathways and circadian regulation of sleep; lower estradiol and higher FSH independently predict awakenings, even after accounting for hot flashes and mood (Source: [[Sleep and the Menopausal Transition — PMC Reviews]]).
- **Reduced melatonin**: endogenous melatonin declines with age and drops significantly at menopause, weakening circadian signaling and sleep consolidation.
- **Vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes)**: women with moderate/severe hot flashes are ~3× more likely to have frequent night awakenings. Notably, many women *wake just before* a hot flash — the same brain change may trigger both.

## Management

- [[CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia)|CBT-I]] remains first-line and is effective in this population.
- Cooling the [[Sleep Environment Optimization|sleep environment]] addresses vasomotor awakenings.
- Hormone therapy and other medical options are discussed with a clinician.

> [!gap] Whether menopause-related melatonin supplementation meaningfully reduces awakenings is not firmly established — evidence is mixed.
