# St. Petersburg as Setting

The city functions almost as a character in *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]*. Dostoevsky's [[St. Petersburg as Setting|St. Petersburg]] is not the imperial capital of grand facades but the summer slum — airless heat, stench, dust, filthy canals, cramped drinking dens, and coffin-sized rooms like [[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov's]] garret.

The oppressive, claustrophobic environment mirrors and intensifies Raskolnikov's mental disintegration: the heat and squalor press on him, the crowds heighten his [[Alienation and Isolation|isolation]], and the labyrinth of streets and staircases becomes an externalization of his feverish, trapped psychology. Poverty in this city is not a backdrop but an active force that grinds down the Marmeladovs, corners [[Dunya (Avdotya Raskolnikova)|Dunya]], and helps push Raskolnikov toward his crime.

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
