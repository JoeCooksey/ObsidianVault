# Post-Acute Withdrawal Syndrome (PAWS)

The **second wave** of alcohol withdrawal. After acute physical withdrawal (days 1–7: shakes, sweating, nausea) subsides, a longer **emotional and cognitive** phase sets in — and it's the phase where depression actually shows up.

> [!key] Counter-intuitive but well-documented: people often feel *worse* emotionally in weeks 2–12 of sobriety than in week 1. The body recovers first; the brain's mood chemistry takes much longer.

## Symptoms
Low mood and depression, anxiety, irritability, mood instability, fatigue, hopelessness, trouble concentrating, sleep disturbance, and **anhedonia** (inability to feel pleasure — see [[Anhedonia and Dopamine Recovery in Early Sobriety]]).

## Timeline
- **Days 1–7** — acute, mostly physical withdrawal.
- **Weeks 2–12** — the most common window for significant depression; this is peak PAWS.
- **Months 3–6** — mood can *transiently worsen* here as the brain keeps recalibrating, then turns.
- **6+ months** — most people report clearly improved mood and energy.
- Sleep, anxiety, and mood instability can linger up to ~2 years after heavy long-term use (the heavier/longer the use, the longer PAWS runs).

(Source: [[PAWS — Depression in Sobriety (Journey Hillside)]], [[Anhedonia After Addiction (Ria Health)]].)

## Why it matters for family
PAWS is **temporary and a sign the brain is healing**, not a sign sobriety "isn't working" or that he should drink again. Knowing the curve lets you reframe his flat mood as *recovery in progress* — and makes this the single most important window to **support and reinforce**, never criticize ([[CRAFT (Community Reinforcement and Family Training)]]).
