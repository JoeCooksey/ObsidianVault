# Ways to Raise Morning Cortisol (Tier List)

You raise cortisol when the goal is a **steep morning peak** — for daytime energy, focus, and mood, and to rebuild a *flattened* rhythm (late-phase burnout, jet lag, shift work). The aim is **timing, not maximizing**: drive cortisol up early so it can fall by night (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]). For clinically low cortisol (Addison's/adrenal insufficiency), see the warning at the bottom.

Ranked by leverage × evidence for *properly timing the morning rise*.

---

## S-Tier — do these first

### ☀️ Morning sunlight within 30–60 min of waking
The single highest-leverage cue. Viewing bright outdoor light early **entrains the cortisol rhythm to peak in the morning** (where you want it) and improves daytime focus and mood (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]). Light is one of the strongest regulators of the CAR (Source: [[Stalder et al 2025 — Cortisol Awakening Response (Endocrine Reviews)]]).
- **How:** 5–10 min outside (10–30 min if overcast), no sunglasses, don't stare at the sun. A 10,000-lux therapy lamp is a backup on dark mornings.

### 😴 Consistent sleep + wake time
The CAR is triggered by the sleep→wake transition and is shaped by sleep timing. A **regular wake time** is what gives you a reliable, robust morning peak; erratic sleep flattens it (Source: [[Stalder et al 2025 — Cortisol Awakening Response (Endocrine Reviews)]]). Most "low morning cortisol / no energy" problems are really sleep-timing problems.

---

## A-Tier — strong, well-timed levers

### 🏃 Morning exercise
Exercise is a potent **entrainment cue** and acutely raises cortisol — useful *in the morning* to reinforce the peak (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]). The same property makes it a bad idea late at night (see [[Ways to Reduce Cortisol (Tier List)]]). Higher-intensity work raises cortisol more than easy work.

### ☕ Caffeine — but *delayed* 60–90 min after waking
Caffeine raises cortisol. Taken **first thing**, it blunts the natural CAR and sets up a steeper afternoon crash; **delaying caffeine 60–90 minutes** after waking lets your own cortisol peak first, then caffeine extends alertness (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]). See [[Caffeine Timing Protocol]].

### 💧 Hydration + electrolytes on waking
Huberman lists morning hydration as a tool to support the morning cortisol rise and brain/body function: **~16–32 oz water**, plain or with electrolytes, soon after waking (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]). Adequate **sodium** matters especially for people with low cortisol/adrenal issues (Source: [[Cortisol — Cleveland Clinic]]).

## B-Tier — situational / smaller

### ❄️ Deliberate cold exposure
Cold (cold shower/plunge) raises catecholamines and **can** elevate cortisol — useful as a morning "wake-up" stimulus, but Huberman flags the cortisol effect as less definitive than its dopamine/adrenaline effects (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]). Keep it in the morning, not the evening.

### 🍳 Eat a real breakfast (protein + healthy fat + B/C vitamins)
A balanced morning meal supports adrenal function and a stable rhythm; relevant mostly for people running low/depleted (Source: [[Cortisol — Cleveland Clinic]]).

### 🧠 Anticipation / having a reason to get up
The CAR is larger when the brain anticipates demands for the day ahead — a known psychological modulator of the morning rise (Source: [[Stalder et al 2025 — Cortisol Awakening Response (Endocrine Reviews)]]).

---

## Quick Protocol (rebuild a flat morning)
1. Fixed wake time, 7 days a week.
2. Sunlight in the eyes within 30–60 min.
3. Water + electrolytes immediately.
4. Movement (walk or workout) in the morning.
5. Caffeine **after** 60–90 min.
6. Protein-forward breakfast.

> [!warning] **This is rhythm-tuning, not a treatment for true adrenal insufficiency.** If you have symptoms of clinically low cortisol — persistent fatigue, weight loss, low blood pressure, dizziness on standing, salt craving, darkening skin — see a doctor. **Addison's disease requires corticosteroid replacement and is life-threatening untreated** (Source: [[Cortisol — Cleveland Clinic]]). See [[Cortisol Complete Guide]].
