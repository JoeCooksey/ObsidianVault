# CGM Self-Testing for Food Response

The most concrete answer to **"what tests can I do on myself to decide which foods to buy?"** Generic food tier lists tell you what's good *on average*; a **continuous glucose monitor (CGM)** tells you how *your* body responds to specific foods — because glycemic response varies dramatically between healthy people (Source: [[CGM in Healthy Populations (Metabolism 2023 + CGM-HYPE)]]).

## The Self-Test Protocol
1. **Wear a CGM for 2–4 weeks** (consumer options: Stelo, Lingo, Abbott Libre, or a Levels-style program). No prescription needed for the OTC ones.
2. **Eat normally and log foods.** Watch the post-meal curve — you want to **avoid large spikes followed by crashes**, not chase a flat line obsessively.
3. **Run controlled pairings** (the actual experiment):
   - Same carb **alone** vs. **with protein/fat/fiber** → protein+fiber blunts the spike.
   - Carb **before** vs. **after a 10-min walk** → the walk flattens it.
   - White rice vs. brown vs. oats vs. potato → find which starch keeps *you* steady.
   - Fruit alone vs. fruit + nuts.
4. **Keep what keeps you flat; swap or re-sequence the spikers.**

## What The Evidence Shows
- **Large inter-individual variability:** one person's "healthy" meal spikes another's blood sugar — generic glycemic-index charts are only an average.
- **Carb-rich foods** trigger the biggest spikes; **psychological stress** and **anaerobic exercise** also raise glucose (CGM-HYPE trial).
- Smoother glucose is *associated* with steadier energy, less hunger, and better sleep — but in **non-diabetics the long-term health payoff is unproven**.

## Other "Test Yourself" Options (lower priority)
- **Annual bloodwork / metabolic panel** — the highest-value baseline test (fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipids, ApoB, vitamin D, ferritin). See [[Bloodwork Food & Supplement Guide]].
- **Elimination diet** — the only validated "food sensitivity" test: remove a suspect food 2–4 weeks, reintroduce, observe. Mail-in **IgG "food sensitivity" panels are not validated** — skip them.
- **DEXA / body composition** — to know if your sourcing/eating is actually moving the needle.

## Caveats
> [!gap] In healthy people, chasing flat glucose curves is **optimization, not medicine** — benefit is unproven. Use a CGM as a 2–4 week *experiment*, not a permanent scoreboard; it can feed food anxiety/orthorexia in some people.
- CGMs read interstitial fluid (5–10 min lag) and are noisy — judge **patterns**, not single readings.

## Key Principle
**Personalize, don't dogmatize.** Use the [[Food Health Tier List — Macronutrients|tier lists]] and [[Macronutrient Sourcing Guide (What to Buy)|sourcing guide]] as the default; use a CGM to discover the handful of "healthy" foods that happen to spike *you*, and adjust your shopping accordingly.
