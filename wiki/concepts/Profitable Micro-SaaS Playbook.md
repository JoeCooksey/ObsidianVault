# Profitable Micro-SaaS Playbook

How a solo builder turns vibe coding into recurring revenue. Micro-SaaS = a small, narrowly-focused software product run by one person (or a tiny team). (Source: 2026 indie-hacker guides; [[Pieter Levels — How He Builds (Case Study)]])

## The market reality

- Micro-SaaS market projected **$15.7B → $59.6B by 2030** (~30%/yr). (Source: 2026 guides)
- Solo founders build **$5K–$50K MRR** businesses; many reach **$1K–$10K MRR within 3–6 months**.
- Typical spend before first revenue: **under $25–$200/month total.** "Spending more before you need to is lighting margin on fire."
- But **~85% of SaaS ventures fail within 18 months** — almost always from building before validating. → [[Micro-SaaS Idea Validation Framework]] is step zero.

## The 3-phase / 10-stop journey (vibecoder.me)

**Phase 1 — Technical foundation**
- Get past the "70% performance wall" of pure vibing → adopt daily **prompt-review-test** cycles.
- Master one stack (React / Next.js / Tailwind is the common indie default).
- Build a reusable SaaS dashboard pattern.

**Phase 2 — Revenue infrastructure**
- **Auth + database**: Supabase.
- **Payments**: Lemon Squeezy (global B2C, handles tax/merchant-of-record) or Stripe (B2B / US).
- Pre-launch deployment checklist.

**Phase 3 — Live operations**
- Deploy via **GitHub → Vercel** with a custom domain.
- Keep infra at **$50–$200/month**, scaling only with revenue.
- Landing-page conversion funnel.

## The doctrine (from [[Pieter Levels]])

- **Ship before ready; charge immediately.** "The first paying customer changes everything you think you know about your product."
- **Niche hard**: "CRM for everyone" fails; "CRM for fitness coaches" wins.
- **Keep tech boring and simple** — ship features, not architecture.
- **Audience is the distribution moat** → [[Building in Public]].
- Play the **volume game** (Levels: 12 startups in 12 months).

## Hottest 2026 niches

AI content/workflow automation (repurposing, meeting notes, competitor analysis — $19–$99/mo), payment recovery/dunning (70–90% margins), vertical compliance tools (health/legal/finance), niche B2B integrations.
