# Fiction Roadmap for EE Skills (Sci-Fi to Skill Map)

> [!important] Honest framing first. **Fiction does not teach you Laplace transforms or how to size a MOSFET.** No novel replaces a textbook or a lab. What hard science fiction *does* train is the **engineering mind** — constraint-driven problem solving, systems thinking, first-principles reasoning, resilience under failure, and engineering ethics. It also supplies the thing textbooks can't: **motivation.** Many practicing engineers trace their careers to a book they read young. (Source: [[Public Books — 16 Books That Make You Want to Be an Engineer]])

## How to Use This Page
Fiction is the **mindset layer** under the [[EE Student Skill Stack (Ranked)]]. Read one of these alongside your coursework, not instead of it. Each entry maps a book to the *engineering disposition* it strengthens.

## The Map — Book → Mindset It Builds

### Core: Constraint-Driven Problem Solving
- **The Martian — Andy Weir (2011)** — The single best "engineering joy" novel. Mark Watney survives Mars by solving one constrained problem after another with chemistry, mechanics, and orbital math — every solution reflects real NASA science. This *is* what circuit debugging and design-under-spec feels like. (Source: [[Public Books — 16 Books That Make You Want to Be an Engineer]], [[Book List — Fiction Must-Reads]])
- **Project Hail Mary — Andy Weir (2021)** — A puzzle-box of first-contact physics, chemistry, and improvised instrumentation; rewards the reader for thinking like a lab scientist. Superb on audio.

### Systems Thinking at Scale
- **Dune — Frank Herbert (1965)** — Ecology, feedback loops, and unintended consequences at a planetary scale; the template for thinking about a *system*, not a part. Also a warning about charismatic leaders. (See [[Book List — Fiction Must-Reads]])
- **Seveneves — Neal Stephenson (2015)** — Takes orbital mechanics, materials, and survival engineering seriously; every decision has physical logic and downstream consequence. Trains long-horizon systems reasoning.
- **The Mars Trilogy — Kim Stanley Robinson** — Terraforming as a multi-generational engineering and political project; systems thinking welded to ethics.

### First-Principles / "Build From What You Have"
- **Rocket Boys (October Sky) — Homer Hickam (1998)** — A memoir-novel of teenagers iterating on homemade rockets: the design → build → test → refine loop, made emotional. The closest thing to a how-it-feels manual for the engineering mindset. (Source: [[Public Books — 16 Books That Make You Want to Be an Engineer]])
- **Flight of the Phoenix — Elleston Trevor (1964)** — Survivors rebuild a flyable aircraft from a crashed wreck; heroic problem-solving plus the friction of collaborative engineering under pressure.
- **Ringworld / Neutron Star — Larry Niven** — "Big dumb object" hard SF; reverse-engineering an artifact from physics alone is exactly the analytical muscle EE rewards.

### Strategy, Optimization & Game Theory
- **Ender's Game — Orson Scott Card (1985)** — Strategy, leadership, and the moral cost of winning; resonates strongly with technical minds. (See [[Book List — Fiction Must-Reads]])
- **The Three-Body Problem — Liu Cixin (2008)** — Physics, computation (the human-computer chapter is famous), and civilizational game theory; trains thinking about adversarial systems and deep time.

### Engineering Ethics (the skill no textbook teaches well)
- **Cat's Cradle — Kurt Vonnegut (1963)** — The cautionary tale of an engineer who prizes "scientific abstraction" over human consequence. Read it the moment you can build something dangerous. (Source: [[Public Books — 16 Books That Make You Want to Be an Engineer]])
- **Frankenstein — Mary Shelley (1818)** — The original "should we build this?" — responsibility for what you create.
- **I, Robot — Isaac Asimov (1950)** — The Three Laws as a thought experiment in encoding ethics into systems; directly echoed in modern robotics and AI safety.

## Phased Reading Order (Joe)
1. **The Martian** — pure engineering joy; lowest friction, highest payoff. Start here.
2. **Project Hail Mary** — same energy, deeper science; great on audio for commutes/training.
3. **Ender's Game** — fast, strategic, gripping.
4. **Rocket Boys** — the design-build-test loop made personal.
5. **Dune** — step up into systems-and-ecology thinking.
6. **Cat's Cradle** *or* **Frankenstein** — the ethics check, once you can actually build things.
7. **Seveneves** / **Three-Body Problem** — the long, hard-SF deep end when hooked.

> [!tip] One book per term, read for enjoyment. The mindset transfer is a byproduct — never a substitute for the [[Self-Study Textbook List + NotebookLM Prompt]].
