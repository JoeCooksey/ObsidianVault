# Probiotic Strains Reference

The key insight in probiotics: **strain specificity matters more than brand or CFU count**. A specific strain (e.g., *L. rhamnosus* GG) is effective for certain conditions; a different strain of the same species may have zero benefit for that same condition. This reference maps strains to evidence-backed uses.

---

## Strain Naming Convention

Probiotic strains are named at three levels:
- **Genus**: e.g., *Lactobacillus*
- **Species**: e.g., *rhamnosus*
- **Strain designation**: e.g., GG (stands for Goldin & Gorbach, the discoverers)

Full name: *Lactobacillus rhamnosus* GG = *Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus* GG (updated taxonomy, same organism)

## Top Strains by Condition

### Antibiotic-Associated Diarrhea (Strongest Evidence)
| Strain | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *L. rhamnosus* GG | **Strong (multiple RCTs)** | Reduced AAD from 22.4% → 12.3% in meta-analysis; most studied single strain; found in Culturelle |
| *S. boulardii* CNCM I-745 | **Strong** | Actually a yeast (not bacteria); survives antibiotics because antibiotics don't kill yeasts; take same time as antibiotic unlike bacterial probiotics |
| Multi-strain combinations | **Strong** | Combining L. rhamnosus GG + S. boulardii is the gold standard protocol |

**Rule**: Take probiotics 2 hours before or after antibiotic dose (not simultaneously).

## What "CFU" Actually Means

CFU = Colony Forming Units = count of live organisms capable of dividing.

**Important caveats:**
- Labels report CFU at **manufacture date** — by expiration, many are dead; look for "CFU at end of shelf life" claims
- More CFU is NOT always better: 10 billion of the right strain beats 100 billion of the wrong strain
- **Survivability matters**: Only organisms that survive stomach acid colonize the gut; spore-formers (*Bacillus subtilis*) near 100%; kefir organisms ~20–40%; yogurt organisms ~5–15% depending on strain
- Fermented foods often outperform supplements because food matrices (fat, protein) buffer stomach acid, protecting the organisms

## Psychobiotic Concept (2025 Status)

The **gut-brain axis** transmits via:
1. **Vagus nerve** (direct neural connection gut → brain)
2. **Short-chain fatty acids** (butyrate, propionate) produced by gut bacteria → cross blood-brain barrier
3. **Serotonin**: 95% of serotonin is produced in the gut by enterochromaffin cells stimulated by gut bacteria
4. **Immune modulation**: neuroinflammation reduction via IL-6, TNF-α reduction

**Practical**: Consistent fermented food intake (kefir daily, kimchi with dinner) is the best accessible psychobiotic protocol currently. Dedicated psychobiotic supplements exist but evidence is 3–5 years behind gut-health applications.
