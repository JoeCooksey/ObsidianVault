# Daily Learning Habit (Operating System)

The practical routine that makes learning happen **every day, automatically**. It sits at the intersection of the vault's two big clusters: the *learning-science* engine ([[Research: How to Learn Anything (The Science of Learning)]] — what to do in the session) and the *habit-formation* engine ([[Habit Loop]], [[Four Laws of Behavior Change]] — what makes you show up). Neither alone produces daily learning; this page is the join.

## The Core Insight

**Distribution beats volume.** [[Cepeda 2006 - Distributed Practice Meta-Analysis|Spacing]] means the same total hours, split across many short daily sessions, retain far better than the same hours massed into an occasional binge. So the winning move isn't "study harder when motivated" — it's "learn a little, every day, forever." That reframes the whole problem from *motivation* to *habit design*.

## The Five-Part Routine

| Part | The move | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Make it small** | One concept / one problem / one deck — a session that fits even a bad day | [[Microlearning]] |
| **2. Anchor it** | Stack onto an existing daily habit: *"After [X], I learn for 15 min"* | [[Habit Stacking]] |
| **3. Make the session real** | Fill the 15 min with retrieval, not re-reading — a [[Desirable Difficulties\|desirable difficulty]] | [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]] |
| **4. Close with reflection** | Three lines: learned / fuzzy / connects-to | [[Learning Log (Daily Reflection)]] |
| **5. Protect the chain** | Define a minimum-viable day; never miss twice | [[Never Miss Twice]] |

## The Timeline (Set Expectations)

[[Lally 2009 - How Habits Are Formed (66 Days)|Lally 2009]]: a daily behavior takes a **median ~66 days** (range 18–254) to feel automatic, faster when small. The "21 days" figure is a myth. So: expect ~2 months of deliberate effort before the block runs on autopilot, and judge the system by *consistency*, not by how it feels day to day — because [[Desirable Difficulties|effortful learning feels worse while working better]].

## The Engine of Friction

Every part above is really about lowering [[Limbic Friction]] — the activation energy to start. Small + anchored + minimum-viable-day means you almost never face the decision "should I learn today?"; you just execute an existing chain. The habit removes the willpower tax that kills most learning plans by week three.

## What This Is NOT

- Not a *technique* — it's the scheduling/consistency layer that *carries* the techniques.
- Not "consume content daily." Passive input (videos, articles) without retrieval and reflection produces familiarity, not learning. The daily *habit* must contain a daily *difficulty*.
- Not perfectionism. The streak is resilient by design ([[Never Miss Twice]]), not fragile.
