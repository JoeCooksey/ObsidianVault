# Spanish Resources Tier List 2026

Complete ranking of every Spanish learning tool, app, podcast, YouTube channel, and method. Ranked by: acquisition speed × long-term retention × evidence base. See [[Spanish Fluency Complete Masterclass]] for how to combine these.

---

## Apps — Ranked S to F

### S-Tier (Core Stack — Use These)

**Anki** (Free)
- Spaced repetition software; the most evidence-backed memorization tool in language learning
- Deck: *Spanish Frequency 1000* (free on AnkiWeb) — the 1,000 most common Spanish words
- Protocol: 10 new cards/day max; review every single day without exception
- Best for: Vocabulary + sentence mining
- Weakness: Zero speaking practice; cards without audio are less effective

**Language Transfer Complete Spanish** (Free)
- 40 audio episodes; no textbook, no reading, no subscriptions
- Method: Founder Mihalis Eleftheriou guides you to *derive* Spanish from English, not memorize it
- Coverage: All core present tense + key irregular verbs + question structures + object pronouns
- Time: ~7.5 hours to complete the full course
- Best for: A1 grammar foundation — listen twice before any other resource
- Can be done while walking, commuting, cooking

---

### A-Tier (High Value)

**Dreaming Spanish** (Free tier + paid)
- Best comprehensible input library for Spanish in existence
- 2,000+ hours of video content at 5 levels: Super Beginner → Advanced
- Method: 100% Spanish from the first minute; no explanations in English
- Free tier: Large content library; Premium (~$10/mo): download offline + full library
- Best for: Months 1 through 18 — the backbone of your listening acquisition
- Joe's starting point: "Super Beginner" playlist (Pablo speaks very slowly, full comprehension)

**italki** (Pay per lesson, ~$5–30/hr)
- Marketplace for native-speaker tutors: "Community Tutors" ($5–15/hr) vs. "Professional Teachers" ($20–30/hr)
- For language learning: Community Tutor is sufficient; saves money for more sessions
- Protocol: Book 1×/week starting Month 3; tell tutor: "B1 conversation practice, error correction, no English"
- Best for: Speaking output + pronunciation feedback + cultural fluency

**Migaku** (Paid, ~$10/mo)
- Browser extension that adds instant dictionary lookup + sentence card creation to Netflix, YouTube, and websites
- Makes sentence mining (see [[Immersion Method Language Learning]]) 10× faster
- Turns any Spanish video into a flashcard factory
- Best for: Intermediate+ learners who want to study from native content

---

### B-Tier (Supplements — Use as Add-ons)

**Pimsleur Spanish** (~$15/mo or $155/year)
- Audio-only, speaking-from-day-one approach; 30-min episodes with call-and-response
- Trains speaking alongside listening from the first session
- Best for: If you want a paid structured audio course; good alternative to Language Transfer for people who prefer a slow formal pace
- Weakness: Expensive for what you get; Language Transfer is free and comparable

**Babbel Spanish** (~$7–14/mo)
- Short 10–15 min lessons; explicit grammar explanations; speech recognition
- Designed by linguists; good lesson quality
- Best for: Supplementing Anki + comprehensible input if you want guided structure
- Weakness: Not sufficient alone; lessons plateau at B1

**Coffee Break Spanish (Podcast)** (Free)
- 20-min episodes; teacher+learner format; excellent for beginners
- 4 seasons: takes you from zero to intermediate
- Best for: Phase 1 listening practice; good complement to Language Transfer
- Download offline for commutes

**HelloTalk / Tandem** (Free tier)
- Language exchange apps connecting you with native Spanish speakers
- Text, voice messages, or video call
- Best for: Free speaking practice alternative to paid italki tutors
- Weakness: Quality of exchange partners varies; can be hit or miss

---

### C-Tier (Limited Value)

**Duolingo** (Free)
- Good for: Building a daily habit via gamification; absolute beginners needing motivation
- Bad for: Actual acquisition — gamification leads to shallow engagement; birds, owls, hearts not language learning
- Best use: Streak maintenance only, as a 5-min supplement when you've missed your real study; don't make it your main tool
- Research says: Duolingo's own 2012 study claiming 34 hours = 1 semester of college Spanish was discredited

**Busuu** (~$9/mo)
- Has a correction feature from native speakers; otherwise similar to Babbel
- Worth trying free tier; not essential

**SpanishPod101** (Free tier + paid)
- Large content library; good for audio content discovery
- Paid version overpriced; podcasts and beginner lessons available free

---

### D-Tier (Not Recommended)

**Rosetta Stone** (~$12+/mo)
- Immersion-via-images method; deliberately teaches like a "child learning"
- Problem: Adults don't learn like children; explicit grammar awareness speeds acquisition significantly
- No audio quality comparison to Language Transfer or Dreaming Spanish
- Vastly overpriced for the results

**Pimsleur as primary method** — good supplement, insufficient as a standalone track to fluency

**Paid vocabulary apps without SRS** — random flashcards without spaced repetition are 3–5× less effective than Anki

## Books and Reading

### Graded Readers (A2–B1)
- *El Principito* (The Little Prince) — A2; classic; ~2,000 words
- *Caperucita Roja* and graded reader collections — A1–A2
- EasySpanish Readers series — purpose-built for learners, A1–B2 levels

### Native Literature (B2–C1)
- *El Túnel* — Ernesto Sábato; B2; dark psychological novel; short (100 pages)
- *La Sombra del Viento* — Carlos Ruiz Zafón; B2; literary mystery set in Barcelona
- *Crónica de una Muerte Anunciada* — García Márquez; B2/C1; ~100 pages; perfect C1 entry
- *Cien Años de Soledad* — García Márquez; C1; the gold standard

### Grammar References
- *Practice Makes Perfect: Spanish Grammar* — A. Kendris; best workbook for A2–B1
- *A New Reference Grammar of Modern Spanish* — Butt & Benjamin; definitive advanced reference
- Real Academia Española (RAE) dictionary — authoritative; free online
