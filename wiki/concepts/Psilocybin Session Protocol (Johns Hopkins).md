# Psilocybin Session Protocol (Johns Hopkins)

How the most-cited psilocybin research program structures the trip itself — the clinical answer to "what should you do while tripping," refined over 25+ years of trials.

## The protocol

- Participant lies on a **couch**, wearing **eyeshades** and **headphones**, for a ~5-hour session
- Two monitors are present the whole time but **do not talk to or guide** the participant — preparation happens before, integration after
- Attention is directed **inward**; the external world is deliberately muted
- This setup supported the JAMA Psychiatry 2020 trial showing psilocybin relieved major depression (Source: [[Johns Hopkins Psilocybin Playlist Articles]])

## The playlist

- **7 hours 40 minutes**, built to match the arc of a medium/high-dose session: soft background on entry → opening up → onset → launch → peak → re-entry
- Heavily classical (Mozart, Bach, Vivaldi, Brahms) — largely **lyric-free in English**, emotionally structured
- Created by psychologist **Bill Richards** with center director Roland Griffiths from 1999, drawing on music therapist **Helen Bonny's** tapes from 1960s-era psychedelic research — the playlist lineage is older than the modern research wave
- Music is treated as an active therapeutic component, not background — see [[Kaelen 2018 - The Hidden Therapist (Music in Psychedelic Therapy)]]

## Why it matters for the activities question

The deepest-studied psilocybin experiences involve **no activities at all**: music + eyeshades + surrender. This anchors the dose rule in [[Psilocybin Trip Activities Tier List]] — the higher the dose, the more the clinical model (inward, lying down, curated sound) is the right answer.
