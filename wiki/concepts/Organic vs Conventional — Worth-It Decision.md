# Organic vs Conventional — Worth-It Decision

When the organic premium is worth paying and when it's wasted money. The honest answer: **organic is a pesticide/heavy-metal *exposure-reduction* purchase, not a "more nutritious" or "makes you healthier" purchase** (Source: [[Organic Nutrition Evidence (Stanford 2012 + Newcastle 2014)]]). So spend selectively, not across the board.

## The Decision Rule

> [!tip] Don't buy everything organic. Buy the **Dirty Dozen** organic; buy the **Clean Fifteen** conventional.

This is the EWG strategy — let USDA pesticide testing do the work for you (Source: [[EWG Shopper's Guide to Pesticides in Produce (2025)]]).

### Buy ORGANIC (2025 Dirty Dozen — highest residue)
Spinach · Strawberries · Kale/collard/mustard greens · Grapes · Peaches · Cherries · Nectarines · Pears · Apples · Blackberries · Blueberries · Potatoes

*Pattern: thin-skinned, leafy, or berries — large surface area, eaten whole/unpeeled.*

### Buy CONVENTIONAL (2025 Clean Fifteen — lowest residue)
Pineapple · Sweet corn · Avocados · Papaya · Onions · Sweet peas · Asparagus · Cabbage · Watermelon · Cauliflower · Bananas · Mangoes · Carrots · Mushrooms · Kiwi

*Pattern: thick-skinned or peeled — the part you eat is protected. ~60% had zero detectable residue.*

## What "Organic" Does and Doesn't Buy You

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| ~30% less likely to carry pesticide residue | ✅ True | Both Stanford 2012 + Newcastle 2014 |
| Lower cadmium (toxic heavy metal) | ✅ True | Newcastle 2014 |
| Higher antioxidants/polyphenols | ⚠️ Maybe (disputed magnitude) | Newcastle yes, Stanford no |
| "More nutritious" (protein/vitamins/minerals) | ❌ No meaningful difference | Stanford 2012 |
| "Makes you healthier" (disease outcomes) | ❌ Unproven — no long-term RCT | 3 systematic reviews |

## Key Principles
1. **The biggest win is eating more produce, period** — conventional fruit/veg beats skipping it over a theoretical residue. Don't let "can't afford organic" become "didn't eat the vegetable."
2. **Wash everything** (organic included) — rinsing + scrubbing removes a meaningful fraction of surface residue, dirt, and microbes.
3. **Frozen organic is the value play** for berries/greens — cheaper, equal nutrition (see [[Frozen vs Fresh Produce]]), and hits the Dirty Dozen items.
4. **"Organic" ≠ "healthy."** Organic cookies are still cookies. The label only addresses how it was *grown*, not whether the food is good for you.
5. **EWG is an advocate, not a regulator** — conventional residues are usually well below EPA safety limits; treat the list as a *prioritization* tool, not a fear signal.
