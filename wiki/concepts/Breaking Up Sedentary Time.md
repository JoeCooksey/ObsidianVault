# Breaking Up Sedentary Time

Prolonged uninterrupted sitting (>7–8 h/day, and especially long unbroken blocks) is an **independent** cardiometabolic risk factor — separate from whether you also do a daily workout. The fix is *frequency of interruption*, not intensity: stand up and move briefly, often.

## The evidence

- Interrupting sitting with short, frequent activity breaks **acutely improves glucose control and blood pressure**; current diabetes guidelines already recommend it (according to PubMed — Alobaid 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcjd.2023.02.003)).
- Proposed mechanisms include normalized inflammatory profile, improved endothelial function, and macrophage M1→M2 polarization via extracellular vesicles (Padilha 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/0113816128244442231018070639)).
- The **minimum effective dose** of frequency × duration is still being pinned down — the Columbia **BREAK2** dose-finding RCT (Diaz 2025, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-025-22250-0)) is testing 25 frequency/duration combinations (e.g. walk every 30 min for 5 min) to set quantitative guidelines.

> [!gap] No single "break every N minutes" number is settled. Practical interim rule from the literature: **move ~1–5 min every 30–60 min** of sitting.

## What a break looks like

Brisk walk to refill water, calf raises, a lap of the office, sit-to-stands, a few squats, or a posture reset. It need not be hard — the act of standing and contracting large leg muscles is the active ingredient.

## Relationship to other ideas

The low-intensity counterpart to [[Exercise Snacks]] (which add intensity). Together they make up the "all-day" layer of the [[Daily Movement Routine (Flexibility, Mobility, Longevity)]]. Volume of total daily movement is captured by [[Daily Steps and Mortality Meta-Analysis (Lancet 2025)|daily step count]].
