# Micro-SaaS Idea Validation Framework

**Validate before you build.** ~85% of SaaS ventures fail within 18 months, mostly from "build it and they will come." Validation is step zero of the [[Profitable Micro-SaaS Playbook]]. (Source: 2026 validation guides; Startup Genome)

## Rob Walling's 5-criteria filter (MicroConf)

Score every idea on:
1. **Product** — can you actually build the solution? (vibe coding lowers this bar)
2. **Price** — will customers pay *enough*?
3. **Market** — does sufficient demand exist?
4. **Marketing channels** — can you reach these customers?
5. **Monetization** — is there a path to profitable unit economics?

## The 4-week validation timeline

- **Weeks 1–2** — Landing page + targeted traffic; collect email signups. **20+ qualified signups = real interest.**
- **Weeks 2–3** — 10–20 problem interviews; ask about **past behavior, not hypotheticals.**
- **Weeks 3–4** — Offer discounted beta; measure who actually *commits to paying.*

## Search-signal research

- Search `"[problem] software"`, `"[problem] tool"`, `"[problem] app"`.
- Want keywords with **1,000+ monthly searches** and commercial intent.
- **Competitors are a good sign** — they prove a market exists. No competitors often means no market.

## The ultimate test: pre-selling

Getting people to **pay before you build** proves willingness to pay, not just curiosity. Rule of thumb: talk to 10 potential customers; if **3+ offer to pay** for a prototype, proceed.

## The Levels counter-note

[[Pieter Levels]] often inverts this: he ships an ugly v1 fast and lets *paying customers* be the validation. Both work — formal validation de-risks before effort; ship-fast validation gets real signal quickest. For a beginner with cheap build cost (vibe coding), a fast ugly launch *is* a valid validation method.

## For Joe

Run this filter before sinking time into any [[Vibe Coding]] build. The cheapest mistake is the product nobody wanted.
