# Never Miss Twice

A consistency rule for surviving the inevitable bad day: **missing once is an accident; missing twice is the start of a new (bad) habit.** The goal is not a perfect streak — it's never letting a single lapse become two.

## The Evidence Behind It

[[Lally 2009 - How Habits Are Formed (66 Days)]] found that **missing a single day did not measurably impair** habit formation — the automaticity curve barely dipped. The real damage isn't the missed day; it's the **all-or-nothing spiral** ("I broke the streak, so it's ruined") that turns one miss into quitting. Never-miss-twice is the explicit countermeasure to that psychology.

## Why Streaks Help (and How They Fail)

A visible streak is motivating through two levers:
- **Loss aversion** — a 30-day chain feels too valuable to break.
- **Identity reinforcement** — each completed day is evidence that *you are a person who learns daily* (see [[Identity-Based vs Goal-Based Habits]]).

But a streak framed as "must be perfect" is **fragile**: one miss zeroes it and motivation collapses. Never-miss-twice converts the streak from a brittle all-or-nothing object into a resilient one — the metric becomes *gaps*, not perfection.

## In Practice

- Define a **minimum viable day**: the smallest version that still counts (one flashcard, one page, one problem). On a terrible day you do the minimum, not nothing — this keeps the chain alive without willpower.
- If you do miss, the *only* rule is: **show up tomorrow.** No make-up debt, no guilt tax.
- Track gaps, not just streaks.
