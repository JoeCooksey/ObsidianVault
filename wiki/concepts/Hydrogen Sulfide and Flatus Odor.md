# Hydrogen Sulfide and Flatus Odor

**H₂S is the single biggest reason farts stink.** It is the most abundant of the three flatus sulfur gases and the one whose concentration best tracks perceived malodor (p ≤ 0.001) (Source: [[Suarez 1998 - Identification of Gases Responsible for Odour of Human Flatus]]).

## What it is
A colorless gas smelling of **rotten eggs**, detectable by the human nose at **parts-per-billion** concentrations. Toxic at high dose — the extreme sensitivity is likely a protective adaptation.

## Where it comes from in the gut
Two ingredients meet in the colon:
1. **Hydrogen (H₂)** — produced when gut bacteria ferment undigested carbohydrate.
2. **Sulfur/sulfate** — from dietary sulfur ([[High-Sulfur Foods and Flatus Odor]]) and sulfated compounds.

**[[Sulfate-Reducing Bacteria (Desulfovibrio)|Sulfate-reducing bacteria]]** combine the two: they consume H₂ and reduce sulfate, releasing H₂S as waste. A parallel route is bacterial breakdown of the sulfur amino acids **methionine and cysteine** (from protein), which yields methanethiol and H₂S.

## Why it matters for prevention
Every effective *odor* strategy targets H₂S:
- **Cut the substrate** — less dietary sulfur → less H₂S.
- **Bind it** — [[Bismuth Subsalicylate for Flatus Odor|bismuth]] forms insoluble bismuth sulfide, cutting H₂S release >95%.
- **Trap it externally** — charcoal garments adsorb >90% of escaping sulfur gas.

> H₂S is also implicated in ulcerative colitis pathogenesis, which is why reducing colonic H₂S has clinical interest beyond odor (Source: [[Suarez 1998 - Bismuth Subsalicylate Decreases Hydrogen Sulfide]]).
