# Freshman Summer Project Plan (Tier List)

Ranked menu of what to actually *do* with the summer after freshman year, scored on **career capital × fit for Joe** (ASU EE, WBG-power-electronics → LLNL/Sandia trajectory). The honest frame: as a rising sophomore, a brand-name internship is hard to land — so the highest-value moves are **self-driven projects + research + a public portfolio**, not waiting for an offer that probably isn't coming (Source: [[Resume Without a Summer Internship — Ohio State ECS]], [[15 Ways to Make Summer Meaningful — JHU]]).

> [!tip] Pick 2–3 across tiers, not all of them. One finished S-tier artifact beats five half-built ones. Quality over quantity is the universal hiring signal — aim for 3–5 portfolio pieces *total*, not a pile.

## S-Tier — the differentiators (do at least one)

1. **Build ONE real power-electronics project end-to-end.** Design → [[LTSpice Complete Skills Guide|LTspice]] sim → breadboard → measure on the bench. A regulated **buck/boost converter or multi-output bench power supply** is the canonical EE artifact and maps straight to Joe's WBG/power track (Source: [[6 Inspiring EE Projects — NJIT]]). This is the single highest-fit project; document the problem → design → prototype → measured result with plots. See the Deep-Work S-tier in [[Research - Long-Term Compounding Daily Projects]].
2. **Apply to ASU FURI + cold-email 3–5 power-electronics professors NOW.** Freshmen are FURI-eligible from their **2nd semester onward**; $1,500 stipend + up to $400 supplies per funded semester (Source: [[ASU FURI — Fulton Undergraduate Research Initiative]]). Use the summer to lock a **fall** research spot. Faculty research beats a generic internship for a freshman and produces a recommendation letter. Tactics live in [[Research - EE Mentorship at ASU]].
3. **Public GitHub engineering log / portfolio.** Work the [[Python EE Project Ladder]] and commit in public. Code is *permissionless leverage* — it replicates at zero cost and is career evidence + skill practice + public artifact in one action (Source: [[Compounding Daily Projects Tier List]]). Every repo needs a README with the why.

## A-Tier — high value, lower differentiation

4. **Land any summer job — ideally STEM tutoring.** Money + a work-ethic signal recruiters respect; tutoring math/physics is the best-fit option and reinforces fundamentals. Full playbook in [[Research - Summer Part-Time Job Guide Livermore College Student]].
5. **Go deep on ONE tool to a portfolio piece:** KiCad (PCB layout of the S-tier converter), Arduino/STM32 embedded, or Python `control`. Depth in one tool > shallow exposure to five (Source: [[15 Ways to Make Summer Meaningful — JHU]]).
6. **Micro-internships + informational interviews.** Parker Dewey micro-internships give real project experience without a full offer; informational interviews with LLNL/Sandia engineers and ASU alumni map the path and build the network (Source: [[Resume Without a Summer Internship — Ohio State ECS]]).

## B-Tier — useful supplements, not the main event

7. **One online course + cert** in circuits, embedded, or ML (Coursera/edX). Supplements a project; never a substitute for building one.
8. **Read 2–3 career/technical books** from [[Book List — Science and Technology]] / [[Book List — Career and Leadership]] — low effort, compounds.
9. **Get ahead on fall math/physics** (calc, diff eq). Year 1 has no EE courses yet — the math gap is the freshman leverage point ([[ASU EE Year 1-2 Curriculum Map]]).

## Always-On (run alongside, near-zero daily cost)

- **Automated index-fund DCA** — the one *literal* compounder ([[Index Fund Investing]]).
- **Fitness + sleep** — [[Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol]]; protects the whole engine.

## D-Tier — don't call these a "summer project"

- Grinding LeetCode with **no** projects to show; collecting certificates without building; any "passive income" scheme (dropshipping, crypto bots) — negative-EV, zero career capital ([[Online Income Methods Tier List]]).

## The one-line answer

**Build one real converter, lock a fall research spot, and put both on GitHub** — then backfill with a paid job, deep tool practice, and getting ahead in math. Everything else is optional.
