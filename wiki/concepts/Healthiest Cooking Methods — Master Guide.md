# Healthiest Cooking Methods — Master Guide

How to cook each major food on the [[Food Health Tier List — Overall|tier lists]] for **maximum nutrition and minimum harm**. The whole guide reduces to two goals that mostly point the same way: **keep the good stuff in** (vitamins, omega-3s — Source: [[Nutrient Retention by Cooking Method]]) and **keep the bad stuff from forming** (HCAs, PAHs, AGEs, acrylamide — Source: [[Heat-Formed Food Toxins (HCAs, PAHs, AGEs, Acrylamide)]]).

## The 3 Dials That Decide Everything
> [!tip] Healthier cooking = **lower temperature · more moisture · less time/browning.** Plus two add-ons: **marinate meat** and **keep the cooking liquid when you boil.**

Both nutrient loss *and* toxin formation rise with heat, dryness, and char. So the same habits win across almost every food.

## General Method Ranking (best → worst, health-wise)
1. **Steam / poach / sous-vide** — gentle, moist, no leaching, lowest toxins
2. **Microwave** — short time, little water; underrated and excellent
3. **Pressure cook / stew / boil-and-keep-the-broth** — moist; vitamins stay in the liquid
4. **Stir-fry / quick sauté** — fast + a little good fat; fine
5. **Roast / bake** — moderate; watch over-browning
6. **Grill / broil** — tasty but highest HCA/PAH; mitigate (below)
7. **Deep-fry / char** — worst: oxidized oil, acrylamide, AGEs — occasional only

## Per-Food Playbook

### 🥦 Vegetables
- **Best:** **steam** or **microwave** (keeps >90% vitamin C); quick **stir-fry** with a little olive oil for fat-soluble nutrients.
- **Avoid:** long submerged **boiling** (greens lose up to 50–70% vit C) unless you keep the water for soup.
- **Cook these for MORE nutrition:** tomatoes (lycopene), carrots/sweet potato/spinach (beta-carotene). Eat cruciferous (broccoli, kale) lightly cooked or raw to keep sulforaphane.
- **Roast** is great for flavor — just stop at golden, not charred.

### 🍓 Fruits
- **Best raw** — most fruit nutrients (vit C, polyphenols) are heat-sensitive and there's no reason to cook.
- Berries: eat raw/frozen-thawed; if heating (compote), go low and brief.
- Avoid added-sugar preparations; cooking isn't the issue, the sugar is.

### 🥩 Red Meat (beef, etc.)
- **Best:** lower-and-slower or moist methods (**braise, stew, sous-vide then quick sear**); cook to the safe internal temp, not to char.
- **Always marinate 30+ min** before any high-heat cooking → cuts HCAs up to 90%+ (acid + herbs/spices). This matters **more than** the temperature.
- **Grilling:** trim fat, avoid flare-ups/dripping, flip often, microwave-precook to shorten time, cut off any blackened bits.
- Keeps the [[Food Health Tier List — Macronutrients|whole-vs-processed rule]]: how you cook it matters less than *not* eating it processed/charred.

### 🍗 Poultry
- **Best:** **poach, bake, or pressure-cook** to a juicy safe temp. Marinate before grilling (HCAs form readily on chicken — PhIP).
- Skin-on for moisture is fine; the cooking method matters more than the skin.

### 🐟 Fish & Seafood
- **Best:** **bake, steam, poach, or en papillote** (in parchment) — these **protect the omega-3s** you're eating fish *for*.
- **Avoid deep-frying** — it oxidizes the omega-3 and adds bad fats. (Battered fried fish is the worst-case.)
- Light pan-sear or grill is fine; don't overcook (dry = oxidized fats).
- Small oily fish (sardines, salmon) → canned/steamed keeps the fats intact.

### 🥚 Eggs
- **Best:** **poached, soft-boiled, or scrambled/fried over LOW–medium heat.** Low heat = ~half the AGEs and protects the cholesterol from oxidizing.
- Runny-yolk methods preserve more heat-sensitive nutrients than hard, dry, high-heat cooking.
- Cooking the white fully maximizes protein digestibility — so soft yolk + set white is the sweet spot.

### 🥔 Potatoes & Starchy Foods
- **Best:** **boil, steam, or bake whole** — low acrylamide, keeps the skin's fiber/nutrients.
- **Acrylamide control when frying/roasting:** **soak cut potatoes 15–30 min** or **par-boil first**, cook to **golden not brown**, **air-fry** over deep-fry (78 vs 290 ppm).
- Store potatoes in the **pantry, not the fridge** (cold → more reducing sugars → more acrylamide).
- **Resistant-starch hack:** cook then **cool** potatoes/rice (then optionally reheat) → forms resistant starch = lower glycemic hit + gut-feeding fiber.

### 🌾 Grains, Rice & Oats
- **Best:** simmer/boil and **absorb the water** (rice, oats) — no leaching when the water is absorbed, not discarded.
- Oats: soak/overnight or quick-cook — no toxin concern; keep it whole, not instant-sweetened.
- Whole-grain bread: **toast to light golden**, not dark (acrylamide).
- Cook-and-cool rice → resistant starch (and food-safety: cool fast, fridge promptly — see [[Meal Prep Food Safety — Storage Times]]).

### 🫘 Legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas)
- **Best:** **soak then boil/pressure-cook** thoroughly — required to deactivate lectins and improve digestibility.
- Discard soak water; pressure cooking is fastest and cuts anti-nutrients well.
- Canned are fine — rinse to cut sodium.

### 🥜 Nuts & Seeds
- **Best raw or lightly dry-toasted.** High heat/roasting oxidizes their delicate fats and degrades omega-3 (esp. walnuts, flax, chia).
- Buy raw, toast briefly at low temp if you want flavor; store cool/sealed to prevent rancidity.
- **Flax must be ground** (not cooked hot) to absorb the omega-3.

### 🥛 Dairy
- Eat fermented dairy (kefir, yogurt) **unheated** to keep live probiotics ([[Food Health Tier List — Dairy]]).
- Cooking/heating cheese is fine nutritionally; just don't scorch.

### 🫒 Which Fats to COOK With (smoke point + stability)
| Use | Best fats | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **High heat** (sear, stir-fry, roast) | Avocado oil, refined olive, ghee, coconut | High smoke point, oxidation-stable |
| **Medium / everyday** | **Extra-virgin olive oil** | Stable enough for most home cooking + polyphenols; the default |
| **No heat** (dressings, finishing) | EVOO, flax, walnut oil | Preserve delicate fats |
| **Avoid for frying** | Reused oil, heavily heated seed oils | Oxidation → harmful aldehydes |
- Myth-check: **EVOO is fine for most sautéing/roasting** — its antioxidants make it more stable than its modest smoke point suggests. Don't *deep-fry* in it (cost + flavor), but everyday cooking is good.
- Never reuse frying oil; discard oil that smokes.
- **Full breakdown:** see [[Cooking Oils — Master Guide]] for best oil per method, a smoke-point chart, and the best toppings/finishing oils.

## One-Glance Cheat Sheet
- **Veg:** steam/microwave > boil. Cook tomatoes & carrots.
- **Meat:** marinate + don't char; braise/sous-vide over grill.
- **Fish:** bake/steam/poach to protect omega-3; never deep-fry.
- **Eggs:** low heat, runny yolk.
- **Potatoes:** boil/bake whole; soak before frying; golden not brown; cool for resistant starch.
- **Grains/legumes:** absorb the water; soak legumes.
- **Nuts/seeds:** raw or light toast.
- **Fat:** cook in EVOO/avocado oil; finish with EVOO; never reuse oil.
- **Universal:** lower heat · more moisture · less browning · keep the broth.
