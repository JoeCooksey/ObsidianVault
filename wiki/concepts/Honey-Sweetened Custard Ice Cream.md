# Honey-Sweetened Custard Ice Cream

The technique behind [[Ice Cream for Bears]]: a **French-style frozen custard** (egg-yolk base) sweetened with **honey instead of sugar**, with **no gums or stabilizers** — the yolks and honey do the emulsifying.

## The clone base (3 core ingredients + cream)
Match the brand's "grass-fed milk + raw honey + egg yolks," scaled to a home batch (Source: [[The Cook's Treat — Vanilla Honey Custard Ice Cream]]):
- **3 egg yolks** (emulsifier — replaces gums/stabilizers)
- **½ cup (6 oz) raw, unfiltered honey** (only sweetener)
- **1 cup whole grass-fed milk**
- **2 cups heavy cream** (use high-fat, ~40%, for richness)
- ¼ tsp salt · 1 Tbsp real vanilla (flavor)

## Method
1. Whisk yolks + honey + milk + salt in a saucepan; heat over medium, whisking constantly, **just until it thickens / bubbles begin** (~170°F custard, don't scramble). Tempering — ladling hot liquid into the yolks first — is the safe route.
2. Off heat, stir in cream + vanilla. Cool, press plastic wrap to the surface, **chill 6–24 h**.
3. Churn in any ice cream maker (or freeze + spin in a [[Ninja CREAMi]]); freeze ≥2 h.

## Honey-specific science
- **Honey ≈ 1.25–1.5× as sweet as sugar** → use ~¾ cup honey per 1 cup sugar a recipe called for.
- **Hygroscopic** (holds water) → lowers the freezing point, so the pint stays **soft and scoopable straight from the freezer** — a feature, not a bug.
- Keeps custard **thinner**; it won't set as firm as a sugar custard.
- **Raw, unfiltered honey** matters — its floral notes are the whole point; cheap honey is often watered down or cut with corn syrup.

## To truly mimic "Ice Cream for Bears"
- Use **organic grass-fed dairy** and **free-range yolks** for the ancestral, clean-label profile.
- Keep add-ins **refined-sugar-free** (the brand's rule): real fruit, cacao nibs, honey-sweetened chocolate.
- Flavor riffs from their menu: vanilla bean, coffee + cacao nib, mint + chocolate, blueberry, butterscotch-caramel made with honey.
