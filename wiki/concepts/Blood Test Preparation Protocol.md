# Blood Test Preparation Protocol

How to prep so the draw measures *you*, not yesterday's workout or last night's dinner. A poorly-prepped panel produces false signals that trigger needless worry or re-tests. The single biggest sources of error: **non-fasting, recent intense exercise, recent alcohol, and biotin.**

## The Checklist (the day/days before)

| When | Do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **48–72 h before** | **No intense exercise** (heavy lifting, HIIT, long rides) | Muscle leaks **AST, ALT, CK**; skews **testosterone/cortisol** ([[Exercise Effects on Blood Test Results]]) |
| **48–72 h before** | **No alcohol** (72 h+ if you drank heavily) | Raises **triglycerides** 24–48 h, **GGT/ALT/AST** for days–weeks |
| **72 h before** | **Stop biotin / B7 / hair-skin-nails supps** | Biotin interferes with **thyroid + hormone** immunoassays (false highs/lows) |
| **48 h before** | **No seafood** *(only if testing heavy metals/mercury)* | Avoids transient mercury spike |
| **8–12 h before** | **Fast** — water only (10–12 h for a lipid panel) | Food skews **glucose, insulin, triglycerides** |
| **Night before** | **Normal sleep**, hydrate well | Sleep loss raises cortisol/glucose, lowers testosterone; hydration = easier draw + accurate urinalysis |
| **Morning of** | **Draw 7–10 AM**, fasted, before any exercise | **Testosterone & cortisol** peak in the morning and are reference-ranged to it |
| **Always** | **Keep taking prescription meds** unless your doctor says otherwise | Stopping meds is more dangerous than any test artifact |

## What "fasting" means
- **Allowed:** plain water (drink plenty).
- **Not allowed:** food, coffee/tea (even black), juice, gum, sweetened/flavored drinks, smoking/nicotine.
- Black coffee is debated — safest is water-only to avoid skewing glucose/cortisol.

## Supplement timing
- **Biotin** is the only must-stop (72 h) — it's in most multivitamins, B-complexes, and beauty supplements; check labels.
- Fish oil, creatine, and most others can continue, but if you want a clean *baseline* read, a 2–3 day hold on everything non-essential removes ambiguity (creatine can nudge creatinine; high-dose niacin shifts lipids).
- Note what you took — context helps interpret a borderline result.

## Joe's stack — keep vs pause (Function Health draws)

Mapped to the [[Supplement Priority Stack for Young Males]]. Reuse this table before every draw.

### Pause

| Supplement | Stop when | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Biotin / B-complex / multivitamin / "hair-skin-nails"** | **72 h before** — the only hard must-stop | Corrupts thyroid + hormone immunoassays (false highs/lows). Directly relevant: the 2025-12 Free T4 flag (1.5, slightly high) needs rechecking *with biotin held*. Biotin hides in most multis — check labels. |
| **Creatine** | 2–3 days before (optional, recommended) | Nudges creatinine → skews eGFR kidney number. Short hold removes ambiguity. |
| **Bacopa / Rhodiola / any Tier 3** | 2–3 days before | Non-essential; holding everything optional gives a clean baseline. |
| **Caffeine + L-theanine** | Morning of | Fasting is water-only (no coffee, even black); caffeine skews cortisol. |

### Keep

| Supplement | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Omega-3 / fish oil** | Keep taking normally. The omega-3 index (6.3% last panel, target >8%) reflects ~120 days of RBC membrane composition — a few days off changes nothing, and the test should capture real habitual intake. Skip the morning-of dose (fasted anyway). |
| **Vitamin D3 + K2** | Keep. 25-OH vitamin D reflects weeks of intake; a recent dose doesn't meaningfully spike it. Skip morning-of. |
| **Magnesium glycinate** | Keep. Serum magnesium barely reflects supplementation either way; evening dose fine. |
| **Prescription meds** | Always keep unless the doctor says otherwise. |

Two non-supplement reminders that matter MORE for Joe: **no intense exercise 48–72 h** (the ALT 69 / AST 39 flags are most likely training artifacts — the rested re-draw is the point) and **no seafood 48 h** (heavy-metals panel; purified fish oil is fine, actual fish is not).

## Why morning + fasted + rested matters most for a young male
Testosterone, LH, and cortisol are **diurnal** (highest AM) and **exercise-sensitive**. Liver enzymes (ALT/AST) and CK are **exercise-sensitive**. A fasted, rested, early draw is the only way these numbers mean anything — an afternoon draw the day after a heavy session can manufacture "low testosterone" and "high liver enzymes" out of thin air.
