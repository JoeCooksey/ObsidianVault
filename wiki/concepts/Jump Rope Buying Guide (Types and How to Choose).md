# Jump Rope Buying Guide (Types and How to Choose)

One-line: **buy the rope that matches your goal** — speed, strength, rhythm, or tracking — then pick the cheapest one in that category that has decent bearings.

## The Five Types

| Type | Weight | What it's for | Trade-off |
|------|--------|---------------|-----------|
| **Speed rope** | Thin steel cable, light handles (~6 oz) | Double-unders, high-RPM cardio, CrossFit | Fast and punishing to learn on; **indoor only** (cable frays on concrete) |
| **Weighted rope** | Heavy cable and/or handle weights (½–2 lb) | Upper-body strength, conditioning, building rhythm | Heavier, less portable; slower |
| **Beaded rope** | Plastic beads on a cord | Learning rhythm (audible click), boxing footwork, **outdoor** durability | Slower rotation than bearings; beads can fray |
| **Smart rope** | Sensor in handle + LED/app | Counting jumps, tracking calories, guided workouts | Costs more; may need a subscription |
| **Basic PVC / licorice** | Thick plastic cord | Beginners, kids, casual fitness, outdoor | No fast rotation system |

## How to Choose

1. **Goal first.**
   - Speed / double-unders → **speed rope** (thin cable, ball/dual bearings).
   - Strength / conditioning → **weighted** (½ lb is the common sweet spot).
   - Just learning to skip → **beaded or basic PVC** (slightly heavier = easier to feel the rhythm).
   - Want data / motivation → **smart rope**.
2. **Bearings matter most for feel.** Ball-bearing or dual-bearing handles spin smoothly; cheap fixed-eyelet ropes tangle and stutter. Even $9 ropes now include ball bearings.
3. **Size the cable.** Stand on the rope's midpoint; handles should reach roughly **armpit to sternum**. Most quality ropes are adjustable; some (foam-grip/weighted) ship at a fixed length.
4. **Handle material.** Aluminum (knurled) = lightest and fastest but indoor-only and pricier; foam/rubber = comfiest grip; plastic = cheapest.
5. **Indoor vs outdoor.** Thin steel speed cables shred on rough surfaces — use **beaded or thick PVC** outside.

## Sizing Cheat Sheet
- Stand on the cable center, pull handles up: tips should land between **belly button and shoulders** (chest height is ideal for most).
- Beginners can go slightly *longer*; advanced jumpers go *shorter* for faster, tighter turns.

## Beginner Note
A **slightly heavier or thicker rope** helps beginners feel the rope's position and build timing. Featherweight speed ropes are for people who already have the rhythm. (See [[Research - Best Jump Ropes to Buy (Ranked by Budget)]] for specific picks.)
