# Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model

The **Hedonic Adaptation Prevention (HAP) model** (Sheldon & Lyubomirsky) explains *why* the happiness from any positive change fades — and, crucially, names the **two things that can stop it from fading.** If [[Hedonic Treadmill|hedonic adaptation]] is the problem ("you adapt back to baseline"), the HAP model is the operating manual for resisting it. It is the reason "falling in love with your life" requires ongoing practice rather than a one-time mindset switch.

## The Two Erosion Routes

A positive life change (new job, relationship, home, even a new gratitude habit) loses its wellbeing boost through two processes (Source: [[Sheldon Lyubomirsky — Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model]]):

1. **Bottom-up erosion** — the positive *emotions* the change generated simply decline in frequency over time; the novelty wears off.
2. **Top-down erosion (rising aspirations)** — you raise the bar. What thrilled you becomes the new normal, and you now want *more* to feel the same lift. (This is the "arrival fallacy" / the [[Hedonic Treadmill]]'s engine.)

## The Two Moderators (The Whole Point)

The model's value is that it identifies two levers that **slow** both erosion routes:

1. **APPRECIATION** — continued, deliberate appreciation of the original positive change. Keep noticing and being grateful for it instead of taking it for granted. Operationalized via [[Gratitude Practice (Evidence Review)]] and [[Savoring]].
2. **VARIETY** — continued *variety* in the experiences related to the change. Novelty resists bottom-up decline because varied experiences keep generating fresh positive emotion. *"Variety is the spice of happiness."*

So the durability formula is: **a positive change stays a source of happiness only as long as you keep appreciating it AND keep experiencing it in varied ways.** Let either lapse and you adapt back to baseline.

## Why This Reframes "Loving Your Life"

The naïve model says: get the right life, then feel good about it permanently. The HAP model says that is impossible — **adaptation is automatic, so wellbeing is a *maintained practice*, not an achieved state.** Falling in love with your life is therefore less about changing your circumstances and more about (a) appreciating what is already there and (b) keeping variety in how you engage it. This is precisely why [[Savoring]], [[Gratitude Practice (Evidence Review)|gratitude]], and [[Awe (Everyday Wonder)|awe]] are the practical toolkit: they *are* appreciation, and they reset attention onto the positive that has gone invisible through adaptation.

## Practical Implications

- **Don't bank on circumstance.** A raise, purchase, or even a new relationship will adapt; budget for the fade.
- **Pair every positive change with an appreciation habit** so it doesn't go invisible.
- **Engineer variety deliberately** — rotate routines, novelty, and the *form* of your appreciation practices (a fixed daily gratitude list adapts; a varied one resists).
- **Invest in things that supply variety natively** — relationships, skill/flow activities, and learning change as you grow, so they adapt slowly (mirrors the "what doesn't adapt" list in [[Hedonic Treadmill]]).

> [!note] Confidence
> The two-route / two-moderator structure is well-specified and supported by Sheldon & Lyubomirsky's longitudinal tests (medium-high). Exact magnitudes are study-specific.
