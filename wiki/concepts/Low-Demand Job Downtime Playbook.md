# Low-Demand Job Downtime Playbook

**The runnable answer** to: *"My job only needs ~10 minutes of customer talk per hour — what do I do with the other ~50, on my phone, without permanent deep focus?"*

The whole answer follows from one fact: the hourly call is an **interruption**, and interruptions only tax work that holds a lot of context ([[Interruption Resumption Cost]]). So fill the gaps with **[[Fragmentation-Tolerant Activities|fragmentation-tolerant]]** work and bank the deep stuff for off the clock.

## The one rule

> **Match the activity to the resumption cost.** If a customer call would wreck it, don't start it at work. If you can drop it mid-sentence and pick it back up cold, it's fair game.

## Tier S — effortful *and* interruptible (best use of the time)

These build a real skill **and** survive being chopped up. This is where the 50 minutes should mostly go.

- **Spaced-repetition flashcards** — [[Anki]] / Anki-style decks on the phone. The canonical fragmentation-tolerant unit: one card = one self-contained rep ([[Spaced Repetition]] + [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]]). For Joe: EE/circuits decks, semiconductor physics, math facts, a language deck.
- **Language drills** — Duolingo / Anki / Language Transfer (free podcast). Each prompt is atomic; pause anywhere.
- **One easy problem at a time** — a single LeetCode-Easy, a Brilliant module, a Project Euler warm-up. Pick *short* problems so a call lands between them, not inside one ([[Problem-Solving Practice Activities (Concrete Menu)]]).
- **Active reading in chunks** — a saved article or textbook section, with the [[Generation Effect (Handwriting)|one-line capture]] after each chunk so it's retrieval, not just exposure.

## Tier A — passive learning (low effort, near-zero resumption cost)

Great for *exposure* and for the hours you're too tired for Tier S. The medium holds the state — rewind and you've lost nothing.

- **Audiobooks** — same comprehension benefit as reading for non-fiction (Willingham). Career, EE, finance, books off Joe's [[Library Availability — Master Book List (Livermore)|Libby list]].
- **Podcasts / recorded lectures** — MIT OCW audio, technical interviews ([[Dwarkesh Patel]]), industry shows.
- **Catch:** passive input is low-density ([[Active vs Passive Leisure]]). To make it *stick*, add one retrieval beat — a sentence of notes per episode — or it's just nicer background.

## Tier B — admin & life-maintenance (clears the deck, frees real evenings)

Low resumption cost, and doing them here protects your *uninterrupted* time for deep work.

- Inbox/email triage, pre-drafting replies, calendar and week planning ([[Time Management Habits]]).
- Resume/portfolio touch-ups, one networking message (mentor, peer — news/trends/advice).
- Personal admin: appointments, lists, budgeting, knock out small errands-by-phone.
- Get faster at the *actual job*: drill the knowledge base/scripts so the 10 customer-minutes get easier.

## Tier C — recovery & body (don't spend every gap on output)

- **Park a hard problem on purpose** before a call — let the [[Focused vs Diffuse Mode|diffuse mode]] work it while you talk. (This only works for problems you *deliberately* set down, not ones you're mid-stride on.)
- Discreet movement: stand, stretch, walk, posture reset every gap ([[Exercise Snacks]], [[Breaking Up Sedentary Time]]) — you're seated for a full shift; this is high-leverage.
- Genuine micro-rest: look away from the screen, breathe. Restores attention; scrolling doesn't ([[Phone-Reflex in Idle Moments]]).

## What NOT to do between calls

- **Don't start deep work** — a proof, debugging your STM32 firmware, an essay, an edge-of-ability practice rep. The call shreds it and you get the stress without the depth ([[Interruption Resumption Cost]]). Bank these for continuous off-clock blocks ([[Deep Work]]).
- **Don't default to the feed** — short-video switching *raises* boredom and leaves you neither rested nor advanced ([[Digital Switching]], [[Phone-Reflex in Idle Moments]]).

## Make it automatic (engineer the default)

Same move as the [[Killing Time Substitution Playbook]]: don't rely on willpower at minute 50 of every hour.

1. **Pre-load the good option** — Anki widget on the home screen, audiobook queued, one problem already open. The thing you'll do is the thing that's *already there*.
2. **Add friction to the feed** — social apps off the home screen / logged out / greyscale during the shift.
3. **Pick a default per energy level** — fresh → Tier S; tired → Tier A; fried → Tier C. Decide the rule once, not every hour.
4. **One retrieval beat** turns passive Tier A into real learning — a sentence of notes beats another hour of pure listening.

## The one-line takeaway

> Your job hands you ~50 fragmented minutes an hour. Don't fight the fragmentation — feed it work that's *built* from small pieces (flashcards, drills, one problem, an audiobook), and save anything that needs an unbroken mind for when your mind is actually unbroken.
