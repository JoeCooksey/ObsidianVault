# Asset Allocation by Age

> Your stock/bond split should track your **time horizon**, not your mood. Younger = more stocks, because you have decades to ride out volatility and the upside compounds.

## Rules of Thumb (% in stocks) — confidence: medium (convention)

| Rule | Formula | Risk profile | At age 19 |
|------|---------|--------------|-----------|
| Age in bonds | stocks = 100 − age | conservative (often *too* conservative) | 81% stocks |
| Rule of 110 | stocks = 110 − age | moderate | 91% stocks |
| Rule of 120 | stocks = 120 − age | aggressive; ≈ what target-date funds do for the young | 101% → cap at 100% |

These are starting points, not laws (Source: [[Asset Allocation by Age (Optimized Portfolio)]]).

## What the Pros Actually Do

- Target-date funds hold **≤5% bonds for investors in their 20s–40s**, rising to ~8.5% in the 50s, then climbing steeply toward retirement.
- International is **20–40% of the stock sleeve** (Vanguard uses 40%).

## The Glide Path

```
Age 19–40:   90–100% stocks / 0–10% bonds     (accumulate aggressively)
Age 40–50:   ~80% stocks / ~20% bonds          (start de-risking)
Age 50–60:   ~70% / ~30%
Age 60+:     ~50–60% / ~40–50%                 (protect what you have)
```

> [!tip] For a 19-year-old
> Bonds mostly do nothing useful for you yet — they cap the upside you most need. A ~90–100% equity allocation (US + international index funds) is appropriate and matches what target-date 2070 funds actually hold. Add bonds gradually starting in your 40s.

## Stocks ≠ Volatility You Can't Handle

The reason the young can run 90–100% equities: a 40+ year horizon means even a −50% crash is a *buying opportunity*, not a loss — you only lock in losses by selling. See the behavioral points in [[Index Fund Investing]] and the tail-risk framing in [[Skewness of Stock Returns]].
