# Core-Satellite Portfolio

> The evidence-compatible way to pick stocks anyway: an indexed core that guarantees market returns on most of your wealth, plus a hard-capped satellite for individual picks. The core does the compounding; the satellite does the learning.

## Structure

- **Core: 80–90%+** in S-tier broad index funds ([[Index Fund Tier List]]) on automatic DCA. Never traded, never timed.
- **Satellite: 10–20% max** for individual stocks, tilts (QQQ/SCHD-type), or theses. Sized so that even a −100% satellite outcome doesn't change your financial trajectory.

Common advisory framing is ~80/20 (confidence: medium — convention, not a derived optimum; more conservative writers say 90/10 or 95/5).

## Why the Cap Is the Whole Mechanism

The satellite cap converts the three anti-picking evidence layers from fatal to affordable:

- ~90% of professionals lose to the index long-term → your core never plays that game (Source: [[SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index]])
- The typical stock loses to T-bills → satellite losses are bounded ([[Skewness of Stock Returns]])
- Retail behavior costs ~3.6%/yr and active traders lag buy-and-hold by ~7 pp/yr → the behavior tax only applies to 10–20% of assets (Source: [[Barber and Odean - Individual Investor Performance]])

Meanwhile the real benefits of picking survive: engagement, accounting/valuation skill-building, domain-edge lottery tickets, and the honest scoreboard.

## Rules That Make It Work

1. **Cap is enforced by flow, not willpower** — new money goes to the core automatically; the satellite only grows by performance.
2. **Benchmark the satellite annually vs VTI** — if it lags 3 years running, the experiment produced its answer.
3. **Satellite picks still follow process** — [[Fundamental Analysis Valuation Metrics]], [[Position Sizing and Risk Management]], [[Sell Discipline (When to Sell a Stock)]].
4. **Never raid the core to fund a conviction** — the moment the cap moves for a "sure thing," the structure is dead.
