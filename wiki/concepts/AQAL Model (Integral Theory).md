# AQAL Model (Integral Theory)

## Definition
**AQAL** — short for **"All Quadrants, All Levels"** (also all lines, states, and types) — is **[[Ken Wilber]]**'s framework for **Integral Theory**, an attempt to synthesize all human knowledge into one map for navigating reality, consciousness, and human development. In **[[Craig Perry]]**'s video it's used as "the map of all knowledge" that lets you build more complete [[Good Explanations (Hard-to-Vary)|explanations]]. Wilber stresses it is a **work in progress, not final** — to be built upon.

## Why It Matters
Without a map of knowledge as a whole, Perry argues, "you keep solving the wrong problems in the wrong quadrant, wondering why nothing ever changes." AQAL gives a checklist for completeness: it tells you **what** to look at (quadrants), **how clearly** you're seeing it (levels), and **where** your growth is lopsided (lines) — so an explanation isn't accidentally confined to one slice of reality.

## How It Works
The video uses three of AQAL's components (omitting *states* and *types*) as a sequence — **quadrants → levels → lines**:
1. **[[Four Quadrants (AQAL)|Quadrants]]** — four irreducible perspectives on any situation (I / it / we / its). *What to look at.*
2. **[[Nine Levels of Thinking|Levels]]** — your altitude of awareness, determining how clearly you see each quadrant. *How to look.*
3. **[[Lines of Development|Lines]]** — the many independent dimensions (cognitive, emotional, moral…) you grow along at different rates. *Where you're uneven.*

Together these are meant to give a "holistic approach to understanding reality, consciousness, and human development."

## Cross-Domain Connections
- A meta-framework: it tries to *contain* other models (psychology, science, spirituality) rather than compete with them — which is also the main critique (it's an integrating philosophy, not an empirically tested theory).
- The developmental "levels" overlap heavily with **Spiral Dynamics** and ego-development / constructive-developmental psychology.
- Directly supports [[Good Explanations (Hard-to-Vary)]]: completeness across quadrants/levels/lines is Perry's operational definition of a good explanation.
