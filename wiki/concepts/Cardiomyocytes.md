# Cardiomyocytes

Heart muscle cells that contract in synchrony to pump blood. **Ranked #2** in [[Research - Most Important Cells in the Human Body]] — single point of failure for the whole organism.

## Why they rank so high
- **Essentially non-regenerating.** Adult cardiomyocytes renew at only ~1%/year and decline with age; heart failure is principally a disease of cardiomyocyte loss. (confidence high)
- **No redundancy.** Unlike skin or gut, lost pumping capacity is not rebuilt — damage (e.g. heart attack) is permanent scar.
- **Extremely mitochondria-dense** (~30–40% of cell volume) — they never rest, so [[Mitochondria]] health is critical here.

## How to optimize them (actionable)
1. **Aerobic / endurance exercise** — improves cardiac efficiency, mitochondrial density, and stroke volume; the heart adapts like any trained muscle.
2. **Control blood pressure** — chronic hypertension forces pathological hypertrophy and cardiomyocyte loss.
3. **Protect the vessels that feed them** — optimize [[Endothelial Cells]]; coronary endothelial dysfunction starves cardiomyocytes.
4. **Limit alcohol** — directly cardiotoxic at chronic high intake (alcoholic cardiomyopathy).
5. **Omega-3 fatty acids** — support membrane stability and may reduce arrhythmia risk.
6. **Manage metabolic health** — diabetes and obesity accelerate cardiomyocyte dysfunction.
