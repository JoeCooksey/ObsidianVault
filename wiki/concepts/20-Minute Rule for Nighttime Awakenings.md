# 20-Minute Rule for Nighttime Awakenings

The central tactic of **stimulus control therapy**: if you wake in the night and cannot return to sleep within ~15–20 minutes, **get out of bed** and do something quiet and relaxing in dim light until sleepy, then return (Source: [[CBT-I Primer — Walker et al. 2022]]; [[Harvard Health — Awake at 3 a.m.]]). Lying awake in bed teaches the brain to associate the bed with wakefulness; leaving preserves the bed-equals-sleep association (confidence: high).

## How to do it

- **Don't watch the clock.** Estimate, don't measure — turn clocks away. Clock-watching raises stress and makes return harder.
- **Leave the bed** (and ideally the bedroom) for a calm activity: reading on paper, gentle stretching, quiet music.
- **Keep lights dim** and avoid screens — blue light suppresses melatonin.
- **No stimulation**: no work, no doom-scrolling, no eating.
- **Return only when sleepy**, not just tired.

## Relaxation aids while awake

- **Breathing exercises** and calm music can ease the return to sleep.
- **Progressive muscle relaxation**: tense each muscle group at ~¾ strength for ~5 seconds, then release all at once.

> [!tip] Pair this with consistent wake times and [[Sleep Restriction Therapy]] so sleep drive is high enough that returning to sleep gets easier over weeks.
