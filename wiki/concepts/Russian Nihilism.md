# Russian Nihilism

The radical intellectual movement of 1860s Russia that rejected traditional authority — religion, family, the state, inherited morality — in favor of reason, science, materialism, and social utility. The term was popularized by Turgenev's *Fathers and Sons* (1862). Nihilists held that no belief should be accepted without rational proof, and that existing institutions should be swept away to build society on rational foundations.

[[Fyodor Dostoevsky]] saw nihilism as a spiritual catastrophe in the making, and *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]* is in large part a response to it. [[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov's]] willingness to murder on the strength of a theory is what happens when a mind absorbs nihilist and [[Rational Egoism|rational-egoist]] premises and follows them to their conclusion. The novel's counter-thesis — [[Redemption Through Suffering]] through faith and love — is Dostoevsky's answer to the nihilist claim that only reason and self-interest are real.
