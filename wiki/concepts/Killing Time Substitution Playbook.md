# Killing Time Substitution Playbook

The "I'm about to kill time — what should I do instead?" answer. Where [[Boredom Activity Playbook]] handles acute *boredom*, this handles **idle pockets and dead-time** — the waits, gaps, and transitions you'd normally fill with the phone.

The whole thing rests on one reframe: every idle pocket is either **[[Alive Time vs Dead Time|alive time or dead time]]** — same minutes, the only variable is what you do with them.

## Step 0 — Catch the reflex

The default move is the [[Phone-Reflex in Idle Moments|phone reflex]], and it's the one move proven to make idle time worse ([[Digital Switching]]). Name the pause: *"I have N free minutes — alive or dead?"* That half-second of awareness is the whole game.

## Step 1 — Match the pocket to its size

Dead time comes in sizes. Pick by what the pocket can actually hold.

### Micro (30 sec – 5 min) — queues, lifts, kettle, ad breaks
- Look around / people-watch / notice surroundings (restores attention)
- Two-minute physical thing: posture reset, mobility, breathe ([[Exercise Snacks]])
- Let the mind wander on a real problem — the [[Focused vs Diffuse Mode|diffuse mode]] window
- One useful text: thank someone, make a plan, check in on a friend

### Short (5 – 30 min) — commute, waiting room, between classes
- Read (book / saved article) — the highest-density default
- Phone-free walk, ideally near anything green ([[Attention Restoration Theory]])
- One small rep of a skill: language app, a problem, a few bars on an instrument
- Capture: journal a line, jot an idea, plan the next block

### Open (30 min +) — a free evening, a dead Sunday
- An **effortful / active** session: project, sport, instrument, cooking, a hard game ([[Active vs Passive Leisure]], [[Effortful Leisure (Meaning vs Enjoyment)]])
- See a person in real life (highest well-being lever — [[Free Time Tier List]])
- Deliberate rest if depleted: walk, nap, sit — *not* scrolling

## Step 2 — Pick effortful over easy

When two options are available, choose the one with **a little friction and a skill in it**. Effort is what converts a passed hour into a *meaningful* one, and in real life it doesn't even cost enjoyment ([[Effortful Leisure (Meaning vs Enjoyment)]]). The frictionless option wins by default, not by preference — so beat it on setup, not willpower.

## Step 3 — Engineer the defaults (the real work)

You won't out-discipline the slot machine in the moment. Change what's easiest:

- **Make the good option frictionless**: book on the pillow, instrument on the stand, walking shoes by the door, puzzle open on the desk.
- **Make the bad option costly**: phone in another room, greyscale, app timers, log out.
- **Defend continuous blocks** so they don't shred into [[Time Confetti|time confetti]]: batch notifications, schedule leisure like a meeting.

## The one-line rule

> A pause is not a void to fill — it's the only unstructured time the day gives you. Spend it on something with a skill in it, or rest on purpose. Never feed it to the slot machine.

## When it's genuinely rest you need

Killing time and resting are different. If you're fried, **rest deliberately** — walk, nap, sit, one chosen passive thing — which restores you. Scrolling stimulates without restoring, so you end up neither rested nor having done anything. Choose rest *as* an activity, not as a failure to choose.
