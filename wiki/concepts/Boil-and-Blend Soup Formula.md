# Boil-and-Blend Soup Formula

**One method, infinite soups.** Pick one item from each category, boil everything together, blend smooth. Want to use many vegetables at once? See the **Multi-Vegetable Blend** section below.

---

## Multi-Vegetable Blend: Using 4–6 Vegetables at Once

You can throw in as many vegetables as you want — with two rules:

**Rule 1: Always include 1 STARCHY ANCHOR**
Potato, sweet potato, parsnip, or butternut squash. This is the binder — it gives the blended soup body and makes everything cohesive instead of watery. Without it, a multi-vegetable soup often turns thin and separated.

**Rule 2: Stay within a COLOR FAMILY**
Blending across color groups (e.g., carrot + broccoli + beet) produces a brown, unappetizing color even if it tastes fine. Pick your vegetables from one group for a soup that looks as good as it tastes.

### Color Families — Pick All Your Vegetables From One

**Orange Family** — warm, sweet, hearty
Carrot · butternut squash · sweet potato · red lentils · parsnip

*Example combo*: carrot + sweet potato + butternut squash + parsnip → Curry or Ginger+Citrus spice profile

---

**White/Cream Family** — mild, silky, neutral
Cauliflower · russet potato · parsnip · celeriac · leek · white onion

*Example combo*: cauliflower + potato + leek + parsnip → Garlic Butter or Classic spice profile

---

**Green Family** — fresh, vegetal, bright
Broccoli · zucchini · frozen peas · spinach · celery · green onion

*Example combo*: broccoli + zucchini + peas + celery → Classic or Garlic Butter; finish with lemon

---

**Red/Tomato Family** — tangy, acidic, rich
Roma tomatoes · red bell pepper · canned diced tomatoes

*Example combo*: fresh tomatoes + red bell pepper + onion + garlic → Italian/Herb or Smoky/Paprika spice profile

---

### Multi-Veg Ratio Rule
```
Total vegetables = ~2–2.5 lbs
  → 30–40% STARCHY ANCHOR (potato, sweet potato, parsnip, squash)
  → 60–70% the rest of your color family

Liquid = 5–6 cups broth (slightly more than single-veg — diverse veg absorbs more)
```

### The "Everything from the Fridge" Approach
If you have odds and ends — half a zucchini, a few carrots, some cauliflower florets — sort them into which color family dominates, add a potato regardless, and pick the spice profile that fits. This is the French **potage** method: no precise recipe, just whatever's available boiled together with an aromatic base and a flavor direction.

---

## The Method (Always the Same)

1. Rough chop all vegetables — size doesn't matter, it all gets blended
2. Add BASE + AROMATICS + LIQUID to a pot
3. Bring to boil, reduce to simmer **20–30 min** until everything is fork-tender
4. Add SPICE PROFILE (dried spices can go in during the boil; fresh herbs add at end)
5. Blend smooth — immersion blender in pot is easiest; regular blender works in batches
6. Stir in ENRICHER if using
7. Adjust thickness with more broth or water; adjust seasoning with salt

**That's it.** No sautéing required. No roasting. Just chop, boil, blend.

---

## The Formula

```
BASE (1.5–2 lbs)
+ AROMATICS (always: 1 onion + 3 garlic cloves)
+ LIQUID (4–6 cups broth — less = thicker, more = thinner)
+ SPICE PROFILE (pick one)
+ ENRICHER (optional)
→ boil 25 min → blend → season → done
```

## Category B: AROMATICS
*Always include onion + garlic. Add one optional.*

| Aromatic | Amount | Effect |
|----------|--------|--------|
| **Onion** (always) | 1 medium | Body, sweetness, depth |
| **Garlic** (almost always) | 3–5 cloves | Savory depth |
| Fresh ginger | 1-inch piece | Warmth, brightness — use with curry or Asian profiles |
| Leek | 1 stalk | Milder, sweeter than onion — pair with potato, broccoli |
| Celery | 2–3 stalks | Savory backbone — good with neutral bases (potato, cauliflower) |

## Category C: SPICE PROFILES
*Pick exactly one. Add spices to the pot during the boil.*

### Classic / Pure
- Salt + black pepper + 1 bay leaf
- Best base: potato, cauliflower, zucchini
- Result: clean, vegetable-forward — lets the base flavor shine

### Garlic Butter
- Salt + pepper + 4–6 extra garlic cloves in the boil + 1 tbsp butter stirred in after blending
- Best base: potato, broccoli, cauliflower
- Result: rich, savory, crowd-pleaser

### Curry / Warm Spice
- 2 tsp curry powder + 1 tsp Kirkland turmeric + 1 tsp cumin + ½ tsp coriander + pinch cayenne
- Best base: carrot, sweet potato, butternut squash
- Result: aromatic, warming, slightly complex

### Ginger + Citrus
- 1-inch fresh ginger + zest of 1 lemon or orange (add after blending) + pinch nutmeg
- Best base: carrot, butternut squash, sweet potato
- Result: bright, fresh, slightly exotic

### Smoky / Paprika
- 1.5 tsp smoked paprika + 1 tsp cumin + ½ tsp coriander
- Best base: red bell pepper, tomato, sweet potato
- Result: smoky, robust, Spanish-style

### Italian / Herb
- 1 tsp dried basil + 1 tsp oregano + ½ tsp thyme + pinch red pepper flakes
- Best base: tomato, zucchini, carrot
- Result: bright, herbal, familiar

### Coconut + Thai
- Add 1 can Kirkland coconut milk as part of the liquid + 1-inch ginger + 1 tsp curry powder + squeeze lime after blending
- Best base: butternut squash, carrot, sweet potato
- Result: creamy, tropical, slightly sweet

## Category D: ENRICHERS (Optional — Add After Blending)
*Pick 0 or 1. Stir in after blending, over low heat.*

| Enricher | Amount | Effect |
|----------|--------|--------|
| Kirkland coconut milk | ¼–½ can | Creamy, rich, slightly sweet — great with curry or ginger profiles |
| Heavy cream | 2–4 tbsp | Silky, neutral richness — pairs with any profile |
| Butter | 1–2 tbsp | Gloss, richness, rounds out acid |
| Lemon juice | 1–2 tsp | Brightens and lifts — good with green bases or curry |
| Parmesan (grated) | 2 tbsp | Savory umami — Italian profile, potato or broccoli base |

## Thickness Reference
| Broth Amount (per 1.5 lbs base veg) | Result |
|--------------------------------------|--------|
| 3–4 cups | Very thick — almost a purée; serve as a side or thin at table |
| 5 cups | Standard soup thickness |
| 6–7 cups | Lighter, brothier — better when adding enricher |

**Rule**: Start with less broth. You can always thin after blending. You can't thicken (without simmering longer).

## Notes

**Can you combine categories?** Yes — mixing two bases works well when they're in the same row (e.g., carrot + sweet potato, potato + cauliflower). Avoid mixing across very different flavor profiles (e.g., broccoli + tomato won't blend well).

**How to fix common problems:**
- Too bland → more salt first, then a squeeze of lemon
- Too thin → simmer uncovered 10 min after blending to reduce
- Too thick → add broth ¼ cup at a time while blending
- Too bitter (broccoli/cauliflower) → add a pinch of sugar or ½ tsp honey before blending
