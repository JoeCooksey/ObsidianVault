# Sleep Restriction Therapy

A core component of [[CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia)|CBT-I]] that treats fragmented sleep by **limiting time in bed to match actual sleep ability**, then expanding it gradually as sleep consolidates (Source: [[CBT-I Primer — Walker et al. 2022]]). When applied specifically to nighttime wakefulness it is sometimes called Sleep Consolidation Therapy.

## Mechanism

Spending excess time in bed dilutes sleep across more hours, producing light, broken sleep and long awake periods. Restricting time in bed:
- **Raises homeostatic sleep drive** (more adenosine pressure) so sleep is deeper and less interruptible.
- **Increases sleep efficiency** (time asleep ÷ time in bed), shrinking mid-night awakenings.

## How it works in practice

1. Use a sleep diary to find average actual sleep time (e.g., 6 h).
2. Set time in bed to roughly that amount with a fixed wake time.
3. Once sleep efficiency is consistently high (~85–90%), add ~15–30 min to time in bed weekly.

> [!warning] Causes short-term daytime sleepiness during the restriction phase. This is expected and temporary — do not drive drowsy. Best done with clinician guidance.

> [!gap] Restriction therapy is distinct from involuntary sleep *deprivation*, which harms metabolic and cognitive function (Source: [[Leproult Van Cauter Sleep Restriction RCT 2011]]). The therapeutic version is a controlled, temporary tool to rebuild consolidated sleep.
