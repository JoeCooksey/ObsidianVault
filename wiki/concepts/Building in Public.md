# Building in Public

Doing your learning and project work **where others can see it** — public commits, blog posts, code walkthroughs, contributions — so the byproduct of practicing is a growing, discoverable reputation. The daily work and the career asset become the same action. (Sources: developer-community and open-source-contribution analyses, 2025–2026)

## Why it compounds

- **Visible portfolio.** Open-source contributions and public repos become live portfolio items hiring managers can inspect — your code style, documentation, test coverage, ability to follow conventions.
- **Reputation per merge.** "As your pull requests land, your reputation rises with each merge — that momentum compounds into interviews, offers, and leadership invitations."
- **Direct hiring pull.** GitHub's Octoverse documents large public-contribution growth; teams that depend on a library often recruit its maintainers. Dev.to authors report small consistent posts leading to mentoring offers, speaking invites, and hiring interest.
- **It's permissionless leverage.** Code and media require no gatekeeper. (Source: [[Permissionless Leverage]])

## What actually impresses (2026 hiring view)

- **One merged PR to a real project > five solo clone projects.** A real contribution proves you can navigate an unfamiliar codebase, accept feedback, and ship into a system you didn't design — day-one job skills.
- **Quality over quantity:** three excellent projects with a clear reason to exist beat twelve unremarkable ones. You'll discuss them under pressure, so build things you genuinely care about.
- **Solve a real problem**, not a Netflix/Spotify/Twitter clone — clones look impressive but show no product thinking.
- **Every repo needs a README** that explains the project to a stranger who finds it.
