# Milk Kefir Making — Step-by-Step Guide

Milk kefir is a fermented dairy drink made by culturing milk with **kefir grains** — a symbiotic colony of bacteria and yeast (SCOBY) that looks like soft, rubbery cauliflower florets. The grains are reusable indefinitely; one teaspoon ferments milk forever if cared for (Source: [[Cultured.guru — How to Make Fermented Milk Kefir]], [[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]]).

## The Core Ratio

- **~1 Tbsp (≈10–15 g) of active grains per 1–2 cups (250–500 mL) of milk** is the standard working ratio (confidence: **high** — agrees across all method sources).
- A practical target: **1:10 to 1:15 grains-to-milk by volume**, adjusted so the kefir thickens in **14–24 hours**.
- More grains per cup = **faster, more sour** ferment (grains eat lactose faster). Fewer grains = milder, slower.

## Equipment

| Item | Why |
|------|-----|
| Glass jar | Metal weakens grains over time; **never ferment in plastic** (can sour the kefir) (Source: [[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]]) |
| Plastic, nylon, or silicone strainer | Avoid prolonged metal contact when straining |
| Breathable cover (coffee filter, paper towel, cloth) + rubber band | Keeps dust/insects out, lets gas escape |
| Wooden or plastic spoon | For stirring/straining |

> [!note] Brief contact with stainless steel (a quick strain) is fine. The concern is *prolonged* metal exposure, not a momentary touch.

## First Fermentation — Step by Step

1. **Add grains + milk to a glass jar.** ~1 Tbsp grains to 2–4 cups whole milk.
2. **Cover** with a breathable cloth/coffee filter, secure with a rubber band.
3. **Ferment in a warm, dark spot**, ideally **18–24 °C (65–75 °F)**; tolerable range **18–29 °C (65–85 °F)** (Source: [[Cultured.guru — How to Make Fermented Milk Kefir]]).
4. **Wait 12–36 hours** (typically ~24 h at 70 °F). Warmer rooms ≈ 12 h; cooler rooms up to 48 h.
5. **Check for doneness:** kefir has thickened slightly, smells tangy/yeasty, and may show small curds or whey separation. Slight separation = slightly over-fermented but still good.
6. **Strain** the grains out into a clean jar. Gently stir/push the kefir through the strainer.
7. **Restart immediately:** put the strained grains into fresh milk to begin the next batch.
8. **Store the finished kefir** with a lid in the fridge for up to ~2 weeks.

## Second Fermentation (Optional) — Flavor + Mellowing

Once grains are removed, the kefir can be cultured again **without the grains** to mellow sourness and add flavor (Source: [[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]]).

- **Method:** seal strained kefir in a jar (add flavorings), leave at room temp **6–12 h**, then refrigerate.
- **Effect:** mellows sharp acidity, can build mild carbonation, develops B vitamins.
- **Flavorings:** vanilla, cinnamon, cocoa powder (no need to remove); fruit, citrus peel, ginger (remove after). Add **sweeteners after** the second ferment if you don't want fizz.
- For a *less* sour, creamier result, do the second ferment **in the fridge** instead of at room temp.

## Controlling Sourness (Most Common Complaint)

Milk kefir being too tart is the #1 issue. Fixes (Source: [[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]]):

- **Use fewer grains per cup of milk** (the biggest lever).
- **Shorten fermentation time** or move the jar to the fridge to finish after 4–12 h at room temp.
- **Do a second ferment** to smooth aggressive tartness.
- **Use glass, not plastic.**

## Milk Choice

- **Whole, fresh, organic dairy milk** is best for grain health and a thick result (Source: [[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]]).
- **Avoid UHT/ultra-pasteurized** milk while troubleshooting — it lacks nutrients grains thrive on.
- Low-fat works but yields thinner kefir; grains may slow.
- Non-dairy milks (coconut, almond) can be cultured but **grains must periodically return to dairy** to feed on lactose, or they weaken.
