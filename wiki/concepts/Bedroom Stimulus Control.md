# Bedroom Stimulus Control

Using the bedroom **only for sleep** turns the environment itself into a conditioned cue: walking in becomes a signal that it is time to sleep. This is classical (Pavlovian) conditioning — the same mechanism by which a kitchen cues hunger.

When the bed and room are also used for working, phone scrolling, or hanging out, the association weakens and the brain no longer reliably interprets "in bed" as "time to sleep," which lengthens sleep latency (time to fall asleep).

> [!example] Separating the spaces
> The [[Hypertrophy Academy]] creator kept his clothes in a different room so he had no daytime reason to enter the bedroom. Entering it became an unambiguous "time for bed" trigger, which he credits with faster, easier sleep onset.

**Practical rules:**
- Don't work, study, or scroll your phone in bed.
- Ideally don't enter the bedroom during the day at all.
- Reserve the bed for sleep (and sex) only.

This is a core component of the [[Sleepmaxxing Protocol]] and complements the physical setup in [[Sleep Environment Optimization]]. Stimulus control is also one of the established pillars of clinical CBT-I (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia).
