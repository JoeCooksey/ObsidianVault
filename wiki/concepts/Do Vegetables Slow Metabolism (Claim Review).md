# Do Vegetables Slow Metabolism (Claim Review)

The "vegetables destroy metabolism" claim is vaguer than the gut claim and rests on three sub-arguments. None survive contact with human evidence at normal intakes.

## Claim 1: Goitrogens Suppress the Thyroid → Slow Metabolism

The mechanistic story: cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, kale, cabbage, Brussels sprouts) and soy contain **glucosinolates/thiocyanates** that can inhibit iodine uptake or thyroid peroxidase, lowering thyroid hormone output and thus metabolic rate.

**What the human evidence shows:**
- 150 g of **cooked Brussels sprouts daily for 4 weeks** produced **no negative effect** on thyroid parameters (Source: [[Perceived Problematic Plant Compounds (Narrative Review)]]).
- **Two human studies** using high-isothiocyanate broccoli sprout extract found **no thyroid effect even in people with autoimmune thyroid disease** (Source: [[Antinutrients-Plant-Defense-Compounds]]).
- The enzyme that generates the goitrogenic compounds is **deactivated by cooking**, so cooked crucifers carry far less.
- Effects appear meaningfully only with **iodine deficiency** — rare where salt is iodized.

> [!high] In iodine-replete people eating normal (especially cooked) servings, cruciferous vegetables do not impair thyroid function. Confidence: high. The "metabolism-wrecking goitrogen" story is the most legitimate *mechanism* in the anti-vegetable case, and it still fails at real-world doses.

## Claim 2: Carbs/Fiber Spike Insulin → Store Fat → Slow Metabolism

The low-carb/carnivore framing treats vegetable carbohydrates and fiber as drivers of insulin and fat storage. This is the weakest version:
- Non-starchy vegetables are **low-calorie, low-glycemic, and high in fiber** — fiber *blunts* glucose response rather than spiking it.
- There is **no evidence that vegetable intake lowers resting metabolic rate.** If anything, the **thermic effect of food** is higher for less-processed, higher-fiber meals, and fiber increases satiety (helping energy balance, not hurting it).

## Claim 3: Anti-Nutrients Block Minerals → Deficiency → Metabolic Dysfunction

Oxalates, phytates, lectins, and tannins can reduce absorption of iron, zinc, and calcium **from the same meal**. But:
- The effect is **meal-specific, not systemic**, and a varied diet supplies enough (Source: [[Harvard Nutrition Source - Are Anti-Nutrients Harmful]]).
- **Cooking, soaking, sprouting, and fermentation** sharply reduce these compounds.
- Plant foods carry these compounds *alongside* the minerals and beneficial compounds, netting positive — populations eating the most plants don't show metabolic harm; they show **less** type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease ([[Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality]]).

## The Inversion

The actual population data point the opposite direction from the claim: higher vegetable intake associates with **lower** rates of obesity, type 2 diabetes, and metabolic syndrome — the conditions that genuinely impair metabolic health. There is no body of human evidence showing vegetables lower metabolic rate or cause metabolic disease.

## Verdict

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Goitrogens slow metabolism | Real mechanism, **fails at normal/cooked intake with adequate iodine** |
| Veg carbs/fiber slow metabolism via insulin | **Not supported**; fiber improves glycemic response & satiety |
| Anti-nutrients cause deficiency → metabolic harm | **Overstated**; meal-specific, mitigated by cooking & variety |
