# Controlled Articular Rotations (CARs)

**CARs** are slow, active rotations that take a single joint through its largest possible range of motion while the surrounding muscles stay tensed and the rest of the body stays still. They are the core daily-mobility drill from Dr. Andreo Spina's **Functional Range Conditioning (FRC)** system.

## Mobility vs flexibility

- **Flexibility** = passive range (how far a joint can be moved by an outside force, e.g. a stretch).
- **Mobility** = *active, controlled* range (how far you can move a joint under your own power). Mobility is the more functional, more protective quality — and what CARs train.

## How to do them

- One joint at a time; move through the full circle **slowly** (~10–20 s per rep), exploring the outer edges.
- Keep ~20–50% tension throughout (irradiation) so it's active, not floppy.
- 2–3 reps per direction per joint. A full-body morning sequence takes **5–10 minutes**.

**Joints to cover (head to toe):** neck → shoulders → elbows/wrists → spine (segmental) → hips → knees → ankles. Hips, shoulders, ankles, and thoracic spine are the highest-value for desk-bound people.

## Why daily

Claimed benefits (mechanistically plausible, low-grade evidence): circulates synovial fluid to nourish cartilage, maintains the nervous system's "ownership" of end-range positions so it doesn't down-regulate range you stop using, and gives a daily self-assessment of each joint. Morning is ideal — it primes the day.

> [!gap] CARs are popular and physiologically sensible but lack large RCTs proving longevity or injury-prevention benefit. The strong-evidence longevity levers remain VO₂max, strength, and step volume; CARs serve the **mobility/function** goal.
