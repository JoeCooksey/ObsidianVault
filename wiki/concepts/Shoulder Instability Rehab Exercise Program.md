# Shoulder Instability Rehab Exercise Program

> [!warning] Not medical advice. This is a general, conservative home program built from the principles in [[Shoulder Instability Conservative Rehab]]. A shoulder that **repeatedly pops out / dislocates** should be assessed in person (ideally MRI + a sports-med doc or shoulder surgeon) — exercises manage symptoms but **cannot re-attach a torn labrum**. Stop any movement that causes a sharp pinch, a "slipping" sensation, or that reproduces the dislocation feeling. Pain should stay ≤3/10 and settle within ~24 h.

## The four jobs of the program
A loose/popping shoulder is rehabbed by making the **dynamic stabilizers** do the work the damaged static stabilizers (labrum/capsule) no longer do:
1. **Rotator cuff** — keeps the ball centered in the socket
2. **Scapular stabilizers** (serratus anterior, lower/mid trap, rhomboids) — keeps the socket under the ball
3. **Proprioception / neuromuscular control** — the shoulder reacts *before* it slips
4. **Avoid the danger zone** — abduction + external rotation (arm up and rotated back, the "throwing/apprehension" position), especially loaded, until late stages

> [!note] Equipment: one **light + one medium resistance band**, a wall, a small towel, and optionally a pair of **2–5 lb dumbbells**. That's the whole kit.

---

## Phase 1 — Calm & Activate (Weeks 1–3)
Goal: wake up the cuff and scapula without provoking the shoulder. Keep the elbow **at or below shoulder height** the entire phase. Do this block **once daily**.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| **Pendulum swings** | 1 × 30 s each direction | Lean on a table with the good arm; let the bad arm hang and circle gently from the hips, not the shoulder |
| **Scapular setting (retract + depress)** | 3 × 10 (5 s hold) | "Tuck shoulder blades into your back pockets." No shrug |
| **Isometric external rotation** | 3 × 5 (10 s hold) | Elbow at side, bent 90°, push the back of your hand into a wall/doorframe — *no movement*, ~50% effort |
| **Isometric internal rotation** | 3 × 5 (10 s hold) | Same setup, push palm into the wall |
| **Isometric abduction** | 3 × 5 (10 s hold) | Outside of elbow into a wall, push out gently — *no movement* |
| **Serratus wall slides** | 3 × 10 | Forearms on wall, slide up only as far as pain-free; press into wall at top ("reach long") |

Progress to Phase 2 when: isometrics are pain-free and you can do daily life (dressing, reaching low) without apprehension.

---

## Phase 2 — Strengthen the Cuff & Scapula (Weeks 4–8)
Goal: build real strength through a **safe, mid-range** arc. Keep the arm **in front of the body plane** (no reaching behind). 4–5×/week.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| **Band external rotation** (elbow at side, towel roll in armpit) | 3 × 12 | Rotate hand outward ~45°, slow return. The towel keeps the elbow pinned |
| **Band internal rotation** | 3 × 12 | Mirror of above, rotate hand toward belly |
| **Band rows** | 3 × 12 | Squeeze shoulder blades; elbows brush the ribs |
| **Prone "Y" raises** (lying face down, light/no weight) | 3 × 10 | Thumbs up, lift arms to a "Y"; targets lower trap |
| **Prone "T" raises** | 3 × 10 | Arms out to a "T," thumbs up, squeeze blades |
| **Serratus punch** (on back, light DB, arm vertical) | 3 × 12 | Press the shoulder blade off the floor at the top — small range |
| **Scapular wall push-up plus** | 3 × 12 | Push-up against wall; at the top, "push away" extra to round the upper back |

Rule: **all reps in front of the body, below shoulder height** unless pain-free and confident. Add light dumbbells (2–5 lb) only once band work is easy.

Progress to Phase 3 when: full pain-free overhead reach, no apprehension with daily movements, cuff exercises feel strong.

---

## Phase 3 — Control, Proprioception & Overhead (Weeks 9–12+)
Goal: train the shoulder to stabilize itself under changing load and in higher positions. Approach the abduction + external-rotation arc **gradually and controlled**. 3–4×/week.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhythmic stabilization** | 3 × 20–30 s | Arm out in front, partner or your own free hand taps it from different directions — resist and hold position. The core proprioception drill |
| **Wall ball circles** | 3 × 20 s each way | Press a ball into the wall at shoulder height, draw small slow circles |
| **Band "throwing" decelerations** (controlled) | 3 × 10 | Slow, controlled external→internal rotation at progressively higher arm angles — *only if no apprehension* |
| **Quadruped weight shifts → plank** | 3 × 30 s | On hands and knees, shift weight side to side; progress to a plank as control improves (closed-chain stability) |
| **Overhead band press** (start low, raise the start height over weeks) | 3 × 12 | Only enter overhead range once mid-range is bulletproof |
| **Full "I-Y-T-W"** sequence (light) | 2 × 8 each | The complete scapular series |

Maintenance after Phase 3: keep 2×/week of cuff + scapular work indefinitely — this is the "live with it" insurance, especially if surgery isn't on the table.

---

## How to run it
- **Tempo:** slow and controlled (≈3 s out, 3 s back). Speed is the enemy of a loose shoulder.
- **Pain rule:** keep it ≤3/10 during, and back to baseline within 24 h. Sharp pinch or a "giving way" feeling = stop that exercise.
- **Frequency:** isometrics (Phase 1) tolerate daily; loaded work (Phase 2–3) needs a rest day between sessions for the same muscles.
- **Don't rush phases.** Re-dislocating resets the clock and, per [[Shoulder Instability Recurrence Risk Factors]], each event makes a future repair work worse.

## The honest limit of this program
Per the evidence in [[Research - Rotator Cuff Tear and Recurrent Shoulder Instability]]: in a **young, active person with a traumatic labral tear**, conservative rehab still leaves a **~53–67% re-dislocation rate** vs ~8–10% with surgery. So if the shoulder keeps popping out *despite* doing this consistently for ~3 months, that's the signal to discuss **stabilization surgery** — not to grind more reps. For ordinary cuff *tendinopathy* (pain, no instability), the [[GRASP Trial 2021 — Exercise vs Advice for Rotator Cuff Disorders|GRASP trial]] found a simple home program + good advice is about as effective as a full supervised course — so consistency matters more than complexity.
