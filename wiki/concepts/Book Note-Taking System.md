# Book Note-Taking System

The complete journal-and-note-taking guide for books: **what to write, when to write it, and the structure of a finished book note.** This is the *capture* layer. For the questions that drive reflection see [[Book Reflection Questions]]; for what to do with the notes afterward see [[From Highlights to Permanent Notes]].

## The Three Layers (every note has a moment)

Good book note-taking is not one activity — it is three, separated in time. Conflating them is why most note-taking fails.

| Layer | When | What you write | Effort |
|-------|------|----------------|--------|
| **1. Annotate** | *While reading* | Marginalia, underlines, flags — raw reactions | Low, fast, messy |
| **2. Journal** | *After each chapter / the book* | A structured book entry in your own words | Medium, deliberate |
| **3. Process** | *Days–weeks later* | Atomic notes / cards extracted from the entry → [[From Highlights to Permanent Notes]] | High, the payoff |

The single most important rule: **separate capture from processing.** [[Ryan Holiday]] waits 2–3 weeks before transcribing — *"waiting helps you separate the wheat from the chaff"* (Source: [[The Notecard System - Ryan Holiday]]).

## Layer 1 — How to Annotate While Reading

Read with a pen. [[Mortimer Adler|Adler's]] marking toolkit (Source: [[How to Mark a Book - Mortimer Adler]]):

- **Underline** — major points and forceful statements
- **Vertical margin line** — a passage too long to underline
- **Star (✶), used sparingly** — the ~10 most important passages in the whole book
- **Margin numbers** — the sequence of an argument's steps
- **"Cf. p. 62"** — cross-reference where the author repeats or contradicts himself
- **Circle** — key terms and definitions
- **Margin / endpaper notes** — your questions, objections, and connections; build your own index of the author's points on the back endpapers

Add one personal symbol Adler doesn't: a **"ME" / → mark** for anything you could apply in your own life — these become your action items (Source: [[Reading Application Framework]]).

> Don't break flow to journal mid-chapter. Annotation is fast capture; thinking happens in Layer 2.

## Layer 2 — What to Write in the Book Journal Entry

After a chapter (technical books) or the whole book (most non-fiction), write a structured entry **in your own words**. The anatomy of a complete book note:

**Header / metadata**
- Title, author, date started/finished, format (print / ebook / audio), star rating
- *Why I picked this up* (one line — anchors why it mattered)

**The substance**
- **One-sentence thesis** — the whole book's argument in a single line
- **Summary** — 3–5 sentences in your own words (NOT the blurb). If you can't, you didn't understand it.
- **Key ideas** — 3–7 bullets, each an idea + why it matters (the core of the entry)
- **Best quotes** — verbatim, with page numbers, circled quotation marks so attribution survives
- **Arguments & evidence** — for non-fiction: what claims, what evidence, is it convincing?
- **Pushback** — where you disagree, what felt thin, what biases the author may have

**The "so what"**
- **What I'll do differently** — the ONE action (see [[Reading Application Framework]])
- **Connections** — what other books/notes this links to (`[[wikilinks]]`)
- **Open questions / to explore** — threads to pull later

Answer the prompts in [[Book Reflection Questions]] to fill these fields — they *are* the questions that produce a good entry.

## The Reading-Journal Template (copy-paste)

```markdown
# {{Title}} — {{Author}}
Dates: {{start}}–{{finish}} · Format: {{}} · Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Why I read it:

## Thesis (one sentence)

## Summary (my words, 3–5 sentences)

## Key Ideas
- Idea — why it matters
-

## Quotes (with page #)
> "" (p. )

## Pushback / open questions
-

## What I'll do differently (ONE thing)
- I will [action] at [time] in [place].

## Connects to
- [[ ]]
```

## Formats — pick by purpose

| You want… | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| To remember and *use* ideas (non-fiction, technical) | Structured entry above + [[From Highlights to Permanent Notes]] |
| To track what you read | One-line log: title · author · date · rating |
| To build toward writing | [[Ryan Holiday|Holiday]] notecards by theme → [[The Notecard System - Ryan Holiday]] |
| A networked second brain | Atomic notes in this vault (Zettelkasten) |

## Fiction vs Non-Fiction

- **Non-fiction**: thesis, key ideas, evidence quality, application. The argument is the point.
- **Fiction**: themes, character arcs, passages that struck you, what it made you feel/see differently. The experience is the point.
- **Technical**: notes are worked examples, derivations, and *a thing you build* — you don't understand it until you've done the problem (Source: [[Reading Application Framework]]).
