# High Testosterone Signs (Men)

The signs of elevated testosterone in men. **Key framing: naturally high testosterone in a healthy man is rare and usually causes no symptoms** — the HPG axis self-limits ([[Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals|the normal-ceiling principle]]). Almost every symptomatic "high T" case is **exogenous**: prescribed testosterone (TRT) above target, or **anabolic-androgenic steroid (AAS) use** (medium–high; mostly from clinical/TRT literature, not RCTs of "naturally high" men).

## Direct signs of androgen excess
- **Acne and oily skin**
- **Accelerated male-pattern baldness** (androgens drive scalp hair loss)
- **Rapid muscle gain** / increased muscle mass
- **High or increased libido**
- **Aggression, irritability, mood swings, risk-taking**; in severe AAS abuse, poor judgment and rarely delusions/mania
- **Insomnia / disrupted sleep**
- **Headaches**

## The paradox: high exogenous T also causes "low-T" signs
Exogenous testosterone shuts down the body's own production via negative feedback ([[Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals|HPG axis]]), so AAS/over-dosed TRT often produces the **same** signs as deficiency:
- **Testicular shrinkage**
- **Low sperm count / infertility**
- **Gynecomastia** — excess T is aromatized to estradiol in fat tissue

This is why "shrinking testes + breast growth" can appear at *both* extremes — the giveaway for the high end is that it coexists with acne, muscle gain, and a history of T use.

## Downstream health signs / risks
- **Polycythemia** — raised hematocrit/red-cell count (thickened blood → clot/stroke risk); a routine TRT monitoring flag
- **Hypertension**, adverse cholesterol shifts
- **Fluid retention** — swelling of legs/feet
- **Worsening BPH** (enlarged prostate) → urinary difficulty
- **Cardiovascular strain** — AAS abuse is linked to higher heart-attack and stroke risk

## Causes
Most common: **testosterone therapy above target** and **anabolic steroid abuse**. Rarer organic causes: androgen-secreting testicular/adrenal tumors, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, hCG-secreting tumors.

> [!gap] Confidence is medium: the symptom list is drawn from clinical/TRT and AAS-abuse literature and patient-education sources, not from controlled studies of constitutionally high-T men. A single high reading should be confirmed and the cause (often undisclosed AAS use) investigated.
