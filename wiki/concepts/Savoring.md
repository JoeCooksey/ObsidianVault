# Savoring

**Savoring** is the deliberate effort to notice, appreciate, and *amplify* a positive experience — the upside counterpart to coping. Where coping regulates negative emotion, savoring regulates and prolongs positive emotion. It is the core mechanism under [[Romanticizing Your Life]] and the most direct lever for "falling in love with your life."

The concept was developed by social psychologist [[Fred Bryant]] (with Joseph Veroff), considered the father of savoring research (Source: [[Bryant Veroff — Savoring, A New Model of Positive Experience]]). The key insight: **savoring is not merely *having* positive feelings — it is the active, mindful engagement that makes a positive experience register and last.** The same objective event produces more wellbeing when consciously attended to.

## Three Temporal Forms

Savoring is not limited to the present. Bryant (2003) identified three time-orientations, and most people are stronger at one than the others:

1. **Anticipation** — savoring a future positive event by looking forward to it (the trip, the dinner, the weekend).
2. **Being in the moment** — lengthening and intensifying a present positive experience by attending to it as it happens.
3. **Reminiscence** — re-experiencing positive emotion by recalling and dwelling on good memories ("positive mental time travel").

A life can be enriched by deliberately exercising all three — not just the present-moment one that mindfulness emphasizes.

## Savoring Strategies (What To Do)

Quoidbach et al. (2010) grouped concrete savoring tactics into four families:

- **Be present** — intentional, attentive focus on the current positive moment (the opposite of letting it pass while distracted).
- **Behavioral display** — letting yourself physically express positive emotion (smiling, laughing, jumping) rather than damping it.
- **Capitalizing (sharing)** — communicating and celebrating the experience with others. **Sharing a positive event is one of the most powerful amplifiers** — telling a friend, photographing, or writing a note reinforces the memory and increases its impact.
- **Positive mental time travel** — deliberately reminiscing about or anticipating positive events.

Bryant also developed validated self-report measures (the Savoring Beliefs Inventory and Ways of Savoring Checklist) for capacity to savor across the three time-frames.

## Dampening: The Opposite of Savoring

The mirror of savoring is **dampening** — mentally minimizing or dismissing positive experience ("this won't last," "I don't deserve this," suppressing the feeling, distracting from it). Dampening predicts lower wellbeing and is a feature of depression. Part of learning to savor is *catching and dropping* dampening responses.

## Evidence

- Savoring interventions reliably increase positive emotion; e.g., savoring tasks increased positive emotions after a social-evaluative stressor (Frontiers, 2022).
- Savoring capacity correlates with happiness, life satisfaction, and optimism, and inversely with depression.
- Savoring is a primary moderator of [[Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model|hedonic adaptation]]: actively appreciating a positive change slows the return to baseline.

> [!note] Confidence
> The *existence and direction* of savoring effects is high-confidence (replicated, multiple labs, validated instruments). Exact effect sizes of brief savoring exercises are medium-confidence — small-to-moderate and dependent on dose and individual savoring style.

## Why It Matters for "Falling in Love With Your Life"

Most positive moments in a day are real but **unprocessed** — they happen while attention is elsewhere. Savoring is the act of moving attention onto them and keeping it there long enough to feel and store them. It is the single most transferable skill behind the whole [[Romanticizing Your Life]] idea, and it is *trainable* — savoring beliefs and habits respond to practice.
