# Cognitive Offloading (Learning Risk)

**Handing a thinking task to a tool instead of doing it yourself.** Useful for memory (that's what notebooks are *for*) — but dangerous when the task you offload is the *understanding itself*. This is the central risk of using AI to study.

## The Evidence

- Frequent AI use shows a **strong negative correlation with critical thinking**, *mediated by offloading* — the more you delegate analysis/reasoning/synthesis, the weaker those skills get (Source: [[Gerlich 2025 — AI Cognitive Offloading and Critical Thinking]]). (confidence: high)
- An RCT found chatbot study aids **impaired retention** vs. traditional methods, by *reducing the cognitive effort that builds durable memory.*
- **Younger users (17–25) are most at risk** — highest offloading, lowest critical-thinking scores. Joe is squarely in this band.

## The Trap, Specifically

If you ask AI to **summarize the chapter before you've understood it**, you skip the exact effortful step ([[Generation Effect (Handwriting)|generating]] the summary yourself) that produces comprehension. You feel like you learned it — *recognition* — but you can't reconstruct it. The summary is in the AI's words, not your head.

This is the inverse of a [[Desirable Difficulties|desirable difficulty]]: AI removes the productive struggle that learning requires.

## The Rule That Defuses It

> [!important] Do the hard cognitive work first, by hand. Let AI in only *after*, as a checker and sparring partner — never as a substitute for the thinking.

Concretely: write your chapter summary from memory **before** opening any AI. Then use AI to test and extend it. This keeps the effort (yours) and adds the leverage (AI's) without trading one for the other. See [[AI-Augmented Reading Workflow]].

## Tell-Tale Signs You're Offloading Wrong

- You read the AI summary instead of the chapter.
- Your notes are copy-pasted AI output, not your paraphrase.
- You can't explain the idea without re-opening the chat.
- You accept AI claims without checking them against the book.
