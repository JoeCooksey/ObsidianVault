# Roth IRA Setup Guide
**2-hour action with a 40-year compound tail — do this week**

Opening a Roth IRA at 19 is the single highest-ROI-per-hour financial action available to Joe. The math is simple: $100/month at age 19, 8% average annual return → **~$525,000 at age 65**. Wait until 25 and the same $100/month → ~$330,000. Every month delayed costs roughly $5,000–$10,000 in compounding tail.

**2026 Roth IRA contribution limit:** $7,500 (for single filer with MAGI < $153,000)
**Eligibility:** Must have earned income (wages, self-employment, tutoring — any job counts)
**Tax treatment:** Contributions are after-tax; all growth and withdrawals in retirement are tax-free

---

## Why Roth IRA at 19 Is Optimal

| Factor | Roth IRA Advantage |
|---|---|
| Tax bracket now | You're in the lowest bracket of your life — paying taxes now instead of in retirement is usually correct |
| Time horizon | 46 years of tax-free compound growth |
| Flexibility | Contributions (not gains) can be withdrawn anytime, penalty-free — emergency backstop |
| First home exception | Up to $10,000 in gains can be withdrawn for a first home purchase without penalty |

## Step-by-Step: Open a Roth IRA at Fidelity

Fidelity is recommended for Joe: no minimum deposit, no account fees, and the FZROX fund has a **0% expense ratio** (the only major U.S. total market fund with no fees at all).

### Step 1: Go to fidelity.com
Navigate to: **Accounts & Trade → Open an Account → Roth IRA**

Or go directly to: `fidelity.com/open-account/roth-ira`

### Step 2: Fill Out the Application (15 min)

You'll need:
- Social Security Number
- Government-issued ID (driver's license or state ID)
- Bank account number + routing number (for funding)
- Employment information (student / part-time worker is fine)
- Estimated annual income (any earned income qualifies — be honest)

**Account type:** Select "Individual Roth IRA"
**Beneficiary:** You can add one later — name a parent for now

### Step 3: Link Your Bank Account

Fidelity uses Plaid for instant bank linking. Have your bank login ready or your account/routing numbers.

Alternative: Fund later via check or bank transfer — the account is still open and valid even if unfunded initially. **But fund it as soon as possible.**

### Step 4: Make Your Initial Deposit

Any amount works. Even $25 gets the clock ticking.

If you have $500+: deposit it now.
If you have less: deposit what you have, then set up an automatic monthly contribution.

**2026 limit is $7,500.** You have until April 15, 2027 to max out contributions for 2026.

### Step 5: Invest the Money (Critical — Don't Skip)

An unfunded Roth IRA sitting in cash earns almost nothing. You must manually invest after depositing.

**Recommended investment: FZROX (Fidelity ZERO Total Market Index Fund)**
- Expense ratio: **0.00%** — literally free to hold
- What it is: Every publicly traded U.S. company, weighted by market cap
- 10-year annualized return: ~12.5% (tracks U.S. total market)
- Buy: Go to "Trade" → search "FZROX" → Buy → "Market order" → all available cash

**Alternative if you want even simpler:** Search "FFNOX" (Fidelity Four-in-One Index) — global stock + bond mix, fully automatic, zero effort.

### Step 6: Set Up Automatic Monthly Contribution

Go to: **Accounts → Transfers → Set Up Automatic Investments**

Set $50–$200/month on the 1st of each month (or whenever your paycheck clears). Choose FZROX as the destination fund.

This is the most important step. Automation eliminates the willpower requirement.

### Step 7: Lock It and Leave It

- Do NOT log in more than once a quarter
- Do NOT sell during market drops (selling in a crash is how people lose money)
- Do NOT try to time the market (consistently underperforms buy-and-hold)
- Set a calendar reminder: **October 31** each year — top off contributions before year-end

## What NOT to Do

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong |
|---|---|
| Leaving the deposit in cash | Cash earns <1%; you need to invest in FZROX |
| Picking individual stocks | Statistically underperforms total market index over 10+ year windows |
| Crypto ETF in a Roth | High variance; Roth is for long-horizon growth, not speculation |
| Not contributing because "the market is high" | Time in market > timing the market; every year you wait costs $5–10k compound tail |
| Using the gains for non-qualified withdrawals | 10% penalty + income tax on gains before 59½ (contributions are always penalty-free) |

## The Long-Term Math

| Monthly contribution | Start age | Assumed return | Age 65 balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| $100/mo | 19 | 8% | ~$525,000 |
| $100/mo | 25 | 8% | ~$330,000 |
| $100/mo | 30 | 8% | ~$225,000 |
| $200/mo | 19 | 8% | ~$1,050,000 |
| $500/mo | 19 | 8% | ~$2,600,000 |

Even $50/month started today beats $200/month started at 30. The only variable that matters more than amount is **when you start**.

## After You Open It

- Confirm the account number and keep it in your records
- Annually in January: check your contribution limit (currently $7,500; indexed to inflation)
- Annually in April: max out if you haven't — last chance before tax deadline
- When you get a real job: increase automatic contributions to eventually max out ($7,500/yr)
- Much later: at high income, switch to backdoor Roth strategy (not relevant now)
