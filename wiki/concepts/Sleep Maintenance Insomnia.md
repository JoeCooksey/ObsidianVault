# Sleep Maintenance Insomnia

Difficulty *returning* to sleep after waking during the night or very early morning — distinct from sleep-*onset* insomnia (trouble falling asleep). Also called middle-of-the-night insomnia. The hallmark is waking for no apparent reason and taking 20–30 minutes or more to fall back asleep (confidence: high — multiple clinical sources).

## Why ~3 a.m. specifically

For adults on a standard schedule, the early-morning hours are dominated by lighter, REM-rich sleep, making arousals more likely to reach full wakefulness (Source: [[Harvard Health — Awake at 3 a.m.]]). The wake itself is often normal; the *inability to return* is the disorder.

## Common causes

Sleep maintenance insomnia is usually **secondary** — driven by something else (Source: [[Cleveland Clinic — Managing Sleep Maintenance Insomnia]]):

- **Lifestyle**: evening [[Alcohol and Sleep Fragmentation|alcohol]], late caffeine, irregular schedule, daytime naps
- **Environment**: warm/bright/noisy bedroom ([[Sleep Environment Optimization]])
- **Physiological**: [[Nocturia]], [[Menopause and Sleep Disruption|hormonal change/menopause]], chronic pain, GERD
- **Medical/psychiatric**: obstructive [[Sleep Apnea and Nose Breathing|sleep apnea]], depression, anxiety, restless legs, hypertension, heart disease
- **Cognitive arousal**: anxiety and clock-watching turn a brief arousal into prolonged wakefulness

## First-line treatment

[[CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia)]] is the gold standard for all forms of insomnia; ~75% of participants improve over 4–8 sessions (confidence: high). Sleeping pills are temporary fixes that do not address root causes. **Intermezzo** (sublingual zolpidem) is the only FDA-approved drug specifically for middle-of-the-night awakening (confidence: medium).

> [!tip] The fix is usually behavioral and environmental, not pharmacological. Identify the *secondary* driver first — see the master guide [[Research: Avoiding Midnight Wakeups]].
