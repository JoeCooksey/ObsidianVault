# Family Triangulation and Cross-Generational Coalition

From **Bowen Family Systems Theory**: a family operates as one emotional unit, and the smallest *stable* unit is a **triangle, not a pair**. When tension rises between two people, a third gets pulled in to relieve it.

> [!key] When a parent and child team up against the other parent — or against the third family member — that is a **cross-generational coalition** (Jay Haley's "perverse triangle"). It stabilizes the anxious pair but **isolates and entrenches** the third person.

## The "ganging up" dynamic
When two family members who agree (e.g. a mother and child who share "common sense" about the father's drinking) repeatedly align *against* the third, the third person:
- feels outnumbered and **defensive**, not persuaded;
- digs in harder on the very behavior being criticized (compounded by [[Psychological Reactance]]);
- experiences the alliance as a verdict already passed — so dialogue feels pointless to him.

Even when the two are *factually right*, the coalition structure guarantees the third person hears it as an attack, not advice. **Being correct and being effective are different problems.** The coalition makes you correct and ineffective.

## The way out
- **De-triangle.** Relate to each person *one-on-one*, as yourself, not as half of a bloc. Have the conversation alone, not as a tag-team.
- **Drop the "we" framing.** "Mom and I both think…" is the trigger phrase. "I" statements keep it a dyad.
- This requires [[Differentiation of Self]]: staying connected to him without merging into the anxious mother-son alliance.
