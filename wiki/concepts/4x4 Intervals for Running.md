# 4x4 Intervals for Running

How to execute the [[Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol]] running, outdoors or on a treadmill. The structure is fixed (4 × 4 min hard / 3 min easy, 85–95% HRmax); the variable is how you generate and hold the effort while running.

## Pacing the Work Intervals

- **Effort anchor:** run **harder than 5K race pace** but at a pace you could hold for the full 4 min and repeat four times. (Source: [[Norwegian 4x4 — PureGym Practical Guide]])
- **Rep-by-rep:** start interval 1 controlled (~88–90% HRmax effort), then add a touch of speed each rep so rep 4 is fastest/hardest. Even splits across all four = correct pacing; fading = started too hot.
- **It takes ~60–90 s for HR to reach the target band**, so commit to a real pace from the gun rather than easing in. (Source: [[Edwardsen 2020 — Heart Rate Response to 4x4 Protocol]])

## Recovery Intervals
3 min of **easy jog or brisk walk** — keep moving. Let HR fall toward ~65–70% HRmax so the next rep is repeatable, but don't stand still or stretch.

## Treadmill Execution
- Treadmill makes pace control easy (no fade, no terrain). (Source: [[Norwegian 4x4 — PureGym Practical Guide]])
- Drive intensity with **speed, incline, or both.** A **≥5% incline** is commonly recommended to hit the target HR without needing leg speed that risks form breakdown.
- Recovery: drop speed and/or incline to an easy jog. Pre-set your work and recovery speeds so transitions are instant.

## Outdoor Execution
- Flat road/track for repeatable splits; or use a steady hill for the work reps and jog back down for recovery (hill 4x4 controls intensity via gradient and is easier on impact than top-end flat speed).
- A GPS/HR watch with interval timers automates the 4/3 min beeps.

## Full Session Template
1. Warm-up: 6–10 min easy jog + a few strides/drills.
2. 4 min hard (HR climbing to 90–95%) → 3 min easy jog. **×4.**
3. Cool-down: 5 min easy jog → walk; light stretch after.
Total ≈ 35–40 min.

## Running-Specific Tips
- Protect form when fatigued — running 4x4 is high-impact; if cadence/form falls apart on rep 4, cap speed and use incline instead.
- Treadmill is the most beginner-friendly way to learn pacing because it removes the temptation to surge.
- One reported result: a 40-min treadmill 4x4 routine cut ~8 min off a 5K over a training block (anecdotal, single-source — directionally consistent with the 7–13% VO2 max literature). (Source: [[Norwegian 4x4 — PureGym Practical Guide]])
