# Psilocybin Legal Status (US)

Psilocybin is a **Schedule I controlled substance under U.S. federal law** — illegal to possess, cultivate, or distribute except in authorized clinical research. State and local law has diverged sharply from this since 2020 (Source: [[Recovered.org / Wikipedia — Psilocybin Legal Status]]).

> [!warning] This page is a factual snapshot for orientation, not legal advice. Cultivating psilocybin mushrooms is a federal felony and illegal in most states. Verify your own jurisdiction's current law before acting. Spores (which contain no psilocybin) occupy a separate, murkier legal category than cultivation.

## Federal

- **Schedule I**: highest-restriction federal class; "no accepted medical use" in the eyes of the Controlled Substances Act.
- Only exception: **clinical research** under FDA/DEA authorization.
- Federal law applies everywhere regardless of state reform.

(Source: [[Recovered.org / Wikipedia — Psilocybin Legal Status]])

## The two legal-access states

| State | Model | Home cultivation? |
|---|---|---|
| **Oregon** | Supervised therapeutic use only (Measure 109). Adults 21+ consume at a licensed **service center** with a trained facilitator. | **No** — no personal home-grow allowance |
| **Colorado** | Regulated access **plus** broader decriminalization (Prop 122). | **Yes** — adults 21+ may grow on a private **12×12 ft** plot for personal use; no possession cap on what they grow |

- **Colorado** issued its first state regulated **Medical Assisted Use** licenses on **March 24, 2025**.
- **Oregon** began offering regulated services in 2023.

(Source: [[Recovered.org / Wikipedia — Psilocybin Legal Status]])

## Decriminalization (a third category)

Beyond Oregon and Colorado, **20+ cities** (Denver, Oakland, Santa Cruz, Ann Arbor, Seattle, and others) have **decriminalized** psilocybin — meaning local enforcement is deprioritized, **not** that it is legal. Decriminalization ≠ legalization, and it does not override federal or state criminal law (Sources: [[Recovered.org / Wikipedia — Psilocybin Legal Status]], Cato Institute).

## The trend

Psychedelic policy reform is **accelerating and highly local** — Oregon and Colorado lead, 20+ other states have considered reform bills, and the regulatory map is changing year to year. Anyone relying on this should re-check current law (Source: Cato Institute, "The Future of Psychedelic Regulation Is Local," 2025).
