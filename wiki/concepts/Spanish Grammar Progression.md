# Spanish Grammar Progression

The ordered curriculum for Spanish grammar — what to learn, in what sequence, and why. Grammar is not the primary driver of fluency (comprehensible input is), but grammar awareness accelerates acquisition by helping you notice patterns. Learn grammar from [[Language Learning Roadmap]]'s Language Transfer course, not from textbooks, until B1.

---

## The Grammar Philosophy

**Don't study grammar tables — acquire grammar through patterns.**

Language Transfer (free) teaches you to *derive* Spanish grammar from English rather than memorize paradigm tables. This is dramatically faster. Once you've completed Language Transfer, use grammar as a *reference* when you encounter confusing patterns in real input — not as a primary curriculum.

Grammar study sequence: Language Transfer (derives patterns) → Real input (reinforces patterns) → Reference book (clarifies edge cases) → italki tutor (corrects production errors).

## Tier 1 — A1 Foundations (Months 1–3)

### 1. Noun Gender
Every Spanish noun is masculine or feminine. Articles, adjectives, and pronouns must match.

**Rules:**
- Nouns ending in **-o** are usually masculine: el libro (book), el chico (boy)
- Nouns ending in **-a** are usually feminine: la mesa (table), la chica (girl)
- Nouns ending in **-ción/-sión** are feminine: la nación, la televisión
- Everything else: must memorize — el árbol (tree, masculine), la mano (hand, feminine — exception)

**Strategy:** Always learn nouns with their article ("el libro," not "libro"). This encodes gender automatically.

---

### 2. Articles
| | Masculine | Feminine |
|--|-----------|----------|
| Singular | el (the), un (a/an) | la (the), una (a/an) |
| Plural | los (the), unos (some) | las (the), unas (some) |

---

### 3. Present Tense — Regular Verbs
Three verb families based on infinitive ending:

**-AR verbs (hablar = to speak):**
| Person | Conjugation |
|--------|-------------|
| yo | habl**o** |
| tú | habl**as** |
| él/ella/usted | habl**a** |
| nosotros | habl**amos** |
| vosotros | habl**áis** |
| ellos/ustedes | habl**an** |

**-ER verbs (comer = to eat):**
yo como, tú comes, él come, nosotros comemos, vosotros coméis, ellos comen

**-IR verbs (vivir = to live):**
yo vivo, tú vives, él vive, nosotros vivimos, vosotros vivís, ellos viven

**Note:** In Latin America, *vosotros* is never used — just *ustedes* for both formal and informal "you all."

---

### 4. Ser vs. Estar — The Hardest A1 Concept
Both mean "to be." The distinction is one of the most important concepts in Spanish.

**SER** (permanent or inherent characteristics):
- Identity: Soy Joe (I am Joe)
- Origin: Soy de California (I'm from California)
- Occupation: Soy estudiante (I'm a student)
- Nationality: Es español (He's Spanish)
- Permanent characteristics: El agua es líquida (Water is liquid)
- Time: Son las tres (It's three o'clock)
- Material: La mesa es de madera (The table is made of wood)

**ESTAR** (temporary states, locations, feelings):
- Location: Estoy en casa (I'm at home)
- Feelings/states: Estoy cansado (I'm tired) / Estoy feliz (I'm happy)
- Conditions: El café está frío (The coffee is cold — temporary state)
- Ongoing actions: Estoy comiendo (I'm eating — estar + gerund)

**The famous gotcha:** "He's dead" = Está muerto (estar — death is treated as a state you're in). But "She's a widow" = Es viuda (ser — identity).

**Memory trick:** DOCTOR for ser (Description, Occupation, Characteristics, Time, Origin, Relationship); PLACE for estar (Position, Location, Action, Condition, Emotion).

---

### 5. Question Words
¿Qué? (what), ¿Cómo? (how), ¿Dónde? (where), ¿Cuándo? (when), ¿Por qué? (why), ¿Quién? (who), ¿Cuánto/a? (how much), ¿Cuántos/as? (how many), ¿Cuál? (which)

---

### 6. Adjective Agreement
Adjectives must match the gender and number of the noun:
- El chico alto (the tall boy — masculine singular)
- La chica alta (the tall girl — feminine singular)
- Los chicos altos (the tall boys — masculine plural)
- Las chicas altas (the tall girls — feminine plural)

Adjectives ending in -e or a consonant don't change for gender: el estudiante inteligente / la estudiante inteligente.

## Tier 2 — A2 Grammar (Months 3–6)

### 7. Key Irregular Present Tense Verbs
These are high-frequency and must be memorized:

| Verb | Yo | Tú | Él/Ella | Nosotros |
|------|----|----|---------|----------|
| ser (to be-perm) | soy | eres | es | somos |
| estar (to be-temp) | estoy | estás | está | estamos |
| ir (to go) | voy | vas | va | vamos |
| tener (to have) | tengo | tienes | tiene | tenemos |
| hacer (to do/make) | hago | haces | hace | hacemos |
| poder (can) | puedo | puedes | puede | podemos |
| querer (to want) | quiero | quieres | quiere | queremos |
| saber (to know) | sé | sabes | sabe | sabemos |
| decir (to say) | digo | dices | dice | decimos |
| venir (to come) | vengo | vienes | viene | venimos |

---

### 8. Object Pronouns
**Direct object pronouns** (replace the thing receiving the action):
- me (me), te (you), lo/la (him/her/it), nos (us), os (you all), los/las (them)
- "¿Ves el libro?" → "Sí, lo veo" (Do you see the book? → Yes, I see it)

**Indirect object pronouns** (replace the person receiving the action):
- me (to me), te (to you), le (to him/her), nos (to us), os (to you all), les (to them)
- "Le doy el libro a María" = I give the book to María

**Placement rule:** Object pronouns go BEFORE conjugated verbs (not after as in English):
- Lo veo (I see it) ← not "veo lo"
- Te quiero (I love you) ← not "quiero te"

---

### 9. Reflexive Verbs
Actions done to oneself. Marked with -se in the infinitive.

Common reflexive verbs:
- llamarse (to be called): me llamo Joe, te llamas...
- levantarse (to get up): me levanto a las 7
- ducharse (to shower): me ducho por la mañana
- dormirse (to fall asleep): me duermo tarde
- sentirse (to feel): me siento bien

---

### 10. Numbers, Time, and Dates
- Numbers 1–100: critical for daily life and listening
- Telling time: Son las tres y media (It's 3:30), Es la una (It's 1:00)
- Days and months: lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo
- Date format: El 22 de mayo de 2026

---

## Tier 3 — B1 Grammar (Months 6–12)

### 11. Preterite Tense — Completed Past Actions
For specific, completed events in the past ("I ate," "I went," "I said").

**-AR verbs (hablar):**
hablé, hablaste, habló, hablamos, hablasteis, hablaron

**-ER/-IR verbs (comer/vivir):**
comí, comiste, comió, comimos, comisteis, comieron

**Key irregular preterites (must memorize):**
| Verb | Preterite (yo) | Full stem |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| ser/ir | fui | fu- |
| tener | tuve | tuv- |
| estar | estuve | estuv- |
| hacer | hice | hic-/hiz- |
| poder | pude | pud- |
| poner | puse | pus- |
| querer | quise | quis- |
| decir | dije | dij- |
| venir | vine | vin- |

---

### 12. Imperfect Tense — Ongoing Past States
For ongoing states, habits, or background context in the past ("I was eating," "I used to go," "It was raining").

**-AR verbs:**
hablaba, hablabas, hablaba, hablábamos, hablabais, hablaban

**-ER/-IR verbs:**
comía, comías, comía, comíamos, comíais, comían

**Only three irregular verbs in the imperfect:** ser (era), ir (iba), ver (veía)

---

### 13. Preterite vs. Imperfect — The Hardest B1 Concept

This is where most English speakers struggle most. Both refer to the past, but:

| When to use... | Preterite | Imperfect |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| Trigger words | ayer, anoche, la semana pasada, de repente | siempre, todos los días, cuando era niño, mientras |
| Action type | Completed, specific event | Ongoing state, habit, background scene |
| Story role | Main plot event | Scene-setting, description |
| English clue | "I ate" | "I was eating" / "I used to eat" |

**Classic example:**
- "Cuando **era** niño (imperfect — ongoing state), **fui** a España" (preterite — one specific trip)
- "Siempre **comía** pizza (imperfect — habit) pero ayer **comí** pasta" (preterite — one day)

---

### 14. Future Tense
The simplest tense in Spanish — add endings to the *full infinitive*:

hablar**é**, hablar**ás**, hablar**á**, hablar**emos**, hablar**éis**, hablar**án**

Irregular stems (same endings): tener→tendr-, poder→podr-, decir→dir-, hacer→har-

**Also use:** "ir + a + infinitive" for near future — "Voy a comer" (I'm going to eat). Used more in conversation than the formal future tense.

---

### 15. Conditional Tense
Used for "would" statements and polite requests. Same irregular stems as future + endings:
-ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían

"¿Podría hablar más despacio?" (Could you speak more slowly?)

---

## Tier 4 — B2 Grammar (Months 12–18)

### 16. Present Subjunctive — The B2 Wall
The subjunctive is a *mood* (not a tense) used to express uncertainty, doubt, wishes, emotions, and hypotheticals. It's the single hardest Spanish concept for English speakers because English has almost no subjunctive left.

**When to use it:** After expressions of:
- Desire: quiero que **vengas** (I want you to come)
- Doubt: no creo que **sea** cierto (I don't think it's true)
- Emotion: me alegra que **estés** aquí (I'm glad you're here)
- Uncertainty: tal vez **llueva** (maybe it'll rain)
- Commands to others: el médico recomienda que **duermas** más

**Key trigger words:** querer que, esperar que, es importante que, no creo que, tal vez, ojalá, aunque, para que

**Formation:** Take yo present tense → drop -o → add opposite vowel endings:
- -AR verbs: -e, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en
- -ER/-IR verbs: -a, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an

hablar → hablo → habl- → hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen
tener → tengo → teng- → tenga, tengas, tenga...

---

### 17. Imperfect Subjunctive
Used for past or hypothetical subjunctive situations:
- Si tuviera dinero, viajaría (If I had money, I would travel)
- Quería que vinieras (I wanted you to come)

Formation: 3rd person plural preterite → drop -ron → add endings (-ra/-se):
hablaron → habla- → hablara, hablaras, hablara, habláramos, hablarais, hablaran

---

### 18. Imperative Mood (Commands)

**Tú (informal) affirmative:** Same as 3rd person singular present
- Habla (speak!), Come (eat!), Escribe (write!)
- Irregular: ve (go), ven (come), ten (have), pon (put), haz (do/make), sé (be)

**Usted (formal) affirmative:** Subjunctive form
- Hable usted (please speak), coma usted

**Negative commands:** Use subjunctive for both tú and usted
- No hables (don't speak), no hablen (don't speak — plural)
