# RCT vs Cohort Evidence Split (Nonsugar Sweeteners)

The single most important idea for making sense of the entire artificial-sweetener literature. **The two strongest study designs point in opposite directions**, and every headline you read is cherry-picking one of them.

## The Split
| | Short-term RCTs | Long-term cohorts |
|---|---|---|
| Finding | Sweeteners **help** — modest weight/BMI loss, better glycemic control vs sugar | Sweeteners **associated with harm** — more T2D, CVD, mortality |
| Examples | [[Harrold 2023 - NNS Beverages vs Water RCT|Harrold 2023]], Azad RCT arm | [[Debras 2022 - Artificial Sweeteners and Cardiovascular Disease (NutriNet-Sante)|Debras 2022]], [[Sun 2024 - Artificial Sweeteners and CVD (UK Biobank)|Sun 2024]] |
| Strength | Causal (randomized) but short, small, surrogate endpoints | Large, long, real-world — but only *associations* |

## Why They Disagree
1. **[[Reverse Causation in Sweetener Cohort Studies|Reverse causation / confounding by indication]]** — people who are already overweight, diabetic, or metabolically sick disproportionately switch to diet drinks. The disease drives the sweetener use, not the reverse. Sun 2024 found ~70% of the sweetener–CVD link is mediated by *pre-existing* diabetes.
2. **The comparator differs.** RCTs usually compare sweetener vs sugar (sweetener wins). Cohorts compare sweetener users vs the general population (many of whom drink water) — a different question.
3. **Timescale.** Benefits are immediate (fewer calories); any real harms would be slow and are hard to isolate.
4. **Real biological effects may be person-specific** ([[Personalized Glycemic Response to Sweeteners]]), so population averages in RCTs wash out subgroup harms that surface in large cohorts.

## The Takeaway
> Neither "diet soda is health food" nor "diet soda causes heart attacks" is supported. The RCT evidence says swapping *sugar → sweetener* is a net positive or neutral; the cohort evidence says *sweetener users* are less healthy, largely because of who they are. The defensible reading: sweeteners are a **useful transition tool, not a destination.**
