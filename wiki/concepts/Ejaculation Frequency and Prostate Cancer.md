# Ejaculation Frequency and Prostate Cancer

A frequently cited "benefit" of masturbation is reduced prostate cancer risk. The evidence is **real but specific**: it is *total ejaculation frequency*, not masturbation per se, that carries the protective signal.

## What the evidence shows
- **2025 dose–response meta-analysis** (29 studies, 315,193 men): ejaculation frequency pooled **OR 0.83 (95% CI 0.77–0.90)** — a significant protective effect. But **masturbation frequency alone: OR 1.12 (0.82–1.54), not significant** (I²=0%). (Source: [[Sexual Activity and Prostate Cancer — Dose-Response Meta-analysis 2025]])
- **Harvard Health Professionals cohort** (n=31,925, 18-yr follow-up): men with the highest ejaculation frequency (~21+/month) had roughly **20% lower** prostate cancer risk vs the lowest.

## Proposed mechanism
The "prostatic stagnation" hypothesis — regular ejaculation flushes potential carcinogens and reduces fluid stasis in the prostate. Plausible but unproven.

## Caveats
- No evidence of protection against *advanced/lethal* prostate cancer specifically.
- Age-dependence: one narrative review noted frequent masturbation as a risk *marker* in the 20s–30s but protective in the 50s — unresolved.
- Observational data only; confounding (overall health, hormones, lifestyle) is hard to exclude.

## Bottom line
Higher total ejaculation frequency is associated with modestly lower prostate cancer risk. This is a reasonable, low-confidence-to-medium "perk," not a medical prescription, and it does not single out masturbation as the cause.
