# Metalearning (Drawing the Map)

**Learn how to learn the thing *before* you start learning it.** Metalearning is the research step at the front of any serious learning project: you map the structure of the subject and design your strategy first. It is principle #1 of [[Scott Young]]'s *Ultralearning* — *"draw a map first."* (Source: [[Scott Young]])

## The idea

Before diving in, spend a small fraction of your total time (Young suggests ~10%) answering three questions:

- **Concepts** — what do I need to *understand*? (things you grasp, like a theorem)
- **Facts** — what do I need to *memorize*? (things you just have to know, like vocabulary)
- **Procedures** — what do I need to *practice doing*? (skills you build by reps)

Each type calls for different tactics: facts → [[Spaced Repetition]] flashcards; concepts → [[The Feynman Technique]]; procedures → [[Deliberate Practice]]. Knowing the mix *before* you start tells you which tools to reach for.

## How to draw the map

1. **Why** — define the actual outcome you want (pass an exam? build a project? hold a conversation?). The goal shapes everything downstream.
2. **What** — break the subject into Concepts / Facts / Procedures.
3. **How** — find the *standard path*: what curriculum, resources, and methods do existing experts and good courses use? Benchmark against them, then decide where to deviate.
4. **Find the best resources** — the textbook, course, or coach that the field actually rates, not the first Google result.

## Why it pays off

A little planning prevents the most common failure mode: pouring hundreds of hours into the *wrong* materials or method. The map also reveals which sub-skills matter most, so you can prioritize (the deconstruction step in [[Rapid Skill Acquisition (First 20 Hours)]]).

## Caution

Don't let map-drawing become procrastination. ~10% of project time is a research budget, not a license to read about learning instead of learning. Draw a *good-enough* map, start, and refine it as you go.

## Where it fits

Metalearning is the **planning layer** that sits on top of every other technique on this guide. It's also the concrete, project-level expression of [[Learning Agility (Meta-Skill)]] — the durable skill of being able to learn new things fast. (Source: [[Research: How to Learn Anything (The Science of Learning)]])

> [!tip] One-line rule
> Spend the first 10% figuring out *how* to learn it. It's the highest-leverage time you'll spend on the whole project.
