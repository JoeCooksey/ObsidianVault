# Sitting-Rising Test

The **Sitting-Rising Test (SRT)** is a 30-second floor test that predicts all-cause mortality. From standing, lower to a cross-legged seated position on the floor and rise again, using as little support as possible.

## Scoring

- Start at **5 points for sitting + 5 for rising = 10 max**.
- Subtract **1 point each time** you use a hand, forearm, knee, or the side of your leg for support.
- Subtract **0.5 points** for visible loss of balance or wobble.

## Why it matters

It bundles the qualities that decline with age — **leg strength, hip and ankle flexibility, balance, and a favorable strength-to-weight ratio** — into one number. Per the [[Sitting-Rising Test and Mortality (Brito Araujo)]] cohorts:
- Each 1-point gain ≈ **21% better survival** (Brito 2014).
- Perfect scorers had a **3.7% natural-death rate vs 42.1%** for the lowest group over ~12 years (Araújo 2025).

## How to use it

Treat it as **both a benchmark and a training target**. A low score points at specific deficits the [[Daily Movement Routine (Flexibility, Mobility, Longevity)|daily routine]] fixes: deep squat/floor sitting, [[Controlled Articular Rotations (CARs)|hip and ankle mobility]], single-leg balance, and leg strength. Re-test monthly.

> [!note] The SRT is a *composite functional* marker — improving the score means improving the underlying capacities, not gaming the movement. It pairs with [[Grip Strength and Longevity|grip strength]] as the two best non-aerobic fitness→mortality predictors.
