# Lowballing

A compliance technique exploiting [[Commitment and Consistency]]: get a person to commit to a deal at an attractive price, then — once the commitment is made — **remove the original reason** by raising the cost or revealing a catch.

Surprisingly, the commitment usually survives. Car dealers are the textbook example: agree a great price, the buyer mentally commits ("I'm buying this car"), then the salesperson "discovers" the price was wrong or options cost extra. The buyer's self-generated reasons for the purchase, plus consistency pressure, keep them in even after the original incentive is gone. Related to [[Foot-in-the-Door]].
