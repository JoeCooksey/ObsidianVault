# Bankart Lesion

A **Bankart lesion** is a tear of the **anterior-inferior labrum** (and attached capsule/ligament) off the front-lower rim of the shoulder socket (glenoid). It is the most common structural injury behind recurrent anterior instability — present in roughly **90% of traumatic anterior dislocations**. (Source: [[Shoulder Instability vs Rotator Cuff Tear]])

## Why it causes recurrent "popping out"
The labrum deepens the shallow glenoid socket and anchors the ligaments — it's the joint's front "doorstop." Once it's torn off, the front of the socket no longer holds the ball, so the humeral head slides forward and out (especially with the arm abducted + externally rotated, e.g. the throwing/cocking position). The torn labrum rarely heals back in the correct, tensioned position on its own, which is why the shoulder keeps subluxing/dislocating.

## Related lesions to know
- **Bony Bankart**: a fracture of the glenoid rim (bone loss), not just soft tissue. Significant glenoid bone loss is a major reason a soft-tissue repair fails and a bone procedure ([[Latarjet Procedure]]) is chosen instead.
- **Hill-Sachs lesion**: a dent in the back of the humeral head from impacting the socket edge during dislocation. A large "engaging" Hill-Sachs can catch and re-dislocate.
- **HAGL, ALPSA, SLAP**: other capsulolabral variants that imaging distinguishes.

## Diagnosis
- History of a dislocation + recurrent slipping/apprehension.
- **MRI, ideally MR-arthrogram** (contrast in the joint) best shows the labral tear; **CT** quantifies bone loss when a bony Bankart or large Hill-Sachs is suspected.

## Treatment implication
- The lesion defines whether surgery is likely needed: strengthening cannot reattach a torn labrum.
- **Soft-tissue Bankart** with little bone loss → [[Arthroscopic Bankart Repair]].
- **Significant glenoid bone loss / failed prior repair / high-risk patient** → [[Latarjet Procedure]] (bone block).

> [!gap] Bone-loss thresholds (commonly cited ~13–20% glenoid loss, plus "glenoid track"/on-track vs off-track Hill-Sachs assessment) guide the Bankart-vs-Latarjet choice but require CT measurement by a surgeon — not estimable from symptoms alone.
