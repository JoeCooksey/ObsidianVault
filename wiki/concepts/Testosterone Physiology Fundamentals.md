# Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals

The minimal model needed to understand *why* the habits work. Every lever on the boost/avoid lists acts somewhere on this chain.

## The HPG axis (the control loop)
1. **Hypothalamus** releases GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)
2. → **Pituitary** releases LH and FSH
3. → **LH** tells the **Leydig cells** in the testes to make testosterone from cholesterol
4. Testosterone feeds back negatively on the hypothalamus and pituitary

Anything that disrupts this loop lowers T. The two big disruptors in the habits research:
- **Cortisol** (chronic stress): suppresses GnRH and LH, and directly inhibits Leydig cells (Source: [[Cortisol and Testosterone Suppression]])
- **Low energy availability** (crash diets, endurance overtraining): shuts down GnRH pulsing (Source: [[Exercise and Testosterone Meta-analyses]])

## Raw material
Testosterone is synthesized **from cholesterol**. This is why very-low-fat diets reduce T (Source: [[Low-Fat Diet Testosterone Meta-analysis 2021]]) and why cortisol — also made from cholesterol — competes for the precursor.

## Where it's lost: aromatase
**Adipose (fat) tissue** contains the enzyme **aromatase**, which converts testosterone into estradiol. More fat → more conversion → lower T and a self-reinforcing loop with insulin resistance. This is the mechanistic core of why fat loss is the single biggest natural lever (Source: [[Weight Loss and Testosterone Review 2024]]).

## Free vs total testosterone & SHBG
- **Total T** = all testosterone in blood
- Most is bound to **SHBG** (sex hormone-binding globulin) and albumin; only the unbound **free T** (~1–2%) is biologically active
- Obesity and insulin resistance lower SHBG; aging and some conditions raise it — so "low total T" and "low free T" don't always move together

## Circadian rhythm
T is **highest in the morning** and secreted in pulses tied to sleep (peaks during REM). This is why:
- Blood tests should be drawn in the morning
- Sleep loss acutely tanks T (Source: [[Leproult Van Cauter Sleep Restriction RCT 2011]])

## The normal-ceiling principle
In a healthy young man, the HPG axis already runs near capacity. Habits mostly **remove suppressors** to restore the natural ceiling — they don't push past it. The dramatic gains in the literature come from *reversing a deficit* (fat loss, sleep correction, fixing a true nutrient deficiency), not from "boosting" an already-healthy man.

## Secular decline context
Population-level male testosterone has been **declining across generations**, attributed to rising obesity, inactivity, poor diet, sleep, stress, and endocrine-disrupting chemical exposure — i.e., the same modifiable habits on these lists (Source: [[Endocrine Disruptors and Testosterone]]).
