# Probiotic Foods Tier List

Ranked by **net health benefit** — a composite of: strain diversity, CFU density, clinical evidence strength, unique compounds produced, and practical daily usability. See [[Probiotic Creation Guides]] for how to make each. See [[Probiotic Strains Reference]] for the science behind specific bacterial strains.

---

## S-Tier — Maximum Benefit

### 1. Milk Kefir
- **CFU**: Up to 10–50 billion CFU/serving
- **Strains**: Up to **61 distinct bacterial and yeast strains** — the single most diverse probiotic food
- **Key strains**: L. kefiri, L. acidophilus, L. rhamnosus, L. reuteri, Bifidobacterium spp., Kluyveromyces marxianus (yeast)
- **Unique benefits**: Gut healing, anti-inflammatory, immune modulation, improved lactose tolerance (kefir bacteria digest ~99% of lactose), bone density (calcium + probiotics synergy), mental health (gut-brain axis)
- **Clinical evidence**: Strong — multiple RCTs for IBS, lactose intolerance, bone density, antibiotic recovery
- **Food pairings**: Oats + berries (prebiotic fiber amplifies), flaxseed, banana, honey, smoothies, overnight oats
- **Why S-tier**: No other food delivers 61 strains. The yeast strains (not in capsule supplements) add antifungal properties and B vitamins.

### 2. Natto (Japanese Fermented Soybeans)
- **CFU**: ~100 million–1 billion CFU/gram
- **Key organism**: *Bacillus subtilis* var. *natto* — a spore-forming bacterium (survives stomach acid nearly 100%)
- **Unique benefits**: 
  - **MK-7 (Vitamin K2)** — highest dietary source; dramatically reduces arterial calcification + improves bone mineral density; K2 deficiency is endemic in Western diets
  - **Nattokinase** — enzyme that dissolves fibrin (blood clots); the only food-source of nattokinase
  - Strong anti-inflammatory; reduces LDL; cardiovascular protection
- **Clinical evidence**: Strong for cardiovascular + bone; moderate for gut
- **Food pairings**: Rice (traditional), soy sauce, karashi mustard, green onions, raw egg (Japanese style), avocado toast
- **Why S-tier**: Unique MK-7 + nattokinase combo found in no other food. Spore-forming bacteria = near-complete survival through stomach acid.

## A-Tier — Excellent Benefit

### 3. Kimchi
- **CFU**: Up to 1–10 billion CFU/serving
- **Key strains**: *L. plantarum*, *L. brevis*, *L. sakei*, *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*
- **Unique benefits**: 
  - Built-in **synbiotic** — garlic in kimchi = inulin (prebiotic) + L. plantarum (probiotic) in the same food
  - Anti-cancer properties (sulforaphane from cabbage, capsaicin from chili)
  - Weight management (low calorie, fills gut, L. plantarum linked to reduced fat absorption)
  - Immune enhancement
- **Clinical evidence**: Good — especially for gut health, weight, and immune function
- **Food pairings**: Rice (kimchi + short-grain rice = classic), eggs (kimchi fried rice), ramen/noodles, tofu, pork belly, avocado
- **Why A-tier**: Unique garlic prebiotic + L. plantarum synbiotic in a single food. Anti-cancer compounds elevate it above plain lacto-ferments.

### 4. Greek Yogurt / Plain Yogurt
- **CFU**: 1–10 billion CFU/serving (commercial); higher if homemade
- **Key strains**: *L. bulgaricus*, *S. thermophilus*, *L. acidophilus*, *Bifidobacterium animalis*, *L. casei*
- **Unique benefits**: High protein (Greek yogurt 15–20g/serving), calcium, B12, improves gut barrier integrity, IBS relief, vaginal microbiome support, immune booster
- **Clinical evidence**: Strongest evidence base of any probiotic food (decades of research)
- **Food pairings**: Granola + berries (synbiotic), honey, flaxseed, walnuts, cucumber (tzatziki), fruit + oats, smoothies
- **Why A-tier**: Most accessible and well-studied. Excellent protein + probiotic combo. Not S-tier because strain diversity is lower than kefir and lacks unique compounds like MK-7.

### 5. Raw Sauerkraut
- **CFU**: Up to 10 billion CFU/serving (must be raw/unpasteurized)
- **Key strains**: *L. plantarum*, *L. brevis*, *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*, *Pediococcus cerevisiae*
- **Unique benefits**: High vitamin C, vitamin K1, gut health, bloating reduction, immune support; long shelf-stable fermentation = very high final CFU counts
- **Clinical evidence**: Good for gut health; moderate for broader benefits
- **Food pairings**: Bratwurst/pork (traditional German), rye bread, avocado, potatoes, sandwiches, tacos, burgers
- **Why A-tier**: Extremely cheap to make at home. High CFU per serving. Drops to C-tier if pasteurized (kills all bacteria — most store-bought is pasteurized).

### 6. Kombucha
- **CFU**: 1–3 billion CFU/per bottle (variable)
- **Key organisms**: *Acetobacter* spp., *Gluconobacter* spp., *Brettanomyces* (yeast), *L. bacterium* spp.
- **Unique benefits**: B vitamins (B1, B6, B12 from fermentation), acetic acid (antimicrobial), liver detoxification support, antioxidant activity from tea polyphenols; light probiotic effect
- **Clinical evidence**: Moderate — most strong evidence is in vitro or animal models; human RCTs are limited but promising
- **Food pairings**: Light salads, sushi, grilled fish, fresh fruit, as a cocktail mixer, afternoon drink
- **Why A-tier**: Enjoyable to drink daily; tea polyphenols + B vitamins are unique bonuses. Lower CFU counts than kefir or sauerkraut.

## B-Tier — Good Benefit

### 7. Miso
- **Key organisms**: *Aspergillus oryzae* (koji mold), *Lactobacillus* spp., *Tetragenococcus halophilus*
- **Unique benefits**: Rich in essential amino acids (complete protein), manganese, zinc, B vitamins, antioxidants; gut health, cancer prevention evidence (Japanese epidemiological studies), reduces LDL
- **Caveat**: Traditional miso soup is heated — most probiotics are destroyed above 115°F. Maximum benefit = miso added to cooled soup or used in cold dressings/dips
- **Food pairings**: Cold miso dressing on salads, tofu + seaweed (when cooled), ramen, miso-glazed salmon/eggplant, stir-fry sauce
- **Why B-tier**: Exceptional nutrition profile but heat-sensitivity limits live probiotic delivery in most preparations.

### 8. Tempeh
- **Key organisms**: *Rhizopus oligosporus* (a mold, not technically a probiotic bacterium)
- **Unique benefits**: Highest protein of any plant food (~20g/100g), complete amino acid profile, fermentation increases mineral bioavailability (reduces phytic acid), B12 traces, prebiotic fiber (resistant starch), anti-inflammatory
- **Caveat**: Rhizopus is a mold — technically a "fermented food" rather than a probiotic in the strict sense (live bacteria/yeast that colonize the gut)
- **Food pairings**: Stir-fried with vegetables and rice, tacos, grain bowls, sandwiches, curries, avocado bowls
- **Why B-tier**: Excellent nutrition and fermentation benefits, but lacks the live probiotic bacteria of Tier S/A foods.

### 9. Water Kefir
- **CFU**: 1–10 billion CFU/serving
- **Key strains**: *L. hilgardii*, *L. nagelii*, *Acetobacter orientalis*, *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*
- **Unique benefits**: Dairy-free probiotic drink, lightly effervescent, great for lactose-intolerant individuals, B vitamins, fruit-fermented options add antioxidants
- **Food pairings**: Fruit juices (lemon, berry, apple), ginger, as a mixer, with salads
- **Why B-tier**: Fewer strains than milk kefir; evidence base is smaller. Excellent dairy-free alternative.

## C-Tier — Moderate Benefit

### 10. Apple Cider Vinegar (Raw, With "Mother")
- **Key organisms**: *Acetobacter* spp. (acetic acid bacteria, not true gut-colonizing probiotics)
- **Unique benefits**: Blood sugar management (pre-meal ACV blunts glucose spikes ~20–30%), antimicrobial, aids digestion
- **Caveat**: Acetobacter doesn't colonize the gut — ACV is more of a digestive aid than a probiotic in the clinical sense
- **Food pairings**: Salad dressings, diluted in water before meals, pickling, marinades
- **Why C-tier**: Blood sugar benefits are real but it is not a genuine probiotic by strict definition.

### 11. Kvass / Fermented Beet Juice
- **CFU**: Variable
- **Unique benefits**: B vitamins, electrolytes, liver support (beet nitrates), lacto-fermented
- **Why C-tier**: Less studied, regional food, limited clinical evidence.

## Synbiotic Pairing Strategy
Pairing probiotic foods with **prebiotic foods** dramatically amplifies benefit — the probiotics have more fuel to colonize:

| Probiotic Food | Best Prebiotic Pairing | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kefir | Oats, flaxseed, banana | Inulin + FOS feeds kefir bacteria |
| Yogurt | Granola, berries | Pectin + FOS from fruit |
| Kimchi | Rice (already has garlic built in) | Garlic inulin is internal synbiotic |
| Sauerkraut | Onion, garlic, rye bread | FOS + beta-glucan |
| Kombucha | Chicory tea, artichoke | Inulin amplifies tea polyphenol effect |
| Natto | Rice, seaweed | Seaweed fiber + natto MK-7 |
| Miso | Leeks, garlic | Classic Japanese synbiotic |
| Tempeh | Asparagus, Jerusalem artichoke | Resistant starch feeds gut flora |

## Key Rules
- **Raw = alive**: Pasteurization kills all probiotics. Always buy raw, unpasteurized sauerkraut, kimchi, and miso
- **Strain diversity > CFU number**: 61 strains at 10 billion beats 1 strain at 100 billion
- **Heat destroys probiotics**: Don't cook fermented foods — add them to cooled dishes or eat cold
- **Consistency beats dose**: Daily small amounts outperform occasional large doses
- **Antibiotic recovery**: Take probiotics at a different time from antibiotics (2-hour gap minimum); L. rhamnosus GG + S. boulardii are strongest for this use
