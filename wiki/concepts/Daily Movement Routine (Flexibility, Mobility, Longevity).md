# Daily Movement Routine (Flexibility, Mobility, Longevity)

**The practical answer to "what should I do throughout the day?"** Most longevity benefit comes not from one workout but from (1) a short daily mobility/flexibility practice, (2) frequent movement woven through the day, and (3) a small number of high-leverage strength + intensity efforts each week. This page is the runnable routine; the evidence lives in the linked pages.

## The one-line answer

> Do a **5–10 min mobility flow most mornings**, **stand and move 1–5 min every 30–60 min**, hit **~7,000 steps/day**, and protect a few **strength + hard-effort sessions per week**. Test yourself with the [[Sitting-Rising Test]].

## Morning (5–10 min) — mobility + flexibility

- **Full-body [[Controlled Articular Rotations (CARs)|CARs]]:** neck → shoulders → spine → hips → ankles, 2–3 slow reps each direction.
- **Dynamic stretches** for the desk-tightened muscles: hip flexors, hamstrings, calves, chest/thoracic spine ([[Stretching and Flexibility for Longevity]]).
- Optional: a **deep squat hold** (30–60 s) to keep hips/ankles able to pass the [[Sitting-Rising Test]].

## Throughout the workday — the all-day layer

- **[[Breaking Up Sedentary Time]]:** every 30–60 min of sitting, stand and move 1–5 min (walk to refill water, lap the room, posture reset).
- **[[Exercise Snacks]]:** 2+ short vigorous bursts daily — a flight of stairs taken hard, 20–30 fast bodyweight squats, calf raises while brushing teeth.
- **Steps:** accumulate toward **~7,000/day** (the inflection point in [[Daily Steps and Mortality Meta-Analysis (Lancet 2025)]]); walking calls and post-meal walks are easy wins.

## Across the week — the heavy levers (not daily)

These carry the strongest longevity evidence ([[Attia Four-Pillar Exercise Framework]]):
- **Strength:** 2–4 sessions/wk — squat, hinge/deadlift, push, pull, carry. Builds the [[Grip Strength and Longevity|grip/whole-body strength]] that predicts survival.
- **VO₂max / intensity:** ~1–2 sessions/wk of hard intervals (e.g. [[Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol]]). Exercise snacks partly cover this on off days.
- **Zone 2 aerobic base:** a few hours/wk of easy cardio (the steps + walks contribute).

## Priority order (if short on time)

1. **Move often + walk** (sedentary breaks + steps) — biggest, easiest mortality gain.
2. **Strength 2–4×/wk** — the second pillar; protects independence.
3. **Hard intervals 1–2×/wk** — best fitness return per minute.
4. **Daily mobility 5–10 min** — keeps function, posture, and SRT score.
5. **Dedicated flexibility** — lowest-evidence; fold into 1 and 4.

> [!note] Flexibility/mobility make daily life better and keep you able to *do* the high-value training; strength and intensity do most of the lifespan work. Do both — but don't mistake a stretching habit for the longevity engine.
