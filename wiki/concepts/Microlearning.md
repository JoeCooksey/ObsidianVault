# Microlearning

Learning delivered in **short, focused, self-contained sessions** (roughly 5–20 minutes), repeated over time, rather than in long blocks. It is the format that makes daily learning physically possible — small enough to fit any day, which is what protects the *streak* that drives [[Habit Strength]].

## Why It Works (Two Mechanisms)

1. **It rides the spacing effect.** Many short sessions are, by construction, distributed practice. Per [[Cepeda 2006 - Distributed Practice Meta-Analysis]], spaced study beats massed in 259/271 comparisons — so the same total time, chopped into daily pieces, retains far better than one long block. The format *is* the spacing.
2. **It respects cognitive load.** Breaking material into manageable chunks reduces working-memory overload and mental fatigue, so each session ends before depletion — which keeps the next one from feeling aversive (low [[Limbic Friction]]).

## Evidence

- [[Frontiers 2025 - Microlearning and Soft Skills]]: six weekly 15–20 min modules produced significant, though discipline-specific, skill gains in 358 students.
- Corporate-training figures (≈150% better retention with spaced reinforcement; up to ~60% retention boost) are widely cited but mostly vendor-sourced → treat as **low confidence**.

## The Trap to Avoid

Microlearning is a *delivery format*, not a *method*. A 10-minute session of passive re-reading is still weak. The format only pays off when the small session is filled with a real technique — [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]], a worked problem, a teach-back — i.e., a [[Desirable Difficulties|desirable difficulty]]. Short ≠ easy.

## In Practice

- One concept, one problem, or one flashcard deck per session.
- Anchor it to an existing routine via [[Habit Stacking]].
- End with the [[Learning Log (Daily Reflection)|learning-log]] question: *what did I just learn, and what's still fuzzy?*
