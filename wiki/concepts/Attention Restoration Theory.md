# Attention Restoration Theory (ART)

Kaplan's theory: **directed attention is a depletable resource, and natural environments restore it.** Urban environments bombard attention with competing stimuli demanding effortful control (traffic, ads, phones); nature engages attention gently and involuntarily ("soft fascination"), letting the directed-attention system recover.

## The evidence

- Berman, Jonides & Kaplan 2008 (*Psychological Science*): walking in nature — or even viewing nature photos — improved directed-attention performance (backwards digit-span, Attention Network Task) versus urban walks. (confidence: high — replicated, foundational)
- A **30-minute nature walk** produced faster, more stable attention-task performance vs. an urban walk; randomized trials (Scientific Reports 2024) show changes in resting brain activity and enhanced neural indices of executive attention. (confidence: high)
- Most-improved functions: selective attention, working memory, cognitive flexibility. Mood improves in parallel — positive affect up, anger down by walk's end. More-stressed people gain more. (confidence: medium-high)

## Why this matters for boredom

Boredom is often an *attention* problem ([[MAC Model of Boredom]]) — sometimes from a depleted attention system rather than a boring environment. A phone-free walk is the rare activity that:

1. restores the attentional resource whose depletion caused the boredom,
2. provides the low-concentration canvas for constructive mind-wandering ([[Mann and Cadman 2014 - Boredom and Creativity]], [[Focused vs Diffuse Mode]]),
3. raises dopamine modestly without a crash ([[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]]).

That triple effect is why "go for a walk, leave the phone" is the single best default answer to "I'm bored."
