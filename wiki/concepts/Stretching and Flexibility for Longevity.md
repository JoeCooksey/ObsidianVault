# Stretching and Flexibility for Longevity

Flexibility is the **range a joint can be moved through** (passively). It is worth maintaining, but its direct longevity evidence is the *weakest* of the fitness components — its value is mostly **function, comfort, and posture**, with a suggestive vascular link.

## Static vs dynamic

- **Dynamic stretching** (active swings/movements through range) — best **before** activity and as a daily warm-up; doesn't blunt strength. Overlaps with [[Controlled Articular Rotations (CARs)]].
- **Static stretching** (holding a lengthened position ~30 s) — best **after** activity or in a dedicated session; improves passive range over weeks. Brief static holds before heavy lifting can transiently reduce power, so save long holds for after.

## What the evidence supports

- Poor **sit-and-reach** flexibility tracks with stiffer arteries in adults over 40 — flexibility was an *independent* correlate of pulse-wave velocity ([[Poor Trunk Flexibility and Arterial Stiffness (Yamamoto 2009)]]). This is associational, not proof that stretching extends life.
- Flexibility is one component of the [[Sitting-Rising Test]], which *does* predict mortality — but there it acts together with strength and balance, not alone.

> [!gap] There is no strong RCT evidence that stretching by itself lowers mortality. Treat flexibility as a **quality-of-movement and injury-resilience** goal, not a primary longevity lever. The heavy lifters for lifespan are VO₂max, strength, and daily step volume.

## Practical priorities

Target the muscles that shorten from sitting: **hip flexors, hamstrings, calves/ankles, chest/anterior shoulder, thoracic spine**. A few minutes most days holds range; you don't need to chase extreme splits.
