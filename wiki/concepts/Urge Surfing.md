# Urge Surfing

A mindfulness technique for riding out an unwanted urge — sexual, addictive, angry, or any craving — **without suppressing it or acting on it**. Developed by **Marlatt and Gordon (1985)** for addiction relapse prevention (Source: [[Urge Surfing — Positive Psychology]]).

## Core idea

An urge is a **wave**: it rises, peaks, and falls on its own. Suppression tends to *amplify* the urge; acting on it reinforces the loop. Observing it non-judgmentally lets it dissolve naturally. Cravings typically pass within minutes to ~20–30 minutes if left unmet. `confidence: high`

## The protocol

1. **Recognize** the urge as it emerges.
2. **Locate** the physical sensation in the body and rate its intensity.
3. **Observe** without judgment — note it's transient.
4. **Breathe** naturally, attention on the breath.
5. **Ride the wave** — watch it rise, peak, decline.
6. **Notice** how the sensation changes.
7. **Continue** until it fully fades.
8. **Self-appreciate** the choice to observe rather than react.

## Why it works

Reframes the goal from "win the fight" to "outlast the wave," which lowers [[Limbic Friction]] — you only have to *not act* for a few minutes, not forever.
