# Dopamine Complete Guide

Dopamine is a catecholamine neurotransmitter and neuromodulator. It is the brain's currency for **motivation, craving, and pursuit** — *not* primarily pleasure. The popular phrase "dopamine hit" misframes it: dopamine drives the *wanting* and the *moving-toward-a-goal*, while the "liking" (consummatory pleasure) is carried more by opioids, endocannabinoids, and serotonin (Source: [[Huberman Lab Episode 39 — Controlling Your Dopamine]]).

This page is the hub of a dopamine masterclass. It covers what dopamine is, how it works, exactly how much different activities raise it, why pleasure has a price, and how to keep the system healthy.

---

## What Dopamine Actually Does

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| Dopamine = pleasure | Dopamine = **motivation/wanting**. Pleasure (liking) uses opioids/endocannabinoids. |
| You get a "dopamine hit" then it's gone | A peak is followed by a **dip below baseline** — the price of the peak. |
| More dopamine is always better | Chronically spiking dopamine **lowers your baseline**, causing anhedonia. |
| You can "detox" dopamine | You cannot — it is always present. You can rest the **behaviors** that spike it. |

Dopamine operates on two timescales (Source: [[Tonic vs Phasic Dopamine]]):
- **Tonic** — slow, steady background level. This is your baseline mood, drive, and sense of well-being.
- **Phasic** — fast bursts (and dips). These encode **reward prediction error** — the gap between what you expected and what you got (Source: [[Reward Prediction Error]]).

## The Core Mechanism: Pleasure Has a Price

Every dopamine peak is followed by a drop in baseline. Anna Lembke frames this as a **pleasure–pain balance** that always seeks homeostasis: tip it toward pleasure, and gremlins hop on the pain side to level it back — then overshoot (Source: [[Anna Lembke — Dopamine Nation]]).

> [!key] The satisfaction of any experience depends on the height of the dopamine peak **relative to your baseline at that moment** — not the absolute height. Spike too often and the baseline sinks, so the same activity stops feeling good. (Source: [[Huberman Lab Episode 39 — Controlling Your Dopamine]])

The deeper this goes:
1. A pleasurable stimulus releases dopamine from the **readily releasable pool** in synaptic vesicles.
2. Afterward, the pool is partially depleted → baseline dips below normal (the "trough" or "hangover").
3. Repeated, potent, frequent spikes → the brain **downregulates dopamine receptors** to protect itself → tolerance → **dopamine deficit state** (Source: [[Dopamine Downregulation and Tolerance]]).
4. In deficit, even normal pleasures feel flat. This is **anhedonia** — the same end-state seen in addiction and depression.

See [[Dopamine Baseline and the Pleasure-Pain Balance]] for the full model.

## How Much Do Activities Raise Dopamine?

This is the quantitative heart of the masterclass. The short version: the steeper, faster, and more reliable the spike, the more addictive — and the bigger the crash.

**Full table with multipliers, durability, and crash profile → [[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]]**

Headline numbers (fold-increase above baseline; popularized by Huberman, traceable to rat microdialysis in [[Di Chiara and Imperato 1988 — Drugs of Abuse and Dopamine]]):

- Chocolate ≈ **1.5×** · Sex ≈ **2×** · Exercise ≈ **2×** · Nicotine ≈ **2.5×** · Cocaine ≈ **2.5×** · Amphetamine/Adderall ≈ **10×**
- Cold water immersion ≈ **2.5×** — but *sustained for hours with no crash* (Source: [[Sramek 2000 — Cold Water Immersion and Catecholamines]]).

> [!gap] The single multipliers are simplifications of animal microdialysis data and small human studies. Treat them as relative magnitudes, not precise human constants.

## Low Dopamine: Symptoms and Causes

Signs of a low-dopamine / deficit state include fatigue, poor concentration, **anhedonia** (loss of pleasure in things you used to enjoy), low motivation, low libido, mood/anxiety changes, and sleep disturbance. Severe dopaminergic loss in motor circuits causes the tremor, rigidity, and bradykinesia of **Parkinson's disease** (Source: Cleveland Clinic; Medical News Today).

Causes of depletion: chronic stimulant/drug use (downregulation), chronic overconsumption of high-dopamine behaviors, certain medications (antipsychotics, anti-nausea dopamine blockers), poor sleep, and inadequate dietary **tyrosine** (the amino-acid precursor).

## Keeping Dopamine Healthy

The goal is a **high, stable baseline** with intact receptor sensitivity — not chasing peaks. Core principles (Source: [[Healthy Dopamine Protocols]]):

1. **Don't stack** dopamine sources (e.g., phone + pre-workout + music + caffeine all during a workout) — you spend tomorrow's motivation today.
2. **Intermittent reward** — reward yourself irregularly, not every time, to protect baseline.
3. **Prefer slow, sustained, crash-free sources** — cold exposure, morning sunlight, exercise, social connection.
4. **Earn the dopamine** — peaks that follow effort sustain the baseline; peaks from passive consumption erode it.
5. **Rest the behaviors** — a ~30-day break from the most potent spike (porn, gaming, doomscrolling, junk food) lets receptors and baseline recover. This is the *real* "[[Dopamine Detox (Evidence Review)|dopamine detox]]" — behavioral, not chemical.
