# Quantitative Trading

> Using math, data, and code — not gut instinct — to decide what and when to trade. Decisions are made by algorithms, enforcing consistency and removing emotional bias.

## The Approach

Quants build mathematical/statistical models over historical data to find **anomalies and inefficiencies that repeat**, then trade them systematically. The thesis: markets contain small, persistent statistical regularities that disciplined data analysis can exploit faster and more consistently than humans. (Source: QuantifiedStrategies, Intelsense)

## Major Strategy Families

| Strategy | Idea | Practitioner |
|----------|------|--------------|
| **Statistical arbitrage** | Trade mispricings between related securities, market-neutral | Thorp, Renaissance — see [[Statistical Arbitrage]] |
| **Mean reversion** | Prices revert toward their average | Renaissance / Medallion |
| **Trend / momentum** | Ride persistent price trends | CTAs, managed futures |
| **Factor investing** | Tilt toward priced risk factors (value, size, momentum, quality) | AQR, Dimensional |
| **High-frequency trading (HFT)** | Capture sub-second spreads/liquidity | Citadel Securities, Jane Street, Virtu |

## Do Quants Actually Win?

**Some, spectacularly. Most, not reliably.**

- **Renaissance's Medallion**: ~39% net annual returns since 1988 — the strongest track record in finance — but **closed to outsiders since 1993** and capped small to protect its edge ([[Renaissance Technologies]]).
- **Ed Thorp**: ~20% net for two decades with no down quarter ([[Ed Thorp]]).
- **2024 platform quants**: D.E. Shaw +18% (Oculus +36%), Citadel Wellington +15.1%, Millennium +15% — excellent, but far below Medallion and accessible mostly to institutions. (Source: [[2024 Hedge Fund Returns Reporting]])
- **The base rate**: per [[SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index]], ~90% of *active managers* underperform the index over 15 years. The handful of winning quants are the rare exception, not the rule, and **their edges decay** as competitors copy them ([[Statistical Arbitrage]]).

## The Takeaway for an Individual

You cannot replicate Medallion. The closest a retail investor gets to a "quant" approach is **rules-based, unemotional, systematic investing** — which for most people means [[Index Fund Investing]] plus a written [[Sell Discipline (When to Sell a Stock)]] and [[Position Sizing and Risk Management]]. The discipline transfers; the secret signals do not.
