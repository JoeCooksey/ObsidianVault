# Commitment and Consistency

[[Robert B. Cialdini|Cialdini's]] second principle: once we make a commitment — especially one that is **active, public, effortful, and seen as freely chosen** — we feel internal and social pressure to behave consistently with it, even when doing so no longer serves us.

Compliance pros secure a small commitment first, then escalate. The two classic techniques are **[[Foot-in-the-Door]]** (get a tiny yes that shifts self-image, then ask big) and **[[Lowballing]]** (lock in a commitment, then raise the cost — the commitment survives the removal of its original reason). **Defense:** ask yourself, *"knowing what I now know, would I make this same commitment again?"* — if not, consistency is leading you somewhere foolish.

*Source: [[Influence - Robert Cialdini]].*
