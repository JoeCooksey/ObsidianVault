# Explanation-Building Writing Practice

## Definition
The single daily habit **[[Craig Perry]]** prescribes as the "minimum viable version" of his whole framework: use **writing** to make your [[Good Explanations (Hard-to-Vary)|explanations]] visible and examinable. *Not* journaling for its own sake, morning pages, or gratitude lists — writing as a tool to build and stress-test explanations. The core principle: **"You cannot edit a thought you cannot see."** Writing externalizes thinking so you can work on it. Takes ~10–20 minutes (less once practiced); costs nothing.

## Why It Matters
Every explanation lives in your head as a vague feeling — "a sense of how things are." Until it's written down it can't be questioned, evaluated, or cracked. Putting it on paper converts an invisible, life-running assumption into an object you can inspect and improve — the practical lever for everything in [[How To Increase Your Intelligence — Craig Perry]].

## How It Works
Perry frames it as **outcomes, not tasks** — binary, yes/no, "pure physics" (did you do it or not?). Four daily questions, one mapped to each part of the [[AQAL Model (Integral Theory)|AQAL]] framework:

1. **Did you write down one explanation today?** Something heavy, a decision you don't understand, a feeling you can't articulate. Don't edit — just get it out (essay or mind map; he prefers mind maps for showing relationships fast).
2. **Did you examine it through all [[Four Quadrants (AQAL)|four quadrants]]?** Four questions:
   - *I* — what is your inner world saying about this?
   - *It* — what does your body, behavior, or physical reality say?
   - *We* — what has your culture / the people around you taught you about this?
   - *Its* — what systems, structures, or external forces are shaping it?
3. **Which [[Lines of Development|line of development]] does this touch?** (cognitive, emotional, interpersonal, moral, aesthetic, needs…) Naming it shows where to direct growth.
4. **Did you find one piece of evidence that challenges the explanation?** Just one — to **stress-test, not destroy**. Good explanations survive pressure; bad ones collapse. This is how you tell them apart.

**Worked example (from the video):** a single negative YouTube comment derails his morning. He runs the loop — Q1 upset; Q2 stress/cortisol response (worsened by lifting + caffeine); Q3 a childhood belief that any disagreement meant he was in trouble; Q4 people share hate online behind a screen. Line: emotional + interpersonal. Counter-evidence: one bad comment out of thousands of views. New explanation: *"One person offered feedback shaped by their own level and lines; it may or may not be useful, and is not an absolute verdict."* That's the shift from a level-4 to a level-7 [[Nine Levels of Thinking|explanation]].

## Cross-Domain Connections
- "Writing is thinking" — overlaps with **[[Sönke Ahrens]]**'s smart-notes view and expressive-writing research.
- Question 4 is a deliberate **consider-the-opposite / disconfirming-evidence** move from debiasing research.
- A concrete on-ramp to level-8 ("construct-aware") thinking — watching your mind build explanations before believing them.
