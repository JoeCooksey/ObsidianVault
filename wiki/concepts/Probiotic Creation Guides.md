# Probiotic Creation Guides

Step-by-step guides for making the top probiotic foods at home. Ranked roughly in order of ease (simplest → most advanced). See [[Probiotic Foods Tier List]] for which to prioritize. See [[Probiotic Strains Reference]] for the bacteria in each.

---

## 1. Milk Kefir (Easiest — S-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: 61 bacterial strains, highest diversity of any probiotic food. Costs ~$0.30/cup vs $4 store-bought.

**What you need**:
- 1 tbsp milk kefir grains (buy online or from a friend; cannot be homegrown)
- 4 cups whole milk (not lactose-free or ultra-pasteurized)
- 1-quart wide-mouth glass jar
- Coffee filter or paper towel + rubber band
- Fine-mesh strainer (non-metal)

**Steps**:
1. **Combine**: Add 1 tbsp grains + 4 cups whole milk to jar
2. **Cover**: Place coffee filter over top, secure with rubber band (needs airflow, not airtight)
3. **Ferment**: Set in warm, dark spot (65–85°F / 18–29°C) for **24 hours**
4. **Check**: Kefir is done when slightly thickened and smells pleasantly fermented/tangy
5. **Strain**: Pour through non-metal strainer into clean jar — the grains stay in strainer
6. **Repeat**: Place grains back in clean jar with fresh milk immediately
7. **Refrigerate**: Store finished kefir in fridge; lasts 2–3 weeks

**Troubleshooting**:
- Too thin: ferment longer (30 hrs) or use whole milk (not 2%)
- Too sour: ferment less (18 hrs) or move to cooler spot
- Grains shrinking: they need whole milk to thrive

**Starter grains**: Purchase from CulturesForHealth.com or Amazon; a small community of friends who ferment is the best free source.

## 2. Raw Sauerkraut (Easiest — A-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: 10 billion CFU/serving, extremely cheap (~$0.50 per jar), no special equipment.

**What you need**:
- 1 medium head green cabbage (~2 lbs)
- 1 tbsp non-iodized salt (sea salt or kosher salt — iodine kills bacteria)
- 1-quart glass jar with lid
- Large bowl

**Steps**:
1. **Shred**: Remove outer leaves, shred cabbage finely (mandolin or knife)
2. **Salt**: Add 1 tbsp salt, massage firmly for 5–10 min until cabbage releases liquid (brine)
3. **Pack**: Press tightly into jar — cabbage must be fully submerged in its own brine
4. **Weight**: Use a zip-lock bag filled with brine as a weight to keep cabbage submerged
5. **Ferment**: Leave at room temperature (65–75°F) for **3–7 days** (longer = more sour)
   - Day 1: No visible activity
   - Day 2–3: Bubbles form — fermentation active
   - Day 4–7: Taste daily until you like the flavor
6. **Move to fridge**: Refrigerate when flavor is right; lasts 6+ months

**Key rule**: Cabbage must stay submerged below brine at all times — exposure to air = mold.

## 3. Kimchi (Easy — A-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: Built-in prebiotic (garlic inulin + probiotics = synbiotic), anti-cancer compounds, rich L. plantarum strains.

**What you need**:
- 1 medium napa cabbage
- 1/4 cup non-iodized salt
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, minced
- 2–4 tbsp Korean red pepper flakes (gochugaru)
- 4 green onions, cut in 1-inch pieces
- 1 tsp sugar or fish sauce (optional)

**Steps**:
1. **Salt cabbage**: Cut cabbage into 2-inch pieces, coat with salt, let sit 1–2 hours (draws out water)
2. **Rinse**: Rinse cabbage 2–3x with cold water, squeeze dry
3. **Make paste**: Mix garlic + ginger + gochugaru + sugar/fish sauce in bowl
4. **Combine**: Toss cabbage + green onions with paste until fully coated
5. **Pack**: Pack tightly into jar, press down firmly so brine rises above vegetables
6. **Ferment at room temp**: Leave out 1–5 days (taste daily):
   - 1 day = fresh, mild
   - 3 days = more tangy and complex
   - 5 days = fully fermented and funky
7. **Refrigerate**: Move to fridge when you like the flavor; lasts 3–6 months

**Note**: Kimchi continues to slowly ferment in the fridge (gets more sour over months — many prefer it at 1–2 months "aged").

## 4. Greek Yogurt (Easy — A-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: Cheapest protein-probiotic combo; can customize cultures; homemade has higher CFU than store-bought.

**What you need**:
- 4 cups whole milk (or 2%)
- 2–3 tbsp plain yogurt with live cultures as starter (or freeze-dried cultures)
- Medium saucepan
- Instant-read thermometer
- 1-quart jar or bowl
- Cheesecloth or coffee filters (for Greek-style straining)

**Steps**:
1. **Heat milk**: Warm to 185–200°F in saucepan over medium heat (don't boil)
2. **Cool**: Remove from heat, cool to 100–110°F (ideal culture activation temp — test with thermometer)
3. **Add starter**: Whisk in 2–3 tbsp live-culture yogurt
4. **Ferment**: Transfer to jar, cover, keep warm for **4–8 hours**:
   - Oven with light on (~100°F) works perfectly
   - Instant Pot yogurt setting (if available)
   - Towel-wrapped jar in warm corner
5. **Check**: Yogurt is done when it sets and pulls away from sides
6. **Refrigerate**: Move to fridge; lasts 2–3 weeks
7. **Strain for Greek**: Pour through cheesecloth in strainer over bowl in fridge for 2–4 hours until thick

**Cultures used**: L. bulgaricus, S. thermophilus, L. acidophilus, Bifidobacterium animalis, L. casei.

## 5. Kombucha (Moderate — A-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: Costs $0.50/bottle vs $4 store-bought; second fermentation lets you customize flavors and carbonation.

**What you need**:
- 1 SCOBY (Symbiotic Culture Of Bacteria and Yeast) + 2 cups starter liquid
- 8 bags black tea (or green/oolong)
- 1 cup white sugar (feeds the SCOBY — most is metabolized)
- 1-gallon glass jar
- Clean cloth or coffee filter + rubber band

**Getting a SCOBY** (two options):
- **Buy**: Available at health food stores, online, or from kombucha brewers
- **Grow from store-bought**: Combine 1 cup unflavored raw kombucha + cooled sweet tea in a jar; cover with cloth; wait 2–4 weeks until 1/4-inch SCOBY disc forms

**First Fermentation Steps**:
1. **Brew tea**: Steep 8 bags in 4 cups boiling water for 5 min, add 1 cup sugar, stir to dissolve
2. **Add cold water**: Add 4 cups cold water to cool the tea to below 85°F
3. **Add SCOBY + starter**: Add SCOBY and 2 cups starter liquid to gallon jar, pour in tea
4. **Cover**: Cloth + rubber band (not airtight — needs airflow)
5. **Ferment**: 7–14 days at 70–85°F, shaded spot
   - Taste at Day 5: Still very sweet
   - Day 7–10: Balanced sweet-tart
   - Day 12–14: More vinegary
6. **Bottle**: Remove SCOBY and 2 cups liquid for next batch; bottle the rest

**Second Fermentation (Optional — for carbonation)**:
- Add fruit juice or fresh fruit to bottled kombucha
- Seal airtight, leave at room temp 2–4 days → builds CO2 carbonation
- **Caution**: Open carefully — pressure builds; burp bottles daily

## 6. Miso (Advanced — B-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: Unmatched umami depth; homemade miso is dramatically superior to commercial. Fermentation timeline: 2 weeks (white miso) to 12 months (red miso).

**What you need**:
- 500g soybeans
- 500g rice koji (Aspergillus oryzae-inoculated rice — buy online)
- 200–250g non-iodized salt
- 1 tsp miso as seed starter (optional, accelerates)
- Large ceramic crock or glass jar
- Plastic wrap + weight (to eliminate air pockets)

**Steps**:
1. **Soak soybeans**: Soak in 3x volume water for 16–20 hours
2. **Cook**: Boil soybeans until easily crushed between fingers (~4 hours), or pressure cook 45 min
3. **Cool + mash**: Cool to below 115°F; mash into paste (food processor or by hand)
4. **Mix**: Combine mashed soybeans + rice koji + most of the salt; mix thoroughly into uniform paste
5. **Pack**: Form paste into tight balls, pack into crock pressing hard to eliminate air pockets; smooth surface
6. **Salt top**: Sprinkle remaining salt on the surface (prevents mold)
7. **Cover**: Press plastic wrap directly on surface, weight on top
8. **Ferment**:
   - White miso: 2–8 weeks at room temp
   - Yellow miso: 3–6 months
   - Red miso: 6–12 months
9. **Check**: If white mold appears on surface, scrape it off — the miso inside is fine
10. **Use**: Add to cooled (below 115°F) dishes to preserve probiotic benefit

**Key**: Koji is non-negotiable — it's the enzymatic engine. Purchase from Amazon or Asian grocery stores.

## 7. Natto (Advanced — S-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: Only reliable dietary source of MK-7 (Vitamin K2) and nattokinase. Very cheap once you have starter.

**What you need**:
- 2 cups dried soybeans
- 1 tsp natto starter spores (Bacillus subtilis; buy online) — OR 1 packet store-bought natto as starter
- Instant Pot, dehydrator, or oven (must hold 100–105°F for 22–24 hours)
- Steamer or baking tray with small holes for air circulation

**Steps**:
1. **Soak**: Soak dried soybeans in 3x cold water for 12–24 hours
2. **Cook**: Drain, pressure cook for 45 min (or simmer 4–5 hours) until beans crush easily
3. **Drain + dry surface**: Pat beans completely dry — surface moisture causes contamination, not natto growth
4. **Inoculate**: Cool beans to below 95°F. Dissolve 1 tsp starter in 2 tsp sterile warm water; pour over beans, mix thoroughly; OR mix in 1/4 packet of store-bought natto as starter
5. **Ferment**: Spread beans 1-inch deep in shallow tray covered with cheesecloth or foil with holes
   - Temperature: **100–105°F exactly** for **22–24 hours**
   - Oven with just the light on (check temp with thermometer), or food dehydrator, or Instant Pot yogurt setting
6. **Check**: Natto is ready when beans are covered in white, silky, stringy coating — pulling one reveals sticky strings
7. **Age**: Refrigerate 24 hours before eating — flavor develops and sharpens
8. **Serve**: Best cold, over rice, with soy sauce + karashi mustard + green onions

**Storage**: Fridge 5–7 days; freezer up to 1 year (freezing doesn't kill Bacillus spores).

## 8. Tempeh (Advanced — B-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: Highest plant protein per gram of any fermented food. Dramatically cheaper than commercial tempeh.

**What you need**:
- 2 cups dried soybeans (or other legumes: lentils, chickpeas)
- 1 tsp tempeh starter (Rhizopus oligosporus; buy online)
- Zip-lock bags or banana leaves (fermentation vessel) + pin to poke holes
- Oven, dehydrator, or Instant Pot (must hold ~86–90°F)

**Steps**:
1. **Soak**: Soak beans 12–16 hours in cold water
2. **Dehull**: Rub beans together firmly with hands to remove skins; skim floating hulls off water
3. **Cook**: Simmer 45–60 min until tender but not mushy; drain
4. **Dry**: Pat completely dry — excess moisture causes contamination (most critical step)
5. **Cool**: Cool to below 95°F (above this = spore death)
6. **Inoculate**: Sprinkle 1 tsp starter evenly, mix thoroughly
7. **Pack**: Spread 1-inch thick in perforated zip-lock bags (poke holes every 2 cm with a pin) or wrap in banana leaves
8. **Ferment**: Hold at **86°F (30°C)** for **24–48 hours**
   - Hours 0–12: No visible activity
   - Hours 12–20: White fuzz begins forming
   - Hours 24–36: Dense white mycelium covers all beans — solid loaf forms = done
   - Hours 36–48: Gray or black spots may appear (spores maturing — still good, just stronger flavor)
9. **Always cook before eating** — homemade tempeh must be cooked (pan fry, bake, steam, stir-fry)

## 9. Water Kefir (Easy — B-Tier Benefit)

**Why make it**: Dairy-free probiotic; great for lactose-intolerant individuals; highly customizable flavor.

**What you need**:
- 3 tbsp water kefir grains
- 4 cups filtered or spring water (not tap — chlorine kills grains)
- 1/4 cup white or cane sugar
- 1 fig or 1/4 tsp molasses (mineral supplement for grains)
- Wide-mouth quart jar

**Steps**:
1. **Dissolve sugar**: Stir sugar into 1 cup warm water until dissolved; add remaining cold water; cool to room temp
2. **Add grains**: Add grains + fig/molasses to jar; pour in sugar water
3. **Cover**: Coffee filter + rubber band
4. **Ferment**: 24–48 hours at room temp (70–80°F)
   - 24 hours = sweeter, less tangy
   - 48 hours = drier, more probiotic, less sugar
5. **Strain**: Pour through strainer, bottle the liquid; return grains to new sugar water
6. **Second ferment** (optional): Add fruit juice to bottled liquid, seal airtight, leave 24 hrs for carbonation
