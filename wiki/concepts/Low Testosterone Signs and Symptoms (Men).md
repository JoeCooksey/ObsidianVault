# Low Testosterone Signs and Symptoms (Men)

The signs of testosterone deficiency (hypogonadism) in men. Read alongside [[Specific vs Non-Specific Symptoms of Low Testosterone]] — the single most important caveat is that **most of these symptoms are non-specific** and cannot diagnose low T on their own (Source: [[Endocrine Society 2018 — Testosterone Therapy Guideline]]).

## Specific signs (more likely to actually mean low T)
These point to the reproductive axis and are harder to explain away:
- **Reduced libido** / fewer sexual thoughts
- **Erectile dysfunction**, especially loss of **spontaneous/morning erections**
- **Small or shrinking testes**
- **Low sperm count / infertility**
- **Gynecomastia** (breast enlargement or tenderness)
- **Reduced body/facial hair**, slower shaving
- **Hot flashes** (only when T is *very* low)
- **Low bone density / osteoporosis**, fragility fractures (long-term)

## Non-specific symptoms (common, but overlap with everything)
These are real but indistinguishable from depression, poor sleep, aging, or other illness:
- Fatigue / reduced energy
- Reduced muscle mass and strength
- Increased body fat
- Depressed mood, irritability
- Poor concentration / "brain fog"
- Reduced motivation or sense of vitality
- **Anemia** (low red blood cell count)

## How it's actually diagnosed
Symptoms are only the trigger to test — not the diagnosis (Source: [[Endocrine Society 2018 — Testosterone Therapy Guideline]]):
1. **Fasting morning (7–10 AM) total testosterone**, accurate assay — T peaks in the morning ([[Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals]]).
2. **Repeat on a separate morning** to confirm (T swings day to day).
3. **Free testosterone** if total is borderline or [[Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals|SHBG]] is altered (obesity lowers it; aging raises it).
4. Diagnose **only** when symptoms *and* consistently low T agree. Normal adult-male total T ≈ **300–1,000 ng/dL** (lab/age-dependent).
5. If confirmed, find the **cause**.

## Common causes
Obesity / type 2 diabetes (the biggest reversible driver — fat tissue aromatizes T to estrogen, see [[Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals]]), chronic illness (liver/kidney disease, HIV), sleep apnea, opioids and corticosteroids, **anabolic steroid use** (suppresses the axis — see [[High Testosterone Signs (Men)]]), pituitary tumors / high prolactin, genetic conditions (Klinefelter, Kallmann, hemochromatosis), testicular injury/infection, chemo/radiation.

> [!gap] "Low T" is over-diagnosed from non-specific symptoms alone. Fatigue + low mood + low libido in a man with normal morning testosterone is **not** hypogonadism — look at sleep, body fat, stress, and depression first ([[Testosterone-Lowering Habits to Avoid]]).
