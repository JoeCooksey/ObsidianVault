# Dietary Fiber and Gut Health

The "vegetables destroy gut health" claim comes almost entirely from the carnivore/animal-based camp, which argues **fiber is a myth** — that it isn't needed, irritates the gut, and that the colon does fine or better without it. This page is the evidence on fiber and the gut, separating the real kernel from the overreach.

## What Fiber Actually Does in the Gut

Dietary fiber is the main fuel for the colonic microbiome. Gut bacteria ferment fermentable fiber into **short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)** — acetate, propionate, and **butyrate**. These are not trivial byproducts:

- **Butyrate is the primary energy source for colonocytes** (the cells lining the colon) (Source: [[Fiber Gut Microbiome and SCFA Research]]).
- SCFAs **stimulate and maintain the colonic mucus layer**, the barrier separating gut bacteria from the gut wall.
- Butyrate has **anti-inflammatory** signalling effects and helps regulate the gut barrier.

> [!high] Fiber → SCFA/butyrate → fuels colonocytes + builds the mucus barrier is among the best-attested mechanisms in gut biology. Confidence: high (multiple mechanistic + animal + human studies).

## What Happens Without Fiber

This is the strongest evidence *against* the "fiber is useless" claim:

- **Fiber deprivation thins the gut's mucus layer.** In mouse models, when dietary fiber is lacking, bacteria switch to **degrading the colonic mucus layer itself** as a fuel source, eroding the barrier. ~7 days of fiber deficiency reduced intestinal length, damaged colonic crypt structure, and impaired mucus secretion (Source: [[Fiber Gut Microbiome and SCFA Research]]).
- **Microbial diversity falls.** Low/zero-fiber intake reduces overall bacterial load and diversity — including the very taxa that produce butyrate. Plant-food *variety* (the "30 plants/week" metric) is the single strongest dietary predictor of gut microbiome alpha-diversity ([[Antinutrients-Plant-Defense-Compounds]]).

A thinner mucus layer is mechanistically linked to a more inflammation-prone, "leakier" gut — the opposite of the carnivore claim that removing plants heals the gut.

## The Kernel of Truth: When LESS Fiber Genuinely Helps

The carnivore camp isn't inventing the symptom relief people report. Reducing fiber and plants **does** help specific people with specific conditions. This is real and worth respecting:

1. **Idiopathic constipation.** In a 6-month study of 63 patients ([[Ho 2012 - Reducing Dietary Fiber and Constipation]]), those who **stopped fiber completely** went from one bowel motion every ~3.75 days to **one per day**, with bloating dropping from 100% (high-fiber) to **0%** (no-fiber). This directly contradicts the reflexive "eat more fiber for constipation" advice for this subgroup.
2. **IBS / bloating (FODMAPs).** Many vegetables (garlic, onion, broccoli, cabbage, asparagus, leeks, mushrooms) are high in **FODMAPs** — fermentable carbs that draw water into the gut and ferment rapidly, causing gas, bloating, and pain in sensitive guts. A **low-FODMAP diet improves IBS symptoms in ~70–76% of patients** ([[Low-FODMAP Diet for IBS Evidence]]). Cutting these vegetables relieves symptoms.
3. **Active flares of IBD (Crohn's/colitis) and strictures.** Low-residue/elemental (zero-fiber) diets are an established tool to rest the gut during flares.

> [!gap] In all three cases the benefit is **symptom relief in a diseased or sensitive gut**, often best handled by *targeted* reduction (low-FODMAP) then reintroduction — not evidence that fiber harms a *healthy* gut, and not a basis for permanent zero-plant eating.

## The Logical Error in "Fiber Is a Myth"

The carnivore argument conflates two different claims:
- **"Some people feel better with less fiber"** — true, for IBS/constipation/IBD subgroups.
- **"Therefore fiber is unnecessary/harmful for everyone"** — not supported; the mechanistic and population evidence runs the other way.

Symptom relief in a subset ≠ fiber being bad. People with peanut allergies feel better avoiding peanuts; that doesn't make peanuts toxic to everyone.

## Practical Synthesis

- **Healthy gut:** fiber from a *variety* of plants feeds butyrate producers and maintains the mucus barrier. Diversity matters more than total grams.
- **Bloating/IBS:** a structured low-FODMAP elimination + reintroduction beats blanket plant removal — you keep the tolerated plants.
- **Constipation that worsens on high fiber:** the Ho 2012 finding means a *trial* of reducing fiber is reasonable; it is not a mandate to go to zero forever.
