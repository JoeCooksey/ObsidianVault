# Sleep Apnea and Nose Breathing

Sleep apnea is a disorder in which breathing repeatedly stops or becomes shallow during sleep, fragmenting it and degrading recovery — often without the sufferer being aware. It frequently coexists with **mouth breathing**, snoring, and anatomical issues like a **deviated septum**.

## Get Screened

Warning signs reported by a sleep partner: loud snoring, choking/gasping in sleep, or very loud breathing. If present, **get an apnea test**. The [[Hypertrophy Academy]] creator had undiagnosed apnea for years (masked by easy sleep onset and being a habitual mouth-breather/snorer with a deviated septum) and says diagnosis and treatment "genuinely changed my life."

> [!important] Apnea can hide behind insomnia
> Insomnia is not purely psychological — it has physiological drivers, and **sleep apnea is one of them.** Someone can have apnea *and* insomnia simultaneously, so a single fix (e.g. melatonin) is often insufficient. This is the protocol's "health problems are usually multiple things" theme.

## Nose Breathing Over Mouth Breathing

Nasal breathing is considered more beneficial than mouth breathing — the nose filters, humidifies, and conditions air in ways the mouth cannot. Tools used:

- **Mouth tape** — encourages nasal breathing during sleep.
- **Nasal strips** — open the nasal passages mechanically.

A deviated septum may only be fully correctable with surgery. These are part of the lifestyle layer of the [[Sleepmaxxing Protocol]]; for severe cases, a formal sleep study is the next step.
