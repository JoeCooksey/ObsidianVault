# Boredom Activity Playbook

The practical "I'm bored, what should I do" answer, built from the evidence in [[Research - Best Activities When Bored]].

## Step 0 — Don't grab the phone

The default move is the one move proven to backfire: scrolling/switching short videos **increases** boredom ([[Digital Switching]], 7 experiments). Boredom also spikes impulsivity and reward sensitivity ([[MAC Model of Boredom]]), so the bored brain wants exactly the junk that makes it worse. Sixty seconds of sitting with it beats the reflex.

## Step 1 — Diagnose (10 seconds)

Boredom = attention problem or meaning problem ([[MAC Model of Boredom]]):

- **Under-challenged?** (task/day too easy) → you have spare capacity: spend it on something *interesting*
- **Over-challenged / fried?** (too hard, depleted) → you need restoration, not more demand
- **"What's the point" feeling?** (meaning deficit) → reconnect to a goal or a person, don't just stimulate

## Step 2 — Pick from the tiers

### Tier 1 — Crash-free defaults (work for every boredom type)
- **Phone-free walk, ideally near anything green** — restores the depleted attention that causes boredom, hosts mind-wandering, mild stable dopamine ([[Attention Restoration Theory]]). The single best default.
- **Exercise / gym** — 2× dopamine, no crash ([[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]], [[Healthy Dopamine Protocols]])
- **Call or see a friend** — fixes the meaning deficit directly; social connection is the strongest well-being lever (Source: National Geographic's boredom researchers' shortlist: call a friend, walk, gym, instrument)

### Tier 2 — Deliberate mind-wandering (the creativity window)
[[Sandi Mann]]'s protocol: an activity needing little or no concentration — walk a familiar route, swim laps, shower, sit eyes closed — **no phone, no music, no podcast** — and let the mind wander. Contested whether this boosts creativity per se ([[Mann and Cadman 2014 - Boredom and Creativity]]), solid that it's where the diffuse mode does its work ([[Focused vs Diffuse Mode]]).

### Tier 3 — Interest-building (compounds; prevents future boredom)
Demanding-but-engaging activities build the cognitive resources that make life less boring permanently ([[Interest vs Enjoyment]]):
- Learn something at the edge of ability ([[Goldilocks Rule]], [[Deliberate Practice]])
- Hobby with skill progression: instrument, cooking/baking, building, drawing — hobby engagement independently predicts lower depression and higher life satisfaction across 16 countries (*Nature Medicine* 2023; HRS longitudinal)
- Read a real book; journal; work a project from the existing ladders (e.g., [[Python EE Project Ladder]])

### Tier 4 — Stuck with a boring task you can't leave
Don't switch — re-engineer ([[MAC Model of Boredom]] routes 1–3):
- Too easy → add a constraint: time limit, no-mistakes run, gamify it
- Too hard → chunk it smaller, kill distractions
- Meaningless → utility-value reframe: write one line on how it serves a goal you actually care about

## The one-line rule

**Boredom is a signal, not a problem. Answer it with effort or rest — never with the slot machine.**
