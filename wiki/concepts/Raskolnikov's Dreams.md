# Raskolnikov's Dreams

Dostoevsky uses dreams as a window into [[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov's]] subconscious in *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]* — the part of him his [[The Extraordinary Man Theory|theory]] cannot reach.

- **The beaten mare (Part I)** — a childhood dream in which a small horse is whipped to death by a drunken crowd while a boy weeps and tries to protect it. It surfaces just before the murder: his living conscience recoiling in horror at the act his intellect has authorized.
- **The ideological plague (Epilogue)** — feverish in Siberia, he dreams of a microbe that infects humanity with the conviction that each person alone possesses the truth; everyone becomes a self-appointed judge, society collapses into mutual slaughter. This is Dostoevsky's verdict on [[Rational Egoism]] and [[Russian Nihilism]]: a world of self-appointed "extraordinary men" annihilates itself. The dream breaks Raskolnikov's pride and opens the way to [[Redemption Through Suffering|his renewal]].

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
