# Most Important Biomarkers (Priority Shortlist)

A 100+ marker panel (Function Health) is mostly *screening* — most markers just confirm "normal." The handful below carry the most **actionable** signal, especially for a young, lean, athletic male. This is the "what to actually look at" filter on top of the full [[Health Biomarkers Complete Panel]].

## Tier 1 — The high-signal core (check every panel)

| Marker | Optimal | Why it's top priority |
|---|---|---|
| **ApoB** (+ LDL particle number) | <70 mg/dL | Best single CVD-risk number — counts atherogenic particles, beats LDL-C. The lever you can move. |
| **Lp(a)** | <75 nmol/L / <30 mg/dL | **Genetic**, measure once — if high, it permanently raises CVD risk and means every other lever must stay tight. ~20% of people are high and don't know. |
| **hs-CRP** | <0.8 mg/L | Systemic inflammation; independent CVD + metabolic risk. |
| **HbA1c + fasting insulin** | 4.8–5.2% / <5 µIU/mL | Earliest metabolic-dysfunction signal — years before glucose rises. Insulin is the sensitive one most panels skip. |
| **ALT / AST** | <25 / <20 U/L | Liver + metabolic health; **exercise-sensitive** (see prep). Persistent elevation when rested = look at fatty liver/supps. |
| **Triglyceride : HDL ratio** | <1.5 | Cheap insulin-resistance proxy hiding inside the standard lipid panel. |

## Tier 2 — Personalize-and-act (young male)

| Marker | Optimal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Total + Free Testosterone, SHBG, LH** | T 600–900 ng/dL | Energy, muscle, mood, libido — draw AM/fasted/rested or it's meaningless. |
| **Vitamin D (25-OH)** | 50–70 ng/mL | Most common deficiency in young adults; cheap fix. |
| **Ferritin + CBC** | 75–150 (men) | Iron status; fatigue/performance; rising RDW predicts mortality. |
| **Omega-3 Index** | >8% | Cardiovascular + anti-inflammatory; directly supports the ApoB/Lp(a) picture. |
| **TSH (+ Free T4/T3)** | TSH 0.5–2.0 | First-line thyroid screen. |
| **Homocysteine** | <9 µmol/L | Methylation/B-vitamin status; CVD + cognitive risk independent of LDL. |

## The mindset
> [!tip] "Normal" ≠ "optimal." Lab reference ranges are built from a population that includes a lot of unhealthy people. Target the **optimal** column, not just "in range." But don't over-react to a single borderline value — confirm with a clean, well-prepped re-draw before acting ([[Blood Test Preparation Protocol]]).
