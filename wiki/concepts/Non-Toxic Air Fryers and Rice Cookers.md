# Non-Toxic Air Fryers and Rice Cookers

Buying guide for kitchen appliances free from PFAS, PTFE, and Teflon. Most mainstream models use PTFE-coated baskets and inner pots — this page covers the safe alternatives with verified non-toxic materials.

---

## The Toxicity Problem

**PTFE (Teflon) and PFAS ("forever chemicals")** are the primary concern:
- PTFE = polytetrafluoroethylene; the Teflon family coating used in most nonstick surfaces
- PFAS = per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances; a class of ~10,000+ chemicals, of which PTFE is one
- Above ~260°C (~500°F), PTFE degrades and releases toxic fumes; air fryers commonly reach 200–230°C, which approaches this threshold with any spike
- **Critical label trap**: "PFOA-free" does NOT mean PFAS-free. PFOA is one specific PFAS compound; a product can drop PFOA and still use dozens of other PFAS chemicals
- **Safe labels to look for**: explicitly "PFAS-free," "PTFE-free," or materials that are inherently non-coated (stainless steel, glass)

**Safe materials** (ranked by safety certainty):
1. **Uncoated stainless steel** — zero coatings, inert, nothing to degrade or flake
2. **Tempered glass** — zero coatings, transparent, easiest to inspect
3. **Verified ceramic (sol-gel)** — sand-derived coating, no PTFE/PFAS/PFOA/lead/cadmium when certified; degrades less predictably than stainless over time

## Key Rules for Buying

1. **"PFOA-free" is NOT enough** — require "PFAS-free" or inherently safe materials (stainless, glass)
2. **Refrigerated vs. shelf-stable principle applies** — "ceramic" can mean many things; demand brand confirmation it's sol-gel ceramic free of PTFE base layer
3. **Stainless steel > ceramic coatings** for certainty — no coating can degrade, no certification required, material is inert
4. **Glass > ceramic** for absolute certainty — visually inspectable, zero chemical ambiguity
5. **Check the basket/inner pot AND the body** — some units have safe baskets but plastic bodies near the heat source; look for stainless or glass-dominant builds
