# Flatus Composition and the Chemistry of Odor

**The central fact: odor and volume are two different things.** What makes gas *loud or large* is unrelated to what makes it *smell*.

## Bulk composition — all odorless
Flatus is ~99% five odorless gases (Source: [[Chemistry World - The Chemistry of Farts]]):
- **Nitrogen + oxygen** — swallowed air (up to ~25%)
- **Carbon dioxide, hydrogen, methane** — produced by gut bacteria fermenting undigested carbs (~75%)

None of these smell. A big, loud fart can be nearly odorless.

## The smell — trace sulfur gases (<1%)
Odor comes from three sulfur-containing gases present at only ~50 ppm each. Ranked by concentration and odor contribution (Source: [[Suarez 1998 - Identification of Gases Responsible for Odour of Human Flatus]]):

| Gas | Smell | Conc. (µmol/L) |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)** | rotten egg | 1.06 — dominant |
| **Methanethiol (CH₃SH)** | rotten cabbage | 0.21 |
| **Dimethyl sulfide ((CH₃)₂S)** | sweet/garlic | 0.08 |

**Malodor correlates with H₂S concentration (p ≤ 0.001).** See [[Hydrogen Sulfide and Flatus Odor]]. Sulfur gases are the *major but not the only* malodorants — zinc acetate removed sulfur gas but not all odor.

## Why a tiny fraction dominates
The human nose detects H₂S at parts-per-billion levels — likely a protective adaptation, since H₂S is toxic at high concentration. So <1% of the gas controls ~100% of the social problem.

## Implication for prevention
Because smell ≠ volume, the levers differ:
- **Reduce smell** → cut sulfur substrate, bind H₂S ([[Bismuth Subsalicylate for Flatus Odor]]).
- **Reduce volume/bloating** → cut fermentable carbs (FODMAPs), [[Dietary Fiber and Gut Health|adapt fiber gradually]], alpha-galactosidase.
