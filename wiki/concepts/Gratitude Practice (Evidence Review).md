# Gratitude Practice (Evidence Review)

**Gratitude** is recognizing and acknowledging the good in one's life and its sources. As a deliberate practice (gratitude journaling, "three good things," gratitude letters), it is one of the most-studied positive-psychology interventions — and one whose real-world effect is **positive but smaller and more conditional than popular media implies.** This page is the honest evidence layer under the "appreciate what you have" half of [[Romanticizing Your Life]].

## What the Evidence Actually Shows

- **Meta-analytic signal is real but modest.** A meta-analysis of 25 RCTs (~6,745 participants) found expressed-gratitude interventions significantly improved psychological wellbeing versus neutral controls; effect sizes are **small-to-medium**. A cross-cultural meta-analysis (PNAS, 2025) likewise found gratitude interventions improve wellbeing across cultures, with magnitude varying by context.
- **The most important caveat — the active-control problem.** A large RCT (n=1,337) had participants write daily gratitude lists for 14 days. Gratitude raised positive affect, happiness, and life satisfaction and lowered depressive symptoms — **but a neutral "daily events" writing condition produced similar gains on most outcomes** (Source: [[Cunha 2019 — Gratitude Interventions RCT (Frontiers)]]). Much of the benefit may come from *deliberate positive attention and reflection*, not gratitude specifically.
- **Effects vary by design.** Outcomes differ by intervention type, dose, follow-up length, format, age, and population. There is no single guaranteed protocol.

> [!note] Confidence
> "Gratitude practice helps wellbeing on average" — high-confidence. "Gratitude specifically (vs. any structured positive reflection) is the active ingredient" — **medium/contested.** "Gratitude is a large, reliable life-changer" — low-confidence / overstated by popular sources.

## Why It Still Earns a Place

Even granting the active-control caveat, gratitude practice is **cheap, low-risk, and a reliable way to direct attention onto the good** — which is the mechanism shared with [[Savoring]] and the appreciation moderator in the [[Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model]]. It is one concrete, sustainable way to do the attentional work that "falling in love with your life" requires.

## How To Practice (Design Choices That Help)

- **Vary it.** A fixed daily list adapts fast; rotate format and timing, aim for *depth over frequency* (richly noting a few things beats listing many). → [[Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model]]
- **Gratitude letters / visits** — writing (and ideally delivering) a letter of thanks produces some of the largest, most durable effects in the literature.
- **Be specific and source-aware** — gratitude *toward people* and concrete events outperforms vague "I'm grateful for everything."
- **Don't force frequency.** Several studies suggest once–twice weekly beats daily for sustained effect (daily invites adaptation and chore-fatigue).

## The Guardrail: Gratitude ≠ Toxic Positivity

> [!warning] Gratitude that suppresses is "toxic gratitude."
> Using thankfulness to dismiss or silence genuine pain ("I shouldn't complain, others have it worse") becomes [[Toxic Positivity]] — it breeds guilt, shame, and resentment and undermines the practice. Authentic gratitude accepts reality fully (the good *and* the bad) and notices the good alongside difficulty, never instead of acknowledging it.
