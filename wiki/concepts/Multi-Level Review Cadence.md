# Multi-Level Review Cadence

A nested reflection system that keeps daily habits aligned with long-term goals by reviewing at four time horizons. Daily action drifts without review; each higher level catches drift the level below it can't see. "Reflection, not wishful thinking, is what separates successful people from the rest" (Source: RescueTime).

## The Four Levels

| Level | Time | Cost | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | end of day, ~5 min | tiny | Did I do the keystone habits? Quick course-correct. |
| **Weekly** | Sunday, ~20 min | small | Tie up loose ends, re-align priorities, plan the week. |
| **Quarterly** | ~1–2 hours | medium | Audit performance, refine tactics, re-prioritize goals. |
| **Yearly** | half a day | large | What worked, failed, mattered. Re-aim for the year. |

## Why It Works
- **Longer timelines reveal patterns** invisible day-to-day — you see the *results* of your habits and can change course while it still matters (Source: RescueTime).
- **The weekly review is the linchpin** — frequent enough to catch drift, infrequent enough to see a trend. Most people anchor it to Sunday to start the week ready.
- **The annual review is the non-negotiable minimum** — if you do nothing else, reflect once a year so you "don't start blind" (Source: Jodie Shaw, Medium).

## Connection to Habits
The cadence is the maintenance layer for any habit system. Daily habits supply the inputs; the review loop checks them against identity and goals (Source: [[Identity-Based vs Goal-Based Habits]]) and decides what to add, drop, or adjust. Without it, the [[Four Laws of Behavior Change]] build habits that may no longer serve the goal.

> [!example] Applied across life domains (health, finance, relationships, faith, learning), each domain gets a line in every review level — see [[Research - Daily Habits Every Man Should Adopt]].
