# Immune Cells

White blood cells (leukocytes) that defend against pathogens and perform cancer surveillance. Key players: **T cells** (adaptive), **B cells** (antibodies), **natural killer (NK) cells** (innate cytotoxicity), and **macrophages**. All descend from hematopoietic [[Stem Cells]]. **Ranked #5** in [[Research - Most Important Cells in the Human Body]].

## Why they matter
- **Defense + surveillance.** They kill pathogens and destroy nascent cancer cells daily (immunosurveillance).
- **Highly modifiable** — among the most responsive cell types to sleep, exercise, and nutrition, making them high-leverage.
- Decline ("immunosenescence") drives infection risk and cancer incidence with age.

## How to optimize them (actionable)
1. **Sleep 7–9 h** — the single most validated lever. Sleep deprivation acutely suppresses **NK-cell cytotoxic activity**. (Source: PubMed sleep-deprivation/NK literature, confidence high; note much foundational human data is from the 1990s.)
2. **Moderate, regular exercise** — enhances immune cell circulation and surveillance; *excessive* endurance load transiently suppresses immunity ("J-curve").
3. **Adequate protein** — antibodies and immune cells are protein-built; deficiency impairs response.
4. **Micronutrients** — vitamin D, zinc, vitamin C, and selenium are required cofactors; correct deficiencies (food-first, supplement if low).
5. **Manage chronic stress** — sustained cortisol suppresses T- and NK-cell function.
6. **Support gut microbiome** — fiber and fermented foods (e.g. [[Research - How to Make Kefir|kefir]]) tune systemic immunity.

> [!gap] "Boosting" immunity has limits — the goal is *balanced* function, not maximal activation (which can drive autoimmunity/inflammation).
