# Gut Transit Time

How long food takes to travel mouth → stool. The single most informative free gut-health marker: it predicts microbiome composition better than stool frequency or consistency (Source: [[Blue Poo Transit Time Study (PREDICT 1)]]).

## The Numbers

- **Median:** 28.7 h in the PREDICT 1 cohort (n=863 logged); range 12 h to several days
- **Healthy band:** ~14–58 h (older radiopaque-marker literature; the blue-poo paper itself avoids declaring an optimum)
- **Constipation territory:** 60+ h · **Too fast:** <12 h — nutrients and water under-absorbed

## Why It Matters

- **Long transit** → microbes exhaust fiber and switch to fermenting protein and degrading the mucus lining (*Akkermansia*, *Bacteroides*, *Alistipes* enriched); independently associated with more visceral fat and worse post-meal glucose/triglyceride responses
- **Short transit** → less time for absorption; *Eubacterium rectale* (fiber-fermenter) enriched
- Transit time discriminated microbiome classes at AUC 0.82 — frequency barely registers

## Measure It at Home (free)

1. Eat a visible marker with a meal: **blue muffin (royal-blue food dye)**, ~1 cup corn, or sesame seeds
2. Note the time; watch for first appearance in stool
3. The interval = your transit time; repeat 2–3× for a stable estimate

## Move the Number

- Too slow → gel-forming fiber (psyllium, [[Fiber Supplement Tier List]]), hydration, daily movement, coffee
- Too fast → check recent antibiotics, alcohol, sweeteners, stress; persistent >2 weeks → [[GI Red Flag Symptoms]]
