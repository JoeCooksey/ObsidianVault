# Crisis and Help Resources (Mental Health and Substance Use)

> [!warning] People with co-occurring depression and alcohol problems are at **increased risk of suicide.** Take any sign of suicidal thinking or behavior seriously and act immediately — do not wait it out.

## U.S. crisis & help lines (free, confidential, 24/7)
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** — call or **text 988**, or chat at 988lifeline.org. For anyone in emotional distress or thinking about suicide, and for worried family members.
- **SAMHSA National Helpline — 1-800-662-HELP (4357)** — free, confidential, 24/7 treatment referrals and information for mental health and substance use; routes to local treatment and support groups.
- **Emergency — 911** (or local emergency services) if someone is in immediate danger.

## Warning signs that warrant acting now
Talk of being a burden, hopelessness, or wanting to die; giving away possessions; withdrawing completely; a sudden calm after deep depression; increased risk-taking; or any direct/indirect mention of suicide.

## Support routes for the family member (you)
- **Families Anonymous**, **SMART Recovery Family & Friends**, **Al-Anon** — peer support for relatives of people with substance use issues.
- A **primary-care doctor** is a valid first step for the depressed person — they can assess and refer to a counselor, psychologist, or psychiatrist.
