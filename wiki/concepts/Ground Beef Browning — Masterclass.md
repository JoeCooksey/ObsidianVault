# Ground Beef Browning — Masterclass

The runnable guide to deeply browned, crusty, flavorful ground beef instead of gray crumbles. The *why* behind each step is in [[Ground Beef Browning Science]].

## Buy right
- **80/20 or 85/15 ground chuck.** Fat = flavor + better browning. (Leaner is fine for health goals but tastes flatter and dries out — see [[Grass-Fed vs Grain-Fed Beef (Healthline + PMC Review)]].)

## The method (≈1 lb)

1. **(Optional but huge) Baking soda.** Toss the raw beef with **¼ tsp baking soda per 12 oz**, let it rest **10–15 min**. Gives a fast deep crust and less watery pan. Don't exceed the dose.
2. **Get the pan screaming hot.** Heavy skillet (cast iron or stainless), medium-high to high, a thin film of oil shimmering.
3. **Don't crowd.** Max ~1 lb in a 10-inch pan. More than that → two batches.
4. **Add beef, press into a thin even layer, and LEAVE IT.** Undisturbed **3–4 min** to build a brown crust before touching it.
5. **Break it up and brown the rest.** Flip the seared slab, chop into chunks, spread again, brown until little liquid remains and bits are deep brown.
6. **Drain the grease** if it pools (keeps it browning, not stewing).
7. **Now season + add aromatics.** Push beef aside, cook onion/garlic in the fat, **bloom spices 30–60 s in the hot fat**, then stir through. (See [[Ground Beef Seasoning and Pairings]].)
8. **Deglaze for free flavor.** Splash of broth/tomato/wine, scrape up the browned *fond*, simmer 1 min.

## Texture goals by dish
| Dish | Want | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tacos / chili / bolognese | small, crusty crumbles | break up fine after the sear |
| Smash burgers | one thin, max-crust patty | baking soda + max contact + no flipping early |
| Salisbury / meatballs | bound, tender | don't overwork; gentle brown |

## The five mistakes that ruin it
1. Cold/underheated pan → no crust.
2. Crowding → steaming.
3. Stirring too soon → never browns.
4. Not draining grease → stewed, greasy.
5. Seasoning a wet raw pile early instead of blooming spices in rendered fat → flat flavor.
