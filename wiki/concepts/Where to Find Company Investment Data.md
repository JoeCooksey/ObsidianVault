# Where to Find Company Investment Data

Every piece of information you need to evaluate a company is publicly available — most of it for free. Primary sources (official filings) are always more reliable than secondary sources (analyst summaries, news).

**Rule:** Always go to the primary source. Read the 10-K yourself. Don't rely on a summary.

---

## Tier 1: Primary Sources (Official + Free)

### SEC EDGAR — The Most Important Resource
**URL:** sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar
**What it is:** The SEC's official database of every filing ever made by every US public company.

**Key filing types:**
| Filing | What It Contains | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **10-K** | Annual report — full year audited financials + business description + risk factors + management discussion | Annual |
| **10-Q** | Quarterly report — 3-month unaudited financials + management discussion | 3× per year |
| **8-K** | Material event disclosures — acquisitions, CEO changes, earnings surprises, restatements | As needed |
| **DEF 14A (Proxy Statement)** | Executive compensation, insider ownership, board composition, shareholder vote items | Annual |
| **Form 4** | Insider trading disclosures — any time an executive or director buys or sells shares | Within 2 days of trade |
| **S-1** | IPO registration statement — first filing from a company going public; extremely detailed |  At IPO |
| **13F** | Institutional investor holdings (hedge funds, pension funds) — what big money is buying/selling | Quarterly |

**How to use EDGAR:**
1. Go to sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar
2. Search by company name or ticker
3. Filter by filing type (e.g., type "10-K" in the Filing Type box)
4. Download the most recent annual report — read Part I (Business) + Part II (Financials) + Risk Factors

### Company Investor Relations (IR) Page
Every public company has an investor relations page on their website (usually at company.com/investors or ir.company.com).

**Contains:**
- Latest 10-K and 10-Q (same as EDGAR, but often better formatted)
- Earnings press releases
- Earnings call transcripts or audio recordings
- Investor presentations / slide decks
- Annual report (glossy version + raw 10-K)
- Financial data tables for analysts
- Guidance and forward-looking statements
- Conference presentation materials

**How to find it:** Google "[Company name] investor relations" — always first result.

### Earnings Call Transcripts
Earnings calls happen quarterly. The CEO/CFO present results and take questions from analysts. These are critical for understanding management's narrative and capital allocation thinking.

**Free sources:**
- Company IR page (audio or PDF transcript)
- SeekingAlpha.com (free with account registration — best transcript library)
- Motley Fool (earnings call transcripts, free)

**What to listen for:**
- Management tone when talking about problems (defensive? honest?)
- Guidance revisions — raised or lowered?
- How do they respond to tough analyst questions?
- Capital allocation announcements (buybacks, dividends, capex plans, acquisitions)

## Tier 2: Free Financial Data Aggregators

These pull data from filings and present it in a usable format. Use for screening and quick checks — always verify critical numbers against the original filing.

| Platform | Best For | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Macrotrends.net** | Historical financials going back 10–20 years; excellent charts | Free |
| **TIKR.com** | Professional-grade financial data, estimates, comps | Free tier (limited) / $29/mo paid |
| **Wisesheets.io** | Financial data in spreadsheet format | Freemium |
| **Finviz.com** | Stock screener — filter by P/E, margin, growth, sector | Free |
| **Stockanalysis.com** | Clean financial statements, ratios, insider trading | Free |
| **Simply Wall St** | Visual fundamental analysis, ownership charts | Free tier |
| **GuruFocus.com** | Buffett/Munger style metrics — ROIC, moat ratings, DCF | Free tier |
| **OpenInsider.com** | Insider buying/selling tracker (Form 4 data, visualized) | Free |

## Understanding the 10-K Structure

The 10-K is the most important document. Here's what's in it and where to focus:

| Section | What It Contains | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Part I, Item 1: Business** | What the company does, segments, products, markets, competition | Must read |
| **Part I, Item 1A: Risk Factors** | Legally mandated honest disclosure of all material risks | Must read |
| **Part II, Item 7: MD&A** | Management's Discussion & Analysis — management explains the results | Must read |
| **Part II, Item 8: Financial Statements** | The actual 3 financial statements + footnotes | Must read |
| **Notes to Financial Statements** | Detailed accounting policies, debt schedules, segment breakdowns | Read if flags found |
| **Part III: Directors, Officers, Compensation** | Insider ownership, executive pay | Skim; detail in proxy |
| **Exhibits** | Legal contracts, credit agreements, subsidiary list | Reference only |

**Reading order:** Risk Factors first (sets context for what could go wrong) → Business section → MD&A → Financial statements.

## Industry Benchmarks and Comparable Data

To do relative valuation, you need to know what the industry trades at.

| Source | What It Provides | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Damodaran Online** (pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar) | Professor Aswath Damodaran's annual industry data: P/E, EV/EBITDA, ROIC, margins by sector | Free (most comprehensive free dataset) |
| **Finviz Screener** | Filter stocks by sector, then compare multiples visually | Free |
| **TIKR** | Comp tables — side-by-side comparison of similar companies | Free tier |
| **S&P Capital IQ** | Professional comp tables | Institutional only |

## Qualitative Research Sources

Beyond the numbers, these help you understand the business:

| Source | What It Provides |
|---|---|
| **Glassdoor** | Employee reviews, CEO approval rating — culture signal |
| **LinkedIn** | Leadership backgrounds, hiring trends (headcount growth by function) |
| **G2/Trustpilot/App Store reviews** | Customer satisfaction for tech/software companies |
| **Google Trends** | Demand trends for consumer-facing businesses |
| **Reddit/Twitter/LinkedIn** | User sentiment about products |
| **Industry trade publications** | Specialist news for niche industries |
| **Conference calls (replay)** | Management communication quality and candor |
| **Competitor 10-Ks** | See how competitors describe the same market; triangulate who's telling the truth |

## Information Research Workflow

A practical order for researching a company from scratch:

1. **Google "[Company] investor relations"** → IR page → download most recent 10-K
2. **Read 10-K Risk Factors** (Item 1A) — what keeps management up at night?
3. **Read Business section** (Part I) — understand the model in full
4. **Read MD&A** (Item 7) — management explains last year's results
5. **Pull financials on Macrotrends** — visualize 10-year trends in revenue, margins, FCF
6. **Check GuruFocus or TIKR** — ROIC, D/E, FCF yield vs. peers
7. **Listen to last 2 earnings calls** on SeekingAlpha — judge management quality and tone
8. **Check insider trading on OpenInsider** (Form 4 data) — who's buying/selling?
9. **Compare valuation multiples** using Damodaran's industry dataset
10. **Read 2–3 competitor 10-K Risk Factors** — triangulate the competitive landscape
