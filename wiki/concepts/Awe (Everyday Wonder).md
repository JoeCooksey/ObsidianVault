# Awe (Everyday Wonder)

**Awe** is the emotion of encountering something vast — physically, conceptually, or socially — that transcends your current understanding and requires you to update your mental models. It is the most overlooked driver of "loving your life," because UC Berkeley psychologist [[Dacher Keltner]]'s research argues that **everyday wonder, not big achievement, is where a large share of human happiness actually comes from** (Source: [[Dacher Keltner — Awe (2023)]]).

## The Key Finding: Awe Is Ordinary and Frequent

Keltner's daily-diary research found people experience awe **two to three times a week** — roughly every couple of days. And the most common source is **not** the Grand Canyon. It is *"moral beauty"* and the **extraordinary in the ordinary**: a stranger's kindness, the play of light and shadow through a tree, a song that returns you to a memory, a friend's generosity, a child's first steps. The eight wonders Keltner catalogs include moral beauty, collective movement, nature, music, visual design, spirituality, life-and-death, and "epiphany." This makes awe a renewable, free, daily resource — exactly the raw material [[Romanticizing Your Life]] points at.

## Evidence-Based Benefits

From Keltner's lab and the broader literature (synthesized in Summer Allen's Greater Good review, 2018):

1. **Mood & life satisfaction** — awe-inducing experiences (even videos) boost wellbeing; rafting trips tied to awe raised life satisfaction in veterans and at-risk youth.
2. **Lower inflammation** — higher dispositional awe predicted lower interleukin-6 (an inflammatory marker), independent of other positive emotions.
3. **Sharper thinking** — awe improved resistance to weak arguments and correlated with better scientific reasoning and rejection of pseudoscience.
4. **Less materialism** — awe shifts preference from material goods toward experiences and lowers the felt value of money.
5. **Humility ("small self")** — Yosemite visitors drew themselves ~33% smaller than tourists at a commercial pier; awe shrinks self-focus.
6. **Time feels expansive** — awe-induced people felt less time-pressured and more patient.
7. **Generosity** — awe increased sharing with strangers and prosocial helping.
8. **Social connection** — awe makes people feel "more one with their community."

The recurring mechanism is the **"small self"**: awe quiets self-referential rumination (and the default mode network), dissolving the narrow, comparison-driven ego that the [[Hedonic Treadmill]] runs on.

> [!note] Confidence
> That awe *exists, is frequent, and clusters with prosocial/wellbeing outcomes* is high-confidence. Several individual effects (inflammation, specific intervention magnitudes) rest on smaller studies — medium-confidence, directionally consistent.

## How To Get More Awe (Practical)

- **Awe walks** — a short, phone-free walk with the explicit intent to notice the vast and novel; Keltner's studies show this reliably elevates awe and prosocial emotion. Pairs with [[Attention Restoration Theory]].
- **Nature** — even brief, ordinary nature exposure (a tree, a sky).
- **Music and collective movement** — concerts, singing, dancing, sport crowds.
- **Moral beauty** — deliberately noticing others' courage, kindness, skill.
- **The cosmic and the finite** — night sky, deep time, mortality reflection ([[Memento Mori Practice]]).
- **Slow down and look up** — awe requires attention be off the self and on the world.
