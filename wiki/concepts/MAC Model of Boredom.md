# MAC Model of Boredom

**Meaning-and-Attentional-Components model** (Westgate & Wilson 2018, *Psychological Review*). Boredom is "an affective indicator of unsuccessful attentional engagement in valued goal-congruent activities" — a signal, like pain, that the current activity has no value *as is*. (Source: [[Westgate 2020 - Why Boredom Is Interesting]])

## The two independent causes

Boredom has exactly two ingredients, and a deficit in **either one alone** is sufficient:

1. **Attention deficit** — you can't successfully engage. Crucially this is **U-shaped in difficulty**: too easy (under-challenge) and too hard (over-challenge) both break attention and both feel boring. This is the empirical basis of the [[Goldilocks Rule]] — peak engagement sits at the edge of current ability.
2. **Meaning deficit** — the activity doesn't connect to your currently active goals, regardless of how objectively meaningful it is.

The two don't interact and aren't correlated (14 studies, 1,364 participants). They produce different flavors of boredom: attentional boredom = mind-wandering and trouble concentrating; meaningless boredom = restlessness, high arousal, and the urge to disengage.

## Diagnosis before treatment

The model's practical power: **different boredom needs different fixes.**

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too easy | Raise demands: add a time limit, a constraint, a game |
| Too hard | Lower demands: chunk it, remove distractions; long-term, build skill |
| Meaningless | Reconnect it to a goal (utility-value reframe) or change goals |
| All else fails | Switch activities — but pick *interesting* over merely *enjoyable* ([[Interest vs Enjoyment]]) |

## Why bored people make bad choices

Boredom increases reward sensitivity and impulsivity — the bored brain reaches for the nearest cheap hit ([[Dopamine Baseline and the Pleasure-Pain Balance]]). In one study, >90% of people watching a tennis clip on loop chose to self-administer electric shocks rather than sit with the boredom. The signal is healthy; the impulsive response to it usually isn't.
