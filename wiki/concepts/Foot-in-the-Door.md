# Foot-in-the-Door

A compliance technique exploiting [[Commitment and Consistency]]: secure a **small initial yes**, which subtly shifts the person's self-image, so that a later, larger request feels consistent with who they now believe they are.

The classic study: homeowners who first agreed to display a tiny "drive safely" window sticker were far more likely to later allow a large, ugly billboard on their lawn. The small act made them see themselves as the kind of person who supports the cause. It is the opposite escalation path from [[Rejection-Then-Retreat]], and a cousin of [[Lowballing]].

*Source: [[Influence - Robert Cialdini]].*
