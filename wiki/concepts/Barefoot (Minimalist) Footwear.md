# Barefoot (Minimalist) Footwear

A shoe designed to let the foot move as if unshod while providing protection underfoot. Defined by three structural features:

- **Zero drop** — the heel and forefoot sit at the same height (heel-to-toe differential of 0 mm). This is a binary spec; there is no "mostly zero drop." (Source: [[Research - Barefoot Shoes Benefits and Best Brands]])
- **Wide / anatomical toe box** — foot-shaped, allowing toes to splay; the big toe stays in line with the inside edge of the shoe.
- **Thin, flexible sole** — typically under 4 mm of stack, with minimal cushioning, so the sole bends and twists with the foot and preserves ground feel (proprioception).

## Objective scientific definition

Per a structural review of footwear biomechanics literature, a minimalist shoe is **≤ 200 g in weight, ≤ 20 mm heel stack height, and ≤ 7 mm heel-toe differential, with a highly flexible sole and upper** (Source: [[Coetzee 2017 - Minimalist Footwear Definition]]). "True" barefoot shoes are the stricter subset (≈0 mm drop, <4 mm stack).

## Zero drop ≠ barefoot

The terms are not interchangeable. "Zero drop" refers only to heel-toe height; "barefoot" is the holistic package (zero drop **plus** wide toe box, thin sole, flexibility, ground feel). [[Altra]]-style shoes are zero drop but have thick cushioned midsoles, so they block proprioception and are not true barefoot shoes. (Source: [[Research - Barefoot Shoes Benefits and Best Brands]])

> [!gap] The 200 g / 20 mm / 7 mm thresholds (Coetzee 2017) describe *minimalist running shoes* broadly; consumer "barefoot" brands market tighter limits (0 mm drop, <4 mm stack). Both definitions are in use.
