# Podcast Active Listening Protocol

The practical answer to "how do I actually keep what I hear." Same iron rule as [[AI-Augmented Reading Workflow]]: **the retrieval is the learning** — a podcast you never recall is entertainment (which is fine, just don't book it as study).

## Triage first: decide the mode before pressing play

1. **Entertainment mode** — chores, gym, wind-down. Zero protocol. No guilt. (This is most listening, and per [[Tobin Guadagno 2022 — Why People Listen (PLOS One)]] it still pays in meaning and connection.)
2. **Exposure mode** — mapping a field, keeping current. Light protocol: one-sentence takeaway per episode.
3. **Study mode** — you actually want to keep it. Full protocol below. Few episodes deserve this; pick deliberately.

## Study-mode protocol

**During (commute/walk is fine — [[Jabbari 2025 — Podcast Learning While Driving RCT]]):**
- Set one question you want answered before pressing play ([[Metalearning (Drawing the Map)]])
- At natural breaks (~every 10–15 min), pause and say aloud, in your own words, what was just argued — a 20-second [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]] rep
- Timestamp-bookmark moments worth keeping (one tap in most apps); don't try to take notes while driving

**After (the part that does the work, ~5 minutes):**
- Same day, write 3–5 bullet takeaways **from memory** before revisiting bookmarks ([[Generation Effect (Handwriting)]])
- Connect each to something you already know or a vault page — unconnected facts evaporate
- If it earned it: one [[Spaced Repetition]] card, or file the insight into the wiki

**Optional AI layer:** pull the transcript and run the [[Contradict My Summary — AI Reading Prompt]] against your from-memory takeaways — works on episodes exactly like book chapters. [[NotebookLM]] accepts audio/YouTube sources for source-grounded Q&A. AI second, recall first ([[Cognitive Offloading (Learning Risk)]]).

## Rules of thumb

- **Don't pair study mode with verbal tasks** (email, messages, conversation) — those compete for the same channel; motor tasks (driving, walking, dishes) don't
- **1× or 1.2× speed for study mode**; high speeds are fine for exposure, but compression eats the pause-and-recall rhythm
- **Re-listening to a great episode beats first-listening a mediocre one** — same logic as re-reading vs more reading, except re-exposure with recall between counts as spaced retrieval
- A podcast that survives study mode is usually pointing at a book or paper — **follow it to print** for anything you need at depth ([[Learning from Podcasts (Evidence Review)]] finding 2)
