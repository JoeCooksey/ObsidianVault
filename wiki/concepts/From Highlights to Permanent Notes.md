# From Highlights to Permanent Notes

**What to do with book notes after you've written them.** Highlights and a journal entry are inert until processed. This is the pipeline that turns them into knowledge you can recall, connect, and use — the answer to "what do I do with all these notes?"

The governing idea (Source: [[How to Take Smart Notes - Sonke Ahrens]]): **the value is not in capturing notes, it's in linking them into one growing network.** An unprocessed highlight is a note that will die in your library.

## The Pipeline

```
Marginalia ─▶ Journal entry ─▶ Atomic notes ─▶ Linked ─▶ Reviewed ─▶ Applied / Created
 (Layer 1)     (Layer 2)        (own words)     (network)  (recall)    (the payoff)
```

### Step 1 — Wait, then revisit
After finishing, **wait days to weeks** before processing. Time filters what actually mattered — *"waiting helps you separate the wheat from the chaff"* ([[Ryan Holiday]], Source: [[The Notecard System - Ryan Holiday]]). Return to your flagged passages and journal entry, not the whole book.

### Step 2 — Extract atomic notes (in your own words)
Convert keepers into **permanent notes**: one idea per note, self-contained, written "as if for print," **in your own words — not a quote** (Source: [[How to Take Smart Notes - Sonke Ahrens]]). The rephrasing IS the understanding. If you can only copy it, you haven't learned it.

- **Digital (Zettelkasten / this vault)**: each idea = one page with a clear title; the citation lives on the [[Book Note-Taking System|book entry]].
- **Analog ([[Ryan Holiday|Holiday/Greene]] method)**: each idea = one **4×6 card**, theme written in the top-right corner, quotation marks circled (Source: [[The Notecard System - Ryan Holiday]]).

### Step 3 — Link aggressively
The step everyone skips and the one that matters most. For each atomic note ask: **"What do I already know that this connects to?"**

- Digital: add `[[wikilinks]]` to related notes — contradictions, confirmations, examples.
- Analog: tag with a **theme/category** so cards can be shuffled into piles (Stoicism, Strategy, Life…).

A note that isn't linked is lost. Elaborating the connection is what drives recall (Source: [[How to Take Smart Notes - Sonke Ahrens]]).

### Step 4 — Review on a schedule (so it sticks)
Notes decay without retrieval. Use **spaced repetition + active recall** (Source: [[Reading Retention Methods]] and Readwise):

- A simple schedule: **Day 1 → 1 week → 1 month → 1 year** (the 1:1:1 rule).
- Each review, **recall from memory first** (list what you remember, then check) — retrieval beats re-reading.
- **Readwise / Anki** automate resurfacing of highlights on this curve.
- Skim old book notes periodically — new reading re-illuminates old notes.

### Step 5 — Use the notes (the actual point)
Notes exist to produce something. Three outlets:

1. **Apply** — convert the best idea into one [[Reading Application Framework|implementation intention]]: "I will [action] at [time] in [place]." One applied idea beats 200 highlighted ones.
2. **Create** — shuffle theme-grouped cards/notes to draft an article, talk, or project; the network *is* your raw material ([[Ryan Holiday|Holiday]] builds books this way).
3. **Teach** — explain it to someone; the generation effect doubles your own retention (Source: [[Reading Retention Methods]]).

## The Two Canonical Systems (same idea, two media)

| | **Zettelkasten** (digital) | **Notecard / Commonplace** (analog) |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | [[Niklas Luhmann]] → [[Sönke Ahrens]] | [[Robert Greene]] → [[Ryan Holiday]] |
| Atomic unit | A linked note | A 4×6 index card |
| Connection | `[[wikilinks]]` | Theme in the corner + physical piles |
| Best for | A networked second brain (this vault) | Writers assembling books/essays |

Both: read → annotate → **wait** → rephrase one idea per unit → connect → reuse.

## Anti-Patterns

| Pattern | Why it fails |
|---------|-------------|
| Highlighting and never processing | Highlights ≠ knowledge; no retrieval = no retention |
| Copying quotes verbatim as your "notes" | Transcription isn't understanding; rephrase |
| One giant note per book | Not atomic, not linkable, never resurfaced |
| Capturing but never linking | The network is the asset, not the note |
| Processing immediately | No time-filter; you keep the chaff |
