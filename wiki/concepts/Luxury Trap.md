# Luxury Trap

[[Yuval Noah Harari|Harari's]] observation that **luxuries quietly become necessities and create new obligations.** A convenience adopted to make life easier (agriculture, email, the washing machine) raises the baseline expectation, so the new standard becomes mandatory rather than optional — and people end up busier, not freer.

Harari's example: farming was meant to provide more food and leisure, but the surplus simply supported more people and more elites, locking everyone into harder work. The pattern recurs throughout history and maps neatly onto modern lifestyle creep and Housel's **[[The Expectations Treadmill|expectations treadmill]]** — each upgrade resets "normal" upward, so the relief it promised never arrives.
