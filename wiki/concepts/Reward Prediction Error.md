# Reward Prediction Error

Dopamine bursts and pauses encode **reward prediction error (RPE)** — the difference between the reward you got and the reward you expected. This is the computational core of how dopamine drives learning, and it explains *surprise*, *anticipation*, and *why rewards stop thrilling you* (Source: [[Schultz Dopamine Reward Prediction Error Hypothesis]]).

---

## The Three Cases

| Situation | Prediction error | Dopamine response |
|---|---|---|
| Reward **better than expected** | positive | **Burst** (phasic spike) |
| Reward **exactly as expected** | zero | **No change** from baseline |
| Reward **worse than expected / omitted** | negative | **Dip / pause** below baseline |

The brain uses these signals to update the estimated value of states and actions, so future decisions improve — this is the biological basis of **reinforcement learning** (Source: [[Schultz Dopamine Reward Prediction Error Hypothesis]]).

---

## Why It Matters for the Masterclass

1. **Anticipation is dopaminergic.** Once a cue reliably predicts reward, the dopamine spike **moves forward in time** to the *cue*, not the reward. The wanting precedes the having. This is why the scroll, the notification, the buildup feel compelling.
2. **Expected rewards stop spiking.** A reward you fully predict produces *zero* RPE — no thrill. This drives **novelty-seeking** and escalation (more extreme content, bigger bets).
3. **Omitted expected rewards hurt.** The dip below baseline when an expected reward fails to arrive is the neural signature of disappointment — and it sharpens craving.
4. **Variable rewards are maximally engaging.** Unpredictable payoff (slot machines, social-media feeds) keeps RPE alive on every trial → the most habit-forming reward schedule. See the variable-ratio rows in [[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]].

> [!key] You can use RPE for good: keep rewards **intermittent and slightly unpredictable** to keep them meaningful, and avoid spoiling your own dopamine by over-predicting every payoff.
