# Liver Glycogen and Overnight Hypoglycemia

The liver stores glucose as glycogen and drips it into the bloodstream overnight to keep blood sugar stable while you fast through sleep. If liver glycogen runs low, blood sugar can **drop overnight (hypoglycemia)**, triggering a stress response that wakes you — often to urinate, or right before the alarm.

## The Kiwi + Honey Snack

A small pre-bed snack (~1 hour before bed) is proposed to address two things at once:

- **Kiwi** — raises melatonin and supplies vitamin C.
- **Honey** — rich in **fructose**, which is preferentially used to **top up liver glycogen** (rather than spiking blood glucose the way pure glucose would), buffering against the overnight crash.

> [!example] The creator's actual habit
> He often skips the kiwi (his sleep latency is already good) and just has **honey on blueberries** — palatable, crushes late-night cravings, and still tops up liver glycogen. Blueberries are a lower-fructose option for people who don't tolerate honey well.

## Cautions

- Eat **≥1 hour before bed**, not right at bedtime — late digestion raises body temperature (digestion is muscle work) and can cause GI discomfort, both of which hurt sleep onset.
- High **fructose** loads (lots of honey) can cause looser stools in sensitive people — see [[Bristol Stool Scale]]. Reduce the dose or shift toward lower-fructose, higher-glucose fruit.
- To see what's actually happening to your glucose overnight, a [[CGM Self-Testing for Food Response|continuous glucose monitor]] can be used.
