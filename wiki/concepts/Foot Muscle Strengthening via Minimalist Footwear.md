# Foot Muscle Strengthening via Minimalist Footwear

The best-evidenced benefit of barefoot/minimalist shoes: they strengthen the intrinsic (in-foot) and extrinsic (lower-leg) muscles, because cushioned, supportive shoes offload those muscles and let them weaken. Two randomized controlled trials carry this claim.

## The two RCTs (confidence: high)

- **Walking in minimalist shoes ≈ doing foot-strengthening exercises.** 57 runners randomized to minimalist-shoe walking (MSW), targeted foot exercises (FS), or control. After 8 weeks, *both* MSW and FS significantly increased foot muscle size and strength; control did not. Just changing footwear was as effective as a dedicated exercise program — and likely easier to stick to. (Source: [[Ridge 2019 - Minimalist Walking Strengthens Foot Muscles]])
- **Minimalist transition grows foot and leg muscle volume.** 20 habitual shod runners transitioned to minimalist shoes over 6 months vs 18 controls; MRI showed significantly larger foot and leg muscle volume in the minimalist group, driven by the **forefoot** (not rearfoot). Muscle gain correlated with how compliantly people wore the shoes. (Source: [[Chen 2016 - Minimalist Shoes Foot Muscle Volume]])

The popularly cited "**~60% increase in foot strength after 6 months**" figure traces to this line of Brigham Young University research. (Source: [[Research - Barefoot Shoes Benefits and Best Brands]])

## Downstream benefits (weaker evidence)

- **Balance & proprioception** — thin soles restore ground feel, plausibly improving balance; mechanism is sound but high-quality outcome trials are thin. (confidence: medium)
- **Altered gait / lower impact** — minimalist shoes shift many runners toward forefoot/midfoot strike, lowering peak impact and loading rate, *especially when paired with gait retraining* (shoes alone have limited effect). (Source: [[Yang 2020 - Gait Retraining Minimalist Shoes]] via [[Research - Barefoot Shoes Benefits and Best Brands]])
- **Running economy** — transitioning runners showed lower energy cost and faster 5 km times. (Source: [[Knapik 2021 - Update on Minimalist Running Shoes]])

> [!gap] Stronger feet are *plausibly* protective against loading injuries (e.g. plantar fasciitis prevention), but direct trial evidence that barefoot shoes reduce injury rates is weak and conflicting — see [[Research - Barefoot Shoes Benefits and Best Brands]].
