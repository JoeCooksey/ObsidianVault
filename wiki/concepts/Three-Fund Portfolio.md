# Three-Fund Portfolio

> The Bogleheads default: own the entire investable market with **three** index funds — total US stock, total international stock, total US bond — and nothing else. Maximal diversification, minimal decisions.

## The Three Slices

| Slice | Fidelity (mutual fund) | ETF (any broker, incl. Robinhood) |
|-------|------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Total **US** stock | FZROX / FSKAX | VTI |
| Total **international** stock | FZILX / FTIHX | VXUS |
| Total **US** bond | FXNAX | BND |

## Why Three Funds Beats Stock-Picking

- Holds ~10,000+ companies across every sector and country — you own the winners automatically without predicting them.
- No single-company risk, no manager risk, near-zero cost.
- ~90% of active funds lose to this over the long run (Source: [[SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index]]).

## Setting the Ratios

You choose only two numbers:
1. **Stocks vs bonds** → by age/horizon ([[Asset Allocation by Age]]). Young = ~90–100% stocks.
2. **US vs international** within stocks → typically 20–40% international (Vanguard uses 40%).

**Example (young, aggressive):** 60% US / 30% international / 10% bonds — or for a 19-year-old, drop bonds toward 0 and run ~70% US / 30% international.

> [!summary]
> Simpler still: a single **target-date index fund** or **VT/VTWAX** (total world) is a one-fund version that auto-handles both ratios. The three-fund portfolio is the same idea with manual control.
