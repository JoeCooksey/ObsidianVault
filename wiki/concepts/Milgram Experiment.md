# Milgram Experiment

Stanley Milgram's famous early-1960s Yale obedience studies, which [[Robert B. Cialdini|Cialdini]] cites as the evidentiary basis for the **[[Authority]]** principle. Participants were instructed by an experimenter in a lab coat to deliver what they believed were increasingly severe electric shocks to another person.

A majority continued to the maximum voltage simply because an authority figure told them to — despite the apparent suffering and their own distress. The lesson is unsettling: ordinary, decent people will go to extreme lengths under the direction of a perceived legitimate authority. It demonstrates how automatic and powerful deference to authority is, and why recognizing it is the first step to resisting its misuse.

*Source: [[Influence - Robert Cialdini]]; Stanley Milgram (1963).*
