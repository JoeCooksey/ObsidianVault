# High-Sulfur Foods and Flatus Odor

**Smell is fed by dietary sulfur.** Eat more sulfur-rich food → gut bacteria make more [[Hydrogen Sulfide and Flatus Odor|H₂S]] → worse smell. (Distinct from carbs/FODMAPs, which drive *volume*.)

## The high-sulfur foods (drive SMELL)
- **Cruciferous vegetables** — broccoli, cabbage, Brussels sprouts, kale, cauliflower
- **Alliums** — garlic, onions, leeks
- **Animal protein** — red meat, poultry, fish, eggs (sulfur amino acids methionine + cysteine)
- **Dairy** (also methionine)
- **Legumes** — beans, lentils (sulfur *and* fermentable carbs → double hit)
- **Dried fruit, wine, beer** — can carry inorganic sulfite/sulfate preservatives

(Sources: [[Cary Gastroenterology - Why Do My Farts Smell So Bad|carygastro]], [[Chemistry World - The Chemistry of Farts]])

## The high-FODMAP foods (drive VOLUME, not necessarily smell)
Fermentable Oligo-, Di-, Mono-saccharides And Polyols — beans, wheat, onion, garlic, certain fruits, high-fructose corn syrup, and polyol sweeteners (sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol). These feed fermentation → more H₂/CO₂ → more *gas*. A **low-FODMAP diet** reduces flatulence volume and bloating. (Source: [[AAFP 2009 - Effective Management of Flatulence]])

> Note: garlic, onions, and beans appear on *both* lists — they raise both volume and smell, which is why they are the classic offenders.

## Practical implication
- To cut **smell**: reduce sulfur load (especially big meat servings + lots of cruciferous/allium at once).
- To cut **volume**: reduce FODMAPs and add fiber gradually so the microbiome adapts (see [[Dietary Fiber and Gut Health]]).
- The gut **adapts over weeks** to beans/fiber — don't quit too early. (Source: [[Chemistry World - The Chemistry of Farts]])
