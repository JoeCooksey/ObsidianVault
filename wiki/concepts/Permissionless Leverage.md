# Permissionless Leverage

Naval Ravikant's framework for *why some daily work compounds and some doesn't*: it depends on the **kind of leverage** the work creates. (Source: [[The Almanack of Naval Ravikant]])

## The four kinds of leverage

| Leverage | What it is | Permissioned? | Compounds? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Labor** | Other people working for you | Yes — they must agree | Hard to scale, "messy" |
| **Capital** | Money amplifying decisions | Yes — gatekept by who gives it | Yes (compound interest) → [[Index Fund Investing]] |
| **Code** | Software with ~zero marginal cost | **No — only needs a computer** | Yes, strongly |
| **Media** | Writing/audio/video, copied free | **No — anyone can publish** | Yes, strongly |

> *"Forget rich versus poor, white-collar versus blue. It's now leveraged versus un-leveraged."*

**Code and media are permissionless**: no boss, investor, or gatekeeper has to say yes. A computer is the only requirement. This is exactly why a GitHub repo and a blog sit at the top of [[Compounding Daily Projects Tier List]] — Joe can build both today without anyone's permission.

## Specific knowledge

The skill you build with leverage should be **specific knowledge**: found by following genuine curiosity, not by chasing what's hot. *"Escape competition through authenticity. When you're authentic, you're not competing; you're just being you."* It can't be taught conventionally — only learned through apprenticeship and self-directed exploration. For Joe, the WBG / power-electronics depth he's compounding *is* the specific knowledge; the leverage layer (code + writing about it) is what makes it pay.

## Productize yourself

- *"You're never going to get rich renting out your time."*
- *"Earn with your mind, not your time."*
- Combine specific knowledge + leverage to build something that **earns while you sleep** — turning yourself from a replaceable worker into an owned asset.

## Long-term games

*"All returns in life — wealth, relationships, knowledge — come from compound interest."* Play **long-term games with long-term people**: reputation and trust compound over decades, and integrity-driven collaborators make each other rich rather than competing for a fixed pie.
