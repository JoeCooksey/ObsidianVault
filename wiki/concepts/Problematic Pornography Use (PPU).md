# Problematic Pornography Use (PPU)

PPU describes pornography use experienced as out-of-control and distressing. It is the most common behavioral expression of [[Compulsive Sexual Behavior Disorder (CSBD)]] and can be diagnosed under it.

## Key insight: use level ≠ problem
The single most important, counter-intuitive finding: **frequency of use does not determine whether use is problematic.** Some people report severe problems at low usage; others use heavily with no dysfunction. (Source: [[Pornography Use and Psychological Science — Grubbs Kraus 2021]])

Two largely independent pathways drive *perceived* problems:
1. **Genuine dysregulation** — true loss of control with functional impairment (the CSBD case).
2. **Moral incongruence** — distress from using porn while morally/religiously disapproving of it; one of the most robust predictors of *self-reported* "porn addiction." → [[Moral Incongruence (Pornography)]]

## Associated outcomes (correlational)
- Lower sexual satisfaction, especially in men (strongest signal, still not causal).
- Self-perceived addiction predicts erectile dysfunction better than actual use; reverse causality likely. → [[Pornography and Sexual Dysfunction — Integrative Review 2019]]
- Small negative correlation with relationship satisfaction (r ≈ −0.09); **joint couple use correlates with higher satisfaction**.

## Treatment
CBT-based therapy is best supported → [[CSBD and Problematic Pornography Use Treatments — Systematic Review 2023]].

> [!gap] A major clinical task is distinguishing genuine dysregulation from moral-conflict distress, because they call for different interventions (behavioral vs values-clarification).
