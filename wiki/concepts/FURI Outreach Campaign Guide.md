# FURI Outreach Campaign Guide
**Step-by-step 12-week campaign to land a FURI research spot — Summer 2026**

The Fulton Undergraduate Research Initiative (FURI) deadline is October. The single biggest variable in acceptance is whether the professor already knows you. This guide is a 12-week campaign to go from zero → accepted research student.

Primary target: [[Mike Ranjram]] (MAPEL lab, MHz magnetics, converter architecture, ML-CEMS)
Secondary target: [[Raja Ayyanar]] (EVSTS, EV/PV/WBG/grid control)
Tertiary targets: 3–5 additional ASU Fulton power electronics / WBG professors

---

## Why Cold Emails to Professors Work (When Done Right)

Emails that reference a **specific paper** and ask a **specific question** get 3–5× higher response rates than generic inquiries. Professors respond to intellectual curiosity, not to requests for opportunities.

**Strong email signals:** "I read your HPPC paper. The flux density drop from 231→66 mT confused me — is that because of the field-canceling winding topology, or something in the core geometry?"

**Weak email signals:** "I'm a freshman interested in power electronics and would love to join your lab."

## Phase 1: Research Preparation (Weeks 1–3)

### Week 1 — Build Your Research Map

1. Go to the [ASU MAPEL Lab page](https://mapel.fulton.asu.edu) — read the lab description and research areas
2. Find 3 recent Ranjram papers (Google Scholar: "Ranjram ASU" or "MAPEL lab ASU")
3. Read the abstracts of all 3; read the full text of the one that interests you most
4. Write a 3-sentence summary of what the paper does and one genuine question you have
5. Repeat for [[Raja Ayyanar]] (search "Ayyanar ASU power electronics")

**Paper reading protocol:**
- Abstract → Introduction → Figures/Tables → Conclusion → Back-fill methods
- Circle anything you don't understand
- Write one honest question that the paper raises for you (not a comprehension question — a "I wonder why they…" question)

### Week 2 — Deepen Technical Context

1. Skim 1–2 more papers per target professor
2. Note the research themes: What problems are they solving? What metrics matter?
3. Look up any new terms in your EE textbooks or Google Scholar
4. Check if there are any ASU grad students in these labs — their thesis titles reveal current projects
5. Search "FURI ASU EEE power electronics" to read past FURI project descriptions in this area

### Week 3 — Prepare Your Materials

**Resume (1 page):**
- Name, email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub
- Education: ASU EE, Class of 2029, GPA (include it if ≥ 3.5)
- Coursework (list Fall 2026 courses as "in progress"): MAT 343, EEE 202, PHY 131
- Projects: Buck Converter Build (12V→5V, STM32, KiCad PCB — list it as "in progress" from Week 1 of build)
- Skills: C/C++, STM32, Python, MATLAB (if any), LTspice, KiCad
- Any other relevant experience (tutoring, clubs, etc.)

**Prepare a "lab brief" for each professor:**
- One paragraph: What does this lab do? What are the 2–3 central research questions?
- One question you would genuinely ask the professor in person
- A note on how your current work (buck converter, etc.) connects

## Phase 2: Cold Email Outreach (Weeks 3–4)

### The Cold Email Template

**Subject:** Undergraduate Research Inquiry — [Specific Paper Title or Topic]

```
Dear Professor [Last Name],

My name is Joe [last name], and I'm a rising sophomore in Electrical Engineering 
at ASU's Ira A. Fulton Schools. I recently read your paper "[Exact Paper Title]" 
and was particularly struck by [one specific finding or result]. It raised a question 
I haven't been able to resolve: [your genuine question in one sentence].

I'm deeply interested in power electronics and WBG devices. This summer I'm 
building a synchronous buck converter (12V→5V, 1A, 100 kHz, STM32 digital control, 
KiCad PCB) to develop hands-on experience with the converter topologies your lab 
works with. I'm planning to apply for FURI in October and hoping to find a research 
direction that connects to [specific topic in professor's work].

Would you be open to a brief conversation — even 15 minutes — to discuss your 
current projects and whether there might be any undergraduate contribution I could 
make, even in a supporting role?

Thank you for your time.

Joe [last name]
EE Sophomore (Class of 2029), ASU
[email] | [phone] | [LinkedIn] | [GitHub]
```

**Personalization rules:**
- NEVER send the same email to two professors without modifying the paper reference and question
- Spell the professor's name correctly — address them as "Professor [Last Name]"
- Keep it under 200 words in the body (excluding signature)
- Attach your 1-page resume

### Send Order
1. Ranjram (primary — most aligned with your WBG track)
2. Ayyanar (secondary — broader EV/grid/PV exposure)
3. 3 additional Fulton EE professors who work in power electronics / WBG
   - Search ASU Fulton EE faculty directory: https://ecee.engineering.asu.edu/faculty
   - Filter by research area: Power Electronics, Energy, Semiconductor Devices

## Phase 3: Follow-Up Protocol (Weeks 4–8)

### If You Get No Response After 1 Week

Send one polite follow-up. Do this exactly once.

```
Subject: Re: Undergraduate Research Inquiry — [Paper Title]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I wanted to follow up on my message from [date] in case it got buried. 
I remain very interested in your research on [topic] and would welcome any 
opportunity to speak briefly if you have time.

Thank you,
Joe [last name]
```

### If You Get a Meeting

**Prepare for the meeting:**
- Know 3 papers from the lab cold
- Have a 30-second summary of your buck converter project ready
- Bring your resume (printed)
- Ask about: current projects, what an undergrad typically does in year 1, whether the FURI timeline works
- DO NOT ask "will you accept me?" — ask "what would a student need to demonstrate to contribute to your work?"

**After the meeting:**
- Send a thank-you email within 24 hours
- Reference one specific thing from the conversation
- Reiterate your FURI timeline and ask what next steps would look like

### Join IEEE PES ASU Chapter
Per [[IEEE PES ASU Chapter]]: the Power and Energy Society sub-chapter creates direct networking pathways to power electronics professors. Go to the first meeting of Fall 2026 semester — professors often attend. This is a secondary channel that reinforces your cold email campaign.

## Phase 4: FURI Application (Weeks 8–12)

### FURI Basics
- **Deadline:** October (check [furi.fulton.asu.edu](https://furi.fulton.asu.edu) for exact date each year)
- **Stipend:** $1,500–$3,000 per semester (varies by project)
- **Output:** Symposium poster + report at end of semester
- **Eligibility:** Min 30 credit hours by start; GPA ≥ 3.0; must have faculty sponsor

### The FURI Application Needs
1. Research proposal (1–2 pages) — what problem, what method, what result
2. Faculty endorsement — this is why the outreach campaign matters
3. Resume
4. Unofficial transcript
5. Short personal statement

### Drafting Your Research Proposal

**Problem statement (2–3 sentences):**
"Power electronics converters operating at high switching frequencies require accurate small-signal models to design stable feedback controllers. Current modeling methods fail to account for [specific gap from a Ranjram paper]. This project proposes to [specific investigation]."

**Method (3–5 sentences):**
"I will use LTspice to simulate [circuit], verify against theory using [technique], and characterize converter behavior using [measurement method]. STM32 digital control will implement [control strategy]. Results will be compared to [existing model/reference]."

**Expected output:**
"A characterized 100 kHz synchronous buck converter with LTspice model, efficiency curve, Bode plot, and step-response measurements. Data will be compared to [theoretical model] and used to validate [hypothesis]."

### Key Differentiator
The buck converter hardware artifact is your differentiator. No other freshman applicant will have a working PCB with three measured FURI-reportable datasets (efficiency curve, load transient, Bode plot). Mention it in the proposal.
