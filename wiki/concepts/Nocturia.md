# Nocturia

Waking to urinate during the night — a common and often-overlooked physiological cause of [[Sleep Maintenance Insomnia|midnight wakeups]]. The bathroom trip may be the *trigger*, but cognitive arousal afterward is often what prevents return to sleep.

## Causes

- **Behavioral**: excessive evening fluid, caffeine, or alcohol intake
- **Fluid redistribution**: fluid pooled in the legs during the day drains while lying flat, increasing nighttime urine volume
- **Medical**: enlarged prostate, overactive bladder, diabetes, UTI, heart failure, sleep apnea, certain medications (confidence: high)

## Reduction strategies

- **Stop fluids a few hours before bed** — but stay well hydrated earlier in the day.
- **Cut evening caffeine and alcohol**, both diuretics.
- **Elevate the legs in the late afternoon/evening** to drain pooled fluid before bedtime, reducing overnight urine production.
- Treat underlying conditions (prostate, bladder, apnea) — see a clinician if it persists.

> [!tip] After a necessary bathroom trip, apply the [[20-Minute Rule for Nighttime Awakenings|20-minute rule]] and keep lights dim to avoid waking fully.
