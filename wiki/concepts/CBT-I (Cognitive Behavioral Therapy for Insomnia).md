# CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia)

The evidence-based, first-line treatment for chronic insomnia — including nighttime awakenings — delivered over **6–8 sessions** (30–90 min each), weekly or bi-weekly (Source: [[CBT-I Primer — Walker et al. 2022]]). Effect sizes of **1.0–1.2** correspond to roughly **50% symptom reduction**, with gains stable up to 24 months (confidence: high). It is as effective as sleeping pills but safer and more durable.

## The five components

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sleep Restriction Therapy** | Limits time in bed to actual sleep ability, building homeostatic sleep drive so sleep consolidates. See [[Sleep Restriction Therapy]]. |
| **Stimulus Control Therapy** | Re-associates bed with sleep, not wakefulness — get out of bed if awake >15–20 min. See [[20-Minute Rule for Nighttime Awakenings]]. |
| **Cognitive Therapy** | Identifies and reframes dysfunctional sleep beliefs (e.g., catastrophizing a bad night) and sets realistic expectations. |
| **Sleep Hygiene** | Education on caffeine, naps, alcohol, environment. *Weak as a standalone* — only minimal effect on its own. |
| **Relaxation / Mindfulness** | Adjunctive: deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, meditation to lower cognitive arousal. |

> [!warning] Sleep hygiene alone is the weakest component. The behavioral core (restriction + stimulus control) drives most of the benefit (Source: [[CBT-I Primer — Walker et al. 2022]]).

## Access

Available in-person with behavioral sleep medicine specialists, and via digital programs. Harvard cites the online program **SHUTi**, where participants reduced mid-sleep awakenings ~50% vs. controls (Source: [[Harvard Health — Awake at 3 a.m.]], confidence: medium).
