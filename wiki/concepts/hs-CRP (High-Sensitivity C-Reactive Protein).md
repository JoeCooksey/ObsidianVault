# hs-CRP (High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein)

**C-reactive protein (CRP)** is a protein made by the liver in response to **[[Immune Cells|inflammation]]** (driven largely by the cytokine IL-6). The **high-sensitivity** assay (**hs-CRP**) measures it down to the low concentrations relevant to *chronic, low-grade* inflammation — the smoldering background inflammation tied to cardiovascular and metabolic disease, as opposed to the big spikes of acute infection that standard CRP is used for.

hs-CRP is a **Tier 1** cardiometabolic biomarker in this vault ([[Most Important Biomarkers (Priority Shortlist)]]); the 2025 ACC guidance names hs-CRP (not standard CRP) as the best routine inflammation marker (see [[Empirical Health Best CVD Biomarkers 2025]]).

## Reference ranges (cardiovascular risk, AHA/CDC)

| hs-CRP | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **< 1.0 mg/L** | Low cardiovascular risk |
| **1.0–3.0 mg/L** | Average risk |
| **> 3.0 mg/L** | High risk |
| **> 10 mg/L** | Likely acute infection/injury or flare — **retest** in a few weeks; don't interpret as chronic risk |

> [!tip] Because a cold, injury, or hard workout in the prior days can transiently spike CRP, measure hs-CRP when healthy and rested, and confirm an elevated chronic reading with a repeat test.

## Why it matters

Chronically elevated hs-CRP tracks with higher risk of heart attack, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and all-cause mortality. It is a **modifiable** marker — it responds to habits within weeks to months, which is why it is a useful feedback dial for lifestyle change. The full breakdown of what moves it up and down is in **[[Research - Habits That Raise and Lower hs-CRP]]**.

**Lowers hs-CRP:** regular exercise, weight/visceral-fat loss, a Mediterranean/anti-inflammatory dietary pattern, omega-3 intake, coffee, adequate sleep, smoking cessation, and stress reduction.
**Raises hs-CRP:** excess adiposity (the single biggest driver), smoking, poor/short or very long sleep, heavy alcohol, chronic psychological stress, sedentary living, and a pro-inflammatory diet ([[Dietary Inflammatory Index|high-DII]], ultra-processed).

> [!note] hs-CRP is downstream of body fat. Much of what any habit does to CRP runs through its effect on adiposity — but exercise and diet quality also lower CRP *independently* of weight loss.
