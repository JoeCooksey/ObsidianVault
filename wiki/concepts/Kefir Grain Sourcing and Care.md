# Kefir Grain Sourcing and Care

Where to buy live milk kefir grains, how price tracks quality, and how to keep grains alive long-term.

## Where to Buy — Quality Tier

Real kefir grains are a **living SCOBY** that multiplies, so a single purchase is a lifetime supply if maintained. Buy **live (fresh)** grains over dried whenever possible — they arrive active and skip the multi-week rehydration. (Source: [[Yemoos — Milk Kefir FAQ]], [[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]])

### Budget / cheapest options

- **Etsy** — the budget floor. Live milk kefir grains commonly **$8–$17**; cheapest listings ~**$8–$10.95** (e.g., $8.00, $10.40 on-sale, $10.95), many with free shipping. Quality varies by seller — read reviews and confirm grains are *live, never dried*. (Source: [[Etsy — Milk Kefir Grains Marketplace]])
- **Amazon** — "Mr. and Mrs. Kefir" and similar sell ~1 tsp live grains; convenient but mixed freshness; transit can stress grains.
- **Free / near-free** — the traditional route: **ask a local fermenter.** Grains multiply, so hobbyists give spares away constantly. Check local **fermentation/sourdough Facebook groups, r/Kefir, Nextdoor, farmers' markets, and "buy nothing" groups.** This is genuinely the highest-quality + lowest-cost source: established grains from a healthy culture, free.

> [!tip] **Recommended buy:** If you want it shipped and reliable, **Yemoos live grains (~$10 + $5)**. If you want it free and don't mind asking around, **a local fermenter's spare grains** beat any purchase — proven, established, $0.

### Live vs. dried grains

- **Live:** active on arrival, ferment immediately, more diverse microbial community.
- **Dried:** shelf-stable (pantry, 6–12 months), cheaper to ship, but need **rehydration over ~3–10 days / several cycles** and often have a **less diverse** microbiome from drying die-off. Buy dried only if you need to delay starting. (Source: [[Yemoos — Milk Kefir FAQ]])

## Caring for Grains

- **Feed daily** at room temp with fresh whole milk (~1 Tbsp grains : 1–2 cups milk). Strain, re-milk, repeat.
- **Growth:** healthy grains multiply, but slowly — **new growth in ~3–21 days**; freshly revived dried/frozen grains can take **3–12 weeks**. Grains can ferment indefinitely even if they never visibly multiply.
- **Ideal temp:** stable **22–25 °C**; avoid cold drafts and heat sources.

### Taking a break / storage

| Method | Duration | How |
|--------|----------|-----|
| **Fridge (in milk)** | 1–2 months | Submerge grains in a jar of fresh milk, lid on, refrigerate. Revives well. (Source: [[Yemoos — Milk Kefir FAQ]]) |
| **Drying** | 6–12 months (room temp), 2+ yr frozen | Rinse, pat dry, air-dry on parchment until hard; store in sealed bag/jar with a cotton ball to absorb moisture |
| **Freezing** | ~3–6 months reliably | Pat dry, freeze in a sealed bag; revival less predictable the longer frozen |

> [!tip] Store grains by **two methods at once** (e.g., active + a dried backup) so one failure doesn't lose your culture.

### Reviving neglected grains

Strain from old milk → rinse with **non-chlorinated** water → place in a **small** amount of fresh milk → wait for visible fermentation before drinking → allow several days/cycles to rebalance. Grains are resilient: documented revivals after **2–3 years** in cold storage. (Source: [[Yemoos — Milk Kefir FAQ]])

### If grains aren't growing

Usually starvation or stress: too many grains per milk, too-long ferments, UHT/low-quality milk, or temperature extremes. Fix by using **full-fat fresh non-UHT milk**, a **1:10–1:15 ratio**, stable **22–25 °C**, and ~24 h cycles. Adding a pinch of powdered milk (extra lactose) or mineral sources can help.
