# Alienation and Isolation

A driving force in *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]*. Even before the murder, [[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov]] has withdrawn from friends, family, and work into brooding solitude — his name derives from the Russian *raskol* ("schism/split"), signaling a man cut off both from society and within himself. His [[The Extraordinary Man Theory|theory]] is partly a rationalization of that pride and separateness.

The crime completes the severance: afterward he literally cannot bear to be touched or loved, recoiling from his mother, sister, and even the devoted [[Dmitri Razumikhin|Razumikhin]]. This isolation, more than any external threat, is the engine of his suffering ([[Psychology of Guilt]]). His salvation therefore takes the form of reconnection — the reaching-out to [[Sonya Marmeladov|Sonya]], the public confession, and finally love in the epilogue — the reversal of alienation being the very shape of [[Redemption Through Suffering|his redemption]].

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
