# Exercise Snacks

**Exercise snacks** are very short bouts (≤1 min, up to ~5 min) of vigorous activity done several times a day, with no warm-up, cool-down, or kit. They are the highest-leverage way to "exercise throughout the day."

## What counts

- **Stair climbing** — the most-studied form; 1–2 flights, hard, a few times daily.
- **Bodyweight bursts** — 20–30 fast bodyweight squats, sit-to-stands, or [[Wall sits / calf raises]] (e.g. calf raises while brushing teeth).
- **Brisk walk / stair sprints** during a coffee break or phone call.
- For **older adults**, the studied forms skew to leg-focused strength and tai chi rather than max-intensity sprints.

## Why they work

Per the [[Exercise Snacks Cardiometabolic Meta-Analysis (2025)]] (27 trials, 970 adults): they raised **VO₂max (SMD = 0.63)** and lowered **systolic BP (−0.67)** and body fat — at low-to-moderate certainty. The mechanism is that brief vigorous efforts repeatedly tap the high-intensity stimulus that drives cardiorespiratory adaptation, without needing a continuous workout.

## How to dose

- **≥2 bouts/day**, most days, for ≥4 weeks before expecting fitness change.
- Each bout should reach **vigorous** intensity (~77%+ HRmax — breathing hard, can't sing).
- Stack onto existing cues (stairs instead of elevator; squats while the kettle boils).

## Relationship to other ideas

Distinct from but complementary to [[Breaking Up Sedentary Time]] (which is about *frequency of interruption* at low intensity) — exercise snacks add an *intensity* stimulus. Both feed the [[Daily Movement Routine (Flexibility, Mobility, Longevity)]]. For a structured high-intensity block see [[Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol]]; for the broader weekly model see [[Attia Four-Pillar Exercise Framework]].
