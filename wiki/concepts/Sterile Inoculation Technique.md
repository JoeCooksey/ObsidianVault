# Sterile Inoculation Technique

**Inoculation** is introducing fungal life — a spore syringe, liquid culture (LC), or agar wedge — into the sterilized grain through the bag's self-healing port. It is the single most contamination-prone moment of the entire grow, so it is done under **still-air** conditions with everything sanitized (Source: [[Midwest Grow Kits — AIO Bag Step-by-Step Guide]]).

## Inoculant options

| Inoculant | Dose into AIO bag | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Spore syringe** | 5–7 cc | Cheapest; slower, less uniform colonization; genetic variation |
| **Liquid culture (LC)** | ~10 cc | Faster, more vigorous, more uniform; lower contamination risk than spores |
| **Agar wedge / grain-to-grain** | n/a (requires opening) | Cleanest genetics but breaks the sealed-bag advantage |

(Source: [[Midwest Grow Kits — AIO Bag Step-by-Step Guide]])

## Still-air procedure

1. **Kill all airflow.** Turn off fans, AC, and heating; close windows and doors. Moving air carries contaminant spores. (A still-air box or flow hood is a step up but not required for AIO bags.)
2. **Sanitize the surface and your hands.** Wipe the work surface, wear nitrile gloves, and spray gloves with 70% isopropyl alcohol.
3. **Sanitize the syringe and port.** Wipe the spore/LC syringe barrel and the **black self-healing injection port** with an alcohol wipe. Flame-sterilize the needle to red-hot, then let it cool ~10 seconds (a hot needle kills the culture).
4. **Inject into the grain layer.** Insert the needle through the port at a slight angle and deposit the dose into the bottom grain layer — not the top substrate.
5. **Withdraw and store.** The port reseals itself. Place the bag at **72–76°F**, out of direct light.

(Sources: [[Midwest Grow Kits — AIO Bag Step-by-Step Guide]], [[North Spore — ShroomTek All-in-One Bag Method]])

> [!warning] Never touch the filter patch with alcohol or wet hands — it blocks the pores and suffocates the mycelium. Spray everything *except* the patch.

## Why still air matters

The bag is sterile inside until the needle creates a temporary opening. Any airborne mold spore (*Trichoderma*, *Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*) that drifts in during those few seconds will race the desired mycelium for the grain. Still air + a sanitized field is what keeps the odds in the cultivator's favor → [[Mushroom Contamination Identification]].
