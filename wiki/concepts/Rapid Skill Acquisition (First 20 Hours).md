# Rapid Skill Acquisition (First 20 Hours)

**You don't need 10,000 hours to get *good enough to be useful* — you need about 20 hours of focused practice.** Josh Kaufman's framework (*The First 20 Hours*, 2013) separates **expertise** (Ericsson's long game) from **competence** (going from zero to functional fast). The steep part of the learning curve happens early. (Source: [[Josh Kaufman]])

## The four steps

1. **Deconstruct the skill.** Break it into the smallest sub-skills. Decide exactly what "good enough" looks like for *your* goal, then identify which sub-skills matter most for that goal — and which you can ignore as a beginner. Practicing the critical few first is the biggest lever.
2. **Learn enough to self-correct.** Get just enough theory/resources (3–5 sources) to recognize when you're doing it wrong and fix it mid-practice. *Don't* binge-research as a way to avoid practicing.
3. **Remove barriers to practice.** Eliminate friction — distractions, missing equipment, the wrong environment, emotional resistance (fear of looking dumb). Make starting frictionless.
4. **Practice at least 20 hours.** Commit upfront to ~20 hours (≈45 min/day for a month). This pre-commitment carries you past the frustrating early phase where you're bad and know it.

## Why 20 hours

The learning curve is steepest at the start: the gap from *incompetent* to *reasonably functional* closes fast, while the gap from *good* to *world-class* takes years. 20 hours of *focused, deliberate* practice — not 20 hours of mindless repetition — gets most everyday skills (a language basics, an instrument, a sport, a tool) to a satisfying functional level.

## How it relates to deliberate practice

This is [[Deliberate Practice]] aimed at **competence rather than mastery**. Same ingredients — target weak sub-skills, get feedback, work at the edge — but with a realistic near-term goal and a hard time budget. Step 1 (deconstruct) is the project-level version of [[Metalearning (Drawing the Map)]].

## How to apply it

- Define the **specific** outcome ("hold a 5-minute Spanish conversation," not "learn Spanish").
- List sub-skills; circle the 20% that deliver 80% of the result.
- Block ~45 min/day for ~30 days; protect it.
- Embrace looking bad early — that's the price of the fast part of the curve.

## Where it fits

This is the **getting-started engine** for the practical side of the guide: it's how you actually begin a new skill without being paralyzed by the myth that mastery requires thousands of hours. (Source: [[Research: How to Learn Anything (The Science of Learning)]])

> [!tip] One-line rule
> Pre-commit to 20 focused hours on the *critical* sub-skills. You'll be shocked how far that gets you.
