# Testosterone Signs in Women (High and Low)

Women make testosterone too (about a tenth of male levels), and the signs of imbalance are **the mirror image of men's**: high T masculinizes; low T is clinically murky.

## High testosterone in women (hyperandrogenism)
Far more clinically important than low T in women. Signs, mild → severe (Source: [[Hirschberg 2023 — Hyperandrogenism in Postmenopausal Women]], high):

**Common / milder:**
- **Hirsutism** — coarse terminal hair in a male pattern (face, chest, back). The single most reliable sign.
- **Acne** and oily skin
- **Androgenic alopecia** — scalp hair thinning / male-pattern loss
- **Menstrual irregularity** (oligomenorrhea), reduced fertility

**Virilization (severe — abrupt onset = red flag):**
- **Clitoromegaly** (clitoral enlargement)
- **Voice deepening**
- **Increased muscle mass**, breast atrophy

**Often accompanied by metabolic signs:** abdominal obesity, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes.

### Causes and the tumor threshold
- **PCOS** — by far the most common cause.
- Ovarian hyperthecosis, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, exogenous androgens.
- **Androgen-producing ovarian or adrenal tumor** — suspect when signs are **rapid, severe, and virilizing**.
- **Threshold:** total **testosterone > 5 nmol/L (≈ 144 ng/dL)** warrants prompt workup to rule out a tumor (Source: [[Hirschberg 2023 — Hyperandrogenism in Postmenopausal Women]], high).

## Low testosterone in women — the contested side
Symptoms *attributed* to low androgens in women: low libido, fatigue, low mood, reduced wellbeing, lower bone density.

> [!gap] There is **no well-defined "female androgen deficiency syndrome."** Major endocrine bodies do not recommend diagnosing it, because no symptom reliably maps to a low blood level and assays are unreliable at female concentrations. Per PubMed (Wierman & Kiseljak-Vassiliades 2022, PMID 35254428, DOI [10.1210/clinem/dgac130](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgac130)), DHEA/androgen therapy shows **no consistent benefit** for sexual function, mood, or wellbeing in otherwise-normal women. The one evidence-supported use of testosterone in women is **hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) after menopause**, where it modestly improves libido (medium).
