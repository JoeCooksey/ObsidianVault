# Psychological Reactance

The motivational force behind [[Scarcity]]: when a freedom or choice is **threatened or taken away, we want it more** and resist the restriction — often valuing the now-forbidden option above what it merited before.

[[Robert B. Cialdini|Cialdini]] illustrates it with the "Romeo and Juliet effect" (parental interference intensifies young love) and the surge of demand that follows bans or limited availability. It is the same drive that makes censored information more enticing and sold-out items more desirable. Reactance is why scarcity tactics work and why heavy-handed restriction frequently backfires. (Morgan Housel invokes the related idea of "Reactance" in *The Psychology of Money* to explain why people resent losing autonomy at work.)

## Why it matters for changing someone's behavior
Reactance is the mechanism that makes **nagging, lecturing, and ultimatums backfire**: telling someone to stop drinking or to eat healthier threatens their autonomy, so they defend the behavior *harder* to reclaim the freedom — regardless of whether you're right. This is precisely why [[Motivational Interviewing]] refuses to push and why [[CRAFT (Community Reinforcement and Family Training)]] replaces confrontation with invitation. The antidote is always **restoring autonomy**: "it's your call." (See [[Empowering Parents — 7 Reasons Why Nagging Doesnt Work]].)
