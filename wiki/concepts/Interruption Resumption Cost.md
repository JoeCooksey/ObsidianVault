# Interruption Resumption Cost

The hidden tax on interrupted work: when you're pulled off a task and later return, you pay a **resumption lag** to rebuild the mental context you lost. This is *the* concept that decides what's worth doing in a job that interrupts you on a schedule.

## The mechanism

A task holds a live "problem representation" in **working memory** — where you are, what's next, what you were holding. An interruption flushes part of it. Resuming means reconstructing it, which costs time, effort, and accuracy (Source: [[Gloria Mark - The Cost of Interrupted Work]]).

- Headline figure: ~**23 minutes** average to return to a task after interruption — but this is an aggregate, not a fixed per-interruption penalty.
- Interrupted people often work *faster* to compensate, but with measurably **more stress and frustration**.
- The cost scales with how much context the task was holding.

## The asymmetry that matters

**Resumption cost is not uniform — it depends on the task:**

| Task type | Context held in WM | Resumption cost |
|---|---|---|
| Deep / creative / single-thread (proof, debugging, writing, a hard [[Deliberate Practice|practice]] rep) | High | **Severe** — interruption shreds it |
| Atomic chunks (a flashcard, one easy problem, a vocab drill) | Low — self-contained | **Trivial** — finish the chunk, drop it |
| Passive input (audiobook, podcast, lecture) | ~None — the medium holds state, you rewind | **Near-zero** |
| Routine admin (email, filing, organizing) | Low | Low |

## Why this is the decision rule

If a job hands you a predictable interruption (e.g. a customer call ~once an hour), starting **high-resumption-cost work** between calls means you pay the 23-minute tax over and over and finish nothing — the worst-of-both outcome. The winning move is to fill the gaps with **low-resumption-cost** work and **batch the deep work for uninterrupted time** off the clock.

This is the same lesson as [[Time Confetti]] from the inside: fragmentation, not scarcity, is the enemy — so pick activities that fragmentation can't damage. See [[Fragmentation-Tolerant Activities]] for the selection filter and [[Low-Demand Job Downtime Playbook]] for the runnable answer.

> [!note] The one upside of forced pauses: they're a natural [[Focused vs Diffuse Mode|diffuse-mode]] window. A hard problem you *parked deliberately* (not one you were mid-stride on) can quietly resolve during a call.
