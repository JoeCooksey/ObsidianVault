# Scrambled Egg Science

Why scrambled eggs go wrong is one thing: **overcooked egg protein**. Everything below is a lever to slow coagulation and keep water trapped, so the eggs stay soft, glossy, and custardy instead of dry, weepy, and rubbery.

## What "cooking" an egg actually is

Egg proteins are coiled. Heat unfolds them and they bond into a network (coagulation). Whites set ~144–149°F, yolks ~149–158°F, a whole beaten egg ~158–165°F. Past that the network tightens, squeezes out the water it held, and you get tough curds sitting in a puddle ("weeping"). **The entire game is stopping before the protein over-tightens.** (Source: high confidence — basic protein chemistry, consistent across sources)

## The levers

**Low heat + constant motion.** Gentle heat gives a wide margin before over-coagulation; stirring breaks curds small and keeps them creamy. The classic French/[[Scrambled Eggs — Masterclass|Ramsay]] soft scramble takes eggs on and off the heat to stay under temperature. (Source: [Christopher Kimball](https://christopherkimball.substack.com/p/the-creamiest-scrambled-eggs); [Gordon Ramsay method](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-make-the-perfect-scrambled-eggs-with-gordon-ramsay))

**Fat coats the proteins.** Butter, cream, mascarpone, or crème fraîche literally lubricate the protein strands, slowing coagulation and making overcooking harder. Higher-fat dairy = creamier result. (Source: [Christopher Kimball](https://christopherkimball.substack.com/p/the-creamiest-scrambled-eggs))

**Water vs. dairy — two different goals.**
- **Water** (≈1 Tbsp/egg) turns to steam and puffs the curds → *fluffier, lighter*.
- **Dairy fat** → *creamier, richer*. Dairy also carries some water, adding a little fluff plus richness.
- (Source: [Food Republic](https://www.foodrepublic.com/1592835/water-versus-milk-fluffiest-scrambled-eggs/), medium confidence)

**Salt timing — the contested lever.**
- **Salt early (Kenji, Keller):** salt dissolves some proteins so they *can't* bond as tightly → more tender, more resistant to overcooking. Best if you wait ~15 min after salting.
- **Salt late (Ramsay):** season at the end so the eggs don't leak.
- **Practical resolution:** mid-to-late salting reliably prevents weeping; early salting is fine if you cook promptly or rest 15 min. Avoid salting and then letting raw eggs sit only a few minutes — that window can make them watery. (Source: [Christopher Kimball](https://christopherkimball.substack.com/p/the-creamiest-scrambled-eggs); medium confidence — chefs disagree)

**Don't over-whisk.** Ramsay breaks eggs straight into the pan and never pre-whisks, arguing heavy whisking makes curds grainy and uniform rather than large and silky. Beat just enough to combine for soft curds; beat fully for an even, fluffy diner-style scramble. (Source: [Gordon Ramsay](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-make-the-perfect-scrambled-eggs-with-gordon-ramsay))

**Starch hack (Kenji).** A small slurry of cornstarch, potato, or tapioca starch lets you cook over *higher* heat without toughening: starch granules absorb water and physically block egg proteins from over-bonding. Tapioca/potato start working at lower temps than cornstarch and run slightly creamier. (Source: [The Kitchn on Kenji](https://www.thekitchn.com/kenji-lopez-alt-scrambled-eggs-review-23143709))

**Carryover cooking is real.** Eggs keep cooking in the hot pan after you kill the heat. Pull them while still slightly underdone and glossy; adding cold crème fraîche at the end both enriches and instantly stops the cooking. (Source: [Gordon Ramsay hack](https://www.thekitchn.com/gordon-ramsay-scrambled-egg-tip-22926369))

## One-line summary
Soft + creamy = low heat, fat, gentle stirring, pull early. Light + fluffy = a splash of water and a bit more air. Either way, **stop before the protein tightens.**
