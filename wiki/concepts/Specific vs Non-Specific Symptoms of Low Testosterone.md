# Specific vs Non-Specific Symptoms of Low Testosterone

The most useful idea for reading testosterone "signs": **not all symptoms carry equal diagnostic weight.** Some track tightly with measured testosterone; most do not.

## The core finding
Large population studies of ageing men (notably the **European Male Ageing Study**, Wu et al., *NEJM* 2010, PMID 20554979) found that of all the symptoms attributed to low T, only the **three sexual symptoms** had a consistent, graded relationship with falling testosterone (high):
1. **Reduced sexual thoughts / low libido**
2. **Reduced morning (spontaneous) erections**
3. **Erectile dysfunction**

This is why the [[Endocrine Society 2018 — Testosterone Therapy Guideline]] anchors the diagnosis on sexual symptoms plus confirmed low blood levels.

## Why the non-sexual symptoms mislead
Fatigue, low mood, irritability, poor concentration, reduced strength, and weight gain are **non-specific** — they are produced just as easily by:
- Poor sleep / sleep apnea
- Obesity and a sedentary lifestyle
- Depression and chronic stress
- Normal aging and other illness

Because these overlap so heavily, they have **low specificity**: present in plenty of men with perfectly normal testosterone. They cannot, by themselves, distinguish low T from the much more common causes.

## The practical rule
> [!important] Symptoms decide *whether to test*, not *whether you have low T*. Confirm with two morning blood tests before attributing anything to testosterone — and treat the common drivers (sleep, body fat, stress, mood) first. See [[Testosterone-Lowering Habits to Avoid]].

This is the mechanism behind the over-diagnosis of "low T": clinics market the non-specific cluster (tired, low drive, soft muscles), which almost any tired modern man recognizes, even when his testosterone is normal.
