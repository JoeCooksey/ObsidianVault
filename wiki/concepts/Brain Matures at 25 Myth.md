# Brain Matures at 25 Myth

The widely repeated claim that the human brain "fully develops" or "fully matures" at age 25 is an oversimplification, not an established scientific fact (Source: [[McGill OSS — Is 25 Really the Magic Number]]). high

## What is actually true
- The **prefrontal cortex** — responsible for planning, impulse control, and decision-making — is the last region to mature and *does* keep developing into the 20s. MRI studies confirm this. high
- The "25" figure traces to Jay Giedd's foundational NIMH longitudinal MRI work (1999, *Nature Neuroscience*) showing frontal gray matter peaks pre-adolescence then thins via synaptic pruning, with the PFC maturing last. high

## Why the "25" claim is misleading
- **No switch flips on your 25th birthday.** Development is a gradual process; some brains keep changing into their 30s, others plateau earlier. high
- Psychologist Larry Steinberg — frequently cited on this — treats 25 as "a ballpark, not a deadline."
- **Structure ≠ behavior.** Brain scans look definitive but fMRI findings are nuanced and prone to misinterpretation; structural change does not cleanly translate to behavioral maturity. high
- There is **no strong evidence** that people under 25 are categorically worse decision-makers.

## So what
Use "before 25" as a motivational Schelling point for building good defaults early — not as biology. Maturity is shaped by experience and environment as much as age; the brain's youthful malleability is *potential*, not a deficiency.
