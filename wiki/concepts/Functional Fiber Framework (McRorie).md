# Functional Fiber Framework (McRorie)

The evidence-based model (McRorie, *Nutrition Today* 2015) explaining **why some fibers work and others don't**. The clinical benefit of a fiber is predicted by two physical properties, not by "soluble vs insoluble."

## The two properties that matter

1. **Viscosity (gel-forming).** A fiber that thickens into a gel in water carries that gel through the gut. The gel is what slows glucose absorption and binds bile acids → the **cholesterol- and glucose-lowering benefits are gel-dependent and proportional to viscosity.**
2. **Fermentation.** A fiber that gut bacteria ferment is **consumed** in the colon — so it loses its water-holding bulk before it can normalize stool, and it produces gas/bloating. Fermentation feeds the microbiome (prebiotic) but does **not** drive the laxation or metabolic benefits.

## The key insight

> The ideal functional fiber is **viscous/gel-forming AND resists fermentation**, so it retains water all the way through the colon.

This is why **psyllium is unique**: it gels but is only minimally fermented → it normalizes stool in **both** directions (firms diarrhea, softens constipation), lowers LDL, improves glycemia, and causes little gas. (Source: [[Fiber Supplement Tier List]])

## Where common fibers fall

| Fiber | Viscous? | Fermented? | Result |
|-------|----------|-----------|--------|
| Psyllium | Yes | Minimally | All benefits, low gas — **best** |
| Methylcellulose | Yes | No | Laxation, low gas; little metabolic |
| β-glucan | Yes | Yes | Cholesterol ↓, some gas |
| Glucomannan | Yes (high) | Partly | Cholesterol/weight; obstruction risk |
| Inulin / FOS | No | Fully | Prebiotic only; high gas; no cholesterol |
| Wheat dextrin | No | Fully | Prebiotic only; gas |
| Wheat bran | No | Low | Roughage; can worsen IBS |

## Takeaway

"Soluble = good, insoluble = bad" is wrong. Ask instead: **does it gel, and does it survive fermentation?** Those two answers predict the clinical payoff.
