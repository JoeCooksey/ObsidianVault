# Interest vs Enjoyment

The key distinction for choosing what to do when bored — from the [[MAC Model of Boredom]]. (Source: [[Westgate 2020 - Why Boredom Is Interesting]])

## The distinction

- **Enjoyment** = low demand + low resources. Simple, familiar, certain things that were rewarding before. A tired commuter playing Candy Crush. Mindless phone games.
- **Interest** = high demand + high resources. Novel, complex things that require cognitive work to make sense of. A documentary, a hard book, a new skill.

Something can be interesting but not enjoyable, or enjoyable but not interesting. Novelty and complexity raise interest and lower enjoyment; certainty does the reverse.

## The junk-food principle

Westgate's metaphor: **"enjoyable activities are like junk food, offering short-term satisfaction at the cost of long-term well-being."**

The cognitive work that interest demands is exactly what builds new schemas and knowledge — the resources that make complex things engaging in the future. Choosing easy enjoyment over demanding interest relieves boredom *now* but guarantees more boredom *later*, because you never build the capacity that prevents it. A compounding loop, in both directions.

This is the cognitive twin of the dopamine story Joe already has filed: cheap spikes lower the baseline ([[Dopamine Baseline and the Pleasure-Pain Balance]]); effort-gated rewards hold it up ([[Healthy Dopamine Protocols]], [[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]]).

## Matching the choice to your state

- **Resources to spare** (bored because under-challenged): go for *interesting* — learn, build, read hard things.
- **Depleted** (bored because over-challenged, or exhausted): enjoyment is legitimate — but pick crash-free enjoyment (walk, music, friend) over the dopamine slot machine ([[Digital Switching]]).
