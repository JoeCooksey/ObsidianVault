# Online Income Methods Tier List

> [!info] Ranking lens: **career-capital fit for Joe**
> This is not a generic "best side hustle" list. Every method is ranked by **(realistic pay) × (does it leverage Joe's existing skills) × (does it build the résumé toward the LLNL/Sandia EE track) × (does it fit a heavy course load + 6×/week training)**. The ranking deliberately flips the internet's usual order: the "scalable passive income" darlings drop to C/D because they burn Joe's scarcest resource — deep-work hours — on skills that **don't compound into engineering**.

## The one insight

For a generic 19-year-old, dropshipping and affiliate marketing sit mid-tier. For **Joe specifically**, the methods that double as **portfolio + résumé toward LLNL/Sandia** (tutoring, AI-training, freelance Python) rise to the top, and everything that pays cash but teaches marketing-not-engineering sinks. Money you earn while building career capital is worth far more than the same dollars earned flipping t-shirts. See opportunity-cost logic in [[Free Time Tier List]].

## S-Tier — pay now AND build the résumé

### 1. STEM / EE / math tutoring (online)
- **Pay:** $25–60/hr; STEM and test-prep command the top of the range (Source: [[Online Tutoring Rates 2026 — Brighterly-Glassdoor]]). Glassdoor avg online math tutor ≈ $45/hr.
- **Why S for Joe:** his single strongest monetizable asset *right now* (already flagged as the highest $/hr no-experience option in [[Research - Summer Part-Time Job Guide for Livermore College Student]]). Teaching is the best comprehension test (Feynman Technique) — every hour paid **reinforces his own MAT/EE coursework**. Builds communication, ranked the #1 early-career EE hiring skill (Siemens). Zero commute, set your own rate, fits around training.
- **Start:** Wyzant + Tutor.com + Varsity Tutors; set rate, list Calc/Physics/EE/Python.

### 2. AI training / data work for coding & STEM
- **Pay:** national avg AI-trainer ≈ $31/hr, but **STEM + coding experts command $40–$100+/hr** (Source: [[AI Training Platforms for Coders 2026 — aitrainer.work]]). Outlier, DataAnnotation, Mercor, Alignerr.
- **Why S for Joe:** directly uses Python + EE/math; fully async/flexible; and it **teaches him how LLMs are actually trained** — AI literacy is worth a documented **56% EE wage premium** ([[Research - EE AI Skills and Applications]]). Reads on a résumé as "AI/ML model evaluation." Best *new* income source for him. → deep dive: [[AI Training Data Work (Online Income)]].

### 3. Freelance Python automation / scripting (productized)
- **Pay:** beginners $15–25/hr, rising to $30–40/hr fast with reviews; API/automation work $30–120/hr (Source: [[Side Hustles for College Students 2026 — Upwork]]).
- **Why S for Joe:** the *work itself* is the portfolio LLNL/Sandia and EE employers read. Niche into **test-automation / data scripts** (PyVISA, PyLTSpice, pandas) and it overlaps his EE project ladder one-to-one — every paid gig becomes a GitHub repo. Career capital = the deliverable.
- **Start:** Upwork/Fiverr, one narrow productized service ("I'll write a Python script to automate X").

## A-Tier — strong fit, slower ramp or more setup

### 4. Technical writing / documentation (freelance)
- **Pay:** entry $25–35/hr, mid $39–50/hr, specialists $50–100/hr (Source: [[Freelance Technical Writer Rates 2026 — Upwork-ZipRecruiter]]).
- **Why A:** "engineers who write clearly advance 2–3 career levels faster" ([[Reading Complete Guide]] / On Writing Well). Niche into EE/power-electronics docs, app notes, README/datasheet work and it builds the exact writing muscle plus domain credibility.

### 5. Kaggle competitions / ML portfolio
- **Pay:** prize money is lottery-ish — treat as **paid learning**, not income.
- **Why A:** the portfolio + scikit-learn/PyTorch reps are *exactly* his EE×DS track ([[EE Data Science Skills Roadmap]]); résumé gold for the AI-EE intersection. The skill compounds even when the prize doesn't.

### 6. Technical digital products (Gumroad)
- **Pay:** semi-passive; create once, sell repeatedly (courses $500–3,000+ lifetime; templates/packs smaller but compounding).
- **Why A:** LTSpice circuit packs, Python-for-EE notebooks, study guides. Builds an audience + compounds. Lower than tutoring only because it's audience-dependent and front-loaded.

## B-Tier — fine, supporting (money with weak career capital)

- **7. Bug bounties** (HackerOne/Bugcrowd) — real payouts $50–$2,000+/bug, builds security skill, but steep curve and **off the EE track**; only if security genuinely interests him.
- **8. Technical YouTube / blog** — 6–18 month income lag, but compounds into reputation + the teaching/writing loop. A long-game brand, not near-term income.
- **9. General freelance gigs** (VA, data entry) — quick cash, near-zero skill leverage; use only as a stopgap.

## C-Tier — low fit (money but ~zero engineering capital, competes with study)

- **10. UGC / short-form content for brands** — pays, but zero EE relevance and a real time sink.
- **11. Print-on-demand / Etsy** — creative; doesn't touch his skills.
- **12. Affiliate marketing / niche blogs** — long ramp; marketing, not engineering.
- **13. Online surveys / cashback / microtask apps** — pennies/hour; opportunity-cost-negative versus one tutoring hour.

## D-Tier — avoid (bad EV, hype, or derails the track)

- **14. Dropshipping** — not passive, overcrowded in 2026, high failure rate, zero career capital (Source: [[Why Most Make-Money-Online Advice Fails 2026 — NSF Tech]]).
- **15. Crypto "AI trading bots" / HYIP / staking-for-yield pitches** — mostly scams or negative-EV; **directly contradicts Joe's own wiki** ([[Index Fund Investing]], [[Research - Stock Buy-Sell Factors and Quant Finance]]).
- **16. Day trading / forex** — ~90% of active traders lose to the index (SPIVA); this is gambling, not income.
- **17. MLM / "passive income" course funnels** — predatory; the only one making money is the person selling the course.

## How to read this list (the honest reality)

The 2026 consensus across sources: **"passive income" is almost always active income with the failures edited out.** Realistic numbers for someone who goes deep on a real skill: ~$500–2,000 in month one, $3k–8k/month within a year — *active, not passive* (Source: [[Why Most Make-Money-Online Advice Fails 2026 — NSF Tech]]). Joe's edge is that he already has the rare inputs (Python, EE/math, discipline). The play is **not** to chase a new business model — it's to monetize the skills he's already compounding.
