# Problem-Solving Skill-Building Protocol

The evidence assembled into a routine you can run. Designed for self-study domains (math, programming, EE) — the ones where you have a feedback loop. (See [[Research: Best Way to Develop Problem-Solving Skills]] for the full evidence base.)

## The non-negotiable prerequisite

**Pick real domains and go deep.** There is no general skill to train in the abstract ([[Domain-Specific Knowledge vs General Problem-Solving Skill]], [[Near vs Far Transfer]]). Better problem solving = more organized domain knowledge + a deliberate process. Choose where it pays off — for Joe that's **math foundations, programming, and EE** (cf. [[Most Self-Teachable High-Value Skills (Tier List for Joe)]]).

## The per-problem loop

1. **Understand before solving** (Pólya step 1). Restate the problem, list givens/unknown, draw it. Spend real time here — it's the step everyone skips.
2. **Plan, out loud or on paper** ([[Metacognition in Problem Solving|metacognition]]). Name your approach and which heuristic you're using.
3. **Struggle first — but bounded.** Attempt it before looking ([[Worked Examples and Productive Failure|productive failure]]). Set a timebox (e.g. 15–20 min); productive struggle, not endless flailing.
4. **Then study the worked solution** if stuck. Don't just read it — self-explain each step in your own words.
5. **Compare two examples.** Find a structurally similar problem that looks different; map what's the same underneath ([[Analogical Encoding (Comparing Cases)]]).
6. **Look back** (Pólya step 4 — the highest-leverage habit). Post-mortem: what was the key move? where did I stall and why? what pattern will I recognize next time?
7. **Re-solve from a blank page** later (retrieval) — see [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]].

## How to structure practice over weeks

- **Interleave problem types** rather than blocking one kind — it builds the skill of *choosing the right method* ([[Interleaving (Learning)]]). Feels worse, retains better.
- **Attack your weakest sub-skill** at the edge of ability with fast feedback ([[Deliberate Practice]]).
- **Space it daily** — small focused reps compound ([[Compound Time (5-Hour Rule)]]).
- **Keep a "key moves" log** — one line per problem on the insight that cracked it. This is how you accumulate transferable schemas.

## What NOT to do

- ❌ Brain-training games / abstract "logic" apps — no far transfer ([[Near vs Far Transfer]]).
- ❌ Re-reading solutions without attempting first — feels productive, builds little.
- ❌ Practicing only what you can already do — that's maintenance, not improvement.

> [!tip] The whole thing in one sentence
> Go deep in real domains, struggle with each problem before checking, compare examples to find the deep structure, and always run a post-mortem — that's what turns hours into skill.
