# Dietary Inflammatory Index

The **Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII)** is a scoring system that rates an overall diet on a spectrum from **anti-inflammatory (negative scores)** to **pro-inflammatory (positive scores)**, based on how each of ~45 food parameters has been shown to move inflammatory biomarkers like **[[hs-CRP (High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein)|hs-CRP]]**, IL-6, and TNF-α in the literature.

- **Anti-inflammatory contributors** (push score down): fiber, fruits and vegetables, whole grains, omega-3 fats, polyphenols/flavonoids, spices (turmeric, ginger), tea, moderate coffee, magnesium, vitamins D/E/C.
- **Pro-inflammatory contributors** (push score up): refined carbohydrates and added sugar, saturated and trans fats, red and processed meat, and total energy excess.

A higher (more pro-inflammatory) DII is associated with higher circulating hs-CRP and higher risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and mortality. In a population study, DII and a Mediterranean-style diet score were both significantly associated with a low-grade systemic inflammation score (CRP/TNF-α/adiponectin), while separately accounting for ultra-processed food intake did **not** add explanatory power — suggesting overall dietary *pattern* captures most of the inflammatory signal (according to PubMed — Bastos 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnesp.2024.02.010); see [[Bastos 2024 — Dietary Inflammatory Index and Systemic Inflammation]]).

> [!note] Practically, the DII formalizes what the [[Research - Habits That Raise and Lower hs-CRP|habit research]] shows: a Mediterranean/whole-food pattern sits at the anti-inflammatory end, and a refined-carb/processed-meat/added-sugar pattern sits at the pro-inflammatory end.
