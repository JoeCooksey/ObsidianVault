# Frozen vs Fresh Produce

Frozen fruits and vegetables are **nutritionally equal to fresh — and often better** than "fresh" produce that has spent days in transit and a fridge. (Source: [[Frozen vs Fresh Produce — Healthline]])

## Key Findings

- Frozen fruit retains **90–100% of vitamin content** vs. fruit harvested at maturity.
- Fresh produce loses nutrients at every supply-chain stage; by the time it's eaten, content can be **30–50% lower than at harvest**.
- Vitamin C in **frozen blueberries, corn, and green beans measured higher** than in the same fresh items after a few days in the fridge.
- Minerals (calcium, magnesium, zinc, iron), fiber, and total phenolics are well-conserved by freezing.
- **Caveat:** blanching before freezing drops some vitamin C and folate, and freezing affects vitamin C and carotenoids somewhat — but the net is still on par with or above aged fresh.

## Why Frozen Wins for Meal Prep

- **Zero spoilage pressure** — no race against the fridge clock.
- Picked and frozen at peak ripeness.
- Cheaper, and ideal for [[Kefir Overnight Oats]], smoothies, and cooked veg.
- No thawing needed for oats/smoothies — stir in frozen.

## Bottom Line

Mostly-frozen fruit (as in Joe's stack) is a strength, not a compromise. (See [[Research - Meal Prep Playbook for Joe's Core Foods]].)
