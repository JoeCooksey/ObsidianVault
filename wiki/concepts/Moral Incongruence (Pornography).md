# Moral Incongruence (Pornography)

**Moral incongruence (MI)** is the psychological dissonance that arises when someone uses pornography while *morally disapproving* of it. The concept, developed by [[Joshua Grubbs]] and colleagues, reframes much of what is popularly called "porn addiction."

## Core finding
MI is **one of the most robust and consistent predictors of self-reported pornography addiction** — more predictive than actual frequency of use. Religiosity predicts *perceived* addiction; use level largely does not. (Source: [[Pornography Use and Psychological Science — Grubbs Kraus 2021]])

The "Pornography Problems due to Moral Incongruence" (PPMI) model holds that for many people reporting distress, the driver is **value conflict, not behavioral dysregulation.** Findings are consistent cross-sectionally, in nationally representative U.S. samples, and longitudinally over 1 year.

## Why it matters
- Someone can use porn rarely yet feel "addicted" because it violates their values; another can use heavily without distress.
- MI is associated with greater distress about use, greater general psychological distress, and more reported problems — **independent of how much porn is actually consumed.**
- The ICD-11 explicitly excludes distress arising *solely* from moral judgment from a [[Compulsive Sexual Behavior Disorder (CSBD)]] diagnosis, operationalizing this distinction.

## Clinical implication
Treatment differs by pathway: genuine dysregulation → behavioral (CBT); moral-conflict distress → values clarification, reducing shame, and aligning behavior with chosen values (ACT-style).

> [!note] MI is not a claim that pornography is harmless — it isolates *why people report distress*, separating value conflict from clinical compulsion.
