# Digital Switching

Flipping between short videos, skipping forward/back, swiping to the next clip — the default behavior of a bored person holding a phone. Named and studied by Katy Tam (University of Toronto), *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 2024. (Source: [[Tam 2024 - Digital Switching Study]])

## The finding

**Switching to escape boredom causes more boredom.** Across 7 experiments (~1,200 people), switching between videos and skipping within them made viewing less satisfying, less engaging, less meaningful — and more boring — than watching one video straight through. People predicted the opposite; the intuition is reliably wrong. (confidence: high)

## Why it backfires

Maps onto both deficits of the [[MAC Model of Boredom]]:

- **Attention**: constant switching never lets attention settle into engagement — you stay in the broken-attention state that *is* boredom.
- **Meaning**: "people don't have time to engage with or understand the content," so everything consumed feels meaningless (Tam).

Plus the dopamine layer: variable, zero-effort novelty is the stickiest reward schedule ([[Reward Prediction Error]]) while doing nothing for the baseline ([[Tonic vs Phasic Dopamine]]) — a slot machine that pays out boredom.

## The fix

1. If you're going to watch, **watch one thing in full** — immersion beats browsing.
2. Better: substitute the reach-for-phone with a real activity — see [[Boredom Activity Playbook]].
3. Structural guards: app timers, phone in another room, [[Dopamine Detox (Evidence Review)]]-style stimulus control.
