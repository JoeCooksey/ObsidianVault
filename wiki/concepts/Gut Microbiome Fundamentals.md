# Gut Microbiome Fundamentals

Core science behind the gut microbiome — what it is, how it works, what breaks it, and what heals it. Feeds into [[Gut Health Foods Tier List]].

## What Is the Gut Microbiome?

The gut microbiome is the ecosystem of ~38 trillion microorganisms (bacteria, fungi, viruses, archaea) living in the gastrointestinal tract. The colon hosts 99% of them. Key features:
- **~1,000 bacterial species** identified in human guts; most people harbor 150–500 species
- **Diversity = health**: higher species diversity consistently correlates with better metabolic, immune, and cardiovascular outcomes (ZOE/Nature 2025, n=34,000+)
- **Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio** is the key high-level health marker; dysbiosis = ratio imbalance

## Key Bacterial Players

| Genus | Role | Best Food Source |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| **Bifidobacterium** | Produces acetate + lactate; supports gut barrier; reduces inflammation | Inulin/FOS (garlic, onions, Jerusalem artichoke), kefir, yogurt |
| **Lactobacillus** | Lactic acid producer; inhibits pathogens; immune modulation | Kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut, yogurt |
| **Akkermansia muciniphila** | Gut lining integrity specialist; inversely correlated with obesity/T2D | Berries, pomegranate, grape polyphenols |
| **Faecalibacterium prausnitzii** | Top butyrate producer; anti-inflammatory; depleted in IBD | High-fiber diet (lentils, oats, barley) |
| **Lachnospiraceae** | Butyrate-producing family; restored by omega-3; disrupted by antibiotics | Omega-3 foods, fiber-rich whole foods |

## The SCFA Pathway (Most Important Mechanism)

```
Prebiotic fiber (inulin, FOS, beta-glucan, pectin)
         ↓
Fermented by Bifidobacterium + Faecalibacterium + Lachnospiraceae
         ↓
Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs):
  • Butyrate (most important) → fuel for colonocytes; anti-inflammatory; gut barrier integrity
  • Propionate → liver metabolism; satiety signaling
  • Acetate → systemic anti-inflammatory; energy
         ↓
Outcomes:
  • Strengthened gut lining (↓ zonulin, ↓ leaky gut)
  • ↓ systemic inflammation (↓ CRP, ↓ IL-6)
  • ↑ insulin sensitivity
  • ↑ immune regulation
```

**Best butyrate-producing foods**: lentils, oats (beta-glucan), garlic, Jerusalem artichokes, barley, bananas (green/unripe), resistant starch

## Dysbiosis — What Breaks the Microbiome

**Dysbiosis** = imbalanced gut microbiome composition. Causes and consequences:

| Cause | Mechanism |
|-------|-----------|
| Ultra-processed food | Emulsifiers disrupt mucosal layer; refined sugars feed Proteobacteria |
| Antibiotics | Broad-spectrum kill beneficial bacteria; Lachnospiraceae most vulnerable |
| Chronic stress | HPA axis → cortisol → gut motility disruption; alters Bifidobacterium |
| Low dietary diversity | <30 plant foods/week → reduced microbial diversity |
| Processed meats | N-nitroso compounds → direct dysbiosis; WHO Group 1 carcinogen |
| Artificial sweeteners | Saccharin + sucralose alter microbiome (Weizmann Institute human trial) |
| High-intensity exercise without recovery | Over-training → gut permeability increase (relevant for Joe's 6×/week cycling) |

**Signs of dysbiosis**: bloating, irregular bowel movements, food intolerances, fatigue, brain fog, frequent illness

## Rebuilding the Microbiome — Evidence-Based Timeline

| Intervention | Timeline for Measurable Change |
|-------------|-------------------------------|
| Fermented foods daily | 2–4 weeks (diversity shift measurable) |
| Prebiotic fiber daily | 4–8 weeks (Bifidobacterium increase) |
| Polyphenol-rich foods 3×/day | 8 weeks (zonulin decrease) |
| Omega-3 2–3 g EPA+DHA/day | 8–12 weeks (F/B ratio restoration) |
| 30+ plant foods/week | 4–6 weeks (alpha diversity increase) |
| Removing ultra-processed foods | 2 weeks (rapid inflammatory marker drop) |

## Key Metrics

- **Alpha diversity**: species richness within one individual — higher = better
- **Beta diversity**: how different two people's microbiomes are — large individual variation is normal
- **Zonulin**: biomarker of intestinal permeability ("leaky gut"); decreased by polyphenols + butyrate
- **Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio**: elevated F/B → obesity/metabolic disease association
- **30 plant foods/week**: American Gut Project (n=10,000+) — strongest dietary predictor of alpha diversity

## Probiotic Supplements vs. Food

| Form | Strains | Survival | Verdict |
|------|---------|----------|---------|
| Kefir | ~61 strains | High (protected by food matrix) | Best overall |
| Plain yogurt | ~7 strains | Moderate | Good daily option |
| Kimchi/sauerkraut | Variable Lactobacillus | High | Good diversity |
| Probiotic supplement | 1–10 strains typically | Variable (many die in stomach) | Use if no fermented foods access |

**Key rule**: refrigerated fermented foods > shelf-stable products (pasteurization kills all bacteria). Probiotic supplements: Lactobacillus acidophilus + Bifidobacterium longum most evidence-backed strains.

## Gut-Brain Axis

The gut produces 90–95% of the body's serotonin. The vagus nerve is a two-way highway between gut and brain:
- Gut dysbiosis → anxiety, depression, cognitive fog
- Chronic stress → gut dysbiosis (bidirectional relationship)
- Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus supplementation → measurable anxiety reduction in human trials
