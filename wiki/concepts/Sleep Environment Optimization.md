# Sleep Environment Optimization

The physical bedroom is the foundation of sleep quality. Three controllable variables — temperature, darkness, and comfort — set the stage before any supplement or behavior matters.

## Temperature (~19–21°C)

To initiate sleep, core body temperature must drop a couple of degrees, part of shifting the central nervous system into a relaxed parasympathetic state. A cooler room helps the body reach that set-point faster. ~20°C is a comfortable target; cooler is generally better (the reason free-AC hotel rooms often sleep well). Without AC, use a fan or open windows.

> [!tip] The hot-shower trick
> A hot shower *before* bed feels warming but actually causes the body to dump heat afterward via peripheral vasodilation, lowering core temperature and speeding sleep onset. Pair "cold room + hot shower."

## Darkness

Make the room as dark as possible — blackout curtains or an eye mask — and tape over stray LEDs (AC controls, light leaking under the door). Light is the master cue for [[Circadian Rhythm Alignment|circadian rhythm]]; see also [[Pre-Bed Light Management]].

## Comfort

A comfortable mattress plus a **slightly firmer, larger pillow** that holds the head in a neutral position (not slouched) supports the airway and neck through the night.

Related: keeping the room single-purpose amplifies all of the above — see [[Bedroom Stimulus Control]].
