# Grip Strength and Longevity

**Grip strength** is one of the most reproducible non-aerobic predictors of how long you live. Measured with a hand dynamometer in kilograms, it serves as a cheap proxy for *whole-body* muscular strength and overall vitality.

## The evidence

Per the [[Handgrip Strength Dose-Response Meta-Analysis (Lopez-Bueno 2022)]]:
- Across **3+ million adults**, lower grip strength tracks higher all-cause, cancer, and cardiovascular mortality.
- All-cause mortality falls in a **near-linear** way as grip rises across **26–50 kg**.
- Companion meta (Wu 2017): **each −5 kg of grip ≈ +16% all-cause mortality**, +21% CVD mortality.

## How to actually build it

Grip is an *index* of strength, not a standalone training target. It improves as a byproduct of:
- **Carries** — farmer's carries, suitcase carries, carrying groceries.
- **Pulling** — rows, deadlifts, pull-ups / dead hangs.
- **Dead hangs** — also decompress the spine and open the shoulders (a mobility two-for-one).

A dedicated gripper helps only the forearm; the mortality signal comes from total strength.

> [!note] Grip and the [[Sitting-Rising Test]] are the two best **non-aerobic** fitness→mortality markers — cheap to test, trainable, and complementary to the aerobic markers (VO₂max, [[Daily Steps and Mortality Meta-Analysis (Lancet 2025)|step count]]).

The strength leg of the [[Daily Movement Routine (Flexibility, Mobility, Longevity)]] and the strength pillar of the [[Attia Four-Pillar Exercise Framework]].
