# Deliberate Practice

**The kind of practice that actually builds expertise** — not just doing the activity, but a highly structured effort aimed at improving specific weaknesses, with tight feedback and repetition. Coined by psychologist [[Anders Ericsson]] (Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer, 1993). (Source: [[Anders Ericsson]])

## Ordinary practice ≠ deliberate practice

Most people plateau because they practice on autopilot — repeating what they can already do. Mere experience barely correlates with performance: research shows **only a weak relationship between length of experience and actual skill**. A doctor or programmer with 20 years of habit can be worse than one with 5 years of deliberate improvement. (Source: [[Anders Ericsson]])

## The defining features

1. **Targets a specific weakness** just beyond current ability — work at the edge, not in the comfort zone (a [[Desirable Difficulties|desirable difficulty]]).
2. **Well-defined goals** for each session ("fix this," not "play for an hour").
3. **Full concentration** — it is effortful and not inherently fun; this is why it pairs with [[Deep Work]].
4. **Immediate, informative feedback** — you must see the gap between what you did and what you intended.
5. **Repetition with refinement** — repeat the hard part, adjust, repeat again.
6. **Builds mental representations** — experts develop richer internal models that let them perceive patterns and self-monitor. (Source: [[Anders Ericsson]])

## The 10,000-hour caveat

Gladwell's popular "10,000-hour rule" is a distortion of Ericsson's work. Ericsson's point was never a magic number — it was that *the type and quality of practice*, not raw hours, separates experts from amateurs. Hours of unfocused repetition compound to very little.

## How to apply it

1. **Find a coach or a feedback loop.** A teacher, a rubric, a test, or a recording of yourself — anything that exposes errors fast.
2. **Isolate the sub-skill you're worst at** and drill it (see [[Rapid Skill Acquisition (First 20 Hours)]] — "attack your weakest point").
3. **Work in focused blocks** (the [[Deep Work]] / Pomodoro pattern), not long unfocused sessions.
4. **Push slightly past comfortable.** If it feels easy, you're maintaining, not improving.
5. **Compound it daily.** Small focused reps daily beat occasional marathons — see [[Compound Time (5-Hour Rule)]].

## Where it fits

Deliberate practice is how the **skill** side of learning advances, the complement to the memory-side stack ([[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]] + [[Spaced Repetition]]). It's the engine behind [[Directness and Drill (Ultralearning)|directness/drill]] in Ultralearning. (Source: [[Research: How to Learn Anything (The Science of Learning)]])

> [!tip] One-line rule
> Don't practice what you're good at; practice what you're bad at, with feedback, at the edge of your ability.
