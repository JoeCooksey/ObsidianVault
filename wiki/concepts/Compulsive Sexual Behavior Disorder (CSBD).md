# Compulsive Sexual Behavior Disorder (CSBD)

CSBD is the formal clinical diagnosis for problematic, out-of-control sexual behavior. The **WHO added it to the ICD-11** as an **impulse control disorder** (not, notably, as an addiction). (Source: WHO ICD-11)

## Diagnostic criteria
A persistent inability to control intense, repetitive sexual impulses or urges, resulting in repetitive sexual behavior over **6+ months**, causing marked distress or impairment in personal, family, social, educational, or occupational functioning. Problematic pornography viewing and compulsive masturbation are common behavioral manifestations. (Source: ICD-11)

> [!important] The ICD-11 explicitly notes that **distress entirely due to moral judgments about sexual behavior is NOT sufficient** for diagnosis — guarding against pathologizing normal behavior over which someone merely feels guilty. See [[Moral Incongruence (Pornography)]].

## Relationship to pornography
The diagnosis captures severe internet-pornography-related symptoms; >80% of people with hypersexuality report excessive/problematic pornography use → [[Problematic Pornography Use (PPU)]].

## The addiction debate
CSBD was deliberately classified as an **impulse-control disorder, not a behavioral addiction**, because the scientific community has *not* reached consensus that sexual behavior or pornography can be "addictive" in the substance sense. See [[Pornography Use and Psychological Science — Grubbs Kraus 2021]].

## Treatment
**CBT has the strongest evidence**; ACT, mindfulness, 12-step, and experiential therapies show benefit; SSRIs and naltrexone have limited/mixed support → [[CSBD and Problematic Pornography Use Treatments — Systematic Review 2023]].
