# Sulfate-Reducing Bacteria (Desulfovibrio)

The colonic microbes that **manufacture the smell**. Predominantly the genus ***Desulfovibrio***.

## What they do
They are part of the gut's **hydrogen-disposal economy**. Fermentation produces large amounts of H₂, which must be removed or it inhibits further fermentation. Three guilds compete for that H₂:
1. **Methanogens** → turn H₂ + CO₂ into methane (CH₄, odorless)
2. **Acetogens** → turn H₂ + CO₂ into acetate
3. **Sulfate-reducing bacteria (SRB)** → turn H₂ + sulfate into **hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)** — the malodorant

Which guild dominates an individual's gut partly explains why some people's gas reliably stinks more. (Source: [[Chemistry World - The Chemistry of Farts]])

## Diet connection
SRB activity scales with **available sulfate/sulfur**. Diets high in sulfur amino acids (protein/meat) and inorganic sulfate (some preservatives, certain waters, cruciferous and allium vegetables) feed SRB → more H₂S. See [[High-Sulfur Foods and Flatus Odor]].

## Why it matters
SRB are the mechanistic link between **what you eat** and **how bad it smells**. They also tie into gut-health research: excess H₂S from SRB is implicated in mucosal irritation and ulcerative colitis. Connects to [[Gut Microbiome Fundamentals]] and the wider [[Why Optimize Your Gut|gut-health]] cluster.

> [!gap] Individual SRB/methanogen balance is hard to modify directly; current practical control is via substrate (diet) and binding (bismuth), not by targeting the bacteria.
