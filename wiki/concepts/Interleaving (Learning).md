# Interleaving (Learning)

**Mix problem types instead of doing them in blocks.** Rather than drilling 20 problems of type A, then 20 of type B (*blocking*), you shuffle them: A, C, B, A, B, C. Interleaving is rated **moderate utility** in the Dunlosky meta-analysis and is one of the three core techniques in *Make It Stick*. (Source: [[Dunlosky 2013 — Improving Students' Learning]])

## The key idea

> "In interleaving, you don't move from a complete practice set of one topic to another — you switch *before* each practice is complete." (Source: [[Make It Stick (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel)]])

Blocking lets you run on autopilot: once you know "this is a type-A problem," you just repeat the procedure. Interleaving forces you to first **identify which kind of problem you're facing** and then **choose the right approach** — the skill you actually need in the real world (and on exams), where problems don't arrive pre-labeled.

## Why it works

- It builds **discrimination** — telling apart situations that call for different methods.
- It is a [[Desirable Difficulties|desirable difficulty]]: performance *during* practice feels worse and slower, but long-term retention and transfer are higher.
- It mixes the *context* of practice, building more connections and entrenching knowledge more firmly. (Source: [[Make It Stick (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel)]])

## The counterintuitive trap

Interleaved practice feels *harder and less successful* than blocked practice while you're doing it, so learners rate it as worse — yet it produces better delayed test performance. Don't trust the in-the-moment feeling of fluency; judge by what you can do after a delay.

## How to do it

1. **Shuffle problem sets** by topic instead of grinding one type at a time.
2. **Mix related skills** in a session (e.g., for music: scales + a passage + sight-reading, not 45 min of one).
3. **Combine with spacing** — interleaving naturally spaces each subtopic across the session.
4. **Interleave *related* material**, not random unrelated topics; the benefit comes from learning to discriminate between confusable categories.

## Where it fits

Interleaving is the third leg of the **[[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)|recall]] + [[Spaced Repetition|spacing]] + interleaving** stack that the learning-science literature consistently endorses. (Source: [[Research: How to Learn Anything (The Science of Learning)]])

> [!tip] One-line rule
> Don't practice until you can do it right with the answer in view — practice until you can pick the right method when you *don't* know which kind of problem it is.
