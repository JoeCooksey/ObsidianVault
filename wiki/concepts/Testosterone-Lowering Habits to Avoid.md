# Testosterone-Lowering Habits to Avoid

The damaging side of the ledger, ranked **worst-first** by effect size × how common the habit is. For most young men, *avoiding these matters more than chasing boosters* — the upside of removing a suppressor is larger and more reliable than any natural booster. See [[Testosterone-Boosting Habits to Include]] for the other half.

---

## F-Tier — most damaging, avoid hard

### 1. Carrying excess body fat / sedentary obesity
The biggest modifiable suppressor. Adipose tissue aromatizes testosterone into estradiol and drives the insulin-resistance + inflammation that depress the HPG axis — a self-reinforcing loop.
- Obesity is one of the strongest correlates of low T; the relationship is **causal and bidirectional** (Mendelian-randomization confirmed) (Source: [[Weight Loss and Testosterone Review 2024]])
- Reversing it is S-tier upside (see boosting list)
- Evidence: **high**

### 2. Chronic sleep deprivation (and untreated sleep apnea)
- 5 h/night for one week → **−10–15% testosterone** in healthy young men (Source: [[Leproult Van Cauter Sleep Restriction RCT 2011]])
- Sleep apnea independently lowers T (Source: [[Harvard Health Testosterone Lifestyle]])
- The most common self-inflicted T killer among otherwise-healthy men
- Evidence: **high**

### 3. Heavy / chronic alcohol use
- Acute large doses begin dropping T within 30 min; suppression peaks 12–16 h later, can persist 24 h
- Even *moderate* drinking ≈ **−6.8%**; chronic heavy use up to **−50%** (Source: [[Alcohol and Testosterone Review 2023]])
- Mechanisms: ↑ HPA-axis activity, inflammation, oxidative stress, direct Leydig-cell toxicity, plus weight gain
- A meta-analysis confirms chronic consumption disrupts the whole gonadal axis (Source: [[Alcohol and Testosterone Review 2023]])
- Evidence: **high** (note: 1 drink/day appears low-risk)

## D-Tier — significant, common

### 4. Chronic psychological stress
Sustained cortisol suppresses GnRH and LH release and directly inhibits Leydig-cell T production; cortisol and T also compete for the same cholesterol precursor. Cortisol spikes predict T drops 0.5–3 days later (Source: [[Cortisol and Testosterone Suppression]]). Evidence: **medium–high**.

### 5. Endocrine-disrupting chemical exposure (plastics, phthalates, BPA)
- Urinary phthalate and BPA metabolites are **negatively associated** with total, free, and bioavailable T, plus FSH/LH (Source: [[Endocrine Disruptors and Testosterone]])
- Phthalates directly impair Leydig- and Sertoli-cell function; DEHP inhibits T production
- Combined BPA + phthalate exposure is worse than either alone; one estimate attributes ~10,700 US male deaths/year to phthalate-driven T loss (Source: [[Endocrine Disruptors and Testosterone]])
- Practical: avoid heating food in plastic, cut plastic food contact, choose fragrance-free personal-care products
- Evidence: **medium** (strong associational + mechanistic; human RCTs impossible)

### 6. Very-low-fat dieting
Cutting dietary fat too far starves the cholesterol→T pathway: low-fat diets lower total T (SMD −0.38) and free T (−0.37) (Source: [[Low-Fat Diet Testosterone Meta-analysis 2021]]). Evidence: **medium**.

### 7. Chronic high-volume endurance overtraining without fueling
The "exercise-hypogonadal male condition": persistently low free/total T in heavily endurance-trained men, without compensatory LH rise. Driven by **low energy availability** + chronically elevated cortisol (Source: [[Exercise and Testosterone Meta-analyses]]). Fix = correct training load, fuel, and sleep before anything else. Evidence: **medium**.

---

## C-Tier — real but smaller / mixed

### 8. Smoking & nicotine
**Mixed/counterintuitive.** Large cross-sections find smokers have *slightly higher* measured T, but smoking damages overall reproductive health, sperm quality, and long-run T via systemic harm. T drops briefly after quitting, then rebounds within a year (Source: [[Smoking Cannabis and Testosterone]]). Net: still avoid. Evidence: **low–medium (conflicting)**.

### 9. Cannabis
Conflicting data; several studies show a slight T decrease, and a study of >2,000 infertile men found cannabis lowered plasma T. Also raises testicular oxidative stress and harms sperm (Source: [[Smoking Cannabis and Testosterone]]). Evidence: **low–medium**.

## Quick Avoid List (worst → least)

| Rank | Habit | Typical hit | Evidence |
|------|-------|-------------|----------|
| F | Excess body fat / obesity | large, self-reinforcing | High |
| F | Chronic short sleep / apnea | −10–15% | High |
| F | Heavy/chronic alcohol | −7% to −50% | High |
| D | Chronic stress (cortisol) | meaningful, delayed | Med–High |
| D | Plastics/phthalates/BPA | meaningful, cumulative | Med |
| D | Very-low-fat dieting | SMD −0.38 total T | Med |
| D | Endurance overtraining + underfueling | meaningful in athletes | Med |
| C | Smoking/nicotine | mixed; net harmful | Low–Med |
| C | Cannabis | slight decrease | Low–Med |

> [!note] Bottom line
> The biggest wins are **subtractive**: lose fat, sleep enough, drink little, manage stress. Get those right before worrying about boosters or supplements.
