# Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol

The most-studied HIIT protocol for raising VO2 max: **four 4-minute work intervals at 85–95% of max heart rate, each separated by 3 minutes of active recovery**, bookended by a warm-up and cool-down. Developed at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) and validated in dozens of trials by [[Jan Helgerud]], Jan Hoff, and [[Ulrik Wisløff]]'s Cardiac Exercise Research Group (CERG).

This is the masterclass hub. Modality-specific execution lives in [[4x4 Intervals for Running]] and [[4x4 Intervals for Rowing]].

## The Protocol (one session ≈ 35–40 min)

| Segment | Duration | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 6–10 min | Easy → build to ~70% HRmax |
| Interval 1 | 4 min | 85–95% HRmax (target ~88–90% first rep) |
| Recovery | 3 min | Active, ~60–70% HRmax |
| Interval 2 | 4 min | 90–93% HRmax |
| Recovery | 3 min | Active |
| Interval 3 | 4 min | 90–94% HRmax |
| Recovery | 3 min | Active |
| Interval 4 | 4 min | 93–95% HRmax (highest) |
| Cool-down | 3–5 min | Easy, gradual slowdown |

- **Work:** 16 min total of hard effort. Heavy breathing; cannot speak in full sentences after ~1 min. RPE 8–9/10.
- **Recovery:** active, not stopped. Easy enough to breathe normally, not so easy that ramping back up is hard. (Source: [[Myworkout — The Norwegian 4x4 Protocol]])
- **Frequency:** 2×/week is the sweet spot; 1×/week maintains fitness; 3×/week is the ceiling for already-fit athletes. Cap at 2 for most — it is demanding and over-frequency causes overreaching with no added benefit. (Source: [[Myworkout — The Norwegian 4x4 Protocol]])

## Why 4 Minutes × 4 (the design logic)

The gain driver is **time spent above ~90% VO2 max**, not raw speed or total duration. Four-minute intervals are long enough that, after the ~1–1.5 min it takes HR to climb into the target band, you bank 2.5–3.5 min of genuine high-intensity stimulus per rep. (Source: [[Edwardsen 2020 — Heart Rate Response to 4x4 Protocol]])

> [!note] Long beats short for aerobic stimulus
> In trained middle-distance runners, 4 × ~3–4 min "long" intervals produced ~5.5 min above 90% VO2 max vs only ~3.4 min for 24 × 30 s "short" intervals — long intervals were significantly superior even when short-interval speed was raised 5%. Interval *organization* matters more than maximizing speed. (Source: [[Long vs Short Intervals — Time Above 90% VO2max 2025]])

This is the practical counterpoint to the short-interval (30/15) variant noted in [[VO2 Max Interval Training]]: for the 4x4 specifically, the 4-minute length is the point.

## Heart-Rate Reality (what actually happens)

Lab telemetry of the 4x4 shows the prescription is approximate, not literal: (Source: [[Edwardsen 2020 — Heart Rate Response to 4x4 Protocol]])

- HR climbs across reps — average ~160 → 169 b/min, peaking at **~93% HRmax (interval 4)**, not a flat 95%.
- It takes **~1.3 min to reach 85% HRmax in interval 1** because of the warm-up→work gap.
- ~12.9 of 16 work minutes (≈80%) land in the 85–95% band; interval 1 contributes the least.
- HR drop during the 3-min recovery varies 10–35 b/min by fitness — fitter athletes drop more and must start each rep harder.

**Implication:** don't sandbag the first interval waiting to "feel" 95%. Start interval 1 firm (~85–90% effort) so HR is already climbing, then nudge intensity up each rep.

## Evidence Base

- **VO2 max:** ~7–13% gains in 8 weeks, roughly **2× the improvement of moderate continuous training (MICT, ~4–6%)** at matched or lower volume. Replicated in moderately active people, obese participants (+10% in 6 wk), and coronary-artery-disease patients. (Source: [[Myworkout — The Norwegian 4x4 Protocol]])
- **Longevity:** in NTNU's 5-year [[Generation 100 Study — HIIT and Mortality 2020]] (1,567 adults aged 70–77), the HIIT arm showed a 2.9-percentage-point absolute reduction in all-cause mortality vs MICT, plus larger VO2 max gains. VO2 max is among the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality.
- Also improves stroke volume and endothelial function. (Source: [[Myworkout — The Norwegian 4x4 Protocol]])

## Universal Tips

1. **Pace for the 4th rep, not the 1st.** Going 95% on rep 1 wrecks the session; target ~88–90% rep 1, climb 1–2% each rep. If you can't hold the same output across all four, you started too hard. (Source: [[Norwegian 4x4 — PureGym Practical Guide]])
2. **Keep recovery active.** Easy jog/spin/row — don't stop or stretch between reps; full rest makes the restart harder and drops time-in-zone.
3. **Never skip the warm-up.** ~1.3 min of every rep is spent just getting HR up; a 6–10 min warm-up shrinks that lag and cuts injury risk.
4. **Use HR as a lagging gauge, RPE/breathing as the live one.** No monitor? "Hard but sustainable, can't talk in sentences, could not keep this up much past 4 min."
5. **Build a base first.** 4x4 on a poor aerobic base is inefficient and injury-prone — pair it with mostly easy volume ([[Polarized Training]]).
6. **Recover.** 36–48 h between sessions; back off if resting HR is >5 bpm above baseline.
