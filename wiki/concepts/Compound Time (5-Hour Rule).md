# Compound Time (5-Hour Rule)

**Compound time** is time spent on activities with long-term, accruing payoffs in knowledge, skill, relationships, or owned assets — as opposed to **linear time** spent on urgent tasks that pay once and vanish. The label is Michael Simmons'; the underlying idea is compound interest applied to attention. (Source: [[Compound Time — Michael Simmons]])

## The 5-hour rule

Invest **~1 hour per day (5 hours/week)** in deliberate learning and reflection, even when busy. Simmons observes that high performers — despite *more* responsibility — protect time to step away from urgent work and invest in long-payoff activities.

## The deliberate-practice math

The reason a daily hour matters is linear input → compounding output:

- **15 min/day = 62.5 hr/year = ~312 hr over 5 years** — more focused skill-building than most professionals do in an entire post-degree career. (Source: [[Compound Time — Michael Simmons]])
- Anders Ericsson's expertise research: what separates elite from competent is not talent but accumulated **deliberate practice** — focused work at the edge of current ability, *with feedback*. (Connects to [[Goldilocks Rule]]: practice at the edge of ability is where motivation and growth both peak.)

## Six compounding activities

1. **Journaling** (reflection prompts)
2. **Napping** (a 1–1.5 hr nap ≈ learning benefit of a full night, per the cited research)
3. **Walking** (refreshes mind, increases creativity)
4. **Reading** (Buffett: ~80% of his career spent reading)
5. **Conversation** (creative partnerships → breakthroughs: Jobs/Wozniak, Kahneman/Tversky)
6. **Experimentation** (Bezos: success = "how many experiments we do per year, per month, per week, per day")

## Why it matters for daily projects

Compound time is the *engine* under [[Compounding Daily Projects Tier List]]. A daily project only compounds if the daily hour is spent on the asset (the repo, the writing, the skill) rather than reactive busywork. The discipline is **ruthlessly removing busywork** to protect the compounding hour.

> [!gap] The cited nap/learning equivalence ("1.5 hr nap ≈ 8 hr sleep for learning") is from a popular-science article, not a primary study — treat as low confidence and a motivational framing, not a literal protocol.
