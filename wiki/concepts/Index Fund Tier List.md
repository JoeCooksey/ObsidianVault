# Index Fund Tier List

> Ranked by suitability as a **long-term, buy-and-hold core holding** for an accumulating investor. Lower tiers are not "bad products" — they are products whose job is narrower than people think.

## S-Tier — Own the Market, Pay Nothing

The whole-portfolio core. Broad, cap-weighted, expense ratios ≤0.04%. Performance differences between these are noise — pick whichever your brokerage makes convenient (Source: [[Index Fund Comparison 2026 (FinanceWonk)]]).

| Fund | Ticker | ER | Tracks |
|------|--------|-----|--------|
| Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF | VTI | 0.03% | Total US (~3,600 stocks) |
| Fidelity Total Market Index | FSKAX | 0.015% | Total US (~3,800) |
| Vanguard S&P 500 ETF | VOO | 0.03% | S&P 500 |
| Fidelity 500 Index | FXAIX | 0.015% | S&P 500 |
| Schwab S&P 500 Index | SWPPX | 0.02% | S&P 500 |
| Vanguard Total World ETF | VT / VTWAX | 0.07% / 0.10% | Global (~9,000) |

- Total-market vs S&P 500: VTI adds the ~15–20% of US market cap outside the 500 largest; 10-year returns are nearly identical (FXAIX 15.34%/yr vs VOO 15.13%/yr) (confidence: high)
- VT is the one-decision option: global cap weighting, auto-rebalancing.

## A-Tier — Excellent, One Caveat Each

- **VXUS / VTIAX / FZILX** (international, 0.00–0.12%) — essential complement to a US-only core, not a standalone core. 63% of active international funds still lose to their index (Source: [[SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index]]).
- **FZROX / FZILX** (Fidelity ZERO, 0.00%) — genuinely free, but mutual-fund-only and **not portable**: leaving Fidelity means selling (taxable event in a brokerage account). Perfect inside a Fidelity Roth IRA. (confidence: medium — portability caveat is well-established but was not in fetched sources)
- **BND / total bond index** — correct tool for its job; the job barely exists for a 19-year-old accumulator.
- **Target-date *index* funds** — S-tier behavior insurance inside a 401k/IRA; A-tier only because of slightly higher ER (~0.08–0.15%) and clumsiness in taxable accounts.

## B-Tier — Legitimate Satellites, Not Cores

- **QQQ / QQQM** (0.20%) — Nasdaq-100 mega-cap growth tilt. Strong record, but it is a concentrated sector bet wearing an index costume; 100 stocks, tech-dominated.
- **SCHD** (0.06%) — quality-dividend screen. "Not a substitute for VTI or VOO — a quality-income satellite position" (Source: [[Index Fund Comparison 2026 (FinanceWonk)]]).
- **Small-cap value factor funds (AVUV-style)** — the one corner where active/factor odds are least bad: 70% of active small-cap funds beat the SmallCap 600 in 2024 (Source: [[SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index]]). Still a tilt, not a core.

## C-Tier — Mostly Unnecessary

- **Sector ETFs (XLK, XLE, SOXX…)** — you are now stock-picking at the sector level; same forecasting problem, fewer companies.
- **High-yield dividend funds** — yield-chasing trades total return for the feeling of income.
- **Equal-weight S&P (RSP)** — a small-cap/value tilt with a 0.20% ER; defensible, rarely needed.

## D-Tier — Cautionary Tales

- **Thematic ETFs (ARKK and kin)** — story-driven concentration. ARKK's 5-year annualized return as of Feb 2026: **−14.67%** while the broad market roughly doubled; every ARK fund trails the S&P 500 on 3- and 5-year windows (confidence: high, multiple sources). Themes are real; the funds buy them after they're priced in.
- **High-fee "closet index" active funds** (0.5–1.5% ER) — index returns minus a wealth tax. The fee table in [[Index Fund Investing]] shows 1.5% vs 0.03% costs ~$690K on $100K over 30 years.

## F-Tier — Wrong Tool for Holding

- **Leveraged / inverse ETFs (TQQQ, SQQQ, SOXL…)** — daily-reset instruments. Volatility decay means TQQQ's 5-year CAGR was 22.9% vs QQQ's 16.1% — nowhere near 3×, with 80–90% drawdowns possible and 0.8%+ fees (confidence: high). Trading tools, not investments.
- **Covered-call income ETFs for accumulators** — sell upside to manufacture "yield"; the opposite of what compounding needs.

> [!summary] One-line version
> Buy S-tier, add A-tier international, allow yourself one B-tier satellite if you must, and treat C–F as entertainment budget.
