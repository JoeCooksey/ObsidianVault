# Getting Your Heart Rate Up on the Rower

The single most common rowing-erg complaint: *"I'm rowing hard but my heart rate won't climb."* For the [[Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol]] this matters — the work intervals only count if you reach **85–95% HRmax**. Here's why HR stays low on the erg and how to fix it.

## First: a low HR on the erg is partly normal
At the **same internal intensity**, rowing HR runs **~4–6 bpm below running** HR — e.g. 170 vs 177 bpm at the same blood lactate, 194 vs 198 at max. Cause: the seated position plus the large muscle mass recruited improve venous return → bigger stroke volume → the heart moves the same blood at fewer beats. (Source: [[Yoshiga 2002 — HR Lower in Rowing than Running]])

> [!tip] Don't chase your *running* HR on the erg. A correctly-worked rowing 4x4 may peak a few bpm lower. Pace by **power/split** first, treat HR as a secondary check.

So part of your "low HR" is real physiology. The *rest* is usually one of the fixable causes below.

## The #1 fix: drive with your legs, not your arms
The erg measures **work per stroke = length × force**, and there's a big "dead" portion of each stroke cycle (the recovery) where you do almost nothing. So to load the heart you must make the **drive** genuinely powerful. Power distribution should be roughly **60% legs / 30% trunk / 10% arms**.

- **Sequence (drive):** legs → back swing → arms. **Recovery:** arms away → body over → knees bend. Initiate every stroke by *pressing the footplate away* with the legs, arms straight until legs are nearly extended.
- **Arms-first rowing is the classic HR-killer** — it's inefficient, fatigues the small muscles, and never loads the big leg muscles that drive HR up.
- Cue: think "explosive leg press / squat-jump punch" into full extension on each drive.

## Power per stroke beats stroke rate
A high stroke rate with a weak pull feels busy but does little work — HR stays flat while you flail.

- If your **split stays the same (or slower) while your stroke rate climbs**, that's a technique/power problem, not fitness. Add force to the drive, don't just row faster.
- Target a strong drive at a moderate rate: **~28–32 spm** for 4x4 work intervals, with real power behind each stroke. (See [[4x4 Intervals for Rowing]].)

## Set the drag factor correctly
- **Drag factor ~120–130 (damper ~3–5)** simulates on-water rowing and lets you hold 28–32 spm with a long, powerful stroke.
- **Higher is not harder** — a high damper just slows the recovery and rewards short, grindy strokes; it teaches bad technique and can actually *lower* sustained work output. If you can't comfortably hold 28–32 spm, drag is too high.

## Pace it hard enough — and give HR time to respond
- **Pace by watts / 500m split**, the objective targets: VO2-max work ≈ **2K pace + ~2–5 s/500m**. If your HR won't reach zone, your split is probably too easy — pick up the power.
- **HR lags effort by 60–90 s.** Commit to a hard pace from the first stroke of each interval; don't ease in waiting for the number to rise, or the 4-min rep is half over before you're in zone. (This is also why interval 1 of a 4x4 always reads lowest.)
- **Don't quit a rep** when HR looks low early — it's still climbing. Hold the power and it arrives.

## Other things that suppress HR
- **Death-grip on the handle** → forearm/shoulder fatigue that caps your drive. Hands are hooks, not clamps; relaxed fingers, neutral wrists.
- **Rushing the recovery** (lunging to the catch) shortens the stroke and burns you out without raising sustained output — the recovery should take *longer* than the drive.
- **Under-fueling / dehydration / heat** flatten HR response; **caffeine, hard prior days, or poor sleep** shift it. Compare sessions, not single days.

## Quick troubleshooting checklist
1. Drag factor 120–130? (not maxed out)
2. Driving legs-first, 60/30/10? (not arm-pulling)
3. Split at 2K + 2–5 s/500m? (hard enough)
4. Holding 28–32 spm with *power*, not just rate?
5. Committed to pace from stroke 1, giving HR 60–90 s to climb?
6. Still low? It may simply be normal rower-vs-runner offset — trust the **split/watts**.
