# Arthroscopic Bankart Repair

Keyhole surgery that **re-attaches the torn labrum and capsule** to the glenoid rim using **suture anchors**, restoring the front "doorstop." It is the most common stabilization procedure for anterior instability with a soft-tissue [[Bankart Lesion]] and minimal bone loss.

## How well it works
| Comparison | Recurrence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| vs conservative (first-time, young/active) | **7.5% vs 53%** | [[Hu et al 2023 — Bankart vs Conservative First-Time Dislocation Meta-analysis]] |
| vs conservative (first-time, sports) | **9.7% vs 67%** | [[Hurley et al 2020 — Bankart vs Conservative Meta-analysis]] |
| long-term (mean 13 yrs) | **~35% recurrence** (drifts up over time) | [[Delgado et al 2025 — Bankart vs Latarjet Long-Term Follow-up]] |

Short/medium-term it dramatically beats not operating; over 10–15 years the recurrence creeps up, especially in high-risk patients — which is the rationale for choosing [[Latarjet Procedure]] in those cases.

## Who it suits
- Recurrent anterior instability with a repairable labral tear and **little/no glenoid bone loss**.
- First-time dislocators who are **young, athletic, in collision/overhead sports** benefit most from doing it early (lowest recurrence, best return to play).

## Recovery (typical)
- Sling ~3–6 weeks; protected motion, then progressive strengthening.
- Return to contact sport commonly **~4–6 months**, guided by strength/criteria.

## Limitations
- Does **not** fix significant bone loss — a large bony Bankart or off-track Hill-Sachs raises failure.
- Recurrence rises with younger age, hyperlaxity, more pre-op dislocations, and contact sport. (Source: [[Shoulder Instability Recurrence Risk Factors]])

> [!note] "Arthroscopic stabilization" beats nonsurgical care for first-time dislocators regardless of whether the nonsurgical arm uses internal- or external-rotation bracing — operative recurrence ~8.8% vs ~21% for external-rotation immobilization.
