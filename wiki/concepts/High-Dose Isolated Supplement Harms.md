# High-Dose Isolated Supplement Harms

A key asymmetry in the whole-foods-vs-supplements comparison: **you essentially cannot overdose on a nutrient by eating whole food, but you can with isolated high-dose supplements — sometimes fatally.** Isolation strips the self-limiting context (volume, fiber, satiety, the [[Food Matrix Effect]]) that caps intake from food.

## The landmark harm signals

### β-carotene → lung cancer in smokers
- **ATBC trial** (29,133 Finnish male smokers): 20 mg/day β-carotene *increased* lung cancer risk ~18%. (Source: [[Beta-Carotene and Vitamin E Supplement Harm (ATBC, CARET)]])
- **CARET trial** (β-carotene + retinol): stopped 21 months early — **46% more lung cancer deaths** in the supplement group. NCI halted the $42M trial.
- The same β-carotene from carrots and sweet potatoes shows no such harm — dose and context differ entirely.

### Vitamin E and mortality
- Meta-analysis: vitamin E above the RDA (>15 mg) and β-carotene above 9.6 mg **significantly increased all-cause mortality.** (Source: [[Beta-Carotene and Vitamin E Supplement Harm (ATBC, CARET)]])

### Calcium supplements → cancer mortality
- Tufts/Annals 2019: calcium *supplements* ≥1,000 mg/day were associated with **increased cancer death** — while calcium from food was not. (Source: [[Dietary Supplements, Nutrient Intake and Mortality (Annals 2019)]])

## The pattern

> [!warning] "More" is not "better" past adequacy
> For fat-soluble vitamins and minerals especially, the dose-response often turns **U-shaped or harmful** at supplemental megadoses. Whole foods sit on the safe part of the curve by design — it is physically hard to eat 20 mg of β-carotene as isolated molecules, but trivial to swallow it.

## Implication for the diet comparison

This is one of the strongest points *against* a supplement-heavy strategy and *for* whole foods: the whole-foods diet has a built-in safety ceiling. A supplement-only or supplement-heavy approach removes that ceiling and introduces overdose and adverse-outcome risk that food does not carry.
