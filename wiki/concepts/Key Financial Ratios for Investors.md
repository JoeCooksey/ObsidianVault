# Key Financial Ratios for Investors

15 ratios every stock investor must know. Organized by category: profitability, efficiency, liquidity, leverage, valuation.

**Rule:** Ratios are comparative tools — they only mean something relative to (1) historical trend for this company, (2) industry average, (3) best-in-class competitors. A 20% ROE in banking is excellent; in software it's mediocre.

---

## Category 1: Profitability Ratios

These measure how efficiently a company converts revenue into profit.

### 1. Gross Margin
**Formula:** (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue × 100
**What it measures:** Pricing power and cost structure efficiency
**Good benchmark:** Depends heavily on industry
- Software/SaaS: 70–90% (excellent)
- Retail: 25–40%
- Manufacturing: 20–40%
- Restaurants: 15–25%

**What to watch:** Trend matters more than level. Expanding gross margin = pricing power growing. Contracting = competition intensifying or input cost inflation.

### 2. Operating Margin (EBIT Margin)
**Formula:** EBIT / Revenue × 100
**What it measures:** Core business profitability before interest and taxes
**Why it's better than net margin for comparison:** Removes the effect of capital structure (debt levels) and tax strategies — makes cross-company comparison cleaner.
**Good benchmark:**
- Tech: 20–35%
- Industrial: 10–20%
- Retail: 3–8%

### 3. Net Profit Margin
**Formula:** Net Income / Revenue × 100
**What it measures:** Bottom-line profitability after everything
**Limitation:** Can be distorted by one-time items, tax rates, and debt levels

### 4. Return on Equity (ROE)
**Formula:** Net Income / Shareholders' Equity × 100
**What it measures:** How much profit per dollar of shareholder capital
**Good benchmark:** >15% sustained = sign of competitive advantage
**Critical limitation:** ROE can be inflated by high debt (leverage shrinks the equity base). Always compare to ROE at low-debt peers or use ROIC instead.

### 5. Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
**Formula:** NOPAT / Invested Capital
- NOPAT = Net Operating Profit After Tax = EBIT × (1 − tax rate)
- Invested Capital = Total Equity + Total Debt − Excess Cash

**Why ROIC > ROE for investors:**
- Cannot be gamed by leverage
- Measures return on ALL capital (debt + equity)
- Directly comparable across capital structures

**The key question:** Is ROIC > WACC (cost of capital)? If ROIC > WACC → company creates shareholder value. If ROIC < WACC → company destroys value even while reporting profits.

**Good benchmark:** >10% = decent; >15% = excellent; >20% = wide moat territory

### 6. Return on Assets (ROA)
**Formula:** Net Income / Total Assets × 100
**What it measures:** How efficiently the company uses its asset base
**Good for:** Comparing capital-intensive businesses (banks, manufacturers)
**Good benchmark:** >5% is decent; >10% is excellent for most industries

## Category 2: Efficiency Ratios

How well does the company manage its operations and working capital?

### 7. Asset Turnover
**Formula:** Revenue / Total Assets
**What it measures:** How much revenue per dollar of assets
**Asset-light companies** (software, finance) will have high turnover; **Asset-heavy** (manufacturing, utilities) will be low. Compare within industry.

### 8. Inventory Turnover
**Formula:** COGS / Average Inventory
**What it measures:** How quickly inventory sells
**Low turnover** = inventory sitting — potential demand problem or obsolescence risk
**High turnover** = efficient inventory management (Costco/Amazon optimize this)

### 9. Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)
**Formula:** (Accounts Receivable / Revenue) × 365
**What it measures:** Average number of days to collect payment from customers
**Rising DSO** = customers are taking longer to pay (or revenue is being recognized before cash arrives — accounting risk)
**Good benchmark:** DSO < 45 days for most businesses

## Category 3: Liquidity Ratios

Can the company meet its short-term obligations?

### 10. Current Ratio
**Formula:** Current Assets / Current Liabilities
**What it measures:** Ability to cover short-term debts with short-term assets
**Good benchmark:** ≥ 1.5 (has 50% buffer above obligations)
**Warning zone:** < 1.0 (current obligations exceed current assets — near-term liquidity risk)

### 11. Quick Ratio (Acid Test)
**Formula:** (Cash + Short-term Investments + Accounts Receivable) / Current Liabilities
**What it measures:** Stricter than current ratio — excludes inventory (which may not be quickly convertible to cash)
**Good benchmark:** ≥ 1.0

## Category 4: Leverage / Solvency Ratios

How much debt does the company carry? Can it service it?

### 12. Debt-to-Equity (D/E) Ratio
**Formula:** Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity
**What it measures:** How leveraged is the company
**Good benchmark:**
- <1.0 = conservative
- 1.0–2.0 = moderate leverage (normal for industrials, real estate)
- >3.0 = heavily leveraged — higher risk in recession
**Context:** Some industries structurally carry more debt (utilities, banks, real estate) — always compare to industry norms

### 13. Debt / EBITDA
**Formula:** Total Debt / EBITDA
**What it measures:** Years of operating profit needed to pay off all debt
**Lender threshold:** >4–5× is considered high leverage; >6× is distress territory
**Good benchmark:** <3× for most operating companies

### 14. Interest Coverage Ratio
**Formula:** EBIT / Interest Expense
**What it measures:** How many times over can the company pay its interest?
**Good benchmark:** >3× = comfortable; <1.5× = risk of default
**Warning:** Companies report adjusted EBITDA to lenders — look at actual EBIT

## Category 5: Growth Rates

Not ratios per se, but the metrics you track over 3–5 years.

### 15. Revenue CAGR + EPS CAGR + FCF CAGR
**Formula:** (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1/years) − 1

Track all three together:
- **Revenue growth** without profit growth = top-line story only
- **EPS growth** without FCF growth = accounting-driven, not real
- **FCF growth** = the real story; the most reliable growth metric

| Growth Rate | Classification |
|---|---|
| FCF CAGR > 20% | High-growth company |
| FCF CAGR 10–20% | Good compounder |
| FCF CAGR 5–10% | Steady earner |
| FCF CAGR < 5% | Slow growth / mature |
