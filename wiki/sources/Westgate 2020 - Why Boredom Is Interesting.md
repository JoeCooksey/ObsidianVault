# Westgate 2020 — Why Boredom Is Interesting

*Current Directions in Psychological Science*, peer-reviewed review article by [[Erin Westgate]] (University of Florida). Summarizes the [[MAC Model of Boredom]] (Westgate & Wilson 2018, *Psychological Review*). Read in full (PDF) 2026-06-11.

## What it contributes

The theoretical backbone for the whole "what to do when bored" question. Boredom is not bad or good — it is a **signal**, like pain: "an affective indicator of unsuccessful attentional engagement in valued goal-congruent activities."

## Key claims

- Boredom has two independent causes: **attention deficits** and **meaning deficits**. Either alone is sufficient. Evidence: 14 studies, 1,364 participants, plus experimental manipulation of each. (confidence: high)
- **Attentional boredom is U-shaped in difficulty**: tasks that are too easy *or* too hard both produce boredom, because both break sustained attention. Frustration, by contrast, rises linearly with difficulty. This is the research basis behind the [[Goldilocks Rule]]. (confidence: high)
- **Meaningless boredom** occurs when the activity doesn't connect to currently active goals — regardless of how objectively meaningful it is. Marked by high arousal, restlessness, and the urge to disengage. (confidence: high)
- Boredom **increases reward sensitivity and impulsivity** (Milyavskaya et al. 2019) — which is exactly why bored people grab the nearest dopamine hit. Links to [[Dopamine Baseline and the Pleasure-Pain Balance]]. (confidence: high)
- Bored people will do almost anything for stimulation: >90% of participants forced to watch an 85-second tennis clip on repeat for an hour chose to **self-administer electric shocks** — an average of 22 times (Havermans et al. 2015). (confidence: high)

## The four regulation routes

1. **Regulate cognitive demands** — make the task harder (time limits, gamification) or easier (chunking, removing distractions)
2. **Regulate cognitive resources** — short-term: sleep, caffeine; long-term: build skill so harder things become engaging
3. **Regulate goal value** — reframe the activity to connect it to goals you care about (utility-value interventions)
4. **Switch activities** — but choose wisely: see [[Interest vs Enjoyment]]

## Bottom line

"Boredom is a signal: it is what we do with that signal that counts. Stifling boredom may work, temporarily, to feel better, but the only lasting solution is to solve the underlying attention and meaning deficits."
