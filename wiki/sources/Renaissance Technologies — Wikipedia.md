# Renaissance Technologies — Wikipedia

## What this source contributes

Primary reference for the verified track record and structure of the most successful quant fund in history. Used to ground claims about the Medallion Fund's returns, fee structure, and the firm's strategy.

## Key Claims

- The **Medallion Fund** averaged **~71.8% annual returns before fees** (≈39% after fees) from 1994 through mid-2014. (confidence: high)
- Fee structure is **5% management + 44% performance** — far above the standard 2-and-20. (confidence: high)
- Medallion gained **98.2% in 2008** while the S&P 500 lost 38.5%; **76% in 2020**. Worst year 2001–2013 was a 21% gain after fees. (confidence: high)
- Founded **1978 as Monemetrics**, renamed Renaissance Technologies **1982**, by **James (Jim) Simons** (mathematician, Cold War codebreaker) with co-founder Howard L. Morgan. (confidence: high)
- Current CEO **Peter Fitzhugh Brown** (joined 1993 from IBM); **Robert Mercer** was co-CEO; **Elwyn Berlekamp** ran the fund 1989–1990; early recruits included cryptanalyst **Leonard Baum** and algebraist **James Ax**. (confidence: high)
- AUM **~$130B (April 2021)**, up to ~$165B with leverage. (confidence: medium — figure drifts year to year)
- **Medallion is closed to outsiders since 1993** — employees and families only. The public **RIEF** (Renaissance Institutional Equities Fund, 2005) trails Medallion by ~17–19%. (confidence: high)
- **2021 IRS settlement**: executives agreed to pay up to **$7B** over a basket-options strategy that converted short-term trading gains into lower-taxed long-term capital gains — one of the largest tax settlements in US history. (confidence: high)

## Notes

The Medallion after-fee returns are extraordinary precisely *because* the fee is so high — the firm keeps Medallion small (capped ~$10B) to avoid eroding its edge, which is why it bought out all outside investors. The public RIEF demonstrates the edge does not scale to large, liquid, long-hold strategies.
