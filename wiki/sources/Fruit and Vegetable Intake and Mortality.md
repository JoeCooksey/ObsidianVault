# Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality (Dose-Response Meta-Analyses)

## What It Is
Two large systematic reviews + dose-response meta-analyses of prospective cohort studies — the population evidence that high vegetable/fruit intake tracks with **lower** mortality. This is the largest body of evidence the anti-vegetable case has to overcome.

## Key Findings
- **Wang 2014 (BMJ):** each additional daily serving of fruit & vegetables → **hazard ratio 0.95** for all-cause mortality (5% lower risk); CVD mortality HR 0.96 per serving. **Threshold ~5 servings/day**, after which all-cause mortality didn't fall further. Cancer mortality: not appreciably associated.
- **Aune 2017 (Int J Epidemiol):** summary relative risk **0.90 per 200 g/day** for all-cause mortality; clear dose-response with risk reductions out to **~800 g/day** for most outcomes (~600 g/day for cancer). ~8% lower CVD risk per 200 g/day.

## What It Contributes
Directly counters "vegetables destroy health/metabolism." Across hundreds of thousands of people, **more** vegetable intake associates with **less** death, cardiovascular disease, and (the conditions of impaired metabolism) — not more. No comparable body of evidence shows harm from vegetables.

## Limits / Confidence
- **Observational** — association, not proven causation; residual confounding (people who eat more veg differ in many ways) is the standard caveat.
- The consistency, dose-response gradient, and scale make confounding-only explanations unlikely.
- Confidence: **high** for the direction of the association.
