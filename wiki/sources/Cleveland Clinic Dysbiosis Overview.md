# Cleveland Clinic Dysbiosis Overview

Cleveland Clinic's patient-facing clinical reference on **dysbiosis** — the medical term for a microbial imbalance in which harmful microbes multiply faster than beneficial ones can keep them in check.

## What it contributes

- **Definition of healthy:** a balanced microbiome has "a healthy variety of microorganisms, where no single bacteria, virus or fungus dominates" — diversity itself is the health marker.
- **Symptom map by site:** digestive (bloating, gas, diarrhea, constipation, stool changes), oral (bleeding gums, tooth decay), skin (acne, atopic dermatitis), urogenital (painful urination, discharge, itching).
- **Systemic associations** (weaker evidence, flagged as "potentially linked"): mood changes, weight fluctuations, chronic fatigue, malabsorption, food intolerances.
- **Causes:** antibiotics/antimicrobials, other medications, smoking, alcohol, environmental toxins, physical and psychological stress, chronic disease, poor diet — consistent with [[Gut-Harmful Foods and Actions Tier List]].

## Caveats

- Patient-education page, not a systematic review; systemic claims are associative.
- Dysbiosis has no agreed diagnostic threshold — it is a pattern, not a lab value (matches the sequencing caveat in [[Gut Health Tests]]).
