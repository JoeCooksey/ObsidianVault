# Bioavailability of Micronutrients from Whole Foods (Frontiers 2020)

Peer-reviewed review in *Frontiers in Nutrition* (zooming in on dairy, vegetables, fruits). Confidence: **high** (peer-reviewed mechanistic review). The foundation for [[Food Matrix Effect]].

## Core thesis
The **food matrix** — nutrients trapped in cells, bound to proteins, packaged with fiber/fat/phytochemicals — determines real-world absorption. "The sum of the parts does not necessarily explain the result of the whole."

## Specifics (mechanisms + numbers)
- **β-carotene:** chloroplast-bound in leafy greens → only **5–10%** bioavailable; raw-vegetable bioefficacy 0.01–7.7% vs **26–48%** for supplemental β-carotene in oil. (One case where the supplement is *more* bioavailable.)
- **Iron + vitamin C:** vitamin C reduces ferric→ferrous iron, boosting non-heme iron uptake. Lactic fermentation **doubled** iron absorption.
- **Calcium in dairy:** casein phosphopeptides protect calcium from precipitation; lactose acts prebiotically.
- **B12:** ~**65%** absorbed from milk in older adults vs ≤50% from other animal foods and **<5% from synthetic supplements.**
- **Folate:** natural polyglutamated folate ~70% bioavailable; 5-MTHF is the best natural form (folic acid supplement is a stable monoglutamate).
- **Antinutrients:** oxalates/phytates bind minerals (spinach Mg poorly absorbed vs kale).

## What it contributes
Provides the **mechanistic balance**: whole foods win on synergy/co-factors, supplements win on removing inhibitors and dose precision — but only food carries the full bioactive package. Nuances the "food always wins" claim while still supporting it overall.
