# Effective Management of Flatulence (AAFP, 2009)

Clinical review in *American Family Physician*. ([source](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2009/0615/p1098.html))

The practical clinical companion to the Suarez/Levitt mechanism papers. Separates **odor** treatments from **volume** treatments.

## Treatment Evidence (with strength-of-recommendation, SOR)

| Treatment | Dose | Target | Effect | SOR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bismuth subsalicylate** | 524 mg 4×/day | Odor | Binds >95% sulfide gases — very effective | C |
| **Charcoal garments (briefs)** | — | Odor | ~100% sulfur-gas absorption | — |
| Charcoal pads (underwear) | — | Odor | 55–77% | — |
| Charcoal seat cushions | — | Odor | ~20% | — |
| **Oral activated charcoal** | — | Odor/volume | **No benefit** | B |
| **Alpha-galactosidase (Beano)** | 300–1,200 GalU | Volume | Significant symptom improvement | B |
| **Simethicone** | — | Volume | **No benefit** for common flatulence | B |
| Probiotics (VSL#3) | — | Volume | 52% vs 33% relief at 4 wk (NNT 6.4) | B |
| Rifaximin (Xifaxan) | 400 mg 2×/day | Volume | 41% vs 23% relief (NNT 5.4) | B |

## Contribution to the Topic
Confirms the **odor vs volume split** clinically and ranks interventions. Key practical points:
- For **smell**: bismuth subsalicylate (episodic) + external charcoal garments.
- For **volume/bloating**: alpha-galactosidase (gas from beans/bran), low-FODMAP diet, rifaximin or probiotics if SIBO/IBS-driven.
- **Oral charcoal and simethicone are popular but not evidence-supported** for this purpose.
