# Bismuth Subsalicylate Markedly Decreases Hydrogen Sulfide Release

**Suarez FL, Furne JK, Springfield J, Levitt MD. _Gastroenterology_ 1998;114(5):923–9.** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0016-5085(98)70311-7), PMID 9558280) — *According to PubMed.*

The primary evidence behind "take Pepto-Bismol to make gas smell less."

## Method
Fecal homogenates (human + rat) incubated ± bismuth subsalicylate, gas measured. Then **10 healthy subjects** gave fecal samples before and after taking **bismuth subsalicylate 524 mg four times daily for 3–7 days**.

## Key Findings
- Fecal homogenates showed a **dose-dependent** drop in H₂S release as bismuth concentration rose.
- In vivo, treatment produced a **>95% reduction in fecal H₂S release**. (Source: this paper)
- Mechanism: bismuth binds sulfide, forming insoluble, non-volatile **bismuth sulfide** (the same chemistry that blackens stool/tongue).

## Contribution to the Topic
Gives the single most effective *odor-specific* intervention an evidence base. Because it acts on H₂S (the dominant malodorant from [[Suarez 1998 - Identification of Gases Responsible for Odour of Human Flatus]]), it targets smell directly rather than gas volume.

> [!warning] Episodic use only. Bismuth subsalicylate carries salicylate, and chronic/high-dose bismuth is linked to reversible neurotoxicity (tremor, confusion). Reserve for social situations, not daily use.
