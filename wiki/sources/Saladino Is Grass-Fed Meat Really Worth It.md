# Saladino — Is Grass-Fed Meat Really Worth It

[[Paul Saladino]]'s own newsletter making his grass-fed case. Interesting for what it leans on — and what it skips.

## His Two Arguments

**1. Nutrient edge (small numbers):** higher vitamin E, beta-carotene, CoQ10 ("especially rich in heart"), and vitamin C — his own figure is ~11.3 mg/lb grass-fed vs ~7.3 mg/lb grain-fed, i.e. a ~4 mg difference (an orange has ~70 mg).

**2. Contaminant avoidance (his real centerpiece):** feedlot grain brings glyphosate and 2,4-D ("likely end up in the muscle meat" — hedged, no measurement cited), atrazine (xenoestrogen), zearalenone (mold toxin/growth promoter in feedlots), and dioxins (from treated-wood troughs). Grass-fed sidesteps the grain pathway.

## Notable Omissions

- **No omega-3 or CLA argument in this piece** — the claims most associated with grass-fed advocacy (and the ones with the best-quantified, smallest absolute effect — see [[Grass-Fed vs Grain-Fed Beef (Healthline + PMC Review)]])
- No animal welfare discussion
- The contaminant claims are mechanistic/plausible-exposure arguments, not measured-residue comparisons in beef

## What It Contributes

The primary-source backbone of [[Saladino Grass-Fed Meat Argument]]: his case is **more about avoiding feedlot contaminants than about added nutrition**, which is a more defensible (and harder to quantify) position than the "superfood" framing.
