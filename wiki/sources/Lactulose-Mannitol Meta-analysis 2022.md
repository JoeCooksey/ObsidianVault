# Lactulose:Mannitol Ratio Meta-analysis (2022)

Meta-analysis quantifying the lactulose:mannitol ratio (LMR) — the closest thing to a validated *functional* gut-permeability test — in coeliac and Crohn's disease.

## How the Test Works

Patient fasts, then drinks a solution of two sugars. **Mannitol** (small) permeates transcellularly through membrane water pores — a baseline for absorptive surface. **Lactulose** (large) only crosses paracellularly through tight junctions — so it leaks more when the barrier is compromised. The **ratio** of the two in urine corrects for confounders (gastric emptying, kidney function) and indexes barrier integrity.

## Pooled Numbers (confidence: high)

| Group | LMR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Healthy controls | 0.014 (0.006–0.022) |
| Untreated coeliac | 0.133 (0.089–0.178) |
| Treated coeliac | 0.037 (0.019–0.055) |
| Active Crohn's | 0.093 (0.031–0.156) |
| Inactive Crohn's | 0.028 (0.015–0.041) |

Untreated coeliac runs **~10× higher** than healthy — large, real signal.

## Diagnostic Accuracy (coeliac screening)
- Sensitivity 0.829, Specificity 0.700, **AUC 0.88** (0.85–0.91)

## Limitation
"High heterogeneity" between studies from non-standardized protocols (sugar doses, urine-collection windows, assay methods). Better standardization is needed before routine clinical use.
