# Could You Live on Supplements Alone (Survey of Popular-Science Sources)

Aggregate of consumer-health explainers (Ohio State Wexner Medical Center, SF Gate/Weekand, Medical Daily, BarBend) on whether a person can live on supplements/meal-replacements. Confidence: **medium** — popular-science consensus, not primary research, but the underlying physiology is uncontroversial.

## Findings
- **Vitamin/mineral pills alone = not survivable.** They provide micronutrients with ~0 calories — no energy, protein, bulk fat, or fiber.
- **Fiber is the standout gap** — most supplements contain none; fiber feeds the microbiome and isn't replaceable by a multivitamin.
- **Meal-replacement shakes** (the realistic substitute) supply macros + fortified micros and *can* sustain short/medium term, but long-term sole use risks monotony, microbiome impoverishment, fortification over/under-dosing, and loss of the intact [[Food Matrix Effect]].
- **Whole foods uniquely provide** digestive enzymes, prebiotic fiber, probiotic bacteria, and the satiety/chewing/social functions of eating.
- **Consensus:** supplements *supplement*; they do not *replace* food.

## What it contributes
Anchors [[Supplement-Only Diet Feasibility]] — establishes that a literal "supplement-only diet" is a non-starter, reframing the real comparison as whole food vs engineered meal-replacement.
