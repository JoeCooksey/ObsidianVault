# Grass-Fed vs Grain-Fed Beef (Healthline + PMC Review)

Synthesis of the popular Healthline explainer and the peer-reviewed fatty-acid review (Daley et al.). The relative differences are real but the **absolute amounts are small**.

## What It Contributes

The honest magnitude check on the most-hyped meat-sourcing upgrade.

### The differences (relative)
- **Omega-3:** grass-fed has **2–5× more** than grain-fed. Omega-6:omega-3 ratio ~**1.5:1** (grass) vs ~**7.7:1** (grain).
- **CLA** (conjugated linoleic acid): grass-fed has ~**2×** more.
- Grass-fed is also higher in vitamin E, beta-carotene, and some antioxidants.

### The reality check (absolute)
- A 3 oz serving of grass-fed ground beef has **~0.015 g** omega-3 vs **~0.003 g** for conventional — both **tiny**.
- You'd need **~100×** the grass-fed beef to match the omega-3 of one salmon serving.
- "Beef in general is not a good source of omega-3s" — feed type doesn't change that.

### Where both agree
- **All beef** is an excellent source of **B12, iron, zinc, and high-quality protein.**
- Both grass-fed and grain-fed are nutritious; the choice is marginal on health grounds.

## Bottom Line (confidence: high)
Grass-fed is a **legitimate but minor** nutritional upgrade. Buy it if taste, leanness, animal welfare, or environment matter to you — **don't buy it to hit your omega-3 needs**; get those from [[Food Health Tier List — Meats and Seafood|fatty fish]] instead. The bigger lever is choosing **lean cuts and avoiding processed meat**, not grass vs. grain.
