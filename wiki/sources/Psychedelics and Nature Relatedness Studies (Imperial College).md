# Psychedelics and Nature Relatedness — Imperial College Research Line

Two key papers: **Kettner et al. 2019**, "From Egoism to Ecoism: Psychedelics Increase Nature Relatedness in a State-Mediated and Context-Dependent Manner" (*IJERPH*), and **Lyons & Carhart-Harris 2018**, increased nature relatedness after psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression (*J. Psychopharmacology*). Plus Forstmann & Sagioglou 2017 on pro-environmental behavior.

## Key findings

- Nature relatedness significantly increased at **2 weeks, 4 weeks, and 2 years** post-experience, correlated with gains in psychological well-being
- The increase depends on **ego-dissolution** during the trip and on the **perceived influence of natural surroundings** during the acute state — being in nature amplifies the effect
- But the effect appears in **clinical settings too** — nature is enhancement, not prerequisite
- Lifetime psychedelic experience predicts self-reported pro-environmental behavior, statistically explained by nature self-identification

## Contribution

Justifies **safe, familiar nature settings as S-tier** in [[Psilocybin Trip Activities Tier List]]: natural surroundings during the acute state measurably shape one of the best-documented lasting outcomes.
