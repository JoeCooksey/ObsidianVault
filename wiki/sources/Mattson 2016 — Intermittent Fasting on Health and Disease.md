# Mattson 2016 — Intermittent Fasting on Health and Disease

According to PubMed. Foundational review by NIA neuroscientist Mark Mattson and USC longevity researcher [[Valter Longo]] on how intermittent fasting (IF) and periodic fasting (PF) reshape cell biology. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2016.10.005)

## What it contributes

This is the mechanistic backbone for **how to optimize stem cells and mitochondria through nutrition timing.**

- Humans evolved to function well in a fasted state; modern 3+ meals/day plus sedentism drives metabolic disease.
- **Cellular mechanisms** of IF benefit: activation of adaptive stress-response pathways that enhance **mitochondrial health, DNA repair, and [[Autophagy]]**. (Source: Mattson 2016, confidence high)
- **Periodic fasting promotes stem-cell-based regeneration** and long-lasting metabolic effects — the key claim for optimizing [[Stem Cells]]. (confidence high — animal models strong, human RCTs still needed)
- Forms defined: IF (16–48 h fasts, e.g. alternate-day or 5:2), PF / fasting-mimicking diet (2–21 days, ~750–1100 kcal), time-restricted feeding (TRF, ≤8 h eating window).
- Human studies show weight loss and improved insulin sensitivity + reduced cardiovascular risk factors.

> [!gap] Authors explicitly note that randomized controlled trials in humans are still required to confirm IF prevents/manages major age-related diseases. Most regeneration evidence is from rodents.
