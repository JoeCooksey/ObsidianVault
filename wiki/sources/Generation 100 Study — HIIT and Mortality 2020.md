# Generation 100 Study — HIIT and Mortality 2020

Landmark 5-year randomized controlled trial from [[Ulrik Wisløff]]'s Cardiac Exercise Research Group (CERG) at NTNU (PMC7539760, *BMJ* 2020). The longevity backbone for the 4x4 / HIIT case.

## What It Contributes
Connects the 4x4-style HIIT stimulus to a hard outcome — all-cause mortality — in a large, long, randomized older-adult cohort.

## Design
- **n = 1,567** of 6,966 invited; born 1936–1942 (aged 70–77).
- Randomized for **5 years** to:
  - **HIIT** ~90% peak HR (4x4-style), 2×/week
  - **MICT** ~70% peak HR (moderate continuous)
  - **Control** — national physical-activity guidelines

## Key Results
- **All-cause mortality:** combined HIIT+MICT vs control not significantly different. Analyzed separately, **HIIT showed a 1.7 pp absolute risk reduction vs control, and 2.9 pp vs MICT** (HIIT as the strongest arm).
- **VO2 max:** HIIT produced greater VO2 max gains than MICT.
- **>95%** of participants survived the 5 years (a fit, self-selected cohort — a study limitation).

## Why It Matters
VO2 max is one of the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality. The 4x4 is the most efficient known way to raise it, and this trial ties that stimulus to a survival signal.

## Confidence
**High** — large, long RCT from the originating research group. Caveats: healthy-volunteer selection bias, control group exercised more than expected (diluting between-group differences), mortality effect borderline-significant.
