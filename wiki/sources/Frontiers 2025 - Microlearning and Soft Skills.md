# Frontiers 2025 — Microlearning and Soft Skills

Quasi-experimental pre/post study of **358 university students** (of 384 recruited, 93% completion) across four disciplines, testing whether short microlearning modules build soft skills (teamwork, leadership, communication, time management, emotional intelligence).

## Design

- **Intervention:** six weekly online modules, **15–20 minutes each**, with interactive materials, case studies, and gamified elements.
- **Analysis:** paired and independent t-tests, ANOVA, Cohen's *d* effect sizes.

## Key Findings

- Short, repeated sessions produced **statistically significant skill gains** post-intervention.
- Improvements were **discipline-specific** (e.g., medical/engineering students gained most in leadership and time management; business/humanities in emotional intelligence) — microlearning is not uniformly potent for everyone or every skill.

## Limitations (why confidence is medium)

- Quasi-experimental, not fully randomized (institutional constraints).
- Short 6-week window.
- **Self-report Likert measures** rather than objective performance tests.
- Single-country (Chinese university) sample.

## What It Contributes

A recent peer-reviewed data point that **brief 15–20 minute sessions, repeated, move the needle** — the format at the heart of a sustainable daily learning habit. The corporate-training literature reports larger numbers (e.g., spaced reinforcement → ~150% better retention; up to ~60% retention boost) but those are mostly vendor sources and treated here as low confidence.
