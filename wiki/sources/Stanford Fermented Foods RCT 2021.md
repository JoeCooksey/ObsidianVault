# Stanford Fermented Foods RCT (Cell, 2021)

Landmark 17-week randomized trial (Sonnenburg & Gardner labs, Stanford) comparing a high-fiber diet vs. a high-fermented-food diet in healthy adults. The strongest human evidence that diet reshapes the microbiome and immune system on a timescale of weeks.

## Key Claims (confidence: high)

- **Fermented foods raised microbiota diversity.** A diet rich in fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut, kombucha, fermented vegetables) "consistently increased microbiota alpha diversity" over 10 weeks — diversity is a top-line marker of a healthy gut.
- **Fermented foods lowered inflammation.** 19 inflammatory proteins decreased, including **IL-6** — a cytokine linked to chronic disease and aging.
- **Fiber alone did not raise diversity (short-term).** The high-fiber arm showed *no* significant alpha-diversity increase in 10 weeks. Instead, fiber boosted microbiome-encoded **glycan-degrading CAZymes** — enhanced metabolic *capacity*. The interpretation: a diversity-depleted modern gut may need to be reseeded (fermented foods) before it can fully exploit fiber.

> [!note] This does NOT mean "skip fiber"
> Fiber is the primary fuel for short-chain-fatty-acid production and long-term microbiome health. The trial's lesson is sequencing/combination, not replacement: do both. See [[Gut Optimization Actions Tier List]].
