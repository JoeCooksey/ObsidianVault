# Sitting-Rising Test and Mortality (Brito / Araújo)

Two prospective cohort studies from the CLINIMEX clinic (Rio de Janeiro) establishing the [[Sitting-Rising Test]] (SRT) as a mortality predictor. According to PubMed.

## What it contributes

Evidence that **non-aerobic musculoskeletal fitness** (the blend of strength, flexibility, balance, and body composition needed to get off the floor unaided) independently predicts how long you live.

**Brito et al. 2014** — *Eur J Prev Cardiol* — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487312471759) (PMID 23242910):
- 2,002 adults aged 51–80 (68% men), median 6.3-yr follow-up, 159 deaths.
- Score 0–10 (sit + rise, −1 per hand/knee support). Lower scores → higher mortality (*P* < 0.001).
- Adjusted HRs (age, sex, BMI) from low to high: **5.44 / 3.44 / 1.84**.
- **Each 1-point increase = 21% improvement in survival.**

**Araújo et al. 2025** — *Eur J Prev Cardiol* — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwaf325):
- 4,282 adults aged 46–75 (67.5% men), median **12.3-yr** follow-up, 665 deaths (231 cardiovascular).
- Natural-death rate fell monotonically with score: **42.1%** (0–4) → 20.4% → 11.1% → 7.0% → **3.7%** (perfect 10).
- Fully adjusted HRs (lowest vs highest): natural **3.84** (2.25–6.97); cardiovascular **6.05** (2.29–20.94).

> [!note] The SRT reflects qualities that respond to the [[Daily Movement Routine (Flexibility, Mobility, Longevity)|daily routine]] — floor mobility, hip/ankle range, leg strength, balance — making it both a target and a self-test.
