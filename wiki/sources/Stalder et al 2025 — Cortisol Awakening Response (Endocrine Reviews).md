# Stalder et al. 2025 — Cortisol Awakening Response

*Endocrine Reviews*, Vol 46(1), Feb 2025. DOI 10.1210/endrev/bnae024. Authoritative review of the **Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR)** — the physiology backbone of the [[Cortisol Complete Guide]].

## Key Facts

**Magnitude & timing.** In healthy adults cortisol rises **50–156%** in the first **30–45 minutes** after waking, then declines through the day. The CAR is the first major cortisol pulse of the active phase and is **largely unrelated to the rest of the day's cortisol** (it's a distinct phenomenon), with notable day-to-day within-person variability.

**Regulation (dual-control system):**
- HPA-axis pathway (hypothalamic paraventricular nucleus → pituitary ACTH → adrenal).
- **Extrapituitary**: direct sympathetic innervation of the adrenal gland via the splanchnic nerve.
- Central control via the **SCN** (master clock), hippocampus, and prefrontal cortex.
- Key modulators: circadian phase, **light exposure**, sleep timing, **cognitive anticipation** of the day, and prior-day emotional state.

**It's the waking transition, not stress.** The CAR is triggered by the sleep→wake transition itself and occurs even in quiet, controlled conditions.

**Clinical significance:**
- **Robust CAR** → healthy adaptive preparation for the day; better executive function, memory, emotion regulation, immune modulation.
- **Blunted CAR** → chronic stress exposure, burnout, PTSD; associated with cardiometabolic, autoimmune, allergic, and psychiatric conditions (e.g., schizophrenia) and elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines.

**Proposed functions:** (1) activity mobilization for anticipated demands; (2) emotional counter-regulation buffering prior-day stress; possibly synchronizes peripheral clocks via glucocorticoid-receptor clock-gene expression.
