# Choi & Je 2024 — Coffee Consumption and C-Reactive Protein

*Nutrition, Metabolism & Cardiovascular Diseases* systematic review and meta-analysis of **11 cross-sectional studies (n=66,691)** (according to PubMed — Choi & Je 2024, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.numecd.2024.06.024)).

Coffee intake showed a **linear inverse association with CRP**: relative to non-drinkers, log-CRP fell by SMD −0.09 at ~2.5 cups/day and −0.14 at ~4.5 cups/day, with no evidence of a non-linear threshold. The effect trended stronger in women and strengthened after adjusting for smoking (a key confounder, since smokers often drink more coffee).

Confidence is **medium** because all pooled studies were **cross-sectional** (association, not proven causation). A separate cohort analysis found coffee-associated CRP reduction partially mediates coffee's protection against type 2 diabetes (Ochoa-Rosales 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2023.02.024)), and that ground/filtered coffee and non-smokers benefit most.

Contributes the claim that **habitual coffee (especially filtered) is associated with modestly lower hs-CRP**.
