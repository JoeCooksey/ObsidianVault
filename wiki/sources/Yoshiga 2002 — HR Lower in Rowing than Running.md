# Yoshiga & Higuchi 2002 — HR Lower in Rowing than Running

*Eur J Appl Physiol.* 2002 Jun;87(2):97-100. 55 healthy men (mean age 21), progressive exercise test on both a rowing ergometer and a treadmill.

## What It Contributes
The peer-reviewed evidence that **a "low" heart rate on the erg is normal physiology, not weak effort.** At the *same* internal intensity (matched blood lactate), rowing HR runs consistently below running HR:

| Blood lactate | Rowing HR | Running HR |
|---|---|---|
| 2 mmol/L | 145 bpm | 150 bpm |
| 4 mmol/L | 170 bpm | 177 bpm |
| 6 mmol/L | 182 bpm | 188 bpm |
| Maximal | 194 bpm | 198 bpm |

All differences significant (P<0.05).

## Why
The authors attribute it to the **seated position and the larger muscle mass recruited in rowing**, which together improve venous return → larger stroke volume → the heart delivers the same cardiac output at a lower rate. Consistent with this, rowing produced a *higher* max VO2 (4.50 vs 4.35 L/min) and higher oxygen pulse — i.e. more work per beat.

## Implication for the 4x4
Do **not** chase the same HR number you'd see running. A rowing 4x4 worked correctly may peak ~4–6 bpm under your running 4x4. Pace by **power/split** (objective) and treat HR as a secondary check. See [[Getting Your Heart Rate Up on the Rower]].

## Confidence
**High** — peer-reviewed, n=55, direct within-subject comparison. Sample is young men; the absolute numbers are individual, but the *direction and mechanism* generalize.
