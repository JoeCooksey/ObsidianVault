# Make It Stick (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel)

*The Science of Successful Learning* (Harvard University Press, 2014). Written by two leading memory researchers (Roediger, McDaniel) plus a novelist (Brown), it is the definitive **popular** account of the cognitive science of learning — the readable companion to [[Dunlosky 2013 — Improving Students' Learning]].

## Central thesis

The strategies that *feel* most productive are often the least effective. **Rereading and massed cramming create fluency** — the material feels familiar — and we mistake that familiarity for mastery. Durable learning comes from **effortful** strategies instead.

## The core techniques

- **Retrieval practice** — "testing isn't just assessment, it's a way of *creating* knowledge." → [[Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)]]
- **Spaced practice** — let memory partly fade, then retrieve; sleep consolidates. → [[Spaced Repetition]]
- **Interleaving** — switch between problem types *before* finishing one, building discrimination. → [[Interleaving (Learning)]]
- **Elaboration** — re-express material in your own words and connect it to what you know; *"there's no known limit to how much you can learn."*
- **Generation** — attempt an answer before being shown the solution.
- **Reflection / calibration** — review what worked; don't trust the feeling of fluency.

## The unifying idea

All of these are **[[Desirable Difficulties]]** (Bjork): they make practice feel slower and harder, which is *exactly* why they produce stronger, more transferable memory. The book's lasting lesson is to **judge learning by delayed performance, not by how easy it felt.**
