# Failure of Activated Charcoal to Reduce the Release of Colonic Gases

**Suarez FL, Furne J, Springfield J, Levitt MD. _Am J Gastroenterol_ 1999;94(1):208–12.** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1572-0241.1999.00798.x), PMID 9934757) — *According to PubMed.*

The study that debunks **oral** activated charcoal for gas.

## Method
**5 healthy volunteers** took **0.52 g activated charcoal four times daily for one week**; fecal gas release measured before/after. In-vitro binding studies followed to explain the result.

## Key Findings
- **No significant reduction** in fecal release of *any* sulfur gas, total gas, or abdominal symptoms. (Source: this paper)
- In vitro, charcoal binds sulfur gas well — but in vivo its **binding sites saturate during passage through the gut**, so by the colon it is spent.

## Contribution to the Topic
Resolves a common contradiction: charcoal *does* adsorb sulfur gas (so charcoal-lined cushions/underwear work, per [[Suarez 1998 - Identification of Gases Responsible for Odour of Human Flatus]]), but **swallowing charcoal does not** because it saturates en route. Practical takeaway: use charcoal in **external garments**, not as an oral supplement.
