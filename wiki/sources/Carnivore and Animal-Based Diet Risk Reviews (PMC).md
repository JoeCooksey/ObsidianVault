# Carnivore and Animal-Based Diet Risk Reviews (PMC)

The peer-reviewed counterweight to the influencer case. Two anchors: *The Impacts of Animal-Based Diets in Cardiovascular Disease Development* (JCDD/PMC10380617, 2023 — mechanistic review) and *Carnivore Diet: A Scoping Review of the Current Evidence* (PMC12845189 — evidence survey). Both are reviews, not RCTs; no long-term RCT of carnivore/animal-based diets exists in either direction.

## Findings

**Lipids:** Saturated fat on animal-based meal plans substantially exceeds the WHO <10% of energy ceiling; several studies report LDL-C and triglyceride increases in adherents. (Some carnivore adherents instead show the "lean mass hyper-responder" pattern — very high LDL with high HDL/low TG; the [[KETO-CTA study|KETO-CTA]] cohort with LDL >190 showed substantial coronary plaque progression within one year — see [[Saladino Diet Critiques (Gene Food + Hone Health)]].)

**Beyond LDL — four independent CVD mechanisms:** elevated TMAO (carnitine/choline → gut bacteria → trimethylamine-N-oxide), serum endotoxins driving toll-like-receptor signaling, oxidized LDL, cardiac lipotoxicity, plus renin-angiotensin upregulation.

**Fiber and microbiome:** the diet contains essentially zero fiber → starves butyrate-producing gut bacteria. Fiber intake is a strong general marker of diet quality because it only comes from minimally processed plants. Connects to the vault's gut cluster: [[Gut Microbiome Fundamentals]], [[Fiber Supplement Tier List]].

**Deficiency risk:** vitamin C, folate, vitamin E, potassium, magnesium flagged — partially mitigated in Saladino's CURRENT version by fruit/honey/dairy (this matters: most published critiques target strict carnivore, and the fruit+dairy additions genuinely fix several of them).

**Conclusion (scoping review):** "long-term adherence to an animal-based diet cannot be recommended" on current evidence.

## Caveats

- Reviews lean on epidemiology and mechanism, not interventional animal-based-diet trials (none exist long-term)
- Short-term self-reported outcomes in carnivore adherents (Harvard survey, n≈2,000) skew positive — selection bias acknowledged
