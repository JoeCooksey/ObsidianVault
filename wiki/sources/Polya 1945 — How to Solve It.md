# How to Solve It (Pólya, 1945)

George Pólya's classic on mathematical method — the foundational practical text on problem-solving process. Still in print and widely cited across math, science, and software.

## What it contributes

A reusable **four-step framework** plus a "dictionary" of ~67 heuristics. The steps are conveyed as **self-questions** that induce useful mental moves and a problem-solving disposition. See the full method in [[Pólya's Problem-Solving Method]].

The four steps:
1. **Understand the problem** — what is unknown, what is given, what is the condition?
2. **Devise a plan** — find the connection; reach for heuristics (analogy, work backwards, solve a simpler/related problem, introduce auxiliary elements).
3. **Carry out the plan** — execute, checking each step.
4. **Look back** — verify, and ask whether the result or method transfers to other problems (this step builds the schema).

## Key heuristics

Analogy · decomposition/recombination · generalization & specialization · working backwards · auxiliary elements (diagrams, notation, intermediate goals) · solve a related/simpler problem first.

## Significance

Pólya reframed problem solving as **teachable, guided discovery** rather than innate talent — the intellectual ancestor of modern work on metacognition and heuristics ([[Metacognition in Problem Solving]]).
