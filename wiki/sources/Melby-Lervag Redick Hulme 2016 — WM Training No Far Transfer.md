# Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme 2016 — WM Training No Far Transfer

Meta-analysis (Perspectives on Psychological Science) of **87 studies / 145 experiments** on working-memory and "brain training" programs.

## What it contributes

The strongest evidence that **you cannot shortcut general problem-solving ability with brain games**. Training produces robust *near* transfer (you get better at the trained task and very similar ones) but **no reliable *far* transfer** to fluid intelligence, reasoning, reading, or arithmetic.

Corroborated by second-order meta-analyses finding far-transfer effects on dissimilar tasks essentially absent; positive findings often reflect small, underpowered studies rather than true effects.

## Why it matters for developing problem-solving skill

It kills the appealing-but-false model of "train a general mental muscle." Improvement is **domain-bound**: you get better at solving the kinds of problems you actually practice. This is the empirical backbone of [[Near vs Far Transfer]] and of why the answer to "how do I get better at problem solving" is *practice real problems in real domains*, not abstract drills.
