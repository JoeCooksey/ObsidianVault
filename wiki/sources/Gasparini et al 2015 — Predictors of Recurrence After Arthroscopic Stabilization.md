# Gasparini et al. 2015 — Predictors of Recurrence After Stabilization

*Knee Surgery, Sports Traumatology, Arthroscopy* (2015). 143 patients with traumatic anterior instability, arthroscopic stabilization, median **81-month** follow-up. Level II prognostic. According to PubMed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00167-015-3785-3)

## Key Findings
- Overall recurrence after surgery: **23.1%** (33/143); 15 needed further surgery.
- **Operating after only one dislocation** → significantly lower failure (p = .027).
- **Operating after the 2nd+ dislocation → OR 3.8 for failure** (95% CI 1.2–11.6) vs first-time dislocators.
- Better functional (Rowe) scores in patients **>24 years** and with **fewer than 8 pre-op dislocations**.
- Rowe score rose from 25 → 100 post-op.

## Contribution
Quantifies a key counterintuitive point: **the more times the shoulder has already dislocated before surgery, the more likely surgery is to fail.** Each instability event causes more labral/bone damage. Argues against indefinitely "waiting it out" once recurrent instability is established.

## Reliability
**Medium** — single-center retrospective prognostic cohort; consistent with the wider instability literature on number-of-dislocations as a risk factor.
