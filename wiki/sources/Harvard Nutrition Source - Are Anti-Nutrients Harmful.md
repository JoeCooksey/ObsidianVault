# Harvard Nutrition Source — Are Anti-Nutrients Harmful?

## What It Is
The anti-nutrients reference page from Harvard's T.H. Chan School of Public Health (The Nutrition Source) — an authoritative institutional summary of the evidence on phytates, lectins, oxalates, glucosinolates/goitrogens, and tannins.

## Core Position
> "The health benefits of eating these foods outweigh any potential negative nutritional effects." Avoiding anti-nutrients entirely is **not recommended**.

## Per-Compound Notes
- **Phytates** (grains/seeds/legumes/nuts): reduce iron/zinc/magnesium/calcium absorption **only when eaten in the same meal**; varied Western diets prevent deficiency. Also lower cholesterol and slow digestion.
- **Lectins** (legumes/grains): interfere with several minerals' absorption; deactivated by cooking.
- **Oxalates** (leafy greens/beans/nuts/beets): bind calcium; high intake showed ~20% higher kidney-stone risk in cohorts, but adequate calcium + hydration are protective.
- **Glucosinolates/goitrogens** (crucifers): can impair iodine absorption; one analysis linked highest intake to 19% higher diabetes risk, but authors called for confirmation before any recommendation.
- **Tannins** (tea/coffee/legumes): decrease iron absorption.

## Mitigation
Cooking, soaking, sprouting, fermentation reduce anti-nutrients. **Boiling for 12 minutes cuts oxalates 30–87%** (leafy greens ~85%). Spacing meals/tea, hydration, and dietary variety neutralize the concern.

## What It Contributes
A high-confidence institutional anchor: the mainstream nutrition-science position is that anti-nutrients are a manageable, mostly-overstated concern and that plant foods are net beneficial.

## Confidence
**High** — authoritative institution synthesizing cohort and trial data. Note it does report the modest real risks (oxalate/kidney stones; the unconfirmed glucosinolate–diabetes signal), so it is not a one-sided source.
