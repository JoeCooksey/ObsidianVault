# Bessembinder — Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?

## What this source contributes

The structural-math leg of the index-vs-picking argument. Peer-reviewed (Journal of Financial Economics, 2018), by an ASU finance professor — Joe's own university. Shifts the question from "can you beat professionals?" to "can you find the 1-in-25 stock that creates all the wealth?"

## Key Claims

- US stocks 1926–2016 (25,967 stocks, CRSP): **4 of 7 stocks had lifetime buy-and-hold returns below one-month T-bills**. (confidence: high)
- **Top ~4.3% of stocks (~1,092 firms) account for all net wealth creation** above T-bills; the remaining ~96% collectively matched T-bills. (confidence: high)
- **86 stocks generated half** of the ~$32T net wealth creation over 90 years; 5 firms (ExxonMobil, Apple, Microsoft, GE, IBM) = 10%. (confidence: high)
- Global extension (1991–2020, 64,000 stocks): **2.4% of firms produced the entire $75.7T** of net global wealth creation; 55.2% of US and 57.4% of non-US stocks underperformed T-bills. (confidence: high)
- Mechanism: long-horizon individual returns are extremely **positively skewed**; the market premium exists at the index level because the index always holds the tail winners. (confidence: high)
- "Active strategies, which tend to be poorly diversified, most often underperform" — picking resembles a lottery more than a systematic edge. (confidence: high)
