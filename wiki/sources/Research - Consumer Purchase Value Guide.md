# Research — Consumer Purchase Value Guide

Synthesis of consumer data, expert buyer guides, and product reviews across 5 price tiers: under $20, $20–$100, $100–$500, $500–$2,000, and $2,000+.

## Key Framework

**The Daily-Use Multiplier Rule**: Divide total cost by number of expected uses in one year. A $40 water bottle used 365 days = $0.11/use. A $200 "special occasion" jacket used 5 times = $40/use. This single ratio explains most worth-it/not-worth-it verdicts.

**4 Criteria for "Worth It"**:
1. Daily or near-daily use
2. Long lifespan (3+ years)
3. Direct health, productivity, or quality-of-life impact
4. No free or cheap substitute that performs equally well

**"Not Worth It" Failure Modes**:
1. Low-frequency use (bought with excitement, rarely used)
2. Single-purpose gadget (knife does it in 5 seconds)
3. App/subscription dependency (value disappears when app dies or subscription stops)
4. Brand premium far exceeding quality difference

## Meta-Principles Across All Tiers

1. **Cost per use beats sticker price**: A $200 chef's knife used 500 times/year = $0.40/use. A $50 blender used 10 times = $5/use. Use this frame on every purchase.
2. **Daily items justify higher price**: Upgrade what you touch every day (pillow, chair, shoes, keyboard) before what you use monthly.
3. **Subscriptions are the hidden money drain**: $20/month feels trivial; $240/year felt is real. Audit all subscriptions quarterly.
4. **Second-hand is almost always the play for depreciating goods**: Cars, furniture, bikes, gym equipment, electronics — 2–3 years old is the sweet spot.
5. **Free alternatives exist for most "nice to have" purchases**: YouTube > gym class apps, Libby > Kindle Unlimited, library > book buying, bodyweight > gadget fitness gear.
6. **Single-purpose gadgets almost always disappoint**: The novelty use case is not your actual use case. Buy multi-purpose tools instead.
7. **Brand premium rarely matches quality premium**: Consumer Reports/Cook's Illustrated/Wirecutter blind tests consistently show store brands and mid-tier products outperform luxury branding for most categories.
