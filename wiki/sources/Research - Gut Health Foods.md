# Research — Best Foods for Gut Health

Autoresearch synthesis across 5 searches: ZOE/Nature 2025 study (n=34,000+), NIH/PMC systematic reviews, King's College London fiber research, American Society for Nutrition prebiotic rankings, and 2024–2025 fermented food RCTs.

## 8 Key Findings

1. **Fermented foods increase microbiome diversity better than high-fiber diets alone** — Stanford RCT (Sonnenburg/Gardner, Cell 2021) showed a 10-week high-fermented-food diet increased microbiome diversity and decreased 19 inflammatory proteins vs. high-fiber diet alone

2. **Top 5 prebiotic foods** (American Society for Nutrition): dandelion greens, Jerusalem artichokes, garlic, leeks, onions — inulin/FOS at 5–8 g/day significantly increases fecal Bifidobacterium at low consumption levels

3. **Polyphenol diversity = microbiome diversity** — 3 servings of polyphenol-rich foods per day for 8 weeks decreased zonulin (leaky gut biomarker) significantly; 4 weeks of grape powder increased alpha diversity + Akkermansia muciniphila

4. **Omega-3 PUFAs restore gut dysbiosis** — increase Bifidobacterium, Lactobacillus, and butyrate-producing Lachnospiraceae; restore Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio; EPA/DHA from fatty fish most bioavailable form

5. **Butyrate is the key SCFA for gut lining** — fermentation of prebiotic fiber → short-chain fatty acids (especially butyrate) → nourishes colonocytes, reduces inflammation, strengthens gut barrier; lentils and oats are top butyrate-precursor foods

6. **Kefir > yogurt for probiotic density** — kefir contains up to 61 probiotic strains vs. ~7 in typical yogurt; fermentation hierarchy: kefir > plain yogurt > tempeh > kimchi/sauerkraut > cheese; always buy plain (unflavored) versions

7. **Ultra-processed foods actively damage the microbiome** — promote growth of damaging bacteria, disrupt Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio; processed meats (WHO Group 1 carcinogen) + refined sugars cause dysbiosis; 60% of American diet is ultra-processed

8. **ZOE Microbiome Health Ranking 2025** (Nature, n=34,000+ US+UK participants) — identified gut species most favorably associated with health markers; high-microbiome-diversity diets correlated with better metabolic, immune, and cardiovascular outcomes

## 5 Open Questions

1. Does the sequence of eating (fermented food before or after fiber) affect SCFA production?
2. How long does it take to measurably shift microbiome composition with diet change alone?
3. Are liquid fermented foods (kefir, kombucha) superior to solid fermented foods (kimchi, tempeh) for probiotic delivery?
4. Is microbiome diversity or specific species composition more predictive of health outcomes?
5. How does Joe's high-intensity cycling 6×/week affect gut permeability and microbiome composition?
