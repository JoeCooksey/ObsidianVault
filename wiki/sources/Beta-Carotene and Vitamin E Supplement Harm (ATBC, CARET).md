# Beta-Carotene and Vitamin E Supplement Harm (ATBC, CARET)

The landmark RCTs showing that **isolated antioxidant supplements can cause harm** — even nutrients that are healthy in food. Confidence: **high** (large RCTs + meta-analyses), though the headline harms are specific to smokers/high doses.

## The trials
- **ATBC** (Finland, 29,133 male smokers): 20 mg/day β-carotene **increased lung cancer ~18%.** Vitamin E (~50 IU) reduced prostate cancer in this cohort.
- **CARET** (β-carotene + retinol): halted 21 months early — **46% more lung cancer deaths** in the supplement arm. NCI ended the $42M trial.
- **Physicians' Health Study** (22,000 male physicians, 50 mg β-carotene every other day, 12 yr): **no effect**, good or bad.

## Meta-analytic dose signal
- β-carotene **>9.6 mg/day** → significantly increased all-cause mortality.
- Vitamin E **>RDA (>15 mg/day)** → significantly increased all-cause mortality.
(Source: Bjelakovic-type pooled mortality meta-analyses, PMC3765487.)

## What it contributes
Hard evidence for the **safety-ceiling argument** in [[High-Dose Isolated Supplement Harms]]: the same β-carotene that is protective in carrots became carcinogenic as an isolated megadose in smokers. Whole foods don't reach these doses or strip the protective matrix. Central to why "more supplements" is not a safe default.

> [!note] Context dependence
> Harm was strongest in smokers and at supraphysiologic doses; this is a caution against megadosing, not proof every antioxidant pill is dangerous.
