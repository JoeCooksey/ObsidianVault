# Bryan Johnson Immortals DIY — Empirical Health

Cost-benefit breakdown of Bryan Johnson's ~$1M/yr "Immortals" longevity program, arguing most of the value is reproducible cheaply.

## What it contributes

A clean tiering of interventions by cost-effectiveness — the empirical backbone for the [[Cost-Barrier Health Hack Ladder]].

**High-value, low-cost:**
- Lifestyle (diet/exercise/sleep): free; exercise alone cuts CV mortality 30–40%.
- Comprehensive bloodwork: $190–399/yr, 100+ biomarkers — "single most important component."
- Core supplements (creatine, omega-3, vitamin D, fiber): $660–1,080/yr.
- Wearable (Apple Watch / Oura / Whoop): $300–500 one-time.
- Statins where indicated: cut heart-attack risk 25–35%.

**Medium tier ($3k–6k/yr):** annual full-body MRI ($1k–2.5k), epigenetic age testing ($500–1k/yr), home sauna ($2k–5k once), CGM ($600–1,800/yr).

**Experimental/expensive ($50k–100k+/yr):** gene therapy (~$25k), stem cells ($5k–25k), HBOT ($6k–15k/yr), 30-person medical team (the bulk of the $1M).

## Verdict

The "sweet spot" reaches ~90% of results for **under $2,000/year**, primarily via biomarker testing + lifestyle rather than exotic therapies. A DIY approach reportedly beat Johnson on 27/50 biomarkers at ~1/3000th the cost.

> [!confidence] high — costs and effect sizes are consistent with mainstream clinical literature.
