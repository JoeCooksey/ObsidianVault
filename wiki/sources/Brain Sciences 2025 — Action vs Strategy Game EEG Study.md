# Brain Sciences 2025 — Action vs Strategy Game EEG Study

**Published:** *Brain Sciences*, December 2025. "Effects of Video Game Type on Cognitive Performance and Brain Functional Connectivity: A Longitudinal EEG Study."

A rare **randomized longitudinal training study** directly comparing an action game against a strategy game on the same cohort — and notably the cohort matches Joe's demographic (mean age 19.8).

## Design
- **30-week longitudinal intervention**, n = 68 college students (mean age 19.8), randomly assigned to two groups.
- Behavioral tests + EEG recordings at **six timepoints**, including a follow-up ~10 weeks after training stopped.
- Game conditions:
  - **Action:** League of Legends (LoL) — fast-paced, real-time, multi-stimulus MOBA.
  - **Strategy:** Legends of the Three Kingdoms — turn-based card game emphasizing planning.

## Key Findings
- **Both groups improved** on spatial attention and spatial working memory. (confidence: medium)
- **The action (LoL) group showed greater improvement AND longer-lasting effects** on spatial attention and working memory at the 10-week follow-up. (confidence: medium)
- Executive-function (conflict) task: no accuracy change in either group (ceiling effect, ~80% baseline), though reaction times dropped.
- EEG: both groups showed increased low-frequency (delta/theta) power and decreased alpha-band functional connectivity; **the LoL group's neural changes were more pronounced.**

## Why It Matters / Contradiction
This **partially contradicts the common "strategy > action for cognitive transfer" framing.** In a head-to-head training design, the action MOBA produced *larger and more durable* gains in spatial attention and working memory than the turn-based strategy game. It suggests fast real-time MOBAs deserve higher placement in a cognitive-benefit tier list than older action-vs-strategy intuitions imply.

> [!gap] Single study, n=68, one action title vs one strategy title. Spatial attention/WM ≠ general IQ. Needs replication before overturning the broader strategy-transfer literature.
