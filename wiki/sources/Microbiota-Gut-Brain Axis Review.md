# The Microbiota-Gut-Brain Axis (Review Cluster)

Synthesis of several peer-reviewed reviews on the gut–brain axis (Frontiers in Endocrinology 2020 PMC7005631; Nature *Signal Transduction & Targeted Therapy* 2024; PMC8321234).

## Key Claims (confidence: high)

- The gut microbiota influences CNS processes **bidirectionally** via the **vagus nerve**, immune-system modulation, the **HPA (stress) axis**, and **tryptophan metabolism**.
- Microbes synthesize/modulate neurotransmitters and produce **short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)** with neuroactive properties — SCFAs "can modulate blood–brain barrier permeability, affect brain immune responses, and regulate gene expression in neurons."
- Under homeostasis the microbiome regulates **microglial maturation and activation** via SCFA release.
- The microbiota also regulates metabolic, endocrine, and immune functions and **synthesizes vitamins K and B**, provides energy via SCFAs, and metabolizes bile acids and sterols.
