# MOOC Certificate Credential Value for Engineering Hiring

Career-advice aggregate (confidence: medium — these are career-content sites, not employer surveys; treat the direction as reliable and the specifics as soft).

## Findings

- **Udemy is not accredited.** Its certificates of completion carry no academic credit and are not a recognized credential.
- **Engineering is the worst case for MOOC certificates.** In regulated professions, accredited credentials (ABET degree, FE/PE licensure) are what gate roles. A Udemy certificate substitutes for none of it.
- **They are read as signals of initiative**, not proof of competence — and a certificate unrelated to the job, overly general, or stale reads as noise.
- **Do not list them under "Licenses & Certifications."** That section implies accreditation.
- **The load-bearing rule**: employers want evidence you can *use* the skill. A certificate proves you finished a video course. A project proves you have the skill.

## Consequence for a Udemy-based plan

The subscription's value is the **instruction**, not the credential. Every course in a roadmap has to terminate in an artifact — a repo, a simulation, a board, a measured result — or the time spent buys nothing a resume can carry.

> [!important] This inverts the natural instinct to "collect certificates." Finish rate and artifact count are the metrics; certificate count is vanity.
