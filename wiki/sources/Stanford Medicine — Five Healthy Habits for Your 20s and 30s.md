# Stanford Medicine — Five Healthy Habits for Your 20s and 30s

Stanford clinicians' five evidence-based habits to establish early, because choices in early adulthood set the baseline for health at 50/60+. high

1. **Strength training ≥2×/week** — push close to fatigue (1–2 reps from failure). Peak bone mass and muscle window; prevents osteoporosis/frailty.
2. **Consistent cardio + keep moving** — 150 min moderate (or 75 vigorous)/week, ≥7,000 steps/day, break up sitting every 30 min (prolonged sitting risk likened to smoking). Cuts heart disease, stroke, T2D, some cancers; improves cognition.
3. **Eat for the long term** — Mediterranean pattern, whole foods, 0.8–1.0 g protein/kg (~55–70 g for a 150 lb person); limit alcohol to ≤1/day (women), ≤2/day (men). Less fast food at 18–30 → lower midlife disease risk.
4. **Sleep hygiene** — >7 hrs/night, consistent schedule incl. weekends, morning light, no screens before bed. Chronic deprivation raises obesity/CVD risk.
5. **Manage stress like a job** — meditation, breathing, self-hypnosis; the other four pillars directly support stress and mental health.
