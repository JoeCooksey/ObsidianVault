# Huberman Lab Episode 39 — Controlling Your Dopamine

Stanford neuroscientist Andrew Huberman's foundational public-facing episode on dopamine for motivation, focus, and satisfaction. It is the most widely cited source of the popular "dopamine multiplier" numbers.

## What It Contributes

- **The activity multipliers** that anchor [[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]]: chocolate ~1.5×, sex ~2×, exercise ~2× (varies by enjoyment), nicotine ~2.5×, cocaine ~2.5×, **amphetamine ~10×**. Caffeine is a modest raiser but **upregulates dopamine receptors**. Cold water ~2.5× with **sustained, crash-free** elevation up to ~3 h.
- **The pool/baseline model**: dopamine releases from a *readily releasable pool*; after a peak the baseline **drops**, and satisfaction depends on **peak height relative to baseline**, not absolute peak → [[Dopamine Baseline and the Pleasure-Pain Balance]].
- **Protocols**: don't stack dopamine sources; randomize/intermittent reward; keep the phone out of activities you want to keep enjoying; a ~30-day reset for the pool → [[Healthy Dopamine Protocols]].
- **Motivation vs pleasure**: dopamine is the "universal currency for moving us toward goals" — wanting, not liking.

## Confidence Note

Confidence **medium**: an authoritative neuroscientist communicating real literature, but the tidy single multipliers are **popularizations** of animal microdialysis ([[Di Chiara and Imperato 1988 — Drugs of Abuse and Dopamine]]) and small human studies. Treat the numbers as relative magnitudes.
