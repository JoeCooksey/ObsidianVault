# Bryant Veroff — Savoring, A New Model of Positive Experience

*Savoring: A New Model of Positive Experience* (Lawrence Erlbaum, 2007) by [[Fred Bryant]] and Joseph Veroff. The foundational text establishing **savoring** as a research construct and providing its measurement tools.

## What It Contributes

Defines and operationalizes the single most transferable mechanism behind "loving your life": the deliberate act of noticing and amplifying positive experience. Provides the theoretical spine for [[Savoring]] and, by extension, [[Romanticizing Your Life]].

## Key Claims

- **Savoring ≠ pleasure.** It is the *mindful engagement* that makes a positive experience register and last — the active counterpart to coping. (high)
- **Three temporal forms**: anticipation (future), being-in-the-moment (present), reminiscence (past); people differ in which they are best at. (high)
- **Strategies** (extended by Quoidbach et al., 2010): be present, behavioral display, capitalizing/sharing, and positive mental time travel. **Sharing a positive event with others is among the strongest amplifiers.** (high)
- **Dampening** — minimizing/dismissing positive feeling ("this won't last") — is the opposite of savoring and predicts lower wellbeing; it is elevated in depression. (medium-high)
- **Measurement**: the Savoring Beliefs Inventory (SBI, 2003) and Ways of Savoring Checklist (WOSC) operationalize savoring capacity. (high)

## Reliability

High-confidence for the *construct and its structure* (validated instruments, broad adoption). Effect sizes of brief savoring interventions are smaller and dose-dependent (medium) — see [[Savoring]] for the intervention evidence.
