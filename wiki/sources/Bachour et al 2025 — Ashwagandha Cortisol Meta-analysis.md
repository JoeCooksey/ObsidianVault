# Bachour et al. 2025 — Ashwagandha & Cortisol Meta-Analysis

*BJPsych Open* (2025). Systematic review + meta-analysis of **15 RCTs, 873 participants**. The anchor evidence for ashwagandha in [[Ways to Reduce Cortisol (Tier List)]].

## Key Findings

- **Cortisol:** significant reduction at **8 weeks** (μ = −2.36, 95% CI −3.26 to −1.46, p<0.0001; standardized mean difference). Classic individual trials report ~**11–32%** serum cortisol reduction depending on dose/duration — e.g., **300 mg root extract twice daily × 60 days → ~28% drop vs ~8% placebo**.
- **Anxiety (HAM-A):** reduced −3.52 at 8 weeks (p=0.0053).
- **Stress (PSS):** reduced −4.88 (p=0.0013) in this analysis.
- **Safety:** judged safe and effective for reducing stress/anxiety in adults; quality-of-life change was not significant.
- **Typical effective dose:** ≥250–600 mg/day, ≥2–8 weeks.

> [!note] A 2025 companion meta-analysis ("Dual impact of Ashwagandha", Albalawi) found a **significant cortisol drop but NO significant change in *perceived* stress** (SMD −0.36, p=0.40). Takeaway: ashwagandha reliably lowers the cortisol *number*; the subjective stress benefit is real in some analyses but less consistent.

## Contribution
Establishes ashwagandha as the best-evidenced cortisol-lowering supplement, with a usable dose (300–600 mg/day, evening) — matching the Huberman protocol while quantifying the effect.

## Reliability
**High** for the cortisol biomarker effect (pooled RCTs). **Medium** for translating that into felt stress relief, given the conflicting perceived-stress results.
