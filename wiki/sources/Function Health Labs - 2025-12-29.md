**Blood:** Your blood test results show that your blood is well-balanced and functioning optimally. Blood transports oxygen and nutrients to your cells and removes waste, which is essential for your overall health. Your red blood cells, which carry oxygen, are at healthy levels, ensuring that your body can efficiently fuel its organs, tissues, and muscles. The balanced levels of hemoglobin and hematocrit support effective oxygen delivery and blood flow, helping to maintain your energy levels and overall vitality. Healthy production of blood components in your bone marrow indicates that your clotting system and immune defenses are working as expected, supporting resilience during physical activity and everyday stress. A well-functioning blood system helps maintain a robust cardiovascular system, ensuring that your heart, arteries, capillaries, and veins work together to support every cell in your body. Understanding these results can empower you to continue making healthy lifestyle choices that support your overall wellness.

**Electrolytes:** Your electrolyte levels are balanced, indicating that your body’s hydration and nutrient transport mechanisms are operating normally. Electrolytes, including minerals such as sodium, potassium, and calcium, play essential roles in nutrient absorption, waste removal, muscle contraction, digestion, and nerve signaling. When these minerals are within a healthy range, your body maintains fluid balance and proper blood pressure regulation. Maintaining balanced electrolytes supports your energy and resilience. Continue to focus on a well-rounded diet and active lifestyle to support these vital processes.

**Environmental Toxins:** Your lab results show that the levels of toxins like mercury and lead are within healthy ranges. Since your toxin levels are optimal, your body is managing these exposures effectively. Continue to be mindful of your environment and potential toxin sources to help maintain your long-term well-being.

Some key markers in your test results are outside the ideal range and affect your cardiovascular risk. Your lipoprotein (a) is elevated, meaning extra cholesterol can adhere to your arterial walls and potentially promote plaque buildup. Your low-density lipoprotein (LDL) Particle Number is higher than optimal, indicating an excess of cholesterol-carrying particles that may increase the risk of arterial blockage. Similarly, an elevated LDL Medium contributes to an increased cholesterol burden that can affect your artery health. A reduced high-density lipoprotein (HDL) Large indicates that you have fewer protective particles needed to transport cholesterol away from your arteries. Lastly, a smaller than recommended LDL Peak Size means the LDL particles you have are more compact, potentially raising the chance of arterial blockage. Understanding these markers can guide you in adopting dietary and lifestyle changes to support a healthier heart.

In addition, a slight increase in your monocytes could reflect ongoing cleanup of previous inflammatory events or exposures. Although your overall white blood cell count remains within the normal range, the shift in cell composition may influence the balance between fast-acting defense and the development of lasting immunity. Supporting immune health through balanced lifestyle choices could be beneficial.
