# Narvani et al. 2020 — Degenerative Rotator Cuff Tear: Repair or Not?

*Annals of the Royal College of Surgeons of England* (2020). PRISMA review (49 of 743 papers, 1960–2018) on counselling patients with **non-traumatic (degenerative)** rotator cuff tears. According to PubMed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1308/rcsann.2019.0173)

## Key Findings
- **Non-surgical route with a proper physiotherapy programme has a definite role** — especially patients with surgical risk factors, those declining surgery, and **small, partial, or irreparable** tears.
- **Repair gives good clinical outcomes**: significant improvement in pain, range of motion, strength, quality of life, and sleep.
- Decision drivers: tendon healing potential (radiological outcome), natural history of the untreated tear, and patient-specific factors. Larger tears and advanced fatty infiltration heal worse and progress faster.

## Contribution
Frames the rotator-cuff half of Joe's question: a true cuff tear is often managed conservatively first, but repair is worthwhile when symptoms persist and the tear is repairable. Complements the natural-history data ([[Hochreiter 2024 fatty infiltration]] equivalent) that big tears with fatty infiltration are the ones to repair earlier.

## Reliability
**High** for the general framework; individual recommendations depend on tear morphology that only imaging can establish.
