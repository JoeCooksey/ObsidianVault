# Identification of Gases Responsible for the Odour of Human Flatus

**Suarez FL, Springfield J, Levitt MD. _Gut_ 1998;43(1):100–4.** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/gut.43.1.100), PMID 9771412) — *According to PubMed.*

The foundational study on flatus odor. Minneapolis VA group. This is the primary source most popular articles trace back to.

## Method
Flatus was quantitatively collected via rectal tube from **16 healthy subjects** who ate pinto beans + lactulose to boost output. Sulfur-gas concentrations per passage were correlated with **odour intensity scored by two trained judges**. Samples were also treated with zinc acetate (binds sulfhydryls) or activated charcoal. A charcoal-lined cushion was tested for its ability to adsorb sulfur gases instilled at the anus (8 subjects, gastight Mylar "pantaloons").

## Key Findings
- The three sulfur gases, by concentration (mean, SEM):
  - **Hydrogen sulfide** — 1.06 (0.2) µmol/L — *rotten eggs*
  - **Methanethiol** — 0.21 (0.04) µmol/L — *rotten cabbage*
  - **Dimethyl sulfide** — 0.08 (0.01) µmol/L — *sweet/garlic*
- **Malodour correlated significantly with hydrogen sulfide concentration (p ≤ 0.001).** (Source: this paper)
- **Zinc acetate** reduced sulfur-gas content but did **not totally eliminate** odour → sulfur gases are the *major but not the only* malodorous components.
- **Activated charcoal (in vitro)** removed virtually all odour.
- The charcoal-lined **cushion absorbed >90%** of instilled sulfur gases.

## Contribution to the Topic
Establishes the central thesis: **odor ≠ volume**. The bulk gases (N₂, CO₂, H₂, CH₄) are odorless; the smell comes from trace sulfur gases, dominated by H₂S. Drives the whole prevention strategy → reduce sulfur substrate / bind H₂S.
