# Schultz Dopamine Reward Prediction Error Hypothesis

The body of work (Wolfram Schultz's primate recordings; reviewed by Glimcher, *Understanding dopamine and reinforcement learning*, PNAS 2011) establishing that dopamine neurons compute **reward prediction error (RPE)**.

## What It Contributes

- **Phasic dopamine encodes RPE**: a burst for better-than-expected reward, **no change** for fully-predicted reward, a **dip** for omitted/worse-than-expected reward → [[Reward Prediction Error]].
- These signals update the estimated **value** of states and actions — the biological implementation of **reinforcement learning**.
- Supports the **tonic (motivation) vs phasic (learning)** division of dopamine signaling → [[Tonic vs Phasic Dopamine]].

This is the computational backbone explaining anticipation, novelty-seeking, and why fully-predicted rewards stop feeling rewarding.

## Confidence Note

Confidence **high**: among the most replicated findings in systems neuroscience; primate electrophysiology plus human imaging. Ongoing debate exists about whether dopamine *also* carries non-RPE signals (salience, value of work), but the core RPE result is robust.
