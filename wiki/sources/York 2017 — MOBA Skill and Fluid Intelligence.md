# York 2017 — MOBA Skill and Fluid Intelligence

**Published:** *PLOS One*, Nov 2017. "Exploring the relationship between video game expertise and fluid intelligence." University of York.

Establishes that skill at strategically complex multiplayer games tracks fluid intelligence — and that genre matters.

## Design & Findings
- Two studies. Study 1: lab participants played **League of Legends** and took a standard fluid-intelligence (matrix-reasoning) test. Study 2: analyzed large anonymized performance datasets from **League of Legends and Dota 2** (>100M registered players combined), with **first-person shooters (Destiny, Battlefield) as a control genre.**
- **LoL skill correlated with measured fluid intelligence** in controlled lab conditions. (confidence: medium)
- **MOBA performance peaks around age ~25 — the same age curve as raw fluid intelligence (IQ).** FPS / reaction-based games peak earlier (teens) and decline, matching reaction-time rather than reasoning. (confidence: medium)
- Interpretation: MOBAs load on reasoning/planning (g-like), while FPS load on perceptual-motor speed.

## Caveats
- **Correlational** — skilled players may be smarter, not made smarter. Does not show games raise IQ.
- Observational performance data; many uncontrolled confounds in the large-dataset arm.

## Contribution to Topic
Provides the genre-discrimination evidence the [[Klingberg-Sauce 2022 — Gaming and Children IQ]] aggregate study lacks: strategically deep games (MOBAs) align with the IQ construct, twitch shooters align with reaction speed. Supports placing MOBAs and strategy games above pure-reflex games for *intelligence* specifically.
