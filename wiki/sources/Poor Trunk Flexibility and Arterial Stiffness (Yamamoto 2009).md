# Poor Trunk Flexibility and Arterial Stiffness (Yamamoto 2009)

*Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol*, "Poor trunk flexibility is associated with arterial stiffening." According to PubMed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.00061.2009) (PMID 19666849).

## What it contributes

The mechanistic hint that flexibility may not be purely musculoskeletal — it co-varies with vascular aging.

- 526 adults split into young (20–39), middle-aged (40–59), older (60–83), each sorted into poor- vs high-flexibility by **sit-and-reach**.
- Arterial stiffness = brachial-ankle **pulse wave velocity (baPWV)**.
- In middle-aged and older groups, baPWV was **higher in the poor-flexibility group** (older: 1,485 vs 1,384 cm/s, *P* < 0.01). No difference in the young.
- Flexibility was an **independent correlate** of baPWV after adjusting for cardiorespiratory fitness, muscular strength, and age.

> [!gap] Cross-sectional design: it shows flexibility *tracks* arterial stiffness, not that stretching *reduces* it. Small later trials (e.g., regular static stretching lowering PWV) are suggestive but not definitive.

Backs [[Stretching and Flexibility for Longevity]]. The longevity case for flexibility is weaker than for [[Grip Strength and Longevity|strength]] or VO₂max, so flexibility earns its place via mobility/function, not as a primary mortality lever.
