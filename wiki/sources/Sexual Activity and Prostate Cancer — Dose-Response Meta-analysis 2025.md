# Sexual Activity and Prostate Cancer — Dose-Response Meta-analysis 2025

Updated meta-analysis (*BMC Cancer*, 2025) pooling sexual-behavior and prostate-cancer data.

## What it contributes

The most quantitatively rigorous answer to "does ejaculating / masturbating affect prostate cancer risk?"

**Scope:** 29 studies, 315,193 participants. (Source: BMC Cancer, 2025)

| Behavior | Pooled OR (95% CI) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Ejaculation frequency** | **0.83 (0.77–0.90)** | **Significant protective effect** (I²=50%) |
| Masturbation frequency | 1.12 (0.82–1.54) | Not significant (I²=0%) |
| Sexual intercourse frequency | 1.02 (0.88–1.18) | Not significant |
| Number of female partners | 1.01 (0.90–1.12) | Not significant (high heterogeneity) |
| Age at first intercourse | 0.99 (0.90–1.08) | Not significant |

## Interpretation
**Total ejaculation frequency** (across all sources) is protective — consistent with the landmark Harvard cohort (n=31,925) showing ~20% lower risk in highest-frequency men. But **masturbation frequency alone** is not significantly associated. The protective signal tracks *total ejaculation*, not the specific act of masturbation.

> [!gap] Earlier narrative reviews noted age-dependent effects (frequent masturbation a risk marker in 20s–30s, protective in 50s) — not resolved here.

## Confidence
High — large, recent, quantitative meta-analysis. The ejaculation-frequency protective effect has moderate heterogeneity; the masturbation null is very stable (I²=0%).
