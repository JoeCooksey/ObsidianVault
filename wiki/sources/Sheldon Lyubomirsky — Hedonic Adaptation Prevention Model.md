# Sheldon Lyubomirsky — Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model

The **Hedonic Adaptation Prevention (HAP) model**, developed by Kennon Sheldon and Sonja Lyubomirsky. Key publications: *"The Challenge of Staying Happier: Testing the Hedonic Adaptation Prevention Model"* (Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2012) and the model's formal statement in the *Oxford Handbook of Happiness* (Sheldon, Boehm & Lyubomirsky, 2013), revisited 2021.

## What It Contributes

Turns the fatalistic [[Hedonic Treadmill]] ("you always adapt back") into an *actionable* model by identifying the two levers that slow adaptation. This is the theoretical reason "falling in love with your life" must be a maintained practice rather than a one-time fix.

## Key Claims

- **Two erosion routes**: (1) *bottom-up* — the positive emotions a change generated decline over time; (2) *top-down* — aspirations rise, so the change becomes the new baseline and you want more. (high)
- **Two moderators that forestall erosion**: (1) **continued appreciation** of the original change (gratitude, savoring, attention to positives); (2) **continued variety** in change-related experiences. (medium-high — supported by their longitudinal tests)
- **"Variety is the spice of happiness"** — variety in the number and type of positive events keeps fresh positive emotion flowing, blocking bottom-up decline. (medium-high)
- Appreciation can be boosted by gratitude activities, attending to life's positives, and keeping reminders of past positive activities. (medium)

## Reliability

High-confidence on structure (clearly specified, tested longitudinally by the original authors). Effect magnitudes are study-specific. Operational forms of "appreciation" connect directly to [[Savoring]] and [[Gratitude Practice (Evidence Review)]].
