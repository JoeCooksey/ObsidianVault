# Micronutrients and Testosterone — Zinc, Vitamin D, Magnesium

Trial + review evidence on the three most-hyped "testosterone minerals."

## What it contributes
The key distinction: **deficiency correction ≠ boosting.**
- **Zinc**: 2022 systematic review — supplementation restores T in **zinc-deficient** men; zinc sulfate raised total and free T in deficient cohorts. No benefit when already replete.
- **Vitamin D**: strong *associational* link, but the double-blind Graz RCT (100 men, low T <10.4 nmol/L, 20,000 IU D3/week × 12 wk) found **no significant T effect** in men who weren't vitamin-D deficient.
- **Magnesium**: may support T bioactivity, especially combined with exercise; mechanism on synthesis poorly understood.

## Practical takeaway
Test levels, correct genuine deficiencies, and stop there. Don't expect supplementation to raise T in a man with normal nutrient status. Supports the "B-tier, corrective only" placement on [[Testosterone-Boosting Habits to Include]].
