# Hirschberg 2023 — Hyperandrogenism in a Postmenopausal Woman

Endocrine Society journal review of **androgen excess in women** — the signs, the differential, and the lab/imaging workup. Source for PubMed (PMID 36409990), DOI [10.1210/clinem/dgac673](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgac673).

## What it contributes to this topic

The clinical authority for **high testosterone signs in women** and the threshold that separates "common/benign" from "investigate for a tumor."

## Key claims

- **Hirsutism** (increased terminal hair in androgen-dependent areas — face, chest, back) is "the most effective measure of hyperandrogenism in women" (high).
- Other signs of androgen excess: **acne** and **androgenic alopecia** (scalp hair loss) (high).
- **Virilization** signals more severe/abrupt excess: **clitoromegaly**, voice deepening, increased muscle mass, breast atrophy (high).
- Androgen excess often travels with **metabolic disease**: abdominal obesity, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes (high).
- **PCOS is the most common cause** of hyperandrogenism, including after menopause; others: ovarian hyperthecosis, androgen-producing ovarian/adrenal tumors (high).
- **Threshold:** serum **testosterone > 5 nmol/L (≈ 144 ng/dL)** is associated with virilization and **requires prompt workup to rule out an androgen-producing tumor** (high). Measure total T, preferably by tandem mass spectrometry.

## Notes

Concordant with the 2024 menopause-society guideline (Fux-Otta et al., Climacteric, DOI [10.1080/13697137.2024.2423874](https://doi.org/10.1080/13697137.2024.2423874)): abrupt, severe signs + virilization + male-range testosterone → rule out tumor.
