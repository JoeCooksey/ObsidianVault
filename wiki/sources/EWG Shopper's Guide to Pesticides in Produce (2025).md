# EWG Shopper's Guide to Pesticides in Produce (2025)

The Environmental Working Group's annual ranking of fresh produce by pesticide-residue load, built on USDA Pesticide Data Program and FDA testing of ~47,000 samples. It is the de-facto "test done for you" that decides **which produce is worth buying organic**.

## What It Contributes

Gives a concrete, prioritized rule instead of "buy everything organic": buy the **Dirty Dozen** organic when affordable; buy the **Clean Fifteen** conventional and save the money.

### 2025 Dirty Dozen (highest residue — buy organic)
1. Spinach
2. Strawberries
3. Kale, collard & mustard greens
4. Grapes
5. Peaches
6. Cherries
7. Nectarines
8. Pears
9. Apples
10. Blackberries *(new in 2025)*
11. Blueberries
12. Potatoes *(new in 2025)*

### 2025 Clean Fifteen (lowest residue — conventional is fine)
1. Pineapple · 2. Sweet corn · 3. Avocados · 4. Papaya · 5. Onions · 6. Sweet peas · 7. Asparagus · 8. Cabbage · 9. Watermelon · 10. Cauliflower · 11. Bananas · 12. Mangoes · 13. Carrots · 14. Mushrooms · 15. Kiwi

### New 2025 methodology
For the first time EWG incorporated **individual pesticide toxicity** into the ranking — combining residue concentration with the EPA's No Adverse Effect Levels (NOAELs), on top of the old metrics (percent of samples contaminated, average pesticide count, total concentration). This shifted the list toward produce carrying *more toxic* pesticides, not just *more* of them.

### Headline stats
- **95%** of Dirty Dozen samples had detectable pesticide residue.
- Nearly **60%** of Clean Fifteen samples had **no** detectable residue; only ~16% had two or more pesticides.

## Caveats (confidence: medium)
- EWG is an **advocacy group**, not a regulator. Toxicologists note the absolute residue levels on conventional produce are almost always **far below EPA safety thresholds**, and that the methodology does not model real-world dose.
- The list does **not** mean "don't eat these foods." The health benefit of eating more fruits and vegetables outweighs the theoretical pesticide risk — washing conventional produce and eating it beats skipping it.
- A thin-skinned/leafy/berry pattern drives the Dirty Dozen (large surface area, eaten whole); thick-skinned or peeled items dominate the Clean Fifteen.
