# Pornography and Sexual Dysfunction — Integrative Review 2019

Integrative review (*Journal of Clinical Medicine*) of observational studies on pornography and male/female sexual function.

## What it contributes

A careful, source-by-source audit that deflates the popular "porn-induced ED" claim while preserving the real signals.

- **Erectile dysfunction:** little or no causal evidence. One longitudinal study linked *problematic* use to ED but acknowledged **reverse causality** (men with ED use more porn). Cross-sectional results mixed; some found no association in younger men. (Source: Dwulit & Rzymski, 2019)
- **Delayed ejaculation:** minimal evidence. Two large cross-sectional samples (Italian students *n*=1,492; European men *n*=3,948) found no significant relationship; confounders (masturbation technique, devices) prevent causal claims.
- **Sexual desire:** contradictory, gender-dependent; large European sample found no significant association on multivariate analysis. Men with lower desire tend to masturbate more regardless of porn.
- **Sexual satisfaction — strongest signal:** 20 of 23 cross-sectional studies showed lower satisfaction, especially in men; longitudinal results inconsistent/bidirectional. **Couples who use porn together report higher satisfaction.** Effects moderated by relationship status, attachment, age at first exposure, religiosity.

## Overall conclusion
Correlations exist; **causality is unestablished.** Prospective studies controlling for relationship quality, psychological factors, and masturbation frequency are urgently needed.

> [!gap] Almost all data are cross-sectional/self-reported. "Porn-induced ED" is plausible but not demonstrated causally.
