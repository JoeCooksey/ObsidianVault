# Debras 2022 — Artificial Sweeteners and CVD (NutriNet-Santé)

The landmark large-scale cohort behind much of the "diet soda → heart disease" headlines. Prospective French web cohort, **103,388 adults**, 2009–2021, 904,206 person-years. Intake from repeated 24-h dietary records (captures all sources, including brand names).

## Key Findings (According to PubMed)
- **Total artificial sweeteners → higher overall CVD risk**: HR **1.09** (95% CI 1.01–1.18), 1,502 events. Absolute incidence 346 vs 314 per 100,000 person-years (higher vs non-consumers).
- **Cerebrovascular (stroke) risk**: HR **1.18** (1.06–1.31).
- **Aspartame** → cerebrovascular events HR **1.17** (1.03–1.33).
- **Acesulfame-K** → coronary heart disease HR **1.40** (1.06–1.84); **sucralose** → CHD HR **1.31** (1.00–1.71).

## What It Actually Means
- **Observational**: shows *association*, not causation. Cannot rule out [[Reverse Causation in Sweetener Cohort Studies|reverse causation]] (at-risk people choosing diet drinks) or residual confounding despite adjustment.
- **Absolute risk is small**: ~32 extra CVD cases per 100,000 person-years — a modest signal, not a dramatic one.
- Cohort female-skewed (79.8%), self-selected health-conscious web volunteers → limited generalizability.
- Consistent in direction with [[Sun 2024 - Artificial Sweeteners and CVD (UK Biobank)]] but both are cohorts sharing the same confounding limitation.
