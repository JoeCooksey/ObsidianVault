# Jabbari et al. 2025 — Driving vs Undistracted Podcast Listening (Randomized Cross-over Trial)

*PLOS ONE*, 2025. The best direct test of the question every commuter cares about: do you actually learn anything from podcasts while driving?

## Method

- 19 medical residents + 22 undergraduates, McMaster University
- Randomized crossover; each participant listened to medical podcast content in three conditions: **seated at a desk**, **city driving** (high-load simulator), **country driving** (low-load simulator)
- Immediate recall (61–63 items/condition) + delayed recall at one month (40 items)

## Findings

- **Immediate recall: no significant overall difference across conditions** (η² = 0.011)
- Country driving slightly beat city driving (p < .05, η² = 0.018) — secondary-task *load* matters at the margin
- **Delayed (1-month) recall: no difference between conditions**; normal forgetting in all of them
- Residents outperformed students (prior knowledge effect), but expertise did not interact with condition

## Interpretation

Supports **threaded cognition**: auditory-verbal processing and visuospatial-motor driving draw on largely separate resources, so a practiced motor task costs little comprehension. Authors: "knowledge acquisition from a podcast is not compromised by the attention needed for driving a vehicle."

Boundary: this licenses *driving/walking/chores*, not attention-demanding secondary tasks (email, conversation, anything verbal). An earlier walking/jogging iPod study found seated listeners outperformed movers — the cost is small but nonzero when the body task is less automatic. And no multitasking study rescues you from the forgetting curve: 1-month decay was substantial in every condition.
