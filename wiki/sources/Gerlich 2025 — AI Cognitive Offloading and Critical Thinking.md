# Gerlich 2025 — AI Tool Use, Cognitive Offloading, and Critical Thinking

Study by **Michael Gerlich** (SBS Swiss Business School), published **3 January 2025** in the journal *Societies*. 666 survey participants + 50 interviews.

## Core Argument

Heavy reliance on AI tools correlates with **weaker critical thinking**, and the mediator is **cognitive offloading** — handing analytical, reasoning, and synthesis tasks to the machine instead of doing them yourself. *"The findings reveal a strong negative correlation between frequent AI tool usage and critical thinking abilities."* (confidence: high)

## Key Findings

- **Younger participants (17–25)** showed higher AI usage, more offloading, and lower critical-thinking scores. **Older participants (46+)** scored higher and depended less.
- **Higher education is a protective buffer** — well-educated users kept robust critical thinking despite frequent AI use.
- Recommendation: *"AI should complement cognitive engagement rather than replace it"* — design use so the human still does the thinking.

> [!warning] Correlation, not causation. The study cannot rule out that weaker thinkers simply prefer AI. But the offloading mechanism is consistent with RCT evidence that chatbot study aids impair retention by reducing cognitive effort.
