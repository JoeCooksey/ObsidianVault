# Adolescent Pornography Exposure and Sexual Behavior — Systematic Review 2023

Systematic review (*JMIR*) of pornography exposure and sexual behavior in adolescents aged 10–19.

## What it contributes

The developmental-harm angle — where the evidence is strongest for concern, though still largely correlational.

- **Scope:** 19 eligible studies; MEDLINE, PsycINFO, Cochrane, CINAHL, Embase, Web of Science, Jan 2010–Nov 2022. (Source: JMIR, 2023)
- **Earlier sexual debut:** 5 of 8 studies (including 1 longitudinal) found a statistically significant association with porn exposure.
- **Attitudes:** online porn use predicts greater willingness to engage in casual sex.
- **Behavioral progression:** high-use adolescents (both sexes) progressed faster to masturbation, petting, and manual sex than low-use peers.

## Limitations
Most studies were cross-sectional or had substantial limitations; exposure was measured inconsistently. **Causal inference is not possible.** Authors call for sex education that adopts evidence-based approaches to minimize potential harms.

> [!gap] Adolescent harm is the most plausible concern but rests largely on cross-sectional data. Reverse causation (earlier-maturing teens seek out porn) is not excluded.

## Confidence
Medium — rigorous review method, but the underlying studies are mostly cross-sectional and heterogeneous. The adolescent/developmental concern is taken seriously across the literature even where causality is unproven.
