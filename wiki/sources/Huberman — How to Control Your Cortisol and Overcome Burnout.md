# Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol & Overcome Burnout

**Andrew Huberman**, *Huberman Lab* podcast, Aug 4 2025. This is the episode behind the "cortisol masterclass" framing. The throughline: **cortisol regulates energy, glucose, and adrenal function, and the goal is to set its rhythm correctly — high in the morning, low at night — not to minimize it.**

## Key Claims

**Reframe.** Most people assume cortisol is harmful and that theirs is "too high." In reality many energy, focus, mood, and sleep problems stem from a **disrupted cortisol rhythm**, which can run either too high *or* too flat.

**Raise/time the morning peak:**
- View **sunlight within ~30–60 min of waking** (10,000-lux lamp as backup) to time the rise and improve daytime focus/mood.
- **Hydrate** on waking (water/electrolytes) to support the morning rise.
- **Delay caffeine 60–90 min** after waking to avoid blunting the natural peak and causing an afternoon crash.
- **Morning exercise** is an entrainment cue that reinforces the peak.

**Lower the evening/chronic level:**
- **Dim overhead/bright light in the evening**; use low, warm/red light to avoid late cortisol spikes that disrupt sleep.
- **Ashwagandha 300–600 mg**, late afternoon/evening/night, can lower cortisol ~11–29%; he cautions against taking it continuously long-term.
- A **~20 min sauna (80–100 °C)** or hot bath lowers cortisol.
- Other mentioned supports: apigenin, magnesium, rhodiola; emphasis-on-exhale breathing for the stress response.
- A modest **evening starch** can help; **very low-carb diets can raise cortisol** in some men.

**Burnout = two opposite patterns** needing opposite fixes:
- **Early-phase:** elevated morning cortisol → use NSDR/Yoga Nidra, manage stress.
- **Late-phase:** flattened/depleted cortisol → rebuild the morning peak (light, exercise, timed caffeine).
- Notes that *clinical burnout is not cleanly reflected in the CAR* — patterns vary by individual.
