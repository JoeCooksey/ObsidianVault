# Social Connection and Mortality (Holt-Lunstad)

Two landmark meta-analyses on social relationships and longevity.

## What it contributes

- **2010** (148 studies, ~308,000 people, *PLOS Medicine*): adequate social relationships → **~50% increased odds of survival** (≈26% lower premature-death risk), an effect **comparable to or larger than obesity, inactivity, and alcohol**.
- **2015** (*Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 3.4M people): **loneliness +26%, social isolation +29%, living alone +32%** mortality risk.

This is why **social connection is a top-tier longevity factor**, not a soft extra — it earns its own row in [[Research - Biohacking Masterclass (Every Habit by Frequency)]] alongside sleep and exercise.

## Caveat / contradiction

The popular **"loneliness = smoking 15 cigarettes/day"** soundbite is debated. A 2021 UK Biobank + ELSA analysis found **smoking a stronger mortality predictor** than isolation/loneliness — the protective *direction* is solid, but the exact magnitude is oversold.
