# Ho 2012 — Stopping or Reducing Dietary Fiber Reduces Constipation

## What It Is
A 6-month intervention study (May 2008–May 2010), **63 patients** with idiopathic constipation after colonoscopy excluded organic causes. Patients first followed a **no-fiber diet for 2 weeks**, then chose to remain no-fiber, reduce fiber to a tolerable level, or return to high fiber. Published in *World Journal of Gastroenterology*.

## Key Findings
- **No fiber:** bowel frequency rose from one motion / 3.75 days → **one motion / 1.0 day**.
- **Reduced fiber:** one motion / 4.19 days → one motion / 1.9 days.
- **High fiber (continued):** essentially unchanged at ~one motion / 6.83 days.
- **Bloating:** present in **0%** (no fiber), 31.3% (reduced), **100%** (high fiber).
- **Straining:** 0% / 43.8% / 100% across the same groups.

## What It Contributes
The single most-cited piece of evidence that for **idiopathic constipation specifically**, reducing or stopping fiber can relieve symptoms — the opposite of standard advice. The carnivore camp leans heavily on this study. It is a legitimate kernel of truth.

## Limits / Confidence
- **Not randomized or blinded**; self-selected groups; single center (Singapore); n=63.
- Population is **idiopathic-constipation patients**, not the general public — does not generalize to healthy guts.
- Says nothing about microbiome diversity, mucus barrier, or long-term outcomes — only symptom relief.
- Confidence: **medium** (real effect, weak design, narrow population).
