# Blurring the Picture in Leaky Gut Research (Zonulin Critique)

Peer-reviewed letter in *Gut* (BMJ), 2021. The sharpest published critique of zonulin as a commercial "leaky gut" test. Important counterweight to wellness-industry marketing of zonulin blood panels.

## What It Contributes

Zonulin is real biology — it is **pre-haptoglobin 2 (preHP2)**, a physiological modulator of intestinal tight junctions (Source: this letter). The problem is the *test*, not the concept.

## Key Claims

- **Commercial ELISAs don't measure zonulin.** The widely-sold kits were built from "the first published sequence, which later has been shown to be unrelated to the zonulin protein." Measurements "do not reflect actual zonulin levels, but concentrations of unknown proteins." (confidence: high)
- **Poor correlation with real permeability.** Zonulin ELISA vs. functional gut-permeability testing:
  - Halasa et al. 2019: R=0.11, p>0.05
  - Linsalata et al. 2018: R=0.17, p>0.05
  - Kuzma et al. 2020: R=0.033, p=0.79
- **Not expressed in mice** as preHP2 — undercutting much of the translational animal research.
- **Better alternatives:** dual-sugar assays (lactulose:mannitol), immunohistochemistry, and zonula occludens (ZO) protein expression profiles.

> [!warning] Practical takeaway
> A direct-to-consumer "zonulin" stool or blood test is **low-value**. The intestinal-permeability concept is legitimate; the popular biomarker for it is not. See [[Leaky Gut and Intestinal Permeability]] and [[Gut Health Tests]].
