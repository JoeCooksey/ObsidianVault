# Low-FODMAP Diet for IBS Evidence

## What It Is
The evidence base for the low-FODMAP diet in irritable bowel syndrome (Altobelli 2017 meta-analysis; Monash University, who developed the protocol; clinical summaries from Harvard Health, Yale, Cleveland Clinic).

## Key Findings
- **FODMAPs** (Fermentable Oligo-, Di-, Mono-saccharides And Polyols) are poorly absorbed in the small intestine, draw water into the gut, and ferment rapidly → gas, bloating, pain, altered bowel habits.
- A **low-FODMAP diet significantly reduces abdominal pain and bloating** vs high-FODMAP in IBS patients; one study found **76% reported improvement**; the 2017 meta-analysis confirmed symptom benefit.
- **High-FODMAP vegetables** include garlic, onions, asparagus, broccoli, cabbage, leeks, mushrooms.
- Sensitivity is **highly individual** — the diet works best as **elimination + structured reintroduction** to find personal triggers, not permanent blanket avoidance.

## What It Contributes
Explains *why* some people genuinely feel better cutting (certain) vegetables — and why the right move is targeted FODMAP management with reintroduction, not total plant elimination. A real kernel of truth inside the anti-vegetable claim.

## Confidence
**High** — meta-analysis + the protocol's originating institution. Caveat: low-FODMAP is intended as a short-term diagnostic elimination; long-term strict adherence can itself reduce beneficial fiber/microbiome diversity.
