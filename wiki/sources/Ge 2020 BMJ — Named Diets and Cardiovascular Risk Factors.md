# Ge 2020 (BMJ) — Comparison of 14 Popular Named Diets

*BMJ* network meta-analysis of **121 randomized trials (n=21,942)** comparing 14 popular diets for weight and cardiovascular risk factors (according to PubMed — Ge 2020, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m696)).

Most macronutrient patterns (low-carb, low-fat) and named diets (Atkins, DASH, Zone) produced **modest weight loss (~4–5 kg) and meaningful blood-pressure and lipid improvements at 6 months**. But a striking negative result for this topic: **no diet significantly improved CRP at 6 months**, and by 12 months the weight and cardiovascular benefits had **largely vanished** for every diet **except the Mediterranean diet**.

Contributes the important **contradiction/nuance**: short-term weight-loss dieting per se does not reliably lower CRP over 6 months, and diet effects tend to fade without sustained adherence. This tempers the "just lose weight" story and supports **dietary *pattern* (Mediterranean) and durability** over short-term calorie cutting for inflammation.
