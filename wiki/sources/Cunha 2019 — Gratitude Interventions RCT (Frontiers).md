# Cunha 2019 — Gratitude Interventions RCT (Frontiers)

*"Positive Psychology and Gratitude Interventions: A Randomized Clinical Trial"* — Cunha, Pellanda & Reppold, *Frontiers in Psychology* (2019). A randomized clinical trial (n ≈ 1,337) assigning participants to write daily gratitude lists for 14 days versus comparison conditions.

## What It Contributes

The **honest counterweight** to gratitude hype. It both confirms gratitude practice helps *and* surfaces the field's central caveat — making it the key citation for [[Gratitude Practice (Evidence Review)]].

## Key Findings

- **Gratitude worked**: the gratitude condition increased positive affect, subjective happiness, and life satisfaction, and reduced negative affect and depressive symptoms. (high)
- **The crucial caveat**: a **neutral "daily events" writing condition produced similar changes on most outcomes**. (high) → much of the benefit may come from *deliberate positive attention / structured reflection* rather than gratitude per se — the active-control problem that recurs across this literature.

## Reliability

High-confidence as a single large RCT. The "active ingredient" interpretation (attention vs. gratitude specifically) is the conservative reading and is consistent with broader meta-analytic findings of *small-to-medium* gratitude effects.

## Context

Sits alongside the meta-analytic evidence (25 RCTs / ~6,745 participants showing significant but modest gratitude effects; PNAS 2025 cross-cultural meta-analysis) that frames gratitude as **real but modest and conditional** — not the life-transforming lever popular media claims. Full synthesis in [[Gratitude Practice (Evidence Review)]].
