# Raw Milk Safety and H5N1 (CDC FDA)

The safety case against the raw-dairy pillar of [[Animal-Based Diet (Saladino)]]. Saladino recommends ~1 L raw milk daily; this is the component with the clearest, least-disputed downside.

## Findings

- **H5N1 bird flu**: FDA detected the virus in raw milk across four states starting April 2024, including commercially sold raw milk; H5N1 has *not* been found in pasteurized milk (milk from sick cows is diverted; pasteurization inactivates the virus). Mouse studies show untreated infected milk transmits infection — human transmission via drinking is unconfirmed but plausible.
- **Classic pathogens**: Salmonella, E. coli O157, Campylobacter, Listeria, Brucella, Cryptosporidium — the original reasons pasteurization exists.
- **Raw-milk cheese loophole closed**: a 2025 study found H5N1 remains infectious in raw-milk cheese for months — the legal 60-day aging rule does not make it safe.
- **Awareness gap**: only ~17% of US adults know H5N1 has been found only in raw milk (2025 survey).

## The Counter-Claim and Its Status

Saladino's claim that raw dairy has "superior nutritional value" is largely unsupported: pasteurization causes minimal nutrient loss (small reductions in vitamin C and some B vitamins; protein, calcium, and fat-soluble vitamins essentially unchanged). The claimed enzyme/probiotic benefits lack human outcome evidence. The nutrition delta is tiny; the pathogen delta is not.

> [!gap] No data on how often Saladino-style sourced raw milk (small herd, tested) actually causes illness vs. commodity raw milk — risk is clearly lower with testing, but not zero, and H5N1 changed the baseline.
