# Nutrient Retention by Cooking Method

How cooking changes the **nutrient** content of food (separate from toxin formation). The losses are mostly **water-soluble vitamins** (C and the B's, including folate) and some heat-sensitive antioxidants.

## What Wins for Nutrient Retention

| Method | Retention | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Microwave** | Excellent (>90% vit C) | Short time + little/no water = mildest loss |
| **Steam** | Excellent (9–15% vit C loss) | Food above water, not submerged; top method for greens |
| **Sous vide** | Excellent | Sealed, low temp, no leaching |
| **Stir-fry / sauté (quick)** | Good | Fast + minimal liquid; fat aids fat-soluble vitamin uptake |
| **Roast/bake** | Moderate | Some vitamin loss to heat; no leaching |
| **Pressure cook** | Good | Short time offsets high heat |
| **Boil** | Poor (up to 50–70% vit C) | Water-soluble vitamins leach into the water |

## Key Nuances
- **Boiling's loss is recoverable** if you **keep the liquid** (soups, stews) — the vitamins are *in the water*, not destroyed.
- **Cooking INCREASES some nutrients' bioavailability:**
  - **Tomatoes** → cooking boosts absorbable **lycopene**.
  - **Carrots, sweet potato, spinach** → cooking frees **beta-carotene**.
  - Cooking also deactivates anti-nutrients (oxalates, lectins, goitrogens) and kills pathogens.
- **Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)** are stable to heat/water but benefit from a little fat in the dish for absorption.
- **Omega-3s in fish** are heat- and oxidation-sensitive → gentle methods (bake/steam/poach) preserve them; deep-frying degrades them.

## The Four Dials (minimize loss)
1. **Less water** (steam/microwave > boil).
2. **Less time** (cook just to tender).
3. **Less cut surface area** (cook whole/large, cut after).
4. **Lower heat** where possible (also cuts toxins).
> Exception: a few foods (tomato, carrot) are *better* cooked — retention isn't the only goal; bioavailability matters too.

## Bottom Line (confidence: high)
**Steam or microwave vegetables; keep the broth when you boil; eat a mix of raw and cooked.** No single method is best for everything — but submerged boiling with the water poured off is the one to avoid for nutrient-dense greens.
