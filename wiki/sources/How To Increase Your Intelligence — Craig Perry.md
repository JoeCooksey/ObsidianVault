> [!tldr]
> A ~42-minute video essay by Substack writer **[[Craig Perry]]** arguing that "intelligence" is really the quality of your **[[Good Explanations (Hard-to-Vary)|explanations]]** — the stories you tell yourself about reality — and that bad ones are invisible and "secretly wreck your life." He builds a practical map from **[[Ken Wilber]]**'s **[[AQAL Model (Integral Theory)|Integral Theory]]**: the **[[Four Quadrants (AQAL)|four quadrants]]** (what to look at), the **[[Nine Levels of Thinking]]** (how clearly you see), and the **[[Lines of Development]]** (where your growth is uneven). The payoff is a 10–20 minute daily **[[Explanation-Building Writing Practice|writing practice]]** to make explanations visible, test them against all four quadrants, and stress-test them. Filed **medium confidence** — a creator's personal synthesis of Wilber/Deutsch/developmental psychology, useful as a thinking framework rather than as established science.

## Core Thesis — Explanations Run Your Life
Perry opens with the David Deutsch line that *"the only way of thinking capable of making progress… is seeking good explanations through creativity and criticism."* His claim: **every thought about what happened, and every story about who you are, is an explanation.** [[Good Explanations (Hard-to-Vary)|Good explanations are "hard to vary"]] and survive pressure; bad ones collapse when the right pressure hits the right spot — but until then they feel "like the way things are." They're invisible, so their only visible symptom is the suffering they cause. He frames his own past (anxious, high-neuroticism (Big Five), believing whatever others told him he was) as an "explanation problem," not a confidence or discipline problem, and cites **[[Carl Jung]]**: *"The world will ask you who you are, and if you do not know, the world will tell you."* The leverage is that explanations are **100% free, need no tools or permission, and can be rebuilt anywhere, anytime.**

## The Map of Knowledge — AQAL and the Four Quadrants
To build better explanations you need a **map of all knowledge**, and Perry adopts **[[Ken Wilber]]**'s **[[AQAL Model (Integral Theory)|AQAL model]]** (the foundation of [[AQAL Model (Integral Theory)|Integral Theory]]). It splits any situation along two axes — subjective/objective and individual/collective — into the **[[Four Quadrants (AQAL)|four quadrants]]**: **I** (inner thoughts/emotions/beliefs), **it** (the body, brain, nervous system — the hard sciences), **we** (shared culture, values, group beliefs), and **its** (systems, structures, institutions). Worked example: a "motivation problem" looks like a personal failing in quadrant I, but quadrant **it** reveals chronic poor sleep and high [[Cortisol Complete Guide|cortisol]], quadrant **we** reveals inherited beliefs about what "effort" should look like, and quadrant **its** reveals a smartphone engineered to drain attention. *"You cannot solve a four-quadrant problem from a one-quadrant level of thinking."* **Stupid thinking = looking through one open window and thinking you see the whole planet** — which is why specialists ("smart people") are often the quickest to make the dumbest decisions in domains outside their one quadrant.

## The Levels — How Clearly You See
The quadrants tell you *what* to look at; the **[[Nine Levels of Thinking]]** determine *how clearly* you see each one. Two people viewing the same inner world can see completely different things depending on their altitude of awareness. Perry chunks the nine levels into three groups: **subconscious** (1 Survive, 2 Connection, 3 Control — ~5% of people, mostly children and "adult children"); **conscious** (4 Belonging, 5 Achieve — ~75%, the ceiling of formal education and the Western-success paradigm); and **integrated** (6 Included, 7 Harmonize, 8 Construct-aware, 9 Unitive — ~15%, reachable only through deliberate self-development). Higher levels can see down; lower levels can't see up — echoing **[[Albert Einstein]]**'s line that you can't solve a problem with the thinking that created it. He links level 8 (watching your own mind build explanations in real time) and level 9 (egoless, self-dissolving awareness as pointed to by **[[Alan Watts]]** and **[[Terence McKenna]]**) to a meditation experience of his own.

## The Lines — Why You're Uneven
You aren't at one level permanently; you're at many levels at once across the **[[Lines of Development]]** — cognitive, emotional, moral, interpersonal, spiritual, aesthetic, kinesthetic, values, needs, self-identity, worldview, and more (Wilber names over a dozen). You develop along all of them simultaneously but at different rates, so you can be level 7 cognitively and level 3 emotionally. Examples: the £100M founder who is cognitively brilliant but emotionally undeveloped and becomes depressed after the exit; the "gym bro" maxed on the kinesthetic line but stuck at level 3 interpersonally ("women are impossible to understand"); the gifted artist who can't market the work ("the world doesn't value real art"). Lines also explain **regression** — stress, hunger, or sleep deprivation drops you several levels on whichever line is under pressure.

## The Practice — Writing to Examine Explanations
The whole framework reduces to one cheap daily habit: **[[Explanation-Building Writing Practice|writing]]** — not journaling or gratitude lists, but using writing to make explanations *examinable*. *"You cannot edit a thought you cannot see."* He favors **outcomes over tasks** (binary, yes/no, "pure physics") and gives four daily questions: (1) Did you write down one explanation? (2) Did you examine it through all four quadrants? (3) Did you identify which line of development it touches? (4) Did you find one piece of evidence that *challenges* it (stress-test, not destroy)? He demos the loop on a real event — one negative YouTube comment that derailed his morning — converting *"it wasn't good enough, so I'm not good enough"* into *"one person offered feedback shaped by their own level and lines; it may or may not be useful, and is not an absolute verdict."* That shift from a level-4 ("my group must approve of me") to a level-7 explanation is what "moving up the levels" looks like in practice.

## Assessment
> [!note] Confidence: medium. This is a creator's personal synthesis, not peer-reviewed science. The backbone — **[[Ken Wilber]]**'s [[AQAL Model (Integral Theory)|Integral Theory]] and its developmental "levels" — is an influential but **contested** integral-philosophy framework (the nine levels track Spiral Dynamics / ego-development models more than mainstream psychology), and "X% of the population at each level" figures are illustrative, not measured. The **[[Good Explanations (Hard-to-Vary)|hard-to-vary explanations]]** idea is genuinely from **[[David Deutsch]]**'s philosophy of science. The most defensible, immediately useful piece is the **[[Explanation-Building Writing Practice|writing practice]]**, which overlaps with well-supported ideas (externalizing thought, perspective-taking, evidence-against-the-claim reasoning). Treat the map as a **thinking tool**, not a validated theory of intelligence.
