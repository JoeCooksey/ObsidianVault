# Pornography Use and Psychological Science: A Call for Consideration (Grubbs & Kraus, 2021)

Influential review in *Current Directions in Psychological Science* arguing for nuance in how psychology treats pornography.

## What it contributes

The central, evidence-based correction to popular "porn is universally harmful / addictive" narratives:

- Effects of pornography **range from positive to neutral to negative**, moderated by context (relationship status, sexual orientation, attachment, beliefs). (Source: Grubbs & Kraus, 2021)
- **High-frequency use is not always — or even often — problematic.** Frequency alone does not predict distress.
- **Moral incongruence** — dissonance from using pornography while morally disapproving of it — is "one of the most robust and consistent predictors of self-reported addiction." Religiosity predicts *perceived* addiction better than actual use does. See [[Moral Incongruence (Pornography)]].

**What evidence supports:** correlational links to lower sexual satisfaction, greater objectification, sexual scripts; relationship instability in some samples.

**What evidence does NOT establish:** causal harm, the validity of a formal "porn addiction" diagnosis, or that distress reflects genuine dysregulation rather than moral conflict.

> [!gap] This is a perspective/review piece; it summarizes correlational literature. Causal claims in either direction remain unproven.

## Confidence
High — peer-reviewed, widely cited, from leading researchers in the field. Represents the mainstream academic-skeptical position on the addiction model.
