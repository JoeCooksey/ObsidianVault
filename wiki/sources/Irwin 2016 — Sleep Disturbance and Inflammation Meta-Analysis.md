# Irwin 2016 — Sleep Disturbance, Sleep Duration, and Inflammation

*Biological Psychiatry* systematic review and meta-analysis of **72 studies (n>50,000)** — the definitive map of the sleep–inflammation link (according to PubMed — Irwin 2016, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.05.014)).

Findings:
- **Sleep disturbance** (insomnia, poor quality) → higher **CRP** (ES 0.12) and **IL-6** (ES 0.20).
- **Sleep duration is U-shaped**: short sleep → higher CRP (ES 0.09); the extreme of **long** sleep → higher CRP (ES 0.17) and IL-6.
- **Acute experimental sleep deprivation/restriction did not raise** CRP, IL-6, or TNF-α — the inflammatory cost comes from *chronic* disturbance and habitual short/long sleep, not a single night.

Contributes the claim that **habitual poor sleep and both sleep extremes raise hs-CRP**, while establishing that the effect is a chronic-exposure phenomenon.
