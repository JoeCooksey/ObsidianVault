# Dacher Keltner — Awe (2023)

*Awe: The New Science of Everyday Wonder and How It Can Transform Your Life* (Penguin, 2023) by UC Berkeley psychologist [[Dacher Keltner]]. The definitive popular synthesis of two decades of awe research from Keltner's lab and the Greater Good Science Center.

## What It Contributes

Reframes awe from rare/grand to **everyday, frequent, and free** — the key reason it belongs in any answer to "how to fall in love with your life." Establishes awe as a renewable daily resource rather than a special-occasion emotion.

## Key Claims

- **Frequency**: daily-diary studies show people feel awe **2–3× per week** (~every couple of days). (high)
- **Eight wonders of life**: moral beauty, collective effervescence (moving together), nature, music, visual design, spirituality/religion, life & death, and epiphany. Most common source is **moral beauty** — other people's kindness, courage, and skill. (high)
- **The "small self"**: awe shrinks self-focus and quiets rumination/the default mode network — interrupting the comparison engine behind hedonic adaptation. (medium-high)
- **Documented benefits** (from associated lab work, see the Greater Good review by Summer Allen, 2018): greater life satisfaction; lower interleukin-6 (inflammation); better critical reasoning; reduced materialism; more humility; expanded time perception; more generosity; stronger social connection. (mixed — strong as a cluster, individual effects on smaller samples)

## How To Use It (for the wiki)

The practical takeaways — awe walks, noticing moral beauty, nature, music — are filed in [[Awe (Everyday Wonder)]]. The author profile is [[Dacher Keltner]].

## Reliability

High-confidence as a synthesis: Keltner is the field's leading researcher and the book is built on peer-reviewed work. Note the standard popular-science caveat — *individual* dramatic findings (specific intervention magnitudes, inflammation) rest on smaller studies and are directionally rather than definitively established.
