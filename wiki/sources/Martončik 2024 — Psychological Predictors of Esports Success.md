# Martončik 2024 — Psychological Predictors of Esports Success

**Published:** *Collabra: Psychology*, May 2024. Confirmatory **registered report** — hypotheses and analyses pre-registered before data collection, which makes the null results unusually trustworthy.

## Design & Findings
- Sample: N = 411 League of Legends players, N = 186 CS:GO players.
- Predictors tested against in-game performance/rank: deliberate practice, non-deliberate practice hours, age, intelligence, reaction time, persistence, game-specific attention.
- **Deliberate practice: null.** r = -.01 (LoL), r = .02 (CS:GO). Structured, goal-directed practice did not predict rank. (confidence: high)
- **Non-deliberate practice hours: r = .26 in LoL.** Sheer volume of ordinary play was the meaningful practice-related predictor. (confidence: high)
- **Age: negative.** r = -.22 (LoL), -.33 (CS:GO) — younger players rank higher. Matches the ~25 skill peak in [[York 2017 — MOBA Skill and Fluid Intelligence]].
- Intelligence, reaction time, and persistence: null or inconclusive in both games.

## Interpretation & Caveats
- Authors point to game design and "esports-specific information density" as why predictors differ across titles.
- Cross-sectional self-report of practice type; "deliberate practice" is hard to measure by questionnaire — players may mislabel their practice. The null may partly reflect measurement, not mechanism.
- Directly challenges naive application of [[Deliberate Practice]] (Ericsson) to esports: in LoL, playing a lot beat practicing "correctly."

## Contribution to Topic
The strongest counterweight to standard improvement-guide orthodoxy: most guides prescribe drills, VOD review, and structured goals, but the best-controlled study finds volume of play is what tracks rank. See [[LoL Improvement Practice System]] for the reconciliation.
