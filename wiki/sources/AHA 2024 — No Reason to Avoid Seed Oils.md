# AHA 2024 — No Reason to Avoid Seed Oils

Position statement from the American Heart Association (Aug 2024), corroborated by Harvard Health and a Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health evidence review (2025). Together they form the authoritative counterweight to the online "seed oils are toxic" narrative.

## What it contributes
The evidence base for [[Seed Oils Health Debate]] — omega-6 / linoleic-acid health effects.

## Key findings
- Harvard analysis (38 studies, >810,000 people): highest linoleic-acid intake → **13% lower** all-cause and CVD mortality.
- Higher linoleic-acid levels → ~**35% lower** type-2-diabetes risk; improved glucose metabolism.
- 2012 meta-analysis (15 RCTs): **no** increase in inflammatory or oxidative-stress markers from linoleic acid.
- AHA, Harvard, and Johns Hopkins all conclude there is no good reason to avoid seed oils; the real concern is their concentration in **ultra-processed foods**.

## Caveats
> [!gap] Not unanimous: some Mendelian-randomization and RCT data are mixed on active cardiovascular *benefit* (per STAT News, 2025). Consensus is "no evidence of harm," not "settled benefit at all doses."
