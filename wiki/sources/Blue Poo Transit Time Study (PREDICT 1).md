# Blue Poo Transit Time Study (PREDICT 1)

**Asnicar et al., *Gut* (BMJ) 2021** — the ZOE/King's College London "blue poop" study. Single-arm, single-blinded intervention inside the PREDICT 1 trial (June 2018 – May 2019). 1,102 enrolled participants ate two blue-dyed muffins and logged the first blue stool in an app; transit time = muffin-to-blue-stool interval.

## What it contributes

- **The number:** median gut transit time = **28.7 hours**; individual range 12 hours to several days. Four classes: fast ~0.4 days, normal ~1 day and ~2 days, slow ~4.2 days.
- **Transit time beats stool metrics.** Microbiome composition discriminated transit-time classes with AUC 0.82; machine-learning classification favored transit time (AUC 0.72) over Bristol Stool Form (AUC 0.65). Transit time explained more microbiome variation than frequency or consistency.
- **Species signatures:** long transit → more *Akkermansia muciniphila*, *Bacteroides* spp., *Alistipes* spp. (mucus-degrading / protein-fermenting shift when food runs out). Short transit → more *Eubacterium rectale* (saccharolytic, fiber-fermenting).
- **Health link:** longer transit time independently associated with higher visceral fat and larger post-meal glucose and triglyceride spikes — both cardiometabolic risk factors.

## Caveats

- Observational associations; transit time is a marker, not a proven causal lever.
- Cohort skewed toward health-engaged UK adults (PREDICT volunteers).
- The study does not declare a single "optimal" transit range — the ~14–58h healthy band quoted elsewhere comes from older radiopaque-marker literature.
