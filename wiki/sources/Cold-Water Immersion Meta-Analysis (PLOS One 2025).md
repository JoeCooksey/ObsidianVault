# Cold-Water Immersion Meta-Analysis (PLOS One, 2025)

Systematic review and meta-analysis of cold-water immersion (CWI) effects on health and wellbeing. Pooled ~3,177 participants across 11 studies.

## What it contributes

This is the most-cited recent attempt to quantify what cold plunges actually do. The headline nuance: CWI is **acutely pro-inflammatory** (not anti-inflammatory as marketed), and its stress/wellbeing benefits are **delayed and short-lived**.

## Findings by outcome

- **Inflammation** — Acute *increase* immediately and at 1h (SMD 1.03 and 1.26, large effects). The body mounts an inflammatory/stress response; chronic anti-inflammatory benefit is not demonstrated.
- **Stress** — No change immediately, 1h, 24h, or 48h. A significant reduction appears only at **12h** (SMD −1.00) — i.e., a transient adaptation window. (Source: this meta-analysis)
- **Sleep** — One study showed improvement vs passive recovery; male-only sample limits generalizability.
- **Immunity** — No significant change at 0h/1h in meta-analysis; narrative synthesis flags a large cold-shower trial (n=3,018) with **29% fewer sickness absences**.
- **Mood** — The one eligible RCT found no significant difference vs control, despite positive non-RCT reports.

## Caveats

> [!gap] Evidence base is thin: only 11 studies, mostly single-exposure designs, small samples, heterogeneous protocols, and only one study including women. Long-term effects are unproven.

Separately, a **2024 meta-analysis found CWI immediately after resistance training blunts muscle hypertrophy** — a reason not to ice-bath right after lifting if growth is the goal.
