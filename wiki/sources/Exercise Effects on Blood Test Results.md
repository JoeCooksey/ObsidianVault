# Exercise Effects on Blood Test Results

Why a hard workout before a blood draw corrupts the read — the evidence behind the "no intense exercise 48–72 h" rule.

## What it contributes

- **CK (creatine kinase):** intense exercise is the **#1 cause** of transient elevation; even moderate lifting/running/cycling raises CK noticeably within **24–48 h**. Avoid strenuous exercise **≥48 h** (some say 72 h) before a CK draw.
- **Liver enzymes (AST, ALT):** skeletal muscle contains AST and ALT, so muscle damage from training leaks them into blood — a hard session can manufacture "elevated liver enzymes" in someone with a perfectly healthy liver. This is the most common benign cause of mildly high ALT/AST in lean athletes.
- **Sex hormones:** a short intense bout transiently *raises* testosterone for a few hours, but **prolonged/very intense** exercise can leave testosterone reading **low/low-normal if tested within 48 h**; cortisol reads high. Both distort the testosterone:cortisol ratio.
- **Practical rule:** avoid strenuous activity **48 h** before testing sex hormones, CK, and liver panels; draw cortisol/testosterone in the morning before any activity; log recent training so a still-elevated CK/ALT can be interpreted in context.
