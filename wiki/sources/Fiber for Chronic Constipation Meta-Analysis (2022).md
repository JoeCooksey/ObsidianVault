# Fiber for Chronic Constipation Meta-Analysis (2022)

Updated systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs of fiber supplementation for chronic constipation in adults (*Am J Clin Nutr* / PMC9535527).

## Key findings

- **Psyllium is the clear winner.** Greater treatment response than other fibers — **RR 1.82** (95% CI 1.51–2.20; P < 0.00001), no heterogeneity. Increased stool frequency by **~3 bowel movements/week** — comparable to conventional laxatives.
- **Dose threshold:** fiber doses **>10 g/day** significantly improved response, frequency, and consistency. ≤10 g/day showed minimal effect.
- **Duration:** **≥4 weeks** optimal for stool frequency and gut transit; shorter trials ineffective.
- **Consistency:** fiber improved stool consistency (SMD 0.32; P < 0.0001), psyllium strongest.
- **Pectin** showed promise in one trial (RR 3.71) but limited data.
- **Less effective:** polydextrose, inulin-type fructans, galacto-oligosaccharides did little for stool frequency.
- **Adverse effect:** fiber **worsened flatulence**, especially fermentable types (inulin-type fructans).

## Contribution to the topic

Provides the high-confidence clinical backbone for ranking **psyllium S-tier** and demoting purely fermentable prebiotic fibers (inulin, polydextrose) for the regularity use-case. Establishes the practical dosing rule: **>10 g/day for ≥4 weeks.**
