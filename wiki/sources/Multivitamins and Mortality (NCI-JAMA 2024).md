# Multivitamins and Mortality (NCI/JAMA 2024)

**Loftfield et al., NCI Division of Cancer Epidemiology and Genetics. Published June 26, 2024, in *JAMA Network Open*.** Study type: pooled analysis of three large prospective cohorts. Confidence: **high** (large, long, bias-aware).

## What they did
Analyzed **390,124 generally healthy U.S. adults** across three geographically diverse prospective cohorts, followed for **>20 years**, examining regular multivitamin use vs overall mortality and death from cardiovascular disease and cancer.

## Findings
- **No mortality benefit.** "People who took daily multivitamins did not have a lower risk of death from any cause than people who took no multivitamins."
- No difference in death from **cancer, heart disease, or cerebrovascular disease.**
- The scale and follow-up were specifically chosen to **mitigate biases** (healthy-user effect; sicker people starting supplements) that confounded earlier studies.

## What it contributes to the topic
This is the strongest recent evidence that, for already-healthy adults eating a normal diet, a daily multivitamin **does not extend life** — a central data point for the whole-foods-vs-supplements comparison. It does not address treatment of diagnosed deficiencies (see [[When Supplements Are Justified]]).
