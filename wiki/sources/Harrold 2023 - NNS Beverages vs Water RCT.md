# Harrold 2023 — NNS Beverages vs Water (52-Week RCT)

One of the longest randomized trials directly testing the diet-soda-vs-water question — the cleanest counterpoint to the scary cohort headlines. Parallel-group, open-label equivalence trial, **493 adults (BMI 27–35)** in a behavioral weight-management programme; NNS beverages vs water for 52 weeks.

## Key Findings (According to PubMed)
- Weight loss maintained at 52 weeks: **water 6.1 kg vs NNS beverages 7.5 kg** — difference 1.4 kg **in favor of NNS beverages** (90% CI −2.6 to −0.2; p<0.05).
- The two were **statistically non-equivalent**, i.e. diet drinks were *slightly better* than water, though the authors note the gap is **not clinically significant**.
- Funded in part by industry (ILSI Europe) — note conflict of interest, but the direction agrees with other RCTs (e.g. CHOICE, Peters).

## What It Means
- When you actually **randomize** people, replacing sugary drinks (or drinking NNS drinks alongside a weight programme) does **not** sabotage weight loss versus water, and may modestly help — likely via easier adherence.
- This is the RCT half of the [[RCT vs Cohort Evidence Split (Nonsugar Sweeteners)|central contradiction]]: short-term trials favor sweeteners, long-term cohorts flag harm.
