# Waldinger Schulz — The Good Life (Harvard Study)

*The Good Life: Lessons from the World's Longest Scientific Study of Happiness* (2023) by [[Robert Waldinger]] and Marc Schulz, synthesizing the **Harvard Study of Adult Development** — a longitudinal study running since **1938**, now following 1,300+ descendants of the original participants.

## What It Contributes

The empirical backbone for the **relationships** pillar of a life worth loving. Where savoring/awe/gratitude are about *attention*, this source is about *what to attend to and invest in over a lifetime* — and the answer is people.

## Key Claims

- **Close relationships are the #1 predictor** of who stays happy and healthy into old age — outpredicting money, fame, social class, IQ, and genes. (high — 80+ years of longitudinal data)
- **Good relationships protect body and mind**, delaying physical and cognitive decline. (high)
- **Quality over quantity** — a few reliable, emotionally supportive connections matter more than a large network. (high)
- **Loneliness is toxic** — its health impact is comparable to smoking or obesity. (high)
- Other contributors to a good life named: emotional resilience/positive outlook, healthy lifestyle (exercise, diet, not smoking, moderate alcohol), social engagement, and purposeful work. (high)

## Reliability

High-confidence. One of the most comprehensive longitudinal studies ever conducted; findings are widely replicated in the broader social-connection literature (cf. Holt-Lunstad meta-analyses already in the vault).
