# Maries & Singh 2023 — Helping Students Become Proficient Problem Solvers

A research synthesis (review, arXiv:2304.05588) on how students become proficient physics problem solvers. Chandralekha Singh (University of Pittsburgh) is a leading physics-education researcher.

## What it contributes

Frames problem-solving development around **three research domains**:

1. **Knowledge organization** — experts hold richly organized schemas in long-term memory and categorize problems by deep principle (e.g., "this is a conservation-of-energy problem"); novices categorize by surface features (pulleys, inclines). (Source: builds on Chi, Feltovich & Glaser 1981)
2. **Information processing / cognitive load** — novices overload working memory because they lack chunks; instruction must manage load (worked examples, scaffolding). (Source: [[Cognitive Load Theory]])
3. **Metacognition + heuristics** — explicit problem-solving frameworks (Pólya-style) plus self-monitoring must be *taught*, not assumed.

## Key takeaway

Problem-solving skill in a discipline is not a free-floating talent — it is **organized domain knowledge + a deliberate process + metacognition**, developed together. Feeds [[Expert–Novice Differences in Problem Solving]], [[Metacognition in Problem Solving]], and [[Research: Best Way to Develop Problem-Solving Skills]].
