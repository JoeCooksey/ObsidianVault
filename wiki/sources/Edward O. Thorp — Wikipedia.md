# Edward O. Thorp / Princeton Newport Partners — Wikipedia

## What this source contributes

Primary reference for the origin of quantitative investing and statistical arbitrage, predating Renaissance.

## Key Claims

- **Princeton/Newport Partners (1969–1989)** achieved **~20% annualized return after fees over ~two decades without a single down quarter**, on market-neutral derivatives hedging (convertible-bond and warrant arbitrage). (confidence: high)
- Thorp is credited with **inventing and implementing statistical arbitrage**. (confidence: high)
- His second fund, **Ridgeline Partners (1994–2002)**, ran a stat-arb strategy; Thorp **wound it down in 2002 because the strategy had become crowded** — too many funds competing for the same anomalies shrank returns. (confidence: high)
- Thorp first proved his quantitative edge by **beating blackjack with card counting** (*Beat the Dealer*, 1962) before applying probability to markets. (confidence: high)

## Notes

The Ridgeline wind-down is the single most instructive fact for a retail investor: **edges decay as they are discovered**. A quant strategy is not a permanent money machine; it is an arbitrage that competition eventually arbitrages away. This is the structural reason quant alpha is rare and guarded in secrecy.
