# Research — Staying On Top of News Impartially

Autoresearch synthesis: 8 searches, 4 pages created. Topic: step-by-step guide to staying on top of news while remaining impartial.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Passive consumption is the enemy, not the news itself
70% of news consumption is now passive (social media scrolling). Passive consumption optimizes for engagement, not comprehension, and is directly linked to higher anxiety and lower retention. The fix is a curated, intentional reading system — RSS readers, scheduled windows, and AI digests — not more willpower.

### Finding 2: No source is unbiased — triangulation is the only answer
Every independent rating system (MBFC, AllSides, Ad Fontes) confirms: no single outlet is bias-free. The correct strategy is to build a source stack that represents the factual center (Reuters, AP, PBS) and supplement with outlets from multiple directions for context and depth.

### Finding 3: Wire services are the factual gold standard
Reuters and AP receive "Least Biased / Very High Factuality" ratings from MBFC. Their business model (selling content to outlets across the political spectrum) structurally incentivizes accuracy and neutrality. Use wire service summaries as the factual skeleton of any story.

### Finding 4: Ground News is the best single anti-bias tool
Ground News aggregates 50,000+ sources, tags every story with bias/factuality/ownership, and — uniquely — shows you who is NOT covering a story (the "blindspot" detector). It is the most effective tool for breaking out of echo chambers and understanding how the same event looks from different political viewpoints.

### Finding 5: AllSides is the best free balanced aggregator
AllSides structures its daily curation at exactly 33% Left / 33% Center / 34% Right by design. Its media bias ratings use multi-partisan editorial reviews plus blind bias surveys (participants rate content without knowing the source). Most other aggregators (Google News, Apple News, SmartNews) skew Left or Left-leaning per 2026 AllSides analysis.

### Finding 6: The 30-min/day threshold is research-backed
Studies link more than 30 minutes/day of news consumption to measurable anxiety increases. The average person spends 2.5 hours — 5× the healthy threshold. Doomscrolling exploits the brain's evolutionary negativity bias (developed for physical threat detection, not 24/7 news cycles). The protocol: two scheduled windows, 15–20 min each, no passive scrolling.

### Finding 7: Breaking news is the least accurate news
Initial reports on fast-moving events are frequently wrong. The "overnight test" — waiting 24–48 hours before accepting a narrative — dramatically improves information quality. This is especially important during politically charged events where partisan framing is fastest.

### Finding 8: Media literacy is a learnable skill with specific techniques
The SIFT method (Stop, Investigate, Find better coverage, Trace claims) from Stanford and the University of Washington is the most concise evidence-based framework. Key skills: recognizing emotional framing (outrage = proceed with caution), checking source selection (who is quoted vs. missing), and distinguishing story selection bias from factual inaccuracy.
