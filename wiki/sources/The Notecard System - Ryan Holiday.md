# The Notecard System — Ryan Holiday (2014)

Ryan Holiday's account of the **commonplace / notecard system** he learned as [[Robert Greene]]'s research assistant. It is a hand-built, analog cousin of the [[Niklas Luhmann|Zettelkasten]], optimized for writers who need to *retrieve and reuse* what they read.

## The Ritual (the whole system is the ritual)

1. **Read with a pen.** Mark passages, underline, write marginalia in the margins.
2. **Flag locations.** Fold page corners or use sticky flags where notes live.
3. **Wait 2–3 weeks** after finishing the book before processing. *"Waiting helps you separate the wheat from the chaff."* (Source: ryanholiday.net)
4. **Transcribe keepers onto cards.** Return to the flagged passages and write the best onto **4×6 ruled index cards**.

## What Goes on a Card

> *"Either sentences in my own writing, words I like, questions I have, or examples I think might fit somewhere."* (Source: ryanholiday.net)

- Quotes — with the **opening quotation mark circled** so attribution is never lost
- Personal thoughts and ideas
- A brief note + **page reference** to a story/example (not the whole transcription)
- Single- or two-word entries: a word, a reminder, a topic to explore
- **Theme/category written in the top-right corner** (e.g. Stoicism, Strategy, Life, Writing, Post Ideas, Misc)
- Duplicate a card if it fits two categories; arrows on the front if it spills to the back

## What You Do With the Cards

The cards are a **"backup hard drive of your brain."** Their power is that they can be **physically shuffled and regrouped**:

- Flip through for inspiration or raw material when writing
- Rearrange into category piles to **structure a book, article, or talk**
- Pull relevant cards when stuck on a problem
- The tactile, hand-written act strengthens memory (a "memory palace" effect)

Robert Greene's scale: after ~300–400 books he accumulates **3,000–4,000 cards** — the source material from which his books are built.
