# Organic Diet Intervention Reduces Urinary Pesticides (Hyland 2019)

*Hyland C. et al., "Organic diet intervention significantly reduces urinary pesticide levels in U.S. children and adults," Environmental Research, 2019.*

## What it is
A crossover dietary intervention in 16 participants (children + adults) across four U.S. families (Oakland, Minneapolis, Atlanta, Baltimore). Six days on their normal **conventional** diet, then six days on an **all-organic** diet. 158 urine samples analyzed for 14 pesticide biomarkers.

## Key findings
- **Significant drops in 13 pesticide metabolites/parent compounds** after switching to organic — covering organophosphate (OP), neonicotinoid, and pyrethroid insecticides and the herbicide 2,4-D (confidence: high).
- Largest reductions:
  - **Malathion (MDA): −95.0%**
  - **Clothianidin (neonicotinoid): −82.7%**
  - **Chlorpyrifos (TCPy): −60.7%**
- The change was **rapid** — measurable within days, not weeks.

## Why it matters for the stir-fry question
This is the strongest direct evidence that **organic is fundamentally an *exposure-reduction* purchase**: eating organic measurably lowers the pesticide load your body is metabolizing. It does **not** measure disease outcomes — it stops at biomarkers. So it answers "does organic reduce pesticide intake?" (yes, a lot) but not "does that make you healthier?" (unproven).

> [!gap] Biomarker reductions are real but the health *significance* of low-level conventional residues remains unproven — no long-term RCT links them to disease.
