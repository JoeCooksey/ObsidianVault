# Egg and Fish Sourcing Labels (GoodRx + FDA)

Decodes the animal-product labels that actually carry meaning vs. marketing.

## Egg Labels (best → worst access)
| Label | What it actually requires | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pasture-raised** | ~108 sq ft/hen outdoors, grass + bugs | Best — but **no legal USDA definition**; needs 3rd-party cert |
| **Free-range** | Cage-free + *some* outdoor access | Outdoor space undefined; often a tiny token door |
| **Cage-free** | Indoors, no cages, can move/nest | No minimum space; can be as little as 1 sq ft/hen |
| **Conventional** | Caged | Cheapest |

- **The label that protects you: "Certified Humane" or "Animal Welfare Approved"** — without 3rd-party certification "pasture-raised" and "free-range" are largely unverified.
- **Nutrition:** mixed evidence, but pasture-raised eggs have been measured at ~**2× omega-3, 3× vitamin D, 4× vitamin E, 7× beta-carotene** vs. conventional-feed eggs (from outdoor foraging). Protein/calories are the same.

## Wild-Caught vs Farmed Fish
- **Mercury:** farmed fish often have **lower** mercury (controlled feed) — but **small wild fish** (sardines, anchovies, herring) are also very low *and* higher in omega-3.
- **Omega-3:** wild generally has a **better omega-6:omega-3 ratio**; farmed salmon can be fattier overall but skews higher omega-6.
- **Contaminants:** wild is less likely to carry antibiotics/dyes; farmed varies hugely by operation and country.
- **FDA "Best Choices" (low-mercury, eat 2–3×/wk):** salmon, sardines, anchovies, trout, herring, mackerel (Atlantic), shrimp, pollock, catfish.
- **Avoid/limit (high-mercury):** shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish, bigeye tuna; limit albacore/yellowfin tuna.

## Bottom Line (confidence: medium)
- Eggs: **pasture-raised + Certified Humane** is the only egg upgrade with both welfare and a plausible nutrition edge; otherwise cage-free is a modest step.
- Fish: **"small + wild" is the cheat code** — sardines/anchovies/wild salmon win on mercury *and* omega-3 *and* price. Don't overthink wild-vs-farmed salmon; both are good.
