# IARC / JECFA 2023 — Aspartame Assessment

Two WHO bodies evaluated aspartame in July 2023, and the **hazard vs risk distinction** is the whole story. Announced 14 July 2023; hazard identification published in *Lancet Oncology* (Riboli et al. 2023).

## The Two Verdicts
1. **IARC (hazard identification)** — classified aspartame **Group 2B, "possibly carcinogenic to humans"**, citing **"limited evidence"** for cancer (specifically hepatocellular carcinoma) in humans. Group 2B is the **3rd of 4 tiers** and is the same category as aloe vera extract and pickled vegetables. IARC judges *whether* something could cause cancer, **not how much exposure it takes**.
2. **JECFA (risk assessment)** — **reaffirmed the acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0–40 mg/kg body weight** and found **no sufficient reason to change it**; no safety concern at current use levels.

## The Diet-Soda Number That Matters
- The ADI (40 mg/kg) equals **~9–14 cans (330–500 mL) of diet soda per day** for a 70 kg adult, consumed every day for life. Typical consumption is a small fraction of this.
- US FDA (ADI 50 mg/kg) publicly **disagreed with IARC** and reaffirmed aspartame is safe. EFSA's 2013 review (ADI 40 mg/kg) also found no cancer concern.

## What It Means
- "Possibly carcinogenic" is a statement about **strength of evidence, not magnitude of risk**. A 2B classification with a reaffirmed generous ADI is, in practice, a **reassuring** result dressed in alarming language.
- The human cancer epidemiology is described by JECFA as **"not convincing,"** with reverse causality, chance, bias, and confounding unable to be ruled out (Goodman 2023).
