# Weight Loss, Obesity and Testosterone in Men — Review Evidence (2024)

Composite of a 2024 PMC review on BMI and testosterone, a Nature *International Journal of Obesity* long-term weight-loss study, and bi-directional Mendelian-randomization analyses.

## What it contributes
Establishes **fat loss as the single largest natural testosterone lever**, with dose-response numbers:
- **+0.6% total T per 1 kg** lost (3-year horizon)
- **BMI 30 → 25 ≈ +13–15%** serum T
- Diet-driven loss (6–17% body weight) → modest rise (≈ +2.9–5.1 nmol/L); bariatric surgery (28–44% loss) → T rose from ~7.8 to ~12.5 nmol/L
- Mendelian randomization confirms the obesity → low-T link is **causal**, not just correlational

Mechanism: adipose aromatase converts T → estradiol; obesity also drives insulin resistance and inflammation that suppress the HPG axis.

## Caveats
- "Up to +30%" figures (popular press) reflect large deficits, not typical results
- Surgical numbers come from morbidly obese cohorts.
