# Tam 2024 — Digital Switching Makes Boredom Worse

University of Toronto study led by Katy Tam (with Michael Inzlicht), published in *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, August 2024. Seven experiments, ~1,200 participants (U.S. and Canada). Covered by [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/08/23/1198910610/digital-switching-scrolling-youtube-tiktok-instagram-boredom), [PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/social-media-videos-boredom-digital-switching/), CNN, and the Washington Post.

## What it contributes

The single most directly actionable finding for the bored-and-reaching-for-the-phone moment: **the default response (scrolling) is causally counterproductive.**

## Key findings

- Rapidly switching between short videos, or skipping within videos, **increased boredom** compared to watching one longer video in full. (confidence: high — 7 experiments)
- Participants **incorrectly predicted** switching would make them less bored. The intuition is backwards. (confidence: high)
- Example design: watch one 10-minute YouTube video straight through vs. freely switch among seven 5-minute videos. The single continuous video was rated more satisfying, more engaging, and more meaningful.
- **Mechanism** (Tam): "Digital switching may make the content of online videos seem meaningless, because people don't have time to engage with or understand the content." This maps cleanly onto both halves of the [[MAC Model of Boredom]] — switching fragments *attention* and strips *meaning*.

## Recommendations from the authors

- Watch things in full instead of skipping through them; immersion beats browsing
- Use app timers / "take a break" features
- Substitute the phone with reading or journaling

## Limitations

Several experiments used Canadian college students; generalizability across ages is untested. (noted in coverage)
