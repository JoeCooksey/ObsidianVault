# The Sleepmaxxing Protocol (with science)

**Source**: [[Hypertrophy Academy]] — YouTube video (~24 min), published 2026-06-09
**Topic**: A practical, category-by-category protocol for improving sleep quality to support muscle growth, fat loss, and overall performance
**Audience**: The creator's stated demographic is healthy, high-performing 15–30 year olds

```vid
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NIBY5q1EAwM
```

> [!tldr]
> Hypertrophy Academy walks through a complete [[Sleepmaxxing Protocol]] organized into environment, lifestyle, supplements, food, and tracking. The headline levers are a cool dark single-purpose bedroom, a circadian-anchored schedule with morning light, a supplement stack (glycine, magnesium, L-theanine, optional melatonin), a kiwi-and-honey pre-bed snack to prevent overnight blood-sugar crashes, and getting screened for [[Sleep Apnea and Nose Breathing|sleep apnea]] if you snore. The closing argument: sleep is the one pillar of training and nutrition where "there are no second chances," so optimize it first. *(Note: this is a fitness-creator protocol with anecdote and selective science, not clinical guidance — confidence is medium.)*

---

## Environment

The room comes first. Three knobs:

- **Temperature** — Aim for roughly **19–21°C**. Core body temperature has to drop a couple of degrees to initiate sleep, part of shifting the central nervous system into a relaxed state. Cooler is generally better (hence why hotel rooms with free AC tend to produce good sleep). A **hot shower before bed** works counterintuitively: it triggers the body to dump heat afterward, *lowering* core temperature and easing sleep onset. See [[Sleep Environment Optimization]].
- **Darkness** — Make the room as dark as possible: blackout curtains or an eye mask, and tape over stray LED lights (AC controls, light under the door).
- **Comfort** — A comfortable bed and a slightly firmer, larger pillow that holds the head in a **neutral position** (rather than slouched) for airway and neck reasons.

A fourth, subtler point: **only go into the bedroom to sleep**. The creator kept his clothes in a separate room so he had no daytime reason to enter the bedroom — walking in became a conditioned signal for sleep. This is framed explicitly as Pavlovian [[Bedroom Stimulus Control|conditioning]], and the payoff is faster sleep latency.

## Lifestyle

- **Stimulants** — [[Caffeine Timing Protocol|Caffeine]] up to ~200–500 mg/day is considered fine for healthy young high performers **as long as it's consumed before midday**, so it's cleared by bedtime. Coffee and caffeine are framed as net-beneficial — "the poison's in the dose." Nicotine and energy drinks are flagged as harder on gut health.
- **Work hours** — A hard rule of **no work, phone, or laptop in the 90 minutes before bed**. Almost nothing is urgent enough to outweigh the sleep cost of staying stimulated late.
- **Bed/wake timing** — Align sleep with darkness (e.g. a 9-to-5 or 10-to-6 schedule) to stay in sync with the sun. Get **morning and afternoon sun exposure** to regulate circadian rhythm; keep a consistent wake time, and on weekends sleep in no more than ~30 minutes before it starts shifting your rhythm. See [[Circadian Rhythm Alignment]]. (Anecdote: worse sleep was associated with eating ~350 extra calories the next day via increased cravings.)
- **Light management** — Avoid bright overhead "hospital lights" in the evening; prefer lamps, [[Pre-Bed Light Management|red-light glasses]], and night mode on screens. Morning sunlight is best; red light is a fallback.
- **Apnea & breathing** — If you snore, mouth-breathe, or have been told you choke in your sleep, **get a sleep apnea test**. The creator had undiagnosed apnea for years (plus a deviated septum) and says treating it "genuinely changed my life." He uses **mouth tape** and **nasal strips** because nose breathing is more beneficial than mouth breathing. See [[Sleep Apnea and Nose Breathing]].
- **Fitness** — Lower body fat and better cardiovascular fitness both improve sleep. "Do your cardio, do your training."

## Supplements

His personal [[Sleep Optimization Supplements|sleep stack]] (explicitly not medical advice):

- **Glycine — 5 g**: calming effect on the central nervous system.
- **Magnesium — ~800 mg/day** (400 mg bisglycinate + 400 mg taurate), dose dependent on blood work. Improves latency, reduces insomnia, raises sleep quality. **Avoid magnesium oxide** — it's a poorly absorbed laxative. (He also dismisses typical multivitamins as underdosed laxatives.)
- **L-Theanine — 200 mg** at night for its calming effect and improved latency (he takes it at night rather than daytime because he *wants* to feel his coffee — contrast with the daytime [[Caffeine and L-Theanine Stack]]).
- **Melatonin — 3 mg, optional**: he stopped because his latency is already good and higher doses left him foggy in the morning; clients with insomnia have used 5–10 mg in extreme cases. Note that insomnia has physiological drivers (apnea among them), so a single fix is often insufficient.
- **Cherry tart juice** as a natural melatonin booster if you can't/won't use melatonin.
- **Creatine — high bolus 10–20 g** to blunt the effects of *acute* sleep deprivation; watch for GI/loose-stool effects at high doses. See [[Creatine for Acute Sleep Deprivation]] and the cognitive angle in [[Creatine for Cognitive Performance]].

## Food Before Bed

Two considerations: **comfort** and **blood sugar**.

- Eating too close to bed can cause GI discomfort and raise body temperature (digestion is muscle work), both of which hurt sleep onset — so leave **≥1 hour** between the last meal and bed. (Some people eat a big meal and sleep fine; individual variation matters.)
- The recommended pre-bed snack is **kiwi + honey** (~1 hr before bed): kiwi raises melatonin and supplies vitamin C, while honey's **fructose tops up liver glycogen**. A common cause of waking in the night (to pee, or right before the alarm) is going **hypoglycemic overnight** — fructose stabilizes liver glycogen to prevent the crash. People sensitive to fructose can reduce the dose or use a lower-fructose fruit like blueberries. See [[Liver Glycogen and Overnight Hypoglycemia]].

## Tracking

Track at least **total hours slept** — write it down, especially while experimenting, since subjective quality ratings are unreliable from memory. Wearables like the [[Oura Ring]] and [[Whoop]] can help data-gatherers, but the creator warns against **score-checking anxiety** (obsessively reading your "readiness" score every morning). He uses the Oura Ring (chosen over Whoop because he dislikes wrist wearables) only when something feels off, not as a daily ritual — a point that connects to [[Wearable Health Metric Validity]].

For deeper issues, he points to a [[CGM Self-Testing for Food Response|continuous glucose monitor]], a formal sleep study, and getting blood work done.

## Closing Argument

- The single biggest lifestyle lever beyond everything above: **don't use your phone in bed**. No doomscrolling, news, or comment sections — read a book or do something relaxing instead.
- Many topics here could be full videos on their own; some people with specific conditions will need clinical help.
- **Sleep is the top priority.** "You can mess around in the gym and make great progress. When it comes to sleep, there's no messing around — there's no second chances." Sleep underpins muscle growth, fat loss, and everything else.
