# IFFGD GI Alarm Symptoms

The International Foundation for Gastrointestinal Disorders' reference on **alarm symptoms** — the dividing line between functional gut complaints (IBS-type: altered motility, nerve sensitivity, brain–gut signaling, no structural damage) and organic disease that demands workup.

## What it contributes

- **The core principle:** functional disorders cause no overt inflammation or structural damage, so bleeding, fever, weight loss, and dysphagia are *never* attributable to them — "alarm symptoms cannot be attributed to functional disorders alone."
- **Bleeding decoded:** bright red blood on the stool surface suggests an anal source; blood mixed into stool suggests colon; dark/tarry stool (melena) suggests upper GI (esophagus, stomach, duodenum). All warrant investigation, some emergently.
- **Vomiting blood** is always structural — ulcer, stomach cancer, esophageal varices, or esophagitis.
- **Weight loss threshold:** 10+ pounds over 3 months without explanation.
- **Other flags:** persistent fever, dysphagia (suggests stricture, tumor, or achalasia), chest pain (rule out cardiac first), sudden bowel-habit change after 50, nighttime GI pain, family history of colon cancer or IBD, palpable abdominal mass.
