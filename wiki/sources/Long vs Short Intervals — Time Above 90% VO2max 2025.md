# Long vs Short Intervals — Time Above 90% VO2max 2025

Crossover study (PMC11743937) in 12 highly-trained middle-distance runners comparing long vs short VO2 max interval formats. Supports the 4-minute design of the Norwegian 4x4.

## What It Contributes
Direct evidence that **long (3–4 min) intervals accumulate more time above 90% VO2 max than short (30 s) intervals**, even when short-interval speed is raised — the aerobic-stimulus rationale for keeping intervals at 4 min.

## Protocols Compared
- **Long:** 4 × 3 min at 95% vVO2max
- **Short:** 24 × 30 s at 100% vVO2max

## Key Numbers
| Metric | Long | Short |
|---|---|---|
| Time > 90% VO2max | 327.9 ± 146.8 s (~5.5 min) | 201.3 ± 268.4 s (~3.4 min) |
| Time > 95% VO2max | 147.9 ± 127.4 s | 57.5 ± 139.2 s |

Long intervals significantly superior (p = 0.05) despite short intervals being run 5% faster.

## Conclusion
"Intensified 30-s intervals were inferior to traditional 3-min intervals regarding time spent above 90% VO2max." Interval *organization* (duration, intensity, work:rest) outweighs raw speed.

> [!note] Reconciling with the short-interval claim in [[VO2 Max Interval Training]]
> Cycling literature sometimes favors 30/15s for accruing time-in-zone. This running study finds the opposite at 30 s / 24-rep structure. Both can be true: format, modality, and rep count interact. For the Norwegian 4x4 specifically, the 4-min length is supported.

## Confidence
**High** for the comparison; small n (12), elite runners, so absolute transfer to recreational athletes is approximate.
