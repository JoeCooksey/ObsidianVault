# Asset Allocation by Age (Optimized Portfolio / Bogleheads)

Synthesizes the standard age-based stock/bond rules of thumb and the Bogleheads three-fund framework.

## Key Claims (confidence: high — convention, not law)

**Stock allocation rules of thumb** (percent in stocks):
- **100 − age** → conservative ("age in bonds"); widely considered too conservative today.
- **110 − age** → moderate.
- **120 − age** (or "age − 20" in bonds) → aggressive; closest to what target-date fund managers actually do for the young.

**Observed allocations:**
- Investors in their 20s–40s typically hold **≤5% bonds**; 50s rises to ~8.5%.
- Vanguard target funds put **40% of the equity sleeve in international** (raised from 20% in 2010, 30% in 2015).

**Example (20-year-old, three-fund):** 60% US stock (VTI) / 20% international (VXUS) / 20% bonds is the *conservative* end; a young aggressive investor commonly runs 80–90%+ equities with little to no bonds.

## What It Contributes

Anchors the allocation half of the user's question. For a ~19-year-old with a 40+ year horizon, the math and the professionals both point to a stock-heavy (≈90–100% equity) allocation, with bonds added later. International belongs at roughly 20–40% of the stock sleeve.
