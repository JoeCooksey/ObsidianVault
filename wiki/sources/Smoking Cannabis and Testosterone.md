# Smoking, Nicotine, Cannabis and Testosterone

Mixed-evidence area; included for completeness and to flag the counterintuitive smoking finding.

## What it contributes
- **Nicotine/cigarettes**: conflicting. A 2016 review (>13,000 men) found smokers had *higher* measured T than non-smokers; but smoking damages sperm quality, fertility, and erectile function, and harms long-run T via systemic disease. A 2012 study: T dips after quitting, recovers within a year.
- **Cannabis**: several studies show a slight T *decrease*; a study of >2,000 infertile men found cannabis lowered plasma T; others found no significant change.
- **Both**: induce oxidative stress and cell death in testicular tissue, increasing sperm DNA fragmentation.

## Practical takeaway
Despite the odd "higher T in smokers" cross-sectional signal, both substances are net-harmful to male reproductive health → still avoid. Evidence **low** (conflicting, confounded).
