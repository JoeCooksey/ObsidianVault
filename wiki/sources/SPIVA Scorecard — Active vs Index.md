# SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index Performance

## What this source contributes

The definitive, recurring dataset on whether professional stock-pickers beat the market. It is the reality check against the survivorship-biased glamour of star quant funds.

## Key Claims

- Over the **15-year period ending Dec 2024, no category had a majority of active managers beat their benchmark**. (confidence: high)
- In 2024 specifically, **65% of active large-cap funds underperformed the S&P 500**; midcap active had 62% underperformance. (confidence: high)
- Exception: **70% of active small-cap funds *beat* the S&P SmallCap 600 in 2024** — small/inefficient corners of the market are where active management has its best (still inconsistent) odds. (confidence: high)
- Long-run figure routinely cited: **~90% of active equity managers underperform over 15+ years**, driven primarily by fees and the impossibility of consistently repeating outperformance. (confidence: high)

### Year-End 2025 update (added 2026-06-12)
- **79% of active large-cap US funds underperformed the S&P 500 in 2025** — worse than 2024's 65% and the **4th-worst year in the scorecard's 25-year history**. (confidence: high)
- **~92% of domestic funds underperformed over the trailing 20 years**. (confidence: high)
- 63% of international and 76% of global equity funds underperformed in 2025; nearly two-thirds of international funds were shuttered or merged away over the long horizon (survivorship). (confidence: high)
- Short horizons remain active's best look: in 2025, 8 of 22 equity and 6 of 16 fixed-income categories had majority outperformance over 1 year — it does not persist. (confidence: high)
- Caveat: a 2025 industry report challenges SPIVA's methodology (category mapping, survivorship handling); independent replications (Morningstar Active/Passive Barometer) agree directionally, so treat exact percentages as medium, conclusion as high. (confidence: medium)

## Notes

SPIVA covers mutual funds and institutional accounts, **not** secretive hedge funds — so it does not include Medallion. But it is the correct base rate for what a *retail* investor or typical professional achieves. The lesson pairs directly with [[Index Fund Investing]]: the median active stock-picker loses to the index, so the burden of proof on any active strategy (including DIY stock-picking) is high.
