# Handgrip Strength Dose-Response Meta-Analysis (López-Bueno 2022)

*Ageing Research Reviews* systematic review + dose-response meta-analysis. According to PubMed. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2022.101778) (PMID 36332759).

## What it contributes

Grip strength is a cheap proxy for whole-body strength and one of the most reproducible non-aerobic predictors of longevity.

- **48 studies, 3,135,473 participants** (49.6% women, ages 35–85).
- Significant **inverse** association between handgrip strength and all-cause, cancer, and cardiovascular mortality.
- All-cause mortality risk drops in a **close-to-linear** fashion across **26–50 kg**; CV mortality across 24–40 kg; cancer shows a U-shape (16–33 kg).

**Companion (Wu et al. 2017, *JAMDA*)** — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2017.03.011) (PMID 28549705):
- 42 studies, 3,002,203 participants.
- Lowest vs highest grip: all-cause HR **1.41**, CVD HR 1.63.
- **Each −5 kg grip strength ≈ +16% all-cause mortality**, +21% CVD mortality.

> [!note] Grip itself isn't magic — it indexes total muscular strength and is trained as a *byproduct* of resistance work, carries, and hangs, not by squeezing a gripper alone.
