# CGM in Healthy Populations (Metabolism 2023 + CGM-HYPE)

The evidence behind using a **continuous glucose monitor as a personal food test** — the most concrete answer to "tests to do on yourself" for picking carbs.

## What It Contributes

Turns "which carbs are good for me" from a generic tier list into a **personal measurement**.

### Key findings
- **Inter-individual variability is large:** one person's "healthy" meal spikes another person's blood sugar dramatically. Glucose regulation is personal (echoes the Israeli Weizmann *Personalized Nutrition* work).
- Researchers suggest most adults could benefit from a **2–4 week CGM wear** to learn their "**glucotype**" and how specific foods affect them, then adjust.
- **CGM-HYPE trial** (healthy young people, 14-day wear, standardized food/exercise/stress): **carb-rich foods triggered the highest spikes**; **anaerobic exercise** spiked glucose more than aerobic; **psychological stress** caused significant glucose changes.
- Smoother post-meal glucose has been associated with better weight control, suppressed hunger, steadier energy, and sleep — though causal benefit in healthy people is **not established**.

## How To Use It (the self-test)
1. Wear a CGM (e.g. Stelo, Lingo, Abbott Libre, or a Levels-style program) for **2–4 weeks**.
2. Eat your normal foods; log them. Watch the post-meal curve (aim to avoid large spikes + crashes).
3. **Test pairings:** same carb naked vs. with protein/fat/fiber/vinegar, or before vs. after a walk. The walk and the protein/fiber almost always blunt the spike.
4. Keep the foods that keep *you* flat; swap or re-sequence the ones that spike *you*.

## Caveats (confidence: medium)
- In **non-diabetics**, the long-term health payoff of chasing flat curves is **unproven** — treat it as personalized optimization, not medicine.
- CGMs read interstitial fluid (5–10 min lag) and have noise; look at **patterns over single readings**.
- It can feed orthorexia/anxiety in some people — use it as a short experiment, not a permanent scoreboard.
