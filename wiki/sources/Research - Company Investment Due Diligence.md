# Research: Company Investment Due Diligence

**Topic:** Full guide to information needed to evaluate a company for investment — what the information is, how it applies, and where to find it.
**Method:** Autoresearch — 8 web searches synthesized
**Pages Created:** 7

## Key Findings

1. **Due diligence has 5 stages**: business understanding → financial statement review → management quality → risk assessment → valuation. Skipping any stage is the most common retail investor failure.

2. **The 3 financial statements are non-negotiable**: income statement (profit?), balance sheet (healthy?), cash flow statement (real cash?). A company can show clean profits while bleeding cash — only reading all three catches this.

3. **Cash flow beats earnings**: free cash flow (FCF = operating cash flow − capex) is the hardest number to fake. Earnings can be manipulated via accounting; cash cannot. Significant gap between net income and FCF = red flag.

4. **ROIC is the single best profitability metric**: Return on Invested Capital (ROIC > WACC = value creation). ROE can be gamed by taking on debt; ROIC cannot. A company with 15%+ ROIC over 5+ years has a real competitive advantage.

5. **Moats determine long-term returns**: Morningstar's 5 moat sources — network effects, switching costs, cost advantages, intangible assets (brands/patents), efficient scale. A company without a moat will see profits competed away within 5–10 years.

6. **Valuation prevents overpaying for quality**: a great business bought at a terrible price will underperform. The DCF is theoretically correct but sensitive to assumptions; EV/EBITDA and P/E multiples are fast reality checks. Always compare to industry peers.

7. **The 10-K is the Bible**: SEC EDGAR (sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar) hosts every 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K ever filed. Every investor must read the full 10-K before buying — especially the "Risk Factors" section which companies are legally required to disclose honestly.

8. **Management quality is underweighted by retail investors**: promoter/insider ownership, capital allocation track record, and whether FCF is returned to shareholders (dividends/buybacks) or wasted on dilutive acquisitions are the key signals. Form your own view of the business BEFORE reading management's investor presentation.
