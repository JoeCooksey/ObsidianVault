# Bakhru 2005 — Smoking Cessation and Cardiovascular Risk Factors (NHANES III)

*PLoS Medicine* analysis of **15,489 NHANES III participants** examining smoking, smoking cessation, and inflammatory markers (according to PubMed — Bakhru 2005, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0020160)).

**CRP** and other inflammatory markers (white blood cell count, fibrinogen) showed a **dose-dependent** relationship with smoking intensity, and improved as intensity dropped. After cessation, inflammatory markers **resolved more slowly than traditional risk factors**, returning to baseline roughly **5 years** after quitting — matching the timeframe over which cardiovascular risk falls in cohort studies. This established that the inflammatory component of smoking is **reversible**.

Though older (2005), the finding is **foundational and uncontested**: smoking raises hs-CRP dose-dependently, and quitting lowers it over years. Contributes the anchor claim that **smoking raises hs-CRP and cessation is one of the highest-yield ways to lower it** (over time).
