# Knapik 2021 — Update on Minimalist Running Shoes

According to PubMed. Review in *Journal of Special Operations Medicine* 21(3):107–110. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.55460/LWT4-DGIH)

Compares minimalist (MRS) to conventional running shoes (CRS). Transitioning to MRS lowered energy cost and shifted contact to the forefoot, reducing impact forces; after a 6-week transition, energy cost was lower and 5 km time faster in MRS, with no further gain through week 26. **Injury evidence is mixed** — a 30-vs-31 runner study found CRS 37% vs MRS 52% injury incidence (not significant, p=.24), but running-related pain (knee, shin, calf, ankle) was higher in MRS and rose with mileage, and injury risk rose with body weight. Authors recommend most runners cap MRS at **~35% of training time**, reserving it for performance situations (races, fitness tests).

This is the key "caution / nuance" source: real performance benefits, but injury reduction is *not* established and pain can increase if mileage ramps too fast.
