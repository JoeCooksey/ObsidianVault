# Edwardsen 2020 — Heart Rate Response to 4x4 Protocol

Peer-reviewed study (PMC7399937) measuring the actual HR trajectory during a controlled 4×4 protocol, revealing the gap between the "90–95% HRmax" prescription and physiological reality.

## What It Contributes
Turns the 4x4 from a prescription into an executable one by showing *when* you are actually in the target zone.

## Key Findings
- **HR rises across reps:** averages ~160.3 → 166.5 → 167.0 → 169.0 b/min (intervals 1–4); peak **~93% HRmax (172±9 b/min)** in interval 4 — not a flat 95%.
- **Lag to target:** ~1.3 min to reach 85% HRmax in interval 1 (warm-up ≈110 b/min vs work start ≈156 b/min).
- **Time in band:** ~12.9 of 16 work min (≈80%) in 85–95% HRmax; interval 1 only 2.7 min vs 3.4–3.5 min for reps 2–4.
- **Recovery variance:** HR drop during 3-min recovery ranges 10–35 b/min by fitness; fitter athletes drop more and must start each rep harder.

## Practical Recommendations (from the authors)
- Begin interval 1 at 85–100% peak power output (PPO) to hit target HR fast.
- Once HR reaches ~95%, trim load ~5% PPO to avoid overshoot.
- Fitter individuals should allocate near-supramaximal intensity in interval 1 to maximize time-in-zone.

## Confidence
**High** — controlled physiological measurement, directly on-topic. Sample is modest; exact numbers are population-specific but the pattern (lag, progressive HR rise, peak ~93%) is robust.
