# Paul Saladino: Claims Against Oats & Vegetables — Evidence Review

[[Paul Saladino]] (MD, "Carnivore MD") has argued that oats and vegetables are harmful due to various plant defense compounds. This page evaluates each major claim against the scientific evidence and renders a verdict.

**Overall context**: Saladino himself abandoned strict carnivore after his testosterone dropped to ~500 ng/dL, experiencing sleep disturbance and heart palpitations. He now eats ~300g carbs/day from fruit and honey. His original claims were most extreme 2019–2021 during his *The Carnivore Code* era.

---

## Claims About Oats

### 1. Phytic Acid Blocks Mineral Absorption
**Saladino's claim**: Oats are high in phytic acid, which significantly impairs absorption of iron, zinc, calcium, and magnesium.

**Evidence**:
- **True in isolation**: Phytic acid does bind to minerals in the same meal and reduce their absorption. This is measurable in controlled studies.
- **Overstated for healthy people**: The effect is meal-specific — phytate binds minerals from what you eat with it; it does NOT travel the body stripping minerals from tissues or bones. On a varied diet, this is clinically insignificant.
- **Only matters at extremes**: The concern is primarily for populations with severe iron deficiency, extremely monotonous diets, or people eating oats as their sole iron source.
- **Mitigation**: Soaking oats overnight activates phytase and reduces phytic acid by 20–50%. Adding 50mg vitamin C significantly restores iron absorption.
- **Phytic acid has benefits**: Acts as antioxidant; protective against certain cancers; helps regulate blood sugar by slowing carb digestion.

**Verdict: POSITIVE OUTWEIGHS NEGATIVE** — For a person eating a balanced diet, the net effect of oats (massive beta-glucan cardiovascular benefit — see Claim 6) overwhelms the phytic acid concern. Only meaningful for diagnosed mineral deficiency or very poor dietary diversity.

---

### 2. Saponins Damage the Gut / Cause Leaky Gut
**Saladino's claim**: Saponins in oats (avenacosides) damage the intestinal lining and cause intestinal permeability ("leaky gut").

**Evidence**:
- **In vitro concern only**: One rat study found avenacosides at 1 mg/ml increased intestinal permeability to ovalbumin. No significant difference in disaccharidase activities after 19 days of normal feeding.
- **No human evidence**: There are no well-designed human trials showing oat saponins cause leaky gut at normal dietary doses.
- **Potential benefits**: Oat saponins (avenacosides) have shown cholesterol-lowering, immunomodulatory, and anti-cancer properties in research. NCBl PMC meta-analyses flag anti-atherosclerotic potential.
- **Regulatory/expert consensus**: No expert body recommends avoiding oat saponins; the general finding is that cutting them out does more harm than good.

**Verdict: POSITIVE OUTWEIGHS NEGATIVE** — The human evidence for gut harm from oat saponins at normal intake is absent. The potential benefits are real. This claim does not hold.

---

### 3. Lectins Damage the Gut
**Saladino's claim**: Oats (and seeds generally) contain lectins that damage gut lining and cause autoimmune disease.

**Evidence**:
- **True for raw/undercooked legumes**: Lectins in raw or severely undercooked legumes cause food poisoning and intestinal cell damage. Well-documented.
- **False for cooked oatmeal**: Cooking destroys most lectins. No well-designed RCT shows that properly cooked oat lectins cause gut damage, leaky gut, or autoimmune disease in humans.
- **Quality of evidence problem**: Most alarming lectin research uses animal models, concentrated lectin extracts, or raw foods — not normal dietary portions of cooked food.
- **8,000 years of legume/grain consumption**: Humans have consumed cooked lectins continuously across cultures. If they caused the claimed damage at normal doses, the epidemiological signal would be overwhelming — it is not.

**Verdict: POSITIVE OUTWEIGHS NEGATIVE** — The lectin concern for cooked oatmeal is not supported by human evidence. Claim does not hold for normally prepared oats.

---

### 4. Mycotoxin Contamination (DON, Aflatoxins, ZEN)
**Saladino's claim**: Oats are heavily contaminated with mycotoxins (mold toxins) including deoxynivalenol (DON), zearalenone, and aflatoxins.

**Evidence**:
- **True and significant**: A 2024 study of 615 commercial oat samples detected DON in 82%, ZEN in 52%, and aflatoxins in 61%. A 2025 study found DON in 70% of individual kernels; 8% exceeded EU legal limits.
- **Health effects are real**: Fusarium mycotoxins have documented harm in humans and animals at sufficient doses.
- **Regulatory context matters**: Most commercially sold oats are within legal limits. The EU limits (strictest globally) are typically respected in certified products.
- **Mitigation**: Buy certified organic (reduces, not eliminates), buy from reputable brands with testing standards (Quaker, Bob's Red Mill have published mycotoxin testing), and diversify grain sources.

**Verdict: NEGATIVE CONCERN IS REAL** — This is Saladino's strongest argument and is factually grounded. However, the response is "choose better oats," not "avoid oats entirely." The mycotoxin concern is not unique to oats — it applies across the grain supply. Certified clean oats retain all cardiovascular benefits without the contamination concern. For people buying cheap commodity oats with no quality controls, this concern is legitimate.

---

### 5. Glyphosate and Pesticide Contamination
**Saladino's claim**: Oats are heavily contaminated with glyphosate (herbicide) and other pesticides.

**Evidence**:
- **True for conventional oats**: Glyphosate detected in ~48% of oat samples in food surveys. Consumer Reports found glyphosate in nearly all conventional oat products tested.
- **Health concern**: Growing body of evidence links chronic glyphosate exposure to health risks; classified as probable human carcinogen (IARC Group 2A). Studies detect glyphosate in human blood and urine.
- **Mitigation available**: Organic oats have substantially lower glyphosate residues (conventional pre-harvest desiccation with glyphosate is the primary driver of contamination; prohibited in organic farming).

**Verdict: NEGATIVE CONCERN IS REAL, MITIGATION EXISTS** — Like mycotoxins, this is a legitimate concern for conventional oats specifically, not oats categorically. Organic oats remove most of this concern while retaining the full nutritional profile. This is a reason to buy organic, not a reason to abandon oats.

---

### 6. The Counterweight: Beta-Glucan and Population Data
*What Saladino systematically omits about oats*:

**Beta-glucan evidence**:
- FDA-approved health claim: 3g oat beta-glucan/day reduces cardiovascular disease risk.
- Meta-analysis of 58 RCTs (3,974 participants): oat beta-glucan significantly reduces LDL-C (4.2%), non-HDL-C (4.8%), and Apolipoprotein B (2.3%).
- Mechanism: beta-glucan forms viscous gel in gut → traps bile acids → forces liver to convert more cholesterol to bile → lowers serum LDL.
- Reduces postprandial glucose spike by slowing carb absorption.
- Promotes Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium growth (prebiotic); butyrate precursor.

**Population epidemiology**:
- Meta-analysis of 786,076 participants across 14 studies: highest whole grain intake vs. lowest → **17% lower all-cause mortality, 25% lower CVD mortality, 6% lower cancer mortality**.
- This is the largest-scale signal and it points firmly in the direction of benefit.

## Claims About Vegetables

### 7. Oxalates Cause Kidney Stones (Spinach, Kale, Chard)
**Saladino's claim**: High-oxalate vegetables (spinach, kale, etc.) deposit oxalates that form kidney stones and cause other organ damage.

**Evidence**:
- **True for spinach in kidney stone-prone individuals**: Spinach is genuinely high in oxalates (~970 mg/100g). 2 cups/day can significantly increase urinary oxalate excretion. Legitimate concern for the ~12% of people with calcium oxalate kidney stone history.
- **Kale is NOT high oxalate**: Kale contains only ~17 mg oxalate per 100g (vs. spinach's ~970 mg) — a 57× difference. The Northwestern/Medill finding: "kale sheds bum rap on kidney stones." You'd need 600+ cups of kale to approach concerning oxalate levels.
- **Most vegetables are not high-oxalate**: Saladino conflates spinach risk with all vegetables. Broccoli, zucchini, cucumbers, peppers — all low oxalate.
- **Population data**: High vegetable intake is consistently associated with LOWER (not higher) kidney stone risk in large cohort studies — likely due to potassium/citrate effects that inhibit stone formation.

**Verdict: NUANCED — NEGATIVE OUTWEIGHS POSITIVE for spinach consumed in excess by kidney stone-prone individuals; POSITIVE OUTWEIGHS NEGATIVE for nearly all other vegetables including kale.** Saladino's conflation of spinach risk to "vegetables generally" is scientifically inaccurate.

---

### 8. Isothiocyanates in Cruciferous Vegetables Harm the Thyroid
**Saladino's claim**: Isothiocyanates in broccoli, kale, cauliflower harm thyroid function (goitrogenic effect).

**Evidence**:
- **Animal evidence, not human**: Thyroid suppression from cruciferous vegetables was found in animal studies at high doses, with very limited human replication.
- **Human studies show NO harm**: Two human studies using broccoli sprout extract (exceptionally high isothiocyanate concentration — more than typical dietary intake) showed **no effect on thyroid function**, even in people with autoimmune thyroid disease.
- **Iodine status is the real gating factor**: In populations with adequate iodine (table salt is iodized in the US), cruciferous vegetables have little to no measurable thyroid effect.
- **Cancer protection is the larger signal**: Cruciferous vegetable intake is associated with REDUCED thyroid cancer risk, not increased. Isothiocyanates activate Nrf2, a cancer-protective pathway.
- **Saladino confuses goitrin with isothiocyanates**: Goitrin (in raw seeds/mustard) is the problematic goitrogen. Isothiocyanates are actually beneficial compounds.

**Verdict: POSITIVE OUTWEIGHS NEGATIVE** — This claim does not hold for normal dietary intake in people with adequate iodine. Cruciferous vegetables reduce thyroid cancer risk. The goitrogen concern is largely a myth for cooked vegetables at normal serving sizes.

---

### 9. Polyphenols Are Hormone Disruptors
**Saladino's claim**: Polyphenols in plant foods are "hormone disrupting."

**Evidence**:
- **Claim is largely inverted**: The primary research direction on polyphenols and hormones is that polyphenols are **protective against endocrine disrupting chemicals (EDCs)** like BPA, phthalates, and pesticide residues. Multiple PMC reviews document polyphenols counteracting EDC-induced toxicity.
- **Phytoestrogen nuance**: Some polyphenols (isoflavones in soy) are phytoestrogens — they can weakly bind estrogen receptors. However, these are selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs), not estrogen mimics; they tend to be protective against estrogen-dependent cancers in most contexts.
- **The overwhelmingly positive picture**: Polyphenols from vegetables, berries, and dark chocolate are associated with reduced CVD, cancer, type 2 diabetes, and neurodegeneration in hundreds of human studies. The mechanism is antioxidant + anti-inflammatory + beneficial gut microbiome changes.
- **"Hormone disrupting" framing**: This cherry-picks one narrow mechanistic paper about polyphenol-EDC interactions and ignores the entire established body of evidence.

**Verdict: POSITIVE OUTWEIGHS NEGATIVE** — This claim is essentially backward. Polyphenols are among the most evidence-supported beneficial compounds in the human diet. The hormone disruption framing grossly misrepresents the literature.

---

### 10. Phytosterols Are Associated With Accelerated Heart Disease
**Saladino's claim**: Plant sterols (found in vegetables, nuts, seeds) accelerate heart disease and atherosclerosis.

**Evidence**:
- **The legitimate kernel**: Elevated serum phytosterol levels (especially sitosterol) do correlate with increased coronary events in some epidemiological studies. The German Cardiac Society has called for RCTs on hard outcomes for phytosterol supplements. There is a genuine scientific debate here.
- **Critical context — supplemental vs. whole food**: The concern applies primarily to **phytosterol-fortified foods** (Benecol, Flora Proactiv margarine) designed to deliver mega-doses, not to normal whole food consumption. Vegetables and nuts contain modest phytosterol amounts in a food matrix with fibre, polyphenols, and other protective compounds.
- **The other side**: A systematic meta-analysis found **no association** between serum phytosterol concentrations and CVD risk. The European Heart Journal meta-analysis found plant sterol supplementation reduced LDL by 6–12% with no cardiovascular harm signal.
- **Population data**: Populations eating the most plant foods (Mediterranean, Blue Zones) have the lowest cardiovascular disease. The highest phytosterol dietary exposure is in populations with the best heart disease outcomes.

**Verdict: NEGATIVE CONCERN HAS SOME BASIS for high-dose phytosterol supplementation; POSITIVE OUTWEIGHS NEGATIVE for whole food vegetable sources**. This is the most legitimate of Saladino's anti-vegetable claims, but it targets an edge case (fortified supplements) that he misapplies to vegetables generally.

## Summary Scorecard

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Phytic acid blocks minerals (oats) | Positive outweighs negative for most people |
| Saponins damage gut (oats) | Positive outweighs negative — no human evidence of harm |
| Lectins damage gut (oats) | Positive outweighs negative — no evidence for cooked oats |
| Mycotoxin contamination (oats) | **Legitimate concern — choose organic/certified oats** |
| Glyphosate contamination (oats) | **Legitimate concern — choose organic oats** |
| Oxalates cause kidney stones | Nuanced — true for spinach + stone history; false for most veg |
| Isothiocyanates harm thyroid | Positive outweighs negative — claim does not hold |
| Polyphenols are hormone disruptors | Positive outweighs negative — claim is essentially backward |
| Phytosterols accelerate heart disease | Nuanced — legitimate for supplements; not for whole food sources |

**Overall assessment**: Saladino identifies two real issues (mycotoxin and glyphosate contamination of conventional oats) while massively overstating or inverting the evidence on 7 of 9 claims. The solution to the two legitimate concerns is to buy certified organic oats, not eliminate oats — a food with FDA-approved cardiovascular benefits backed by meta-analyses of nearly 800,000 people.
