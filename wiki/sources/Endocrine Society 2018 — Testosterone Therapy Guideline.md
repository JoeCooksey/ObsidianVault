# Endocrine Society 2018 — Testosterone Therapy in Men With Hypogonadism

The authoritative US clinical practice guideline on diagnosing and treating male hypogonadism (testosterone deficiency). Updates the 2010 guideline; developed with GRADE methodology. Source for PubMed (PMID 29562364), DOI [10.1210/jc.2018-00229](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2018-00229).

## What it contributes to this topic

The diagnostic backbone for **low testosterone in men**: which signs/symptoms count, and the lab criteria.

## Key claims

- **Diagnose hypogonadism only when both are present**: symptoms/signs consistent with T deficiency *and* unequivocally, consistently low serum testosterone (high). Symptoms alone are not enough; a low number alone is not enough.
- **Initial test = fasting morning total testosterone**, accurate assay; **confirm by repeating** the morning measurement on a separate day (high). T is highest in the morning and varies day to day.
- **Free testosterone** (by equilibrium dialysis or accurate formula) when total T is near the lower limit of normal or when **SHBG** is altered (e.g., obesity lowers SHBG; aging raises it) (high).
- Once deficiency is confirmed, **investigate the cause** (primary testicular vs secondary/pituitary) (high).
- Treatment aims at **mid-normal range** T; the guideline lists contraindications (prostate/breast cancer, elevated hematocrit, untreated severe sleep apnea, recent MI/stroke, planning fertility, thrombophilia, etc.) (high).

## Notes

The companion patient page ([Endocrine Society — Hypogonadism in Men](https://www.endocrine.org/patient-engagement/endocrine-library/hypogonadism)) gives the plain-language symptom list and cites a normal adult-male total-T range of **~300–1,000 ng/dL** (lab- and age-dependent), with diagnosis requiring **≥2 early-morning (7–10 AM) low readings**.
