# NIU CITL — Reflective Journals and Learning Logs

University (.edu) instructional guide distinguishing two daily-capture tools and the cognitive case for them.

## The Distinction

- **Reflective journal** — subjective: your reactions, feelings, what something meant to you.
- **Learning log** — documentary: what you did, accomplished, the ideas and *questions* it raised.

Both are written *during or shortly after* learning.

## Why It Works

- "Reflection can boost critical thinking, encourage **metacognition** (thinking about your own thinking), and help prepare for assignments and exams."
- The **cyclical record-and-reflect process** over time "leads to improvement and/or insight" — and seeing visible progression is itself motivating.

## The Four-Stage Cycle

1. **Record** — describe the situation / what was learned.
2. **Reflect** — examine reactions and what was learned.
3. **Analyze** — integrate theory with experience for insight.
4. **Conclude & plan** — name what you'll do differently next time.

## What It Contributes

The evidence-grounded rationale for closing each daily learning session with **a few lines of writing**. Reflection is what converts "I sat with the material" into encoded, examined knowledge — and the "open questions" a log surfaces become tomorrow's learning targets, making the practice self-feeding.
