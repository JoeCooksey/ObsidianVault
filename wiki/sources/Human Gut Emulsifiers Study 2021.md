# Direct Impact of Commonly Used Dietary Emulsifiers on Human Gut Microbiota

2021 study (*Microbiome*) from the Gewirtz/Chassaing lab — the group that pioneered emulsifier–gut research. Tested human (not just mouse) microbiota directly.

## Study Design

20 commonly-used dietary emulsifiers screened in the **MiniBioReactor Array (MBRA)** model, which keeps human stool microbiota alive ex vivo under anaerobic conditions. Tested at **0.1% concentration for 144 hours**, with post-treatment observation, across 3 independent experiments.

## Key Claims

- **Carboxymethylcellulose (CMC, E466) and polysorbate 80 (P80, E433)** "significantly impact microbiota composition in a **non-reversible manner**" — the disturbance persisted after the emulsifier was removed. (confidence: high)
- The disturbance is the kind that "promotes chronic intestinal inflammation."
- P80 triggered "significant metatranscriptome alteration" — it changes not just *which* bacteria are present but what genes they express.
- **Most** of the 20 emulsifiers produced detrimental changes; **lecithin** was a notable exception that did not significantly harm the microbiota.

> [!note] Companion human RCT
> The same lab's FRESH trial (Chassaing et al., *Gastroenterology* 2022) fed humans CMC for 11 days and found reduced microbiota diversity, gut-bacteria encroachment into the normally-sterile mucus layer, and altered metabolomics — confirming the ex-vivo signal in living people.
