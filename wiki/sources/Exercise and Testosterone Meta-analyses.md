# Exercise Training and Resting Testosterone — Meta-analyses + Exercise-Hypogonadal Male Condition

Several systematic reviews/meta-analyses on how training changes *resting* testosterone, plus the endurance-overtraining literature.

## What it contributes
Nuanced, often counterintuitive picture:
- **Acute**: resistance exercise produces a transient post-workout T spike
- **Chronic resting T in adults**: effect is **negligible** in insufficiently-active eugonadal men, and not significant by training mode, age, or BMI
- **Older men (≥60)**: resistance training SMD ≈ −0.003 (no effect)
- **Children/adolescents**: resistance training **did** raise T (MD ≈ +3.4 nmol/L); endurance did not
- **Exercise-Hypogonadal Male Condition (EHMC)**: chronically endurance-trained men show persistently low free/total T *without* compensatory LH rise — driven by low energy availability + elevated cortisol

## Practical takeaway
Train for body composition and health (which indirectly supports T), not for a permanent resting-T bump. Avoid the underfueled overtraining trap. Evidence **medium** (strong for "negligible chronic effect"; EHMC is a recognized but population-specific pattern).
