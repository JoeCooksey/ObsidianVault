# Sramek 2000 — Cold Water Immersion and Catecholamines

*"Human physiological responses to immersion into water of different temperatures."* Eur J Appl Physiol 81(5):436–442 (Šrámek, Šimečková, Janský, Šavlíková, Vybíral). The most-cited human study behind the cold-exposure dopamine claim.

## What It Contributes

In healthy subjects, head-out immersion in **14 °C** water produced:
- **Dopamine ↑ ~250%** above baseline (still ~200% at 90 min, ~150% at 2 h, ~120% at 2.5 h)
- **Norepinephrine ↑ ~530%**

The defining feature is that the elevation is **slow, tonic, and sustained for hours with no crash** — distinguishing cold exposure from drug or food spikes, which crash. Mechanistically: locus coeruleus → noradrenergic drive to VTA dopamine neurons → sustained mesolimbic release, with **no receptor downregulation**. This is why cold immersion sits at the top of the 🟢 column in [[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]].

## Confidence Note

Confidence **medium**: a real human physiology study, but a **small sample**, single protocol (14 °C, head-out). The ~250% figure is robust as a directional result; it should not be treated as a universal dose-response constant across all cold-exposure formats (cold showers, brief plunges, varying temperatures will differ).
