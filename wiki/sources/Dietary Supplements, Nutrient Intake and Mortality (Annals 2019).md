# Dietary Supplements, Nutrient Intake and Mortality (Annals 2019)

**Chen, Zhang et al., Tufts University. *Annals of Internal Medicine*, 2019.** Study type: prospective observational, **>27,000 U.S. adults** (NHANES), mortality tracked through Dec 31, 2011. Confidence: **high** for the food-vs-supplement contrast (large, well-known); observational, so associational not causal.

## Findings
- **Nutrients from FOOD predicted lower death:**
  - Vitamin A, vitamin K, zinc → lower all-cause **and** cardiovascular mortality
  - Magnesium → lower all-cause mortality
- **The same nutrients from SUPPLEMENTS showed no such benefit.**
- Multivitamins, vitamin D, calcium, and vitamin C supplements: **no advantage and no added risk** for CVD or premature death prevention.
- **Calcium supplements ≥1,000 mg/day → increased risk of death from cancer** (food calcium did not show this). See [[High-Dose Isolated Supplement Harms]].

## Lead-author quote
"While supplement use contributes to an increased level of total nutrient intake, there are beneficial associations with nutrients from foods that aren't seen with supplements." (Zhang)

## What it contributes
The cornerstone "**food, not pills**" finding: the *source* of a nutrient matters, consistent with the [[Food Matrix Effect]]. Strongest single citation for why a whole-foods diet beats matching the same nutrients via supplements.

> [!gap] Observational
> Cannot fully exclude confounding (food-source nutrients track overall diet quality). But it converges with RCT data ([[Multivitamins and Mortality (NCI/JAMA 2024)]]).
