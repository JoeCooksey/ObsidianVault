# De Alzaa 2018 — Chemical and Physical Changes in Oils During Heating

Lab study (Acta Scientific, 2018;2(6):2-11) that heated 10 common commercial cooking oils at domestic and elevated temperatures and measured the breakdown products. Frequently cited as the basis for "smoke point isn't what matters" guidance.

## What it contributes
- Reframes how to choose a cooking oil: judge by **oxidative stability**, not [[Smoke Point (Cooking Oils)|smoke point]].
- Provides the surprising headline that **EVOO is among the most stable** cooking oils despite a modest smoke point.

## Key findings
- **Smoke point did not correlate** with performance or stability when heated.
- True predictors of stability: **low polyunsaturated fat**, minimal refining, oxidative stability, and natural antioxidant content.
- **EVOO ranked superior** — lowest polar compounds and trans fats after heating; high antioxidant retention.
- **Canola, grapeseed, and rice bran** performed worst, generating elevated harmful compounds.

## Caveats
> [!gap] Funded/published via olive-oil-industry-affiliated channels (Modern Olives lab; promoted by aboutoliveoil.org) and published in a low-impact journal. Direction of findings is corroborated by independent PUFA-stability research, but treat the EVOO ranking as **medium confidence** given the source's interest.
