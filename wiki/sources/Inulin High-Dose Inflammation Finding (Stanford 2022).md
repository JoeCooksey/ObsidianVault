# Inulin High-Dose Inflammation Finding (Stanford 2022)

Stanford fiber-supplement study reporting that **high-dose isolated inulin (30 g/day) caused inflammation in most participants**, a decrease in one, and **liver-enzyme spikes in three** — variation attributed to differences in gut microbiome.

## Why it matters

Cuts against the "more fiber is always better" assumption for **isolated, highly fermentable** fibers. Supports placing **inulin in C-tier** as a supplement: fine in small prebiotic doses, problematic at high isolated doses. Echoes the broader [[High-Dose Isolated Supplement Harms]] pattern — isolated megadoses behave differently than the same compound embedded in whole food.

> [!gap] Single study, modest n; the inflammation/liver-enzyme signal needs replication. Treat as a caution, not a verdict against inulin in normal dietary amounts.

## Contribution to the topic

Provides the safety caveat distinguishing low-dose prebiotic inulin from high-dose supplementation, and reinforces the food-first framing in [[Fiber Supplement Tier List]].
