# Barber & Odean — Individual Investor Performance

## What this source contributes

The retail-behavior leg of the index-vs-picking evidence stack. Canonical academic work (tens of thousands of real brokerage accounts) showing individual investors don't just fail to beat the market — their *activity itself* is the main cost.

## Key Claims

- Individual investors underperform appropriate benchmarks by **~30 bps/month (~3.6%/yr) after costs**; about half from trading costs. (confidence: high)
- The most active 20% of traders earned **11.4%/yr net vs 18.5% for buy-and-hold investors** — a 7 pp/yr penalty for activity ("Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth," 1991–1996 sample). (confidence: high)
- **Selection is negative, not just costly**: stocks individuals buy subsequently underperform stocks they sell. (confidence: high)
- Documented mechanisms: **disposition effect** (sell winners, hold losers), attention-driven buying, naive reinforcement learning, chronic underdiversification. (confidence: high)

## Notes

Data predates zero-commission apps; commissions shrank but the behavioral findings replicated in later studies (Robinhood-era attention-trading work). Sample period matters for the exact return numbers — treat the *gaps*, not the absolute returns, as the durable finding. This is the empirical justification for the hard cap in [[Core-Satellite Portfolio]] and the "price dropped is the worst sell reason" rule in [[Sell Discipline (When to Sell a Stock)]].
