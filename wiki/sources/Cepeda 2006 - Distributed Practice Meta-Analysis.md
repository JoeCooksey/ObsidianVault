# Cepeda 2006 — Distributed Practice Meta-Analysis

Large meta-analysis (*Psychological Bulletin*, 2006) synthesizing **839 assessments across 317 experiments in 184 articles** on the spacing/distributed-practice effect.

## Key Findings

- **Spacing wins almost universally:** participants using spaced study outperformed massed (crammed) study in **259 of 271 comparisons**.
- **A single day of separation already helps:** splitting one study session into sessions ≥1 day apart improves later retention versus studying it all at once. This is the direct empirical warrant for *daily* learning over weekend binges.
- **Optimal inter-study interval scales with retention interval:** the longer you need to retain something, the longer the ideal gap between reviews. For "remember this for months," gaps of days-to-weeks beat gaps of minutes.
- A follow-up (Cepeda et al., 2008, *Psychological Science*) put numbers on it: for a multi-month retention target, optimal gaps land in the range of ~10–20% of the retention interval.

## What It Contributes

This is the authoritative source behind the single most important structural fact for daily learning: **how you distribute time matters more than how much time you spend.** The same total hours, spread across many short daily sessions, produce dramatically better long-term retention than the same hours massed together — which is exactly why a small *daily* practice outperforms an occasional marathon.
