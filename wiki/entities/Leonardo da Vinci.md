# Leonardo da Vinci

**Leonardo di ser Piero da Vinci** (1452–1519) — Renaissance polymath; painter, anatomist, engineer, geologist, botanist, hydraulicist, and military architect. Widely considered the greatest cross-disciplinary mind in human history. Produced ~7,200 surviving notebook pages across 50+ domains and ~17–20 surviving paintings. His biography by Walter Isaacson (2017), based on primary analysis of the notebooks, is the definitive modern account.

## Career Timeline
- **1452** — Born illegitimate in Vinci; raised by uncle Francesco; excluded from formal schools
- **1466** — Apprenticed to Andrea del Verrocchio in Florence; trained with Botticelli
- **c.1475** — Painted the angel in Verrocchio's *Baptism of Christ*; stunned his master
- **1476** — Accused of sodomy; charges filed, then dropped
- **1482** — Moved to Milan; wrote letter to Ludovico Sforza offering himself as military engineer
- **1483** — *The Virgin of the Rocks* (first Milan commission; sfumato fully developed)
- **c.1489** — *Lady with an Ermine* (Cecilia Gallerani; psychologically revolutionary portraiture)
- **1495–1498** — *The Last Supper* (Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan)
- **1499** — French invasion; fled Milan; began wandering period
- **1502–1503** — Military engineer and cartographer for Cesare Borgia; met Machiavelli
- **c.1503** — *Mona Lisa* begun (worked on for ~14 years; never delivered)
- **1503–1506** — *Battle of Anghiari* mural (abandoned, lost; survives in copies only)
- **1510** — Produced most accurate anatomical drawings in history; heart valve function correctly described
- **1513** — Moved to Rome under Giuliano de' Medici; relatively unproductive
- **1516** — Invited to France by King Francis I; settled at Château du Clos Lucé near Amboise
- **1519** — Died May 2; King Francis reportedly wept at his deathbed; left notebooks to Francesco Melzi

## Defining Characteristics

### Insatiable Curiosity
Leonardo's primary trait — trained, not innate. Filled notebooks with questions: "Why is the sky blue?" "How does a woodpecker use its tongue?" "What does the inside of a skull look like?" He treated every observable phenomenon as an open question. Isaacson argues this curiosity is his most reproducible lesson.

### Observation Over Authority
Refused to defer to Aristotle or Galen when his own eyes contradicted them. Dissected 30+ corpses, corrected classical anatomy with his own observations, and described the function of the aortic valves 150+ years before Harvey published the discovery of circulation (1628). His anatomical drawings remained the most accurate in existence until 17th-century science.

### Cross-Disciplinary Synthesis
Art and science were a single inquiry for Leonardo. His painting required optics, anatomy, geology, botany, and atmospheric physics. His engineering required artistic visual imagination. The separation between "artist" and "scientist" is a modern category he would not have recognized.

### Sfumato
Italian for "vanished into smoke." His signature painting technique: eliminating harsh outlines, replacing them with 30+ ultra-thin glazes built up by fingertip. Reflects his scientific understanding that edges in nature are never sharp — and his intellectual comfort with ambiguity.

### The Notebooks
His cognitive workspace, not a finished diary. Shopping lists appear beside flying machine designs; anatomical drawings sit next to geometry proofs. He thought by writing and drawing simultaneously. Never published; scattered after his death. Currently held across Milan (Codex Atlanticus, ~1,119 pages), Windsor Castle, Vatican, Paris, Madrid, and Seattle (Codex Leicester, purchased by Bill Gates in 1994 for $30.8M).
