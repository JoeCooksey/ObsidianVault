# Anders Ericsson

Swedish psychologist (1947–2020), professor at Florida State University; the world's leading researcher on **expert performance** and the originator of the concept of **[[Deliberate Practice]]**.

## Contribution

- Introduced deliberate practice in **Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993)**, *"The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance."*
- Studied violinists, chess players, athletes, and physicians; showed that *what* and *how* you practice — structured, feedback-rich, at the edge of ability — matters far more than raw hours or innate talent.
- Found that **mental representations** (rich internal models built through practice) are what let experts perceive patterns and self-correct.
- Co-author of *Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise* (2016), the popular-audience version of his research.

## The 10,000-hour misattribution

Malcolm Gladwell's "10,000-hour rule" (*Outliers*) popularized — and distorted — Ericsson's work. Ericsson publicly objected: there is no magic number, and *unstructured* hours don't produce expertise. The mechanism is the **quality** of practice, not a quota. (Source: [[Deliberate Practice]])

## Why he matters for learning

Ericsson supplies the **skill-acquisition** half of the science of learning (the memory half comes from the retrieval/spacing researchers). His work underpins the "drill" and "directness" principles of Ultralearning and Josh Kaufman's [[Rapid Skill Acquisition (First 20 Hours)]].
