# Jim Simons

## Profile

**James Harris "Jim" Simons** (1938–2024) was an American mathematician, Cold War codebreaker (NSA/IDA), and founder of **[[Renaissance Technologies]]**. Widely called the **"Quant King,"** he is regarded as the most successful investor of the modern era by *risk-adjusted* return. Before finance he was an award-winning geometer (the Chern–Simons theory bears his name) and chaired the math department at Stony Brook.

## What He Built

Simons founded the firm in 1978 (as Monemetrics, renamed Renaissance in 1982) and assembled a team of **mathematicians, physicists, and ex-cryptanalysts rather than Wall Street traders** — Leonard Baum, James Ax, Elwyn Berlekamp, Robert Mercer, Peter Brown. Their **[[Medallion Fund]]** produced **~66% gross / ~39% net annual returns** since 1988 — including +98.2% in 2008 and +76% in 2020. (Source: [[Renaissance Technologies — Wikipedia]])

## How the Algorithm Works (what's known)

Renaissance keeps its methods secret, but the public picture: **mean-reversion and statistical-arbitrage signals** mined from petabyte-scale historical data, hunting **anomalies that repeat** across many markets. Thousands of small, short-horizon, market-neutral bets — each barely better than a coin-flip — aggregated via the law of large numbers into a consistent edge. (Source: [[Statistical Arbitrage]], [[Quantitative Trading]]) (confidence: medium — proprietary)

## Is He Actually Successful?

**Yes — uniquely so.** Medallion's after-fee return is unmatched and it survived every crisis since 1988. But the success is **deliberately not scalable**: the fund is capped (~$10B), closed to outsiders since 1993, and the firm bought out all external investors by 2005. The public RIEF fund trails Medallion by ~17–19%, proving the edge lives in small, fast, secret strategies — not something an individual can copy.

## Legacy & Philanthropy

The book *The Man Who Solved the Market* (Gregory Zuckerman, 2019) chronicles the story. Simons gave billions through the Simons Foundation to math and basic-science research. Died May 2024.
