# Ninja CREAMi

Countertop frozen-dessert maker (~$200–250) that turns a frozen solid block into scoopable ice cream, gelato, sorbet, or "lite" ice cream. The defining trick is **freeze-then-shave**, not churn-while-freezing like a traditional churn.

## How it works
1. Blend a liquid base (milk + sweetener + flavor + optional protein/thickener) into a smoothie-like batter.
2. Freeze it solid in the canister for **~24 hours** (Source: [[Forks Over Knives — Ninja CREAMi Comparison]]).
3. The machine drives a **"creamerizer" blade** down through the frozen block, shaving off micro-thin layers and whipping in air, producing creamy texture in **under 4 minutes** (Source: [[Forks Over Knives — Ninja CREAMi Comparison]]).
4. **Re-spin** function re-processes a too-crumbly or refrozen pint back to scoopable.

## Why it matters for health
The appliance itself adds nothing nutritionally — **it's a delivery system for whatever base you put in**. Because you control the base, you can build a dessert that is far higher protein and lower in sugar/calories than store-bought (see [[Protein Ice Cream (Ninja CREAMi) Health Profile]]). It accepts unusual bases (silken tofu, oats, cottage cheese, sweet potato, frozen fruit), which competitors handle poorly (Source: [[Forks Over Knives — Ninja CREAMi Comparison]]).

## Trade-offs
- $250 price + 24 h advance freezing.
- Texture depends heavily on fat and a creaminess booster; too-lean bases come out icy.
- Best eaten fresh; refreezing degrades texture (needs Re-spin).

> [!note] "How healthy is a Ninja CREAMi?" is really "how healthy is the recipe I make in it." The machine is neutral.
