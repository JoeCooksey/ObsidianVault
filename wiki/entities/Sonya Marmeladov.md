# Sonya Marmeladov

The moral and spiritual center of *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]* (Sofya Semyonovna Marmeladova). The gentle, deeply religious daughter of the drunken clerk Marmeladov, she is forced into prostitution to feed her destitute family and starving stepsiblings.

Despite her degradation, Sonya's faith and capacity for love remain unbroken — she is the living rebuttal to [[Rational Egoism|rational egoism]] and [[Russian Nihilism|nihilism]], embodying [[Redemption Through Suffering]]. When [[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov]] confesses the murders to her, she responds not with horror but with pity, urging him to go to the crossroads, kiss the earth he has defiled, confess publicly, and accept his suffering. She follows him to the Siberian prison camp, and it is her steadfast love that finally awakens his spiritual renewal in the epilogue. Her reading of the raising of Lazarus to Raskolnikov is the novel's symbolic heart.

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
