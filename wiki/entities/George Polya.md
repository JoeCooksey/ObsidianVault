# George Pólya

**Hungarian-American mathematician (1887–1985)**, professor at ETH Zürich and Stanford. Known for work in analysis, probability, and combinatorics, but most influential for **how he taught problem solving**.

## Significance

Author of *[[Polya 1945 — How to Solve It|How to Solve It]]* (1945), the canonical practical guide to mathematical problem solving. He reframed problem solving as a **teachable skill** built through guided discovery and self-questioning rather than innate genius — see [[Pólya's Problem-Solving Method]]. His heuristics (work backwards, solve a simpler problem, analogy, look back) became standard vocabulary across mathematics, science, and software engineering.

A direct intellectual ancestor of modern research on [[Metacognition in Problem Solving|metacognition]] and heuristic reasoning.
