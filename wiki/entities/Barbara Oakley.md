# Barbara Oakley

Professor of engineering at Oakland University; co-creator (with neuroscientist Terrence Sejnowski) of **_Learning How to Learn_**, the most-enrolled massive open online course (MOOC) in history (millions of learners on Coursera).

## Background

- Self-described former "math-phobe" who flunked her way through math and science in school, then deliberately retrained herself and became an engineering professor — a living case study in [[Carol Dweck|growth mindset]].
- Author of *A Mind for Numbers* (2014), the book behind the course.

## Key ideas she popularized

- **[[Focused vs Diffuse Mode]]** — the brain learns by alternating between concentrated focus and relaxed background processing.
- **Chunking** — building compact, retrievable units of knowledge through focused attention, understanding, and practice; the foundation of expertise.
- **Fighting procrastination** with the Pomodoro Technique (focus on *process*, not *product*).
- **Why you should "sleep on it"** — diffuse mode and sleep do the consolidation work.

## Why she matters for learning

Oakley is the **great popularizer**: she translated dense cognitive science (Bjork's [[Desirable Difficulties]], retrieval practice, memory consolidation) into a free course that taught millions of people the *attention-and-consolidation* layer of learning — how to schedule focus so both depth and insight happen. (Source: [[Research: How to Learn Anything (The Science of Learning)]])
