# Paul Saladino

Physician (MD), author, and podcast host known as "Carnivore MD." Primary advocate for the animal-based diet. Published *The Carnivore Code* (2019). Based in Costa Rica.

## Career Arc

- **2019–2021**: Strict carnivore diet advocate — zero plant foods. Published *The Carnivore Code* arguing plants are universally harmful.
- **2021–present**: Shifted to "animal-based diet" — organs, grass-fed meat, raw dairy, fruit, honey, and selected low-antinutrient plants. Abandoned zero-carb/zero-fruit stance.
- **Why he quit strict carnivore**: After ~5 years, testosterone dropped to ~500 ng/dL (reported low for his goals), sleep deterioration, heart palpitations, and muscle cramps. Adding fruit/honey brought testosterone to ~800 ng/dL. This is a notable self-correction.

## Core Claims (2019–2021 Carnivore Phase)

All claims evaluated in [[Paul-Saladino-Oats-Vegetables-Claims]]:
- Plants contain antinutrients (phytic acid, saponins, lectins) that damage the gut
- Vegetables cause oxalate/lectin/isothiocyanate toxicity
- Oats contain mycotoxins, glyphosate, phytic acid, saponins
- Polyphenols are hormone disruptors
- Phytosterols accelerate heart disease

**Bottom line on claims**: 2 of 9 claims have solid scientific grounding (mycotoxin and glyphosate contamination of conventional oats). The remaining 7 are overstated, lack human evidence, or are inverted relative to the preponderance of evidence.

## Current Diet (Animal-Based, 2022–present)

Allows: organs (liver, heart), grass-fed/pastured rmeat, butter/tallow, raw dairy, **fruit and honey** (300g carbs/day), selected low-toxin plants (zucchini, olive oil).
Avoids: grains, legumes, most vegetables beyond low-antinutrient options.

## Scientific Credibility Assessment

**Strengths**: Raises legitimate questions about food quality, contaminants, and antinutrient biology. Mycotoxin and glyphosate claims are factually grounded and undersupported in mainstream nutrition discourse.

**Weaknesses**: Systematic cherry-picking of animal and in vitro data over human epidemiology. Ignores meta-analyses of hundreds of thousands of participants showing whole grain and vegetable consumption reduces mortality. His self-experiment is n=1. Has a commercial conflict of interest (Heart & Soil supplements sell the animal-organ alternative).
