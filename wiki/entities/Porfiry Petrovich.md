# Porfiry Petrovich

The examining magistrate investigating the pawnbroker's murder in *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]*. Shrewd, genial, and quietly relentless, Porfiry is [[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov's]] great psychological antagonist.

Porfiry never has hard evidence and never needs it. He conducts a cat-and-mouse game across several interviews, using irony, feigned friendliness, and Raskolnikov's own published article on [[The Extraordinary Man Theory|the extraordinary man]] to apply pressure, betting that a guilty psychology will convict itself. He functions as the theory's perfect refuter: rather than arguing against it, he simply watches it fail inside a real human being wracked by [[Psychology of Guilt|guilt]]. In their final private meeting he tells Raskolnikov plainly that he knows, and urges him to confess — both for a lighter sentence and for the sake of his own soul.

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
