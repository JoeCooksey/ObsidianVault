# Elon Musk

**Elon Reeve Musk** (b. 1971) — CEO of SpaceX, Tesla, and X (formerly Twitter); founder of Neuralink, The Boring Company, and xAI. The wealthiest person in history (as of multiple rankings 2021–2025). The only entrepreneur to have built multiple companies each valued at $20B+ from scratch. His biography by Walter Isaacson (2023), based on 2 years of embedded access, is the primary account.

## Career Timeline
- **1971** — Born in Pretoria, South Africa; father Errol Musk (engineer/developer); mother Maye Musk (model/dietitian)
- **1983** — Sold video game *Blastar* to computer magazine at age 12 for ~$500
- **1988** — Left South Africa for Canada to avoid apartheid-era military service
- **1995** — Dropped out of Stanford PhD program after 2 days; co-founded Zip2 with brother Kimbal
- **1999** — Sold Zip2 to Compaq for $307M; received ~$22M; immediately founded X.com (internet banking)
- **2000** — X.com merged with Confinity (PayPal); Musk ousted as CEO on his honeymoon
- **2002** — eBay acquired PayPal for $1.5B; Musk received ~$180M; founded SpaceX
- **2004** — Led Series A investment in Tesla Motors; became chairman
- **2006** — Promoted Tesla's cause; founded SolarCity with cousins
- **2008** — Near-death year: SpaceX's first 3 Falcon 1 failures + Tesla near-bankruptcy + financial crisis; became Tesla CEO; Falcon 1 succeeded on 4th attempt (Sept 28, 2008) with final available funds; NASA COTS contract ($1.6B) arrived weeks later
- **2012** — Model S launched; Consumer Reports 99/100 score
- **2015** — Falcon 9 first-stage landing; reusability proved possible; co-founded OpenAI (left board 2018)
- **2016** — Founded Neuralink, The Boring Company; Gigafactory Nevada opened
- **2017–2018** — Model 3 production hell; slept on factory floor; "funding secured" tweet; SEC settlement
- **2021** — Briefly became world's richest person (first time)
- **2022** — Acquired Twitter for $44B; fired ~75% of staff; renamed to X
- **2023** — Founded xAI; Starship first orbital test attempt (April); Isaacson biography published
- **2024** — Neuralink first human implant; increasing political involvement

## Defining Characteristics

### The Algorithm
Musk's 5-step engineering process (in mandatory order): (1) Question every requirement — someone's name must be on every spec; (2) Delete parts and steps — if you're not re-adding 10%, you deleted too little; (3) Simplify and optimize; (4) Accelerate cycle time; (5) Automate. Most engineers do these in reverse — they automate first. Musk enforces the correct order obsessively.

### First Principles Thinking
Reason from physics limits, not from analogy or convention. If the laws of physics allow it, assume it's achievable; only physics is a hard limit. Applied to SpaceX: the raw materials for a rocket cost ~2% of market launch price — so the 50× markup is a manufacturing problem, not a materials problem. Applied to Tesla batteries: same analysis drove the Gigafactory design.

### Idiot Index
Musk's metric: ratio of a manufactured part's cost to the cost of its raw materials. A high ratio means someone upstream is doing something stupid. SpaceX and Tesla engineering teams were trained to minimize the idiot index by vertical integration and in-house manufacturing.

### Demon Mode
Isaacson's term for Musk's dark psychological episodes — sudden plunges into rage, paranoia, nihilism, or impulsive destructive action. Correlates with major stressors but also with routine frustrations. Employees developed coping strategies: route bad news through trusted intermediaries; wait 48 hours before acting on directives issued in demon mode. Isaacson attributes the episodes to childhood trauma (severe bullying; difficult relationship with father Errol) and possible mood disorder.

### Risk as Design Requirement
Deliberately sets targets engineers call impossible. SpaceX builds rockets to the edge of failure intentionally — the data from near-misses and actual failures accelerates learning faster than cautious incremental testing. "If things are not failing, you are not innovating enough."

### Hardcore Culture
Extreme work demands — long hours, weekend presence, on-site expectations — enforced through direct intimidation and rapid firing. At Twitter: company-wide "hardcore" pledge; ~75% left overnight. Productive in small mission-critical environments; destructive at scale in mature institutions.
