# Robert Root-Bernstein

Physiologist at Michigan State University; co-author (with Michele Root-Bernstein) of *Sparks of Genius* (2001). MacArthur Fellow. The leading empirical researcher on the connection between arts/avocations and scientific creativity.

## Profile

- **Role**: Professor of Physiology, Michigan State University
- **Awards**: MacArthur Fellowship (1981)
- **Key work**: *Sparks of Genius* (2001) — 13 universal thinking tools of history's most creative people; *Discovering* (1989) — science creativity methodology
- **Research focus**: How avocations in arts and crafts predict scientific creativity; polymathy and Nobel laureate studies

## Key Research Findings

- Nobel laureates average **3× the avocations** of average adults
- Eminent scientists are **15–25× more likely** to maintain adult arts/performance avocations
- Visual imaging ability is one of the best predictors of success in STEM fields
- Arts training in women and minorities particularly improves STEM outcomes

## Three Types of Learners

Root-Bernstein distinguishes:
- **Specialists**: deep but narrow
- **Dilettantes**: superficial breadth without integration or mastery
- **Polymaths**: expertise enabling genuine integration — distinguished from dilettantes by the depth required to see cross-domain connections
