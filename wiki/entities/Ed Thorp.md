# Ed Thorp

## Profile

**Edward O. Thorp** (b. 1932) is an American mathematics professor (MIT, UC Irvine), author, and the **"godfather of quants"** — the first person to systematically beat both the casino and the market with mathematics. He proved the template Jim Simons later industrialized.

## Two Careers, One Method

1. **Beating blackjack** — devised card counting; published *Beat the Dealer* (1962), which rewrote casino gambling. Proof of concept that probability beats intuition.
2. **Beating markets** — applied the same edge-and-bankroll logic to securities:
   - **Princeton/Newport Partners (1969–1989)**: **~20% annualized after fees over ~two decades with no down quarter**, via market-neutral convertible/warrant arbitrage. (confidence: high)
   - **Ridgeline Partners (1994–2002)**: pure [[Statistical Arbitrage]] — which **he invented and first implemented**.

## Why He Quit Ridgeline (the key lesson)

Thorp **wound down Ridgeline in 2002 because stat arb had become crowded** — too many funds chasing the same anomalies shrank returns below his standard. (Source: [[Edward O. Thorp — Wikipedia]]) This is the canonical demonstration that **quant edges decay as they're discovered** — the structural reason there is no permanent money machine.

## Contributions That Outlived His Funds

- **Invented statistical arbitrage** (see [[Statistical Arbitrage]]).
- Popularized the **Kelly criterion** for [[Position Sizing and Risk Management]] — bet sized to your edge to maximize long-run growth.
- Mentor-figure to the whole quant field; memoir *A Man for All Markets* (2017).

## Is He Actually Successful?

Yes — two decades of ~20% net with no losing quarter is elite and durable, and his ideas seeded the entire industry. Notably, he had the discipline to *stop* when his edge eroded rather than blow up chasing it.
