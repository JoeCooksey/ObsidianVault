# Robert Waldinger

American psychiatrist, **professor at Harvard Medical School**, Zen priest, and the **fourth director of the Harvard Study of Adult Development** — one of the longest-running longitudinal studies of human life ever conducted (begun 1938). His TED talk on the study is among the most-viewed of all time.

## Key Facts

- **Role**: Clinical professor of psychiatry, Harvard Medical School; Director, Harvard Study of Adult Development
- **Specialty**: adult development, the determinants of lifelong health and happiness
- **Notable**: TED talk "What makes a good life? Lessons from the longest study on happiness" (40M+ views)

## Core Contribution

Waldinger directs the study that set out to answer *what makes a good life*, following participants (and now their 1,300+ children) for 80+ years with health records, interviews, and biomarkers. **The headline finding: the quality of close relationships — more than money, fame, social class, IQ, or genes — best predicts who stays happy and healthy into old age.** Good relationships protect against physical and mental decline; loneliness is as harmful as smoking or obesity.

This is the empirical anchor for the "relationships" pillar of a life worth loving, and it converges with the [[Hedonic Treadmill]] insight that connection is one of the few goods that resists adaptation. → [[Waldinger Schulz — The Good Life (Harvard Study)]]
