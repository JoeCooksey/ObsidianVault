# Dmitri Razumikhin

[[Rodion Raskolnikov|Raskolnikov's]] loyal, warm-hearted friend and fellow ex-student in *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]* (Dmitri Prokofich Razumikhin). Poor like Raskolnikov but energetic, sociable, and hardworking, he serves as the novel's foil to the protagonist.

Where Raskolnikov meets poverty with proud [[Alienation and Isolation|isolation]] and deadly theorizing, Razumikhin meets it with practicality, humor, and human connection — his name echoes the Russian *razum* ("reason/sense"), the healthy reason set against Raskolnikov's diseased intellect. He selflessly nurses Raskolnikov through his fever, protects his mother and sister, and falls in love with [[Dunya (Avdotya Raskolnikova)|Dunya]], whom he eventually marries. He embodies the ordinary decency and belonging that Raskolnikov has cut himself off from.

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
