# David Deutsch

## Overview
David Deutsch (b. 1953) is a British physicist at the University of Oxford and a founding figure of the **theory of quantum computation** — he formulated the first universal quantum computer (the "universal quantum Turing machine," 1985) and the **Deutsch–Jozsa algorithm**. He is also a leading **philosopher of science** in the Popperian tradition, known for two popular books: *The Fabric of Reality* (1997) and ***The Beginning of Infinity*** (2011).

## Key Contributions / Role
- **"Good explanations are hard to vary."** In *The Beginning of Infinity*, Deutsch argues that knowledge grows through **conjecture and criticism**, and that the mark of a good explanation is that its details are tightly constrained — you can't vary them without breaking it. This is the seed of [[Good Explanations (Hard-to-Vary)]].
- The quote that opens **[[Craig Perry]]**'s video: *"There is only one way of thinking that is capable of making progress, or of surviving in the long run, and that is the way of seeking good explanations through creativity and criticism."*
- Strong proponent of **fallibilism** and unbounded human problem-solving ("problems are soluble").
