# Rodion Raskolnikov

The protagonist of *[[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky|Crime and Punishment]]* (full name Rodion Romanovich Raskolnikov, called Rodya). A brilliant but impoverished former law student in [[St. Petersburg as Setting|St. Petersburg]], proud, isolated, and physically wasting away in a coffin-like garret.

Raskolnikov murders the pawnbroker Alyona Ivanovna (and, by chance, her innocent half-sister Lizaveta) to test [[The Extraordinary Man Theory|his theory]] that "extraordinary" people have the right to transgress moral law. The act destroys him: unable to spend the stolen money and consumed by [[Psychology of Guilt|guilt]], fever, and [[Alienation and Isolation|isolation]], he is drawn into a psychological duel with the magistrate [[Porfiry Petrovich]] and toward [[Sonya Marmeladov|Sonya]], who guides him to confession. His surname comes from the Russian *raskol* ("schism"), marking a man split from society and from himself. In the epilogue, sentenced to Siberia, he finally begins [[Redemption Through Suffering|his regeneration]] through Sonya's love.

*Source: [[Crime and Punishment - Fyodor Dostoevsky]].*
