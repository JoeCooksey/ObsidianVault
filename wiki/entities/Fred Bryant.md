# Fred Bryant

American social psychologist at **Loyola University Chicago**, widely regarded as the **father of savoring research**. With Joseph Veroff he developed the formal theory of **savoring** — the regulation of *positive* experience — and built the measurement tools the field uses.

## Key Facts

- **Role**: Professor of Social Psychology, Loyola University Chicago
- **Specialty**: savoring, positive emotion regulation, subjective wellbeing
- **Instruments developed**: Savoring Beliefs Inventory (SBI); Ways of Savoring Checklist (WOSC)

## Core Contribution

Bryant defined **savoring** as mindful engagement with and amplification of positive experience — establishing it as the upside mirror of *coping*. His key distinctions:

- **Three temporal forms** — anticipation (future), being-in-the-moment (present), and reminiscence (past). A person can be strong in one and weak in another.
- **Savoring ≠ pleasure** — it is the *active attention* that makes a positive experience register and last, not the experience itself.
- **Dampening** — the opposite move (minimizing or dismissing positive feeling), associated with lower wellbeing and depression.
