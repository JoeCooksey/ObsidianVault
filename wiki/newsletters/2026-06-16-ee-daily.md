## Top Stories

**1. NVIDIA Issues $25 Billion High-Grade Bond — Shares Rise 3.5%**
NVIDIA announced a $25 billion high-grade bond offering on June 15, with shares closing up 3.5% the same day. The company carries a ~$5.1 trillion market cap and Strong Buy consensus from 59 analysts (avg. 12-month PT: $298.93). The bond proceeds are widely expected to fund continued AI data center infrastructure buildout, including GPU cluster power and cooling systems requiring tens of MW per facility.

> **Why it matters:** For EEs, a $25B raise earmarked for AI infrastructure means accelerating demand for 48 V bus power delivery ICs, liquid cooling systems, optical interconnects, and GPU PCBs. NVIDIA's capex trajectory is the single biggest driver of advanced semiconductor demand into 2027.

**2. HBM4 Certified by All Three Major Memory Makers — NVIDIA Signs Multi-Year MOU with SK Hynix**
During a June 5–8 South Korea visit, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang announced HBM4 has been certified by SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron, and signed a multi-year technical cooperation agreement with SK Hynix. SK Hynix's M15X fab comes online mid-2027; Samsung's P5 Pyeongtaek facility targets 2028. HBM4 uses 16-Hi stacking vs. HBM3E's 12-Hi, targeting ~2.8 TB/s per stack — a ~40% bandwidth increase over HBM3E's ~1.5–2.0 TB/s. HBM3E still accounts for ~⅔ of HBM shipments in 2026; HBM4 share ramps through the year.

> **Why it matters:** The 16-Hi stack introduces new challenges: TSV (Through-Silicon Via) aspect ratio, thermal resistance of the die stack, underfill mechanical stress at elevated temperatures, and PHY signaling at >6.4 Gbps per pin. The SK Hynix–NVIDIA MOU means co-optimization of HBM4 die specs and NVLink 5 interface simultaneously — a tighter vertical integration than previous generations.

**3. ASML Up 77% YTD — Analysts Lift Targets to €1,900 After Earnings Beat**
ASML reported Q1 2026 earnings above consensus and raised its full-year guidance. Bank of America set a target of €1,921, JPMorgan €1,900 (from €1,515), and Barclays €1,900 (from €1,575). ASML demonstrated a 1,000 W EUV source power level and High-NA (0.55 NA) capability in its TWINSCAN EXE:5000 platform; high-NA reduces the number of multi-patterning steps from ~100 to ~10 for sub-2 nm features. The stock closed June 11 up 4.56%.

> **Why it matters:** High-NA EUV is the enabling technology for TSMC N1.4 (Icefish), Intel 18A, and Samsung SF1.4. The 0.55 NA lens shrinks the effective k₁ factor below 0.3, enabling single-exposure patterning of features previously requiring triple-patterning workarounds. ASML's near-monopoly on EUV means any capacity constraints here cascade into chip delivery timelines across every advanced node.

**4. 2D CuInP₂S₆ (CIPS) Ferroelectric Material Demonstrated for Brain-Inspired Neuromorphic Computing**
Published in *Advanced Functional Materials* (2026), researchers demonstrated CuInP₂S₆ (copper indium thiophosphate) van der Waals ferroelectric devices for neuromorphic AI hardware. CIPS exhibits room-temperature ultrathin ferroelectricity in its layered vdW structure, with switchable spontaneous polarization enabling synaptic weight emulation. The same material class (2D vdW ferroelectrics) is now applied to retinomorphic vision sensors integrating sensing, nonvolatile memory, and in-pixel computation.

> **Why it matters:** CIPS-based [[FeFET]]s operate at orders-of-magnitude lower power than CMOS for multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations by storing weights as ferroelectric polarization states rather than charge in capacitors. The 2D nature (sub-1 nm layer thickness) enables sub-10 nm device scaling beyond what HfZrO₂ (HZO) can reach without significant depolarization loss. Watch for IEEE IEDM 2026 demonstrations.

**5. TSMC May 2026 Revenue: +30.1% YoY, +1.5% MoM — Arizona Fully Booked Through 2027**
TSMC reported consolidated net revenue of NT$416.98 billion (~$12.8 B USD) for May 2026, continuing its trajectory of >30% annual growth driven by AI chip demand. TSMC North America, Global, and Arizona guarantees were detailed in the June 10 filing. The Arizona fab remains fully booked through 2027; TSMC targets six-figure monthly wafer output by 2029. The company had earlier announced a 15% price hike on 3 nm wafers for H2 2026.

> **Why it matters:** TSMC's 3 nm price hike directly affects AMD, Apple, and NVIDIA chip costs in H2 2026. The full Arizona booking through 2027 signals that US domestic advanced node capacity remains a bottleneck even as geopolitical pressure pushes more fabs onshore. For fab tool suppliers (ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research), TSMC's capex is the leading indicator.

**6. 14-Inch SiC Crystal Commercialization Advances; PCIM 2026 Highlights WBG Data Center Power Trends**
Tiancheng Semiconductor's 14-inch SiC single crystal (30 mm effective thickness) marks China's transition from 12-inch adoption to 14-inch commercialization. PCIM Europe 2026 highlighted 1200 V SiC high-temperature modules, GaN scaling toward higher voltage classes, and AI-driven design optimization for power converters. Double-sided cooling for SiC modules is emerging as standard for 800V EV drivetrain and 48V data center bus applications.

> **Why it matters:** Larger SiC wafers (14-inch vs. current 8-inch mainstream) reduce die cost through more dies per wafer. For data centers, the transition from 12V to 48V bus architectures is enabling the adoption of GaN-based voltage regulators (VRMs) with higher switching frequencies (>10 MHz), smaller inductors, and improved transient response — critical for powering the ~1 kW per GPU chip loads in Blackwell and future architectures.

## Daily Research Topic: AI Data Center Power Architecture — 48V Bus, Liquid Cooling, and the Road to 1 MW GPU Clusters

**What it is:** Modern AI training clusters (NVIDIA DGX GB200 NVL72, etc.) pack >100 kW into a single rack. The industry is converging on 48V DC bus architectures with direct liquid cooling (DLC) to address the power density limits of air cooling (~25–30 kW/rack). Understanding the power chain from the utility grid through the rectifier → 48V DC bus → point-of-load converters → GPU VRMs is essential for EEs working in hyperscaler or power IC design.

**5 Technical Bullets:**
- **48V Bus Advantage:** Switching from 12V to 48V reduces bus current by 4×, cutting I²R losses by 16× for the same power delivery. At 10 kW per rack slot, 12V bus carries ~833 A; 48V bus carries only ~208 A — a 2 AWG cable vs. a 6 AWG cable difference.
- **GaN VRM Design:** 48V-to-1V GPU core rail requires multi-phase GaN FET buck converters switching at 5–15 MHz. Key specs: 99%+ efficiency at full load, <1% output voltage deviation under 100 A/μs transient, 10 MHz GaN switching enables inductors <100 nH (vs. 300–500 nH at 1 MHz for Si MOSFETs).
- **Liquid Cooling Thermal Math:** A single NVIDIA Blackwell GPU (B200) dissipates ~1 kW. Air cooling maxes at ~100 W/cm² heat flux; direct liquid cooling (DLC) handles 500–1000 W/cm² at 60°C coolant inlet. The thermal resistance θ_ja from junction to coolant must be <0.05 °C/W to keep junction below 85°C at 1 kW.
- **Facility Power Density:** A 1 MW GPU cluster (1,000× B200 at 1 kW each) requires: 1 MW utility connection + 10–15% for cooling overhead = ~1.15 MW facility load. Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) for DLC facilities: 1.05–1.15 vs. 1.4–1.6 for air-cooled.
- **Bus Bar vs. Bus Bar Topology:** At 48V/1 MW, bus bars must handle 20,833 A. Busbars are typically copper, rated for ~300 A/cm², requiring cross-sections of ~70 cm² per bus. Inductance of the bus structure is critical for VRM stability — keep L_bus < 5 nH for GaN VRM loops.

**Where to Start:**
- IEEE Xplore search terms: `"48V bus" AND "GaN" AND "data center"`, `"direct liquid cooling" AND "GPU" AND "power density"`, `"point-of-load" AND "AI server" AND "2026"`

- APEC 2026 proceedings on GaN VRM design for AI servers
- Texas Instruments application note: 48V-to-1V multi-phase buck reference design

**Concrete Calculation to Work Through:**
*Design a 4-phase GaN buck converter for 48V→1.0V at 400 W (single GPU core rail):*
- Duty cycle D = 1.0/48 = 0.021 (2.1%)
- At 10 MHz per phase: inductor ripple ΔI_L = (V_in − V_out) × D / (f_sw × L) = 47 × 0.021 / (10 MHz × L)
- For ΔI_L = 30% of I_out/4 = 0.30 × 100 A / 4 = 7.5 A → L = 47 × 0.021 / (10 MHz × 7.5) = **13 nH per phase**
