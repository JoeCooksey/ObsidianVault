# EE Daily — May 25, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. MIT Silicon Photonics Chip Eliminates Grating Lobes in LiDAR Optical Phased Arrays**

MIT researchers published "Reduced-crosstalk antennas for grating-lobe-free and wide-field-of-view integrated optical phased arrays" in *Nature Communications* (May 7, 2026). The team used a geometric *detuning* technique — intentionally varying antenna pitch in a specific pattern — to suppress evanescent coupling between adjacent waveguides in a silicon-photonics [[Optical Phased Array]] chip. The result is a wide field-of-view beam-steering LiDAR with no grating lobes and no increase in noise floor compared to sparse-array approaches.

> **Why it matters:** Grating lobes are the central unsolved problem in dense [[Optical Phased Array]] LiDAR — close spacing kills the crosstalk, wide spacing brings back grating lobes. This geometric detuning approach breaks that tradeoff on a fully integrated Si-photonics chip, directly on the path to solid-state, chip-scale LiDAR for autonomous vehicles and aerial surveying. This is a printable result — no new materials, no moving parts, manufacturable on standard CMOS photonics fabs.

**2. TSMC N2 GAA Node Now in Volume Production — 50,000 Wafers/Month, Orders Booked Through 2028**

TSMC commenced volume production of its N2 (2 nm-class) process at Fab 22 in Kaohsiung, reporting over 50,000 wafer starts per month with initial yields reaching approximately 70%. N2 is TSMC's first [[Gate-All-Around]] (nanosheet) transistor, delivering a 10–15% speed improvement at ISO power versus N3E, or a 25–30% power reduction at ISO performance. Fifteen customers — predominantly HPC/AI accelerator designers — have committed capacity. N2P (performance-enhanced extension) and the A16 (1.6 nm) node with Backside Power Delivery Networks ([[BSPDN]]) are both slated for H2 2026 production entry.

> **Why it matters:** The GAA era is no longer theoretical — it is shipping silicon. Backside power delivery in A16 is the next architectural step: pulling the power delivery network below the transistor layer frees front-side routing for signal, directly reducing IR drop and enabling higher transistor density. Meanwhile, [[CoWoS]] advanced packaging and [[HBM4]] allocation remain the binding constraint — TSMC's CoWoS capacity is sold out through 2026, and SK Hynix reports its entire 2026 HBM output is already allocated. The chip is no longer the bottleneck; packaging is.

**3. APEC 2026 Standouts: 700V Monolithic Bidirectional GaN and 1 mΩ 1200V SiC Half-Bridge**

The Applied Power Electronics Conference 2026 surfaced two headline hardware demonstrations. STMicroelectronics showed a 7 kW Dual-Active Bridge AC/DC converter enabled by a *monolithic* 700 V GaN bidirectional switch — integrating both the top and bottom switch into a single die, eliminating the separate gate driver and dead-time circuitry required by discrete pairs. Separately, SemiQ launched the QSiC Dual3 family of 1200 V SiC half-bridge MOSFET modules with an RDS(on) of **1 mΩ** and a power density of **240 W/in³**. Renesas additionally unveiled the TP65B110HRU — the first commercially announced bidirectional switch using depletion-mode [[GaN]] capable of blocking positive and negative voltages in a single package.

> **Why it matters:** Monolithic bidirectional GaN at 700 V eliminates a major BoM item in DAB converters used in EV onboard chargers and grid-tie storage. For SiC, 1 mΩ at 1200 V in a module format closes the remaining gap with Si IGBT in power density — this is the spec that opens heavy traction inverter design wins. Both parts ship into the same 800V EV architecture that is rapidly becoming the automotive standard. See [[Silicon Carbide Power Electronics]] and [[Gallium Nitride Power Electronics]].

**4. OpenLight Demonstrates 400G-per-Lane III-V/Si Photonics at OFC 2026**

At OFC 2026 (March 15–19, Los Angeles), OpenLight Photonics showed a 400 Gbps-per-lane electro-absorption (EA) modulator using heterogeneous III-V-on-silicon photonics with flip-chip co-packaging alongside a MACOM driver IC, delivering an optical modulation amplitude (OMA) of **3 dBm** and extinction ratio greater than **3.5 dB**. The company also demonstrated a 1.6T DR8 transceiver evaluation board combining its heterogeneous-integrated PIC (lasers, modulators, amplifiers, photodetectors) with a Marvell Ara 3 nm DSP — total power consumption approximately **2.0 W** for the complete optical engine.

> **Why it matters:** The AI cluster interconnect problem is fundamentally an optical problem — scaling 100k-GPU training fabrics requires co-packaged optics (CPO) that move bandwidth off the copper traces and onto the die edge at sub-2W per 1.6T lane. OpenLight's platform puts the laser, modulator, and amplifier on the same silicon die via heterogeneous III-V integration — no external laser alignment, no fiber pigtail to the chip. At 2.0 W for 1.6T, this approaches the power envelope needed for practical CPO deployment. See [[Silicon Photonics]].

**5. Microsoft + Quantinuum: 12 Logical Qubits, 11× Better Error Rate, Hybrid Quantum Chemistry**

Microsoft Azure Quantum and Quantinuum demonstrated the creation of 12 highly reliable logical qubits on Quantinuum's H2 trapped-ion processor, achieving a logical circuit error rate of **0.002** — 11× better than the corresponding physical qubit error rate of 0.023. The teams used this system to run the first end-to-end hybrid workflow combining error-corrected quantum compute, cloud HPC, and AI to accurately estimate the ground-state energy of the active space of a catalytic intermediate. New QLDPC + concatenated Steane code protocols are reducing both space overhead (physical qubits per logical qubit) and time overhead (logical gate cycle time) simultaneously.

> **Why it matters:** The 0.002 logical error rate is below the threshold where some useful chemistry algorithms become tractable — and the hybrid HPC+AI+quantum architecture is the production model, not pure-quantum. For EEs: the hardware layer is all RF and microwave engineering — trapped-ion control requires precise 1–10 MHz laser modulation, cryogenic microwave resonators, and AWG-driven pulse sequences at nanosecond resolution. [[Quantum Error Correction]] has crossed from theoretical to demonstrated, and the instrumentation stack is an active RF hardware problem.

**6. 2D Ferroelectric Semiconductors: van der Waals FeSFETs Merge Memory, Sensing, and Compute**

A pair of high-impact reviews in *Advanced Materials* and *Advanced Science* (2026) systematize the emerging class of van der Waals ferroelectric semiconductor FETs (vdW FeSFETs): devices combining a ferroelectric gate dielectric with a 2D semiconductor channel (e.g., In₂Se₃, CuInP₂S₆, MoTe₂) in a single van der Waals stack. The unique combination of switchable spontaneous polarization and semiconducting channel in the same atomically thin material enables in-sensor memory — a single device can sense, store, and compute a signal without data movement. Key device challenges identified: polarization fatigue at <10⁶ cycles in current materials, interface trap density at the vdW junction, and scalable interconnects to bulk-CMOS back-end.

> **Why it matters:** In-sensor computing eliminates the ADC-to-memory-to-processor pipeline that dominates edge AI power budgets. A retinomorphic imager array built from vdW FeSFETs processes and stores each pixel locally — no frame buffer, no data bus. The EE design challenge is the interface: 2D materials are at sub-5 nm thickness where standard dielectric deposition (ALD) disrupts the crystal, and contact resistance at the metal/vdW interface is still ~10× worse than bulk CMOS. Watch [[2D Materials]] and [[Ferroelectric Devices]] for follow-on device demonstrations in 2026.

## Daily Research Topic: Optical Phased Arrays — From Antenna Theory to Solid-State LiDAR

**Why now:** The MIT Nature Comms paper published May 7 is the most concrete silicon-photonics chip result of the month, and the underlying problem — how to steer a beam with no moving parts using a phased array of optical antennas — is a direct application of antenna array theory to integrated photonics. This is a 30–60 minute topic with clean math, deep EE roots, and direct industry relevance in the 2026 autonomous vehicle and robotics space.

**What it is:** An [[Optical Phased Array]] (OPA) is an array of optical emitters (waveguide-coupled grating antennas) where the relative phase of each emitter is controlled via thermo-optic or electro-optic phase shifters. By applying a linear phase gradient across the array, the far-field radiation pattern can be steered in angle without any mechanical movement — exactly analogous to phased array radar, but at 1550 nm wavelength instead of microwave frequencies.

**Specific technical concepts to understand:**

- **Array factor and grating lobes:** The far-field intensity of an N-element uniform linear array is `AF(θ) = Σ exp(jnkd·sin(θ) + jφₙ)` where d is element spacing, k = 2π/λ, and φₙ is the applied phase. Grating lobes appear when `d > λ/2` — for optical arrays at λ = 1550 nm, this means element pitch must be <775 nm, which is barely resolvable in silicon photonics. Geometric detuning varies d slightly across the array to suppress grating lobe coherence.
- **Evanescent coupling:** When two Si waveguides are placed within ~200–500 nm of each other, the evanescent tail of each guided mode couples energy to the neighbor. Coupling coefficient κ grows exponentially as gap shrinks — the MIT result uses the varying-pitch detuning to place elements close enough for high fill-factor while breaking the uniform periodicity that enables resonant coupling buildup.
- **Phase-shifting approaches:** Thermo-optic shifters (Δn ~10⁻⁴/°C in Si, ~ms response) vs electro-optic shifters (Δn ~10⁻³–10⁻² in BTO or LiNbO₃ on Si, ~ns response). OPA steering bandwidth is limited by whichever shifter is used — thermal shifters are adequate for static scenes but too slow for automotive LiDAR point-cloud rates (>10 fps, >100k points/s).
- **Fill factor and side-lobe level:** Fill factor = emitter aperture / pitch. Low fill factor → high side-lobe level → noise floor. The MIT design targets high fill factor within the grating-lobe-free constraint, which is why the reduced-crosstalk antenna design is the enabling innovation.
- **FMCW vs ToF detection:** FMCW (frequency-modulated continuous-wave) LiDAR measures range as a beat frequency between transmitted and reflected chirped laser, giving simultaneous range and velocity from a single coherent detector. ToF (time-of-flight) uses pulsed illumination and measures round-trip delay. OPAs are architecturally compatible with FMCW, which is why silicon-photonics OPAs and FMCW detection are converging in the same product roadmap.

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore search terms: `"optical phased array" silicon photonics`, `"grating lobe free" OPA lidar`, `"evanescent coupling" waveguide array`
- Paper: "Reduced-crosstalk antennas for grating-lobe-free and wide-field-of-view integrated optical phased arrays," Crawford-Eng et al., *Nature Communications* 2026
- Background: Fatemi et al., "A Massively Scalable Silicon Photonic OPA," *Optica* 2019 — still the definitive OPA architecture paper
- Wikilinks to create/update: [[Optical Phased Array]], [[Silicon Photonics]], [[LiDAR]]

**Calculation to work through:**

A 64-element OPA has pitch d = 4 μm at λ = 1550 nm. (1) Find the grating lobe angle: what is the smallest steering angle at which a grating lobe appears? (2) What pitch would push the first grating lobe to ±90° (eliminate grating lobes entirely)? (3) If the thermo-optic coefficient of silicon is dn/dT = 1.86 × 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹ and you need a π phase shift over a 500 μm heater waveguide, what ΔT is required? Use these numbers to understand why the MIT geometric detuning approach is needed and why full grating-lobe elimination via pitch alone is impossible in standard Si-photonics lithography.
