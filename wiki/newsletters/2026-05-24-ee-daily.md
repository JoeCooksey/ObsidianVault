# EE Daily — May 24, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

---

## Top Stories

### 1. Semiconductor Market Hits Historic Pace — AI Is the Engine
Global semiconductor sales reached **$298.5 billion in Q1 2026**, up 25% quarter-over-quarter. Full-year 2026 is projected at ~$975 billion — a historic peak. The driver is AI infrastructure: training clusters, inference servers, and memory bandwidth demand. **HBM4 (16Hi configuration)** is targeting volume production in 1H 2026, which will be a key inflection for AI chip performance.

> **Why it matters:** If you're doing anything with high-speed signaling, power delivery, or memory interfacing, HBM4 sets a new baseline for what "state of the art" means.

---

### 2. Co-Packaged Optics Could Cut Networking Power by 70%
Silicon photonics via **co-packaged optics (CPO)** is emerging as the solution to the interconnect bottleneck in AI clusters. By integrating optical transceivers directly into the package (vs. pluggable modules), CPO can reduce networking power consumption by up to 70% while boosting data transfer speeds. 2026 is expected to be the year photonic chips cross from specialized to mainstream manufacturing.

> **Why it matters:** The power wall in AI datacenters is real. CPO and silicon photonics aren't research topics anymore — they're in production roadmaps.

---

### 3. Thin-Film Lithium Niobate (TFLN) Wafers Hit 8-Inch Manufacturing
Silicon Austria Labs fabricated its first **8-inch TFLN wafer** entirely in-house and is opening multi-project wafer (MPW) runs to academic and industrial partners in 2026. TFLN is the leading platform for electro-optic modulators, RF-to-optical conversion, and quantum photonics — and 8-inch is the threshold for cost-competitive volume production.

> **Why it matters:** TFLN is where LiNbO₃'s exceptional electro-optic properties meet modern fab processes. This opens the door to affordable photonic ICs for 6G fronthaul, lidar, and quantum comms.

---

### 4. Atomic-Scale Gap Found in 2D Material Devices — A Fundamental Limit
Researchers discovered that when **2D semiconductor materials** (like MoS₂ or WSe₂) are paired with the insulating layers required for device fabrication, an unavoidable atomic-scale vacuum gap forms at the interface. This gap degrades carrier transport and represents a hard physical barrier to further miniaturization beyond silicon CMOS.

> **Why it matters:** 2D materials are the leading candidate to extend Moore's Law past 2nm-class silicon. This finding either needs a solution or changes the roadmap.

---

### 5. Ultra-Wide Bandgap Materials Set to Define 2026 RF Landscape
2026 is being called the inflection year for **ultra-wide bandgap (UWBG) semiconductors** — materials like AlN, diamond, and Ga₂O₃ that go beyond GaN in breakdown voltage and thermal resilience. Combined with AI-assisted design, 6G, D-band (110–170 GHz), and terahertz systems, these materials are unlocking power amplifiers and switches previously impossible with Si or GaAs.

> **Why it matters:** GaN already transformed RF power. UWBG is the next generation — critical for satellite, radar, and sub-THz 6G base stations.

---

### 6. University of Houston Nanoporous Dielectric Cuts AI Chip Heat and Energy Loss
A breakthrough **thin-film dielectric material** using nanoscale pores lowers the dielectric constant of inter-layer dielectrics (ILD) in chips. Lower k → faster signal propagation, less capacitive coupling, less heat. Developed using reticular chemistry (MOF-inspired), this addresses one of the critical thermal/speed bottlenecks in advanced logic nodes.

> **Why it matters:** Heat density in 3nm-class chips is already a packaging crisis. Materials-level solutions like this are essential alongside architectural ones.

---

### 7. Cisco's Universal Quantum Switch: Quantum Networking Gets an Interconnect Standard
Cisco unveiled the **Universal Quantum Switch**, designed to connect quantum systems using different qubit encodings (photonic, trapped ion, superconducting) over existing telecom fiber infrastructure. This is the "Ethernet switch moment" for quantum networking — enabling heterogeneous quantum systems to interoperate.

> **Why it matters:** Quantum networking has been stuck at single-node experiments. An interconnect-layer abstraction is the prerequisite for any real quantum internet.

## Daily Research Topic

### Ultra-Wide Bandgap (UWBG) Semiconductors

**Why today:** Three of the top stories above (RF, power dielectrics, photonics) converge on a common theme: material limits. UWBG is the research frontier that could unlock the next generation of power electronics and RF simultaneously.

**What to understand:**
- **Bandgap hierarchy:** Si (1.1 eV) → GaAs (1.4 eV) → GaN (3.4 eV) → Ga₂O₃ (4.8 eV) → AlN (6.2 eV) → Diamond (5.5 eV)
- **Figure of merit:** Baliga's FOM (ε·μ·Eₓ³) — why higher bandgap → dramatically better power switching
- **Ga₂O₃ (gallium oxide):** High breakdown, easy substrate growth (melt-grown), but low thermal conductivity — the tradeoff problem
- **Diamond:** Ultimate material properties, but p-type doping remains unsolved
- **AlN:** UWBG RF applications — piezoelectric + wide bandgap in one material (FBAR/BAW resonators)
- **Key challenge:** Defect density and doping control — all UWBG materials are still far behind GaN in maturity

**Where to start:**
1. Review paper: *"Ultra-wide bandgap semiconductors for power electronics"* — search IEEE Xplore
2. [[Gallium Oxide]] — Ga₂O₃ breakdown voltage records
3. [[GaN Power Electronics]] — the predecessor roadmap, useful for comparison
4. [[Silicon Photonics]] — adjacent photonic materials story

**One key question to answer:** *Why does higher bandgap increase breakdown voltage, and what does that mean for on-resistance in a power MOSFET? Work through the math.*
