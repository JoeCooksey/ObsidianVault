# EE Daily — June 10, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. NVIDIA RTX Spark: Grace Blackwell Superchip Brings 1 PFLOP and 128 GB Unified Memory to Windows PCs**

Announced at Computex 2026 in Taipei, the RTX Spark integrates a 20-core Arm Grace CPU with a Blackwell GPU (6,144 CUDA cores, equivalent desktop RTX 5070 performance) on a single package with up to 128 GB unified LPDDR5X memory. It delivers 1 petaflop of AI compute, runs the full CUDA software stack natively on Arm for the first time, and outperforms Apple's M5 Max GPU in ray-traced and CUDA-dependent workloads. OEM devices from Dell, HP, Lenovo, ASUS, and Microsoft Surface ship fall 2026. NVIDIA also confirmed Apple as a major AI client, with Spark silicon powering Apple's on-device inference expansion.

> **Why it matters:** This is NVIDIA's first direct SoC challenge to Apple Silicon's unified memory architecture — and it brings the CUDA ecosystem with it. For EEs: unified memory eliminates PCIe bandwidth bottlenecks that plagued discrete GPU setups; the Grace CPU's Arm cores use ~15 W TDP for the CPU subsystem vs ~45 W for x86 equivalents. Watch the thermal and power delivery design on these thin-and-light systems — 128 GB LPDDR5X at full AI workload is a non-trivial power challenge.

**2. Apple WWDC 2026: Gemini-Powered Siri, Multi-AI Extensions, iOS 27 — and Tim Cook's Final Keynote**

At WWDC 2026, Apple unveiled a rebuilt Siri powered by Google Gemini under a multi-year deal reportedly worth ~$1 billion/year. Siri now ships as a standalone app with a chat interface, supports multimodal input (text/audio/image/video), and operates contextually across the screen. iOS 27 ("Liquid Glass" redesign) promises 30% faster app launch times and 70% faster photo library rendering. Apple's multi-AI Extensions framework also opens the door to Claude and other LLMs as iPhone AI options. Tim Cook delivered what he called his final keynote as CEO.

> **Why it matters:** The Gemini inference pipeline on-device raises critical questions for RF/baseband engineers — Apple has historically used its own silicon (Neural Engine in A-series/M-series) for on-device AI; offloading heavier Gemini requests to Google's cloud adds latency-sensitive network round trips. For EEs designing next-gen silicon photonics interconnects and 6G infrastructure: Apple's cloud-first AI strategy makes low-latency mmWave backhaul even more commercially urgent.

**3. China Breaks 40 µm SiC Laser Lift-Off Barrier; 14-Inch Crystal Progresses Toward Commercialization**

Two parallel SiC manufacturing milestones from China: Zhongwei Precision Instrument announced mass production of 8-inch SiC wafers using laser lift-off technology with total material loss in the **40 µm range**, disrupting the industry-standard 60–80 µm loss barrier. Separately, Tiancheng Semiconductor confirmed successful development of a **14-inch SiC single crystal**, transitioning the market from the "12-inch adoption phase" to early-stage 14-inch commercialization. The GaN/SiC power semiconductor market is projected to grow from $2.53B in 2026 to $16.17B by 2034 (26.1% CAGR), though the 2026 market is characterized by SiC oversupply and weak EV demand.

> **Why it matters:** Reducing kerf loss from 70 µm to 40 µm is a direct yield improvement — for a 150 mm boule, each wafer gained is worth thousands of dollars at current SiC pricing. The 14-inch transition mirrors the 8→12-inch silicon transition from the late 1990s: first mover advantage in tooling qualification is significant. Data center UPS and solar inverter designs are the near-term SiC growth driver (not EVs in 2026). If you're specifying SiC FETs now, note that price erosion is real — Wolfspeed, Onsemi, and STMicro have all cut pricing on 650 V/1200 V devices this year.

**4. AI-Designed Thermoelectric Generators Hit Record Conversion Efficiency (Nature, 2026)**

A Nature paper reports using AI to rapidly design [[Thermoelectric Generator]] (TEG) modules — devices converting waste heat directly to electricity — yielding prototypes with record-level conversion efficiencies. The AI-optimization pipeline explored materials parameters (Seebeck coefficient S, electrical conductivity σ, thermal conductivity κ) simultaneously, bypassing the historical S-σ-κ trade-off bottleneck. Separately, EPFL researchers demonstrated a chip-scale ultrafast laser (femtosecond pulses) performing on par with traditional tabletop systems, with direct applications in silicon photonics integration and quantum computing timing references.

> **Why it matters:** TEGs have been constrained to niche applications (RTGs, industrial waste heat) by a figure-of-merit ZT ≈ 1 ceiling. If AI-designed devices are cracking ZT ≥ 2 with manufacturable materials, the addressable market — server rack waste heat, automotive exhaust recovery, wearables — expands dramatically. The ZT = S²σT/κ formula is the design constraint to understand: search IEEE Xplore for "AI Pareto optimization thermoelectric ZT" to find the paper trail.

**5. 2D Ferroelectric Semiconductors: CuInP₂S₆ Neuromorphic Logic and Fe₂Mo₃O₈ Colossal Polarization**

Two [[2D Ferroelectric Materials]] papers appeared in *Advanced Materials* and *Advanced Functional Materials* (June 2026): (1) Brain-inspired 2D CuInP₂S₆ ferroelectric devices demonstrated neuromorphic computing functionality, implementing both potentiation and depression of synaptic weights; (2) 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ semiconductor single crystals showed colossal ferroelectric polarization, with a narrow bandgap enabling infrared-optical information storage via a multilayer heterojunction. These follow the 2026 surge in ferroelectric hybrid research ("Ferroelectrics Hybrids" review: "Harnessing Multifunctionality of 2D Semiconductors in the Post-Moore Era").

> **Why it matters:** As CMOS scaling approaches limits below 2 nm, ferroelectric field-effect transistors (FeFETs) offer non-volatile compute-in-memory architectures that reduce memory-wall power costs. The 2D material class (MoS₂, CuInP₂S₆, In₂Se₃) enables ferroelectricity at atomic thicknesses where bulk materials lose their properties. IEEE Xplore search: "2D ferroelectric FeFET logic-in-memory 2026" — the relevant design parameters are remnant polarization Pr, coercive field Ec, and retention window.

## 🔬 Daily Deep Dive: Wide-Bandgap Device Selection for Data Center Power — SiC vs. GaN 2026 Decision Matrix

**What it is:** With SiC oversupply driving price cuts and GaN on Si maturing at 650 V, power electronics engineers specifying converters for AI server power supplies (AC-DC and DC-DC stages) face a genuine 2026 decision fork. This is a practical device selection framework.

**Technical bullets:**
- **Breakdown field:** GaN: ~3.3 MV/cm; SiC: ~2.5 MV/cm; Si: ~0.3 MV/cm — both WBG enable thinner drift layers and lower R_on·A for a given voltage rating
- **Switching speed:** GaN switches at 10–100 MHz (low gate charge Q_g, no minority carrier storage); SiC MOSFET switches at 100 kHz–1 MHz (SiO₂/SiC interface defects limit gate charge); Si IGBT: 20–100 kHz
- **Thermal conductivity:** SiC: ~490 W/m·K; GaN-on-Si: ~150 W/m·K (Si substrate dominates); GaN-on-SiC: ~350 W/m·K — critical for power density at high ambient temperatures
- **Application sweet spots (2026):** GaN optimal for 48 V→1.8 V data center VRMs (high frequency = small magnetics); SiC optimal for 480 V AC-DC rectifiers, 3-phase inverters, UPS, EV OBC — where 1200 V rating and robust body diode matter
- **Cost trajectory:** 650 V GaN FET is now price-competitive with SiC in <1 kW range; 1200 V SiC MOSFET remains the cost-effective choice above 5 kW

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore search terms: `"GaN data center power" 2026`, `"SiC MOSFET 1200V switching loss 2026"`, `"48V GaN VRM"`, `"wide bandgap server PSU efficiency"`
- [[Power Electronics (Domain)]], [[GaN HEMT]], [[Silicon Carbide (SiC) Power Devices]]
- Read Wolfspeed's 2026 application note on SiC MOSFET gate driver design (Rg, dv/dt, CMC management)

**Concrete calculation to work through:**
Design a 10 kW, 480 V AC-input to 48 V DC totem-pole PFC stage. Compare:
- CREE C3M0030090K (SiC, 900 V, 30 mΩ) vs GaN Systems GS66516B (GaN, 650 V, 25 mΩ)
- Calculate switching loss at 100 kHz: P_sw = ½ × V_DS × I_D × (t_r + t_f) × f_sw
- Compare heatsink requirement (junction-to-case θ_JC: SiC MOSFET ~0.4 °C/W vs GaN eHEMT ~0.5 °C/W)
- Which achieves 99% efficiency and fits on a 50 × 50 mm thermal footprint?
