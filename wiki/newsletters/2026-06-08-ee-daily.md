## Top Stories

**1. Terafab: SpaceX + Tesla + Intel File $55–119B Texas Semiconductor Fab — ASML CEO Confirms Talks**

SpaceX has filed a property tax abatement application in Grimes County, Texas (90 miles NE of Austin) for a greenfield semiconductor fab planned at $55B in Phase 1, scaling to $119B across all phases. The Terafab joint venture, announced March 21, 2026, involves SpaceX, Tesla, and Intel targeting >1 terawatt/year of AI compute capacity. ASML CEO Christophe Fouquet confirmed direct talks with Elon Musk, telling Reuters that Musk is "very serious." Musk is scheduled to appear virtually at ASML's private technology conference on June 9–10 alongside Fouquet for a fireside chat on Terafab. Analysts raised ASML price targets in response: BofA to EUR 1,921, JPMorgan to EUR 1,900 (Overweight), Morgan Stanley to EUR 1,660. Critical constraint: ASML shipped only 48 EUV systems in 2025, and its full order book is committed to TSMC, Samsung, and Intel through 2027 — Terafab has no confirmed EUV orders yet.

> **Why it matters:** This is the largest announced greenfield fab investment in US history, but Terafab faces the EUV procurement problem that no amount of capital can immediately solve — ASML runs one production line and cannot rapidly scale EUV output. The supply chain math (1 EUV tool ~$380M, 48/yr capacity, fully booked) means Terafab cannot reach advanced nodes before 2029 at the earliest, regardless of funding. The real story is whether Intel's process technology partnership gives Terafab a path to licensed node access.

**2. NVIDIA RTX Spark + TSMC Bring AI Directly Into Semiconductor Manufacturing**

NVIDIA unveiled the RTX Spark Superchip on TSMC's 3N (3nm-class) process, combining a 20-core ARM CPU with a Blackwell-generation GPU featuring 6,144 CUDA cores and unified memory. The chip targets laptop and desktop form factors and will debut in Dell and Lenovo systems this fall. Separately, NVIDIA and TSMC announced they are co-deploying AI and accelerated computing across TSMC's fab operations: computational lithography (replacing classical OPC), transistor simulation, defect inspection via neural networks, and factory scheduling optimization. SIA issued a report simultaneously estimating that semiconductor revenue from AI data centers alone could exceed $1.2 trillion by 2028 — surpassing total global semiconductor sales from 2025 across all end uses — driven by the fact that chips represent 95% of the value in an AI server rack.

> **Why it matters:** Chips now enabling AI are also being used to design and manufacture the next generation of chips — a closed feedback loop that accelerates the process node roadmap. TSMC's adoption of AI-based computational lithography reduces the photomask correction compute burden by orders of magnitude (GPU-accelerated OPC vs. CPU) and could shorten tape-out timelines. For EEs: understand how neural OPC and AI-assisted DRC are changing tapeout verification workflows.

**3. HRL Laboratories 40nm GaN-on-SiC Achieves Manufacturing Readiness Level 6**

HRL Laboratories announced that its 40-nanometer gate-length gallium nitride (GaN) on silicon carbide (SiC) process technology reached Manufacturing Readiness Level (MRL) 6, indicating it is ready for low-rate initial production. The process is optimized for high-power, high-frequency RF applications — relevant for defense radar, electronic warfare, and emerging 6G FR3 power amplifier applications. MRL 6 means the process has demonstrated repeatability in a pilot line with acceptable yields, a critical gateway before DoD or commercial qualification.

> **Why it matters:** 40nm GaN-on-SiC is at the frontier of what's manufacturable for RF power devices. Sub-100nm GaN gate lengths push transition frequencies (f_T) above 200 GHz, enabling Ka-band and W-band operation. MRL 6 is the last milestone before full manufacturing qualification (MRL 8–9) — expect 40nm GaN MMICs to enter defense programs within 18–24 months. This also validates HRL's proprietary N-polar GaN process, which offers higher breakdown voltage at a given gate-drain spacing than conventional Ga-polar devices.

**4. QuiX Quantum Installs Feed-Forward Control Unit — Measurement-Based Photonic QC Takes Shape**

QuiX Quantum installed a Feed-Forward Control Unit (FFCU) enabling real-time adaptive operations in its photonic quantum computing platform. The FFCU achieves ultra-fast feedback loops at ~150ns latency, establishing the real-time classical-to-quantum interface required to scale measurement-based photonic quantum computers (MBQC). In MBQC, the result of measuring one photonic qubit determines the gate applied to subsequent qubits — eliminating that latency bottleneck is a fundamental requirement for fault-tolerant operation. Separately, PsiQuantum's "Omega" chipset, fabricated on full-size 300mm silicon wafers at GlobalFoundries' silicon photonics fab in New York, targets million-qubit-scale systems.

> **Why it matters:** 150ns feed-forward latency is achievable with current photonic and electronic co-design. The challenge is that any latency above the coherence time of the photonic qubit scrambles the computation. QuiX's demonstration establishes the timing architecture proof-of-concept; the next hurdle is integrating sufficient photon-number-resolving detectors and low-loss delay lines. For [[Silicon Photonics]] EEs: this is where on-chip reconfigurable delays, SNSPDs, and fast EO modulators converge.

**5. 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ Ferroelectric: 230 µC/cm² Polarization, 4×10⁹ Cycle Endurance**

Published in *Advanced Materials* (2026): researchers synthesized a two-dimensional Fe₂Mo₃O₈ semiconductor single crystal via an oxygen-self-supply CVD method, demonstrating ultrahigh spontaneous polarization of 230 µC/cm² — well above typical ferroelectric thin films like HZO (~20–40 µC/cm²) or LiNbO₃ (~70 µC/cm²) — with endurance exceeding 4×10⁹ switching cycles. The material is a van der Waals layered compound enabling integration with 2D semiconductor stacks. Separately, a review in *Advanced Materials* (2026) proposes hybrid ferroelectric/2D semiconductor/molecular switch architectures for post-Moore logic-in-memory.

> **Why it matters:** 230 µC/cm² at 2D thicknesses means extremely high remnant polarization per unit area — ideal for FeRAM-style memory scaled to single-digit nm. Endurance at 4×10⁹ cycles rivals or surpasses commercial HZO FeRAM (typically 10⁸–10⁹). The key question is whether the coercive field is compatible with CMOS voltages (<1V operation) — that's the remaining gap between lab performance and device integration. Search [[Ferroelectric Materials]] and [[2D Semiconductor Devices]] for context.

**6. SIA Report: AI Server Rack Value = 95% Semiconductors; $1.2T Chip Revenue by 2028**

The Semiconductor Industry Association released a comprehensive analysis showing that semiconductors constitute approximately 95% of the total value of an AI data center server rack — including logic, memory (HBM), power management, connectivity, and signal processing ICs. Annual semiconductor revenue from AI data center deployments is projected to exceed $1.2 trillion by 2028, representing a nearly 10× increase over four years and exceeding total 2025 global chip sales across all applications by more than 50%.

> **Why it matters:** This quantifies why AI is so anomalous for the semiconductor industry: a single end-market could become larger than the sum of all previous markets. The value breakdown — HBM stacks, Blackwell/Rubin GPU dies, networking silicon, power ICs — maps directly to where the engineering talent and R&D spending are concentrating. Every sub-$10 power management IC in an AI rack now carries the growth leverage of a data center investment.

## Daily Research Topic

### EUV Lithography: Principles, Current Limits, and the Terafab Supply Constraint

**What it is:** Extreme ultraviolet lithography uses 13.5nm wavelength light (from laser-heated tin plasma) to pattern features below 10nm on silicon wafers. A single ASML High-NA EUV (NXE:3800E) system costs ~$380M, weighs 180 tonnes, requires Class 1 cleanroom, and has a throughput of ~185 wafers per hour. ASML produces ~50 EUV machines per year across its Veldhoven, Netherlands campus.

**5 specific technical bullets:**
- **Numerical Aperture and Resolution:** EUV NA=0.33 (current NXE) achieves ~13nm half-pitch; High-NA (0.55, EXE:5000 series) targets ~8nm half-pitch via anamorphic optics (4×/8× reduction). Resolution = k₁ × λ/NA, where k₁≈0.35 for EUV single-patterning.
- **Source Power:** EUV requires a 13.5nm source generating ≥250W in-band power (commercial); generated by focusing a ~50kJ CO₂ laser at 50,000 tin droplets/sec, each vaporizing to 13.5nm-emitting plasma. Source uptime and power limit wafer throughput.
- **Stochastic Defects:** At sub-10nm features, photon shot noise causes stochastic CD variation and bridge/break defects. EUV uses ~10–15 photons/nm² vs. 193nm ArF's 100+; photon statistics dominate process window. This is the principal open research problem in EUV.
- **Pellicle Engineering:** Protecting the EUV mask from particles requires ultra-thin (≤50nm) transmissive membranes. At 0.33 NA a polysilicon pellicle transmits ~82% per pass; High-NA requirements push to carbon nanotube or MoSi₂ pellicles targeting >90% transmission.
- **Multi-Beam Mask Writing:** EUV photomasks are written by IMS Nanofabrication or Nuflare multi-beam e-beam systems at 5nm accuracy; a single EUV mask blank ($20K+) takes 10–15 hours to write. Defect-free blank supply (from AGC, Hoya) and inspection (KLA) are co-bottlenecks with ASML tool availability.

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore search terms: `"EUV lithography stochastic"`, `"High-NA EUV resolution"`, `"EUV source power tin plasma"`, `"EUV pellicle transmittance"`
- [[Silicon Photonics]], [[CUDA Programming Model]], [[CPU Architecture Evolution]] for adjacent concepts
- ASML's Investors Day 2025 technical deck (public PDF) is the clearest overview of the roadmap

**Concrete calculation to work through:**
Using the Rayleigh criterion R = k₁ × λ / NA:
- For current EUV (NA = 0.33, λ = 13.5nm, k₁ = 0.35): R = 0.35 × 13.5 / 0.33 = **14.3nm half-pitch**
- For High-NA EUV (NA = 0.55, same λ and k₁): R = 0.35 × 13.5 / 0.55 = **8.6nm half-pitch**
- How many EUV exposures would be needed to pattern a 2nm-class logic cell (M0 pitch ~12nm) with 0.33 NA? What is the yield hit from requiring double patterning vs. High-NA single pass?
