# EE Daily — May 26, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

---

## Top Stories

**1. Huawei Unveils "LogicFolding" and the Tau Scaling Law — A 3D Architecture for the Post-EUV Era**

Announced at IEEE ISCAS in Shanghai on May 25, Huawei's LogicFolding architecture physically folds and stacks logic circuits onto a dual two-layer framework, achieving a 53.5% increase in transistor density (238 MTr/mm²) and 40% better P-core efficiency at a peak clock of 3.1 GHz — all without EUV lithography. Huawei simultaneously proposed the "Tau (τ) Scaling Law" as a replacement for [[Moore's Law and Dennard Scaling]], using signal propagation delay τ rather than transistor gate length as the guiding metric for chip improvement. The company plans to debut LogicFolding in its autumn Kirin flagship, targeting 1.4nm-equivalent density by 2031 via vertical stacking rather than lithographic shrink.

> **Why it matters:** This is the first credible public demonstration of a sanctions-busting path to sub-2nm-class density using mature lithography. For EEs, the key question is interconnect parasitics: stacking logic layers introduces inter-layer via resistance and capacitance that must be managed through TSV pitch scaling and low-κ dielectrics. Watch Tau closely — if it gains traction, it could reshape how the industry talks about scaling roadmaps beyond the 1nm node.

**2. GlobalFoundries Launches SCALE — Industry's First OCI MSA-Compliant Co-Packaged Optics Platform**

On May 4, GlobalFoundries introduced SCALE (Silicon photonics Co-packaged Advanced Light Engine), the first platform fully compliant with the OCI MSA optical interconnect specification for AI scale-up architectures. The platform supports 50 Gbps and 100 Gbps micro-ring modulators, integrates photodiodes and TSVs, and achieves copper pad pitches down to sub-45 µm for 2.5D/3D stacking. It supports both CWDM and DWDM in 8λ and 16λ configurations, enabling per-fiber bandwidth densities unachievable with legacy pluggable modules. SENKO and GF also demonstrated a wafer-level detachable fiber interface solution — a critical enabling technology for in-process PIC testing.

> **Why it matters:** [[Co-Packaged Optics]] moves the optical engine from a pluggable module into the same package as the switch ASIC, slashing the electrical link distance from centimeters to microns and cutting per-bit energy consumption by ~5×. The OCI MSA compliance means multi-vendor interop is now on the table. For EEs working on AI data center interconnects, the key specs to track are micro-ring insertion loss (target <1 dB), modulator Vπ·L (currently ~0.5 V·cm for GF's platform), and TSV-to-ring alignment tolerances.

**3. Wolfspeed Debuts 3.3 kV SiC Modules at PCIM 2026 — 42% Lower Switching Losses Over Competing SiC**

Wolfspeed announced two new [[Silicon Carbide Power Electronics]] module families this week ahead of PCIM Europe (June 9–11, Nuremberg). The high-power LM-platform half-bridge baseplate module targets >800 A applications and delivers a 42% improvement in switching losses over other commercially available SiC solutions — and >90% over silicon IGBTs — both measured at 125 °C on a 1.8 kV DC bus in identical packages. The full-bridge WolfPACK® baseplate-less family is engineered for solid-state transformers and modular renewable infrastructure, allowing series-stacked or parallel multi-level converter architectures with matched switching performance across modules.

> **Why it matters:** For grid-scale storage, solar, and wind converters operating at medium voltage, the jump from 1700 V to 3300 V SiC directly reduces the number of series-stacked devices required in MMC/NPC topologies. A 42% switching loss reduction at 1.8 kV bus — combined with a thermally stable 125 °C rating — means significantly smaller magnetics and heat sinks. Run the numbers: at 800 A, 3.3 kV, and 2 kHz switching, even a 40% Esw reduction saves hundreds of watts per module in a grid converter.

**4. Soitec + NTU Singapore Report Record GaN PAE for 6G FR3 and mmWave Handsets**

Results from a four-year joint research program announced at MWC 2026 show Gallium Nitride devices on Soitec's advanced epitaxial wafers achieving record-level Power Added Efficiency (PAE) at the low supply voltages required by handset battery operation — in both the FR3 (7–24 GHz) and millimeter-wave bands targeted by 6G. Three peer-reviewed papers were released. The work addresses one of 6G's hardest system constraints: a handset PA operating at mmWave must deliver acceptable output power without the high supply rails available in base station applications. [[Gallium Nitride Power Electronics]] on engineered substrates (GaN-on-SOI, GaN-on-insulator) lower the knee voltage, enabling efficient operation at 3.3–5 V supply.

> **Why it matters:** 6G mmWave handset PAs are arguably the hardest RF power design problem of this decade. At 28 GHz, a 1 dB improvement in PAE translates directly to battery life in mobile devices. The use of GaN-on-SOI suppresses substrate coupling and reduces RF loss compared to GaN-on-Si, but introduces new thermal resistance paths. EEs working on RF front-end modules should track this work closely as a preview of the FR3 and D-band device landscape for 2027–2030 handset SoCs.

**5. 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ Single Crystal Achieves 230 µC/cm² Ferroelectric Polarization — 10–100× Peers**

Published in *Advanced Materials*, researchers synthesized ultrathin single-crystal Fe₂Mo₃O₈ using an oxygen-self-supply CVD strategy that exploits the material's own lattice oxygen to control the growth environment. The resulting crystals achieve a spontaneous polarization of 230 µC/cm² — ten to one hundred times higher than most other 2D ferroelectrics — and endurance of 4 × 10⁹ switching cycles. The polarization mechanism is traced to the long displacement of Fe³⁺ within FeO₄ tetrahedral cages; oxygen vacancies and lattice distortion from the ultrathin geometry further enhance it. Ferroelectric tunnel junctions (FTJs) built from the material show high switching speed and long retention.

> **Why it matters:** Most 2D ferroelectrics struggle with polarization values below 10–20 µC/cm², which limits their use in memory and logic-in-memory applications vs. HfO₂ (typically 20–30 µC/cm² for hafnium-based FeFETs). A 230 µC/cm² 2D material with van der Waals stackability enables FeRAM and FeFET architectures at ultimate thickness limits — directly relevant to post-CMOS memory scaling. The 4 × 10⁹ endurance cycle count brings it within range of DRAM endurance targets (>10¹⁶ is still far off, but this is a major benchmark jump for 2D materials).

**6. Google AlphaQubit Demonstrates 30% Error Reduction vs. Classical Decoders; Willow Runs Algorithm 13,000× Faster Than Classical Supercomputer**

Google's AlphaQubit ML-based quantum error correction decoder has now achieved a 30% reduction in logical error rates compared to traditional algorithmic decoders, with a scaling factor of 2.14× demonstrating exponential logical error rate improvement as the surface code distance increases. Separately, Google's Willow processor running the "Quantum Echoes" out-of-time-order correlator algorithm outperformed classical supercomputers by a factor of 13,000× — representing the first verifiable quantum advantage claim for a specific computational problem. IBM meanwhile reported a 10× speedup in QEC decoding throughput on its Quantum Loon processor, reaching all key component milestones for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

> **Why it matters:** For EEs, the critical technical insight is that QEC decoders are a real-time DSP problem: logical clock cycle time is bounded by the decoder's classical processing latency. IBM's 10× decoding speedup addresses this directly. Google's AlphaQubit result matters because learned decoders trained on hardware noise models can adapt to the specific error signature of each processor — something static MWPM decoders cannot do. The 2.14× scaling factor is particularly significant: it means logical error rates fall faster than the code distance grows, which is the prerequisite for practical fault-tolerant quantum computers.

## Daily Deep Dive: Huawei's Tau Scaling Law and LogicFolding — 3D Chip Architecture as an EUV-Free Scaling Path

**What it is and why it's timely now**

Huawei's LogicFolding announcement directly challenges the assumption that advanced logic scaling requires leading-edge EUV lithography. By folding 2D transistor layouts into dual vertical layers — and redefining chip performance improvement in terms of signal propagation delay τ rather than gate length — Huawei is proposing an architectural path that SMIC's 7nm-class DUV process can execute today. The Kirin autumn debut will be the first public hardware validation. As an EE, understanding *why* this works (and what its hard limits are) requires fluency in transistor density math, 3D interconnect parasitics, and the fundamental relationship between device geometry and signal timing.

**Technical concepts to understand**

- **Transistor density and scaling metrics.** Density is quoted in MTr/mm² and calculated from cell library gate pitch and row height. At 238 MTr/mm², LogicFolding reportedly matches TSMC's N3 node (~290 MTr/mm²) closely. Understand how fin pitch, contacted gate pitch (CPP), and metal pitch interact to produce density — and how folding adds a second logic layer without halving CPP.

- **The Tau (τ) Scaling Law.** Huawei defines τ as the intrinsic signal delay of a logic gate: τ = RC · f(geometry). As gate length L shrinks, R and C scale differently — at some node, further L reduction stops improving τ. LogicFolding argues that a 3D density increase improves τ via reduced interconnect length (shorter wires between stages) rather than device-level shrink. Study how gate delay breaks down into intrinsic delay (CV/I) and wire delay (RC·L), and where each dominates at 7nm and beyond.

- **3D stacking interconnect parasitics.** Every inter-layer via (ILV) in a LogicFolded die introduces a parasitic capacitance (~1–3 fF/via at current pitches) and resistance. Understand how this compares to local metal routing parasitics at BEOL Metal 1, and what constraints this places on the maximum fanout and clock tree depth in a stacked logic architecture.

- **TSV pitch and density limits.** Through-silicon vias for power delivery and global signal routing in stacked logic currently occupy a significant fraction of die area. At a pitch of ~1–2 µm (leading-edge for fine-pitch hybrid bonding), TSV overhead begins to compete seriously with routing resource. Map the TSV density vs. signal bandwidth trade-off.

- **Power delivery in stacked logic.** Folding logic layers doubles local power density in a fixed footprint. Without a corresponding improvement in thermal path (lower junction-to-case resistance) or a power delivery network re-arch (backside power delivery, BSPD), IR drop and hot-spot temperature set a hard ceiling on clock frequency. Huawei's claim of 40% P-core efficiency improvement implies they have mitigated at least part of this — understand what the thermal resistance budget looks like for a dual-layer logic die.

- IEEE Xplore search terms: `"3D logic stacking" NOT memory`, `"hybrid bonding logic die" scaling`, `"backside power delivery network"`, `"contacted gate pitch scaling limits"`, `"gate delay CV/I model"`
- Relevant [[Moore's Law and Dennard Scaling]] wiki page for the foundational scaling context
- [[CPU Architecture Evolution]] for the architectural context of stacked logic in SoC design
- [[Semiconductor Device Fundamentals]] for MOSFET delay/CV·I derivation
- IEDM 2024–2025 proceedings on Intel's "PowerVia" backside PDN and TSMC's 3D Fabric

**Concrete calculation to work through**

A simplified gate delay model: τ = (C_gate + C_wire) · V_DD / I_ON

Assume a standard cell at TSMC N3 equivalent: C_gate = 0.5 fF, C_wire = 1.0 fF (per stage, 50 µm local wire), V_DD = 0.75 V, I_ON = 1 mA/µm × 100 nm gate width = 100 µA.

Calculate τ. Now, for LogicFolding: if folding reduces average wire length by 30% (C_wire drops to 0.7 fF) but adds one ILV per critical path stage (C_ILV = 1.5 fF), does τ improve or worsen? What wire-length reduction would be needed to break even on the ILV capacitive penalty? This directly tests whether LogicFolding's density claim translates into a speed claim.
