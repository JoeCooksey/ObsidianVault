## Top Stories

**1. AMD Gets Citi Upgrade to Buy, $575 PT — Hits 2030 Earnings Target Two Years Early**

Citigroup analyst Atif Malik upgraded AMD from Neutral to Buy on June 13, raising his price target to $575 from $460. Bank of America's Vivek Arya simultaneously raised his Buy PT to $560 from $500. The catalyst: AMD's Q2 2026 revenue of $10.3 billion (+38% YoY) with data center revenue up 57% to $5.8 billion — a run rate that matches the company's 2030 earnings target that analysts had set just 18 months ago. AMD stock is up ~130% YTD vs NVIDIA's ~12%, a reversal driven by AMD's MI300X GPU gaining serious traction in hyperscaler deployments.

> **Why it matters:** AMD's accelerating data center footprint is evidence that AI workloads are diversifying beyond NVIDIA's H100/H200/B200 ecosystem. For EEs sourcing AI accelerators: the MI300X's HBM3 stack (192 GB, 5.3 TB/s bandwidth) is now a credible alternative in inference-heavy deployments where NVIDIA supply is constrained.

**2. TSMC Plans 15% Price Hike on 3nm in H2 2026 as AI Demand Saturates Capacity**

Supply chain sources report TSMC intends to raise 3nm process pricing by up to 15% in H2 2026. The driver: Nvidia, AMD, Google, AWS, and multiple CSPs have accelerated 3nm adoption simultaneously, pushing capacity utilization above 90%. TSMC May 2026 revenue hit NT$416.98 billion — up 1.5% MoM and 30.1% YoY. Separately, NVIDIA confirmed TSMC has deployed cuLitho (computational lithography AI) across fab operations, achieving 20–50% improvement in cost-effectiveness and cycle time vs CPU-based systems.

> **Why it matters:** A 15% die cost increase at 3nm flows directly into system BOM. For chip designers: any design currently targeting 3nm should model a 12-15% die cost increase in H2 and stress-test margin against it. The cuLitho deployment is technically significant — generative AI is now inside the lithography loop, not just analysis.

**3. QuiX Quantum FFCU Enables Real-Time Adaptive Photonic QC at 150 ns Latency**

QuiX Quantum's Feed-Forward Control Unit (FFCU), installed June 2, enables real-time adaptive operations in photonic quantum computing. The unit combines a dual-FPGA processing bus with a 32×32 input/output routing matrix, converting single-photon detector signals into immediate phase modifications at 150 ns latency. Separately, JIJ and ORCA Computing demonstrated a hybrid quantum-classical energy optimization workflow using ORCA's PT-2 photonic processor on June 11. PsiQuantum's silicon photonics platform — fabricated on a commercial 300mm process — continues toward million-qubit integration with co-packaged single-photon sources and superconducting detectors.

> **Why it matters:** The 150 ns FFCU latency is the key engineering number: photonic qubit coherence times are measured in nanoseconds to microseconds, so feed-forward correction at 150 ns is the threshold that makes measurement-based QC practical. This is the photonics analogue of fast classical error-correction feedback in superconducting QC systems.

**4. Valleytronics Chip Operates at Room Temperature — Opens Door to Ultra-Low-Power Logic**

A new light-powered chip exploiting the "valley" degree of freedom in 2D transition metal dichalcogenides operates at room temperature, a key milestone for valleytronics. Previous demonstrations required cryogenic conditions. Valley polarization enables binary encoding of information in the crystal momentum of electrons — distinct from charge or spin. The device platform is also programmable and compact, with potential applications in AI inference at ultra-low power and future quantum computing architectures.

> **Why it matters:** Room-temperature valleytronics removes the biggest engineering barrier to practical devices. For EEs: valley DOF adds a third binary encoding axis alongside charge (CMOS) and spin (spintronics). The energy-per-operation advantage could be significant for always-on edge inference. IEEE Xplore search: `valleytronics TMD room temperature 2026`.

**5. Tiancheng 14-Inch SiC Single Crystal + Zhongwei 8-Inch Laser Lift-Off at 40 μm**

Tiancheng Semiconductor announced successful 14-inch SiC single crystal development, advancing beyond the 12-inch standard. Simultaneously, Zhongwei Precision Instrument mass-produces 8-inch SiC laser lift-off technology with material loss in the 40 μm range — cutting the previous industry benchmark of 60–80 μm loss by ~40%. Combined, these developments push down the cost of SiC wafers: the substrate is 30–40% of SiC device cost, and thinner slicing means more die per boule.

> **Why it matters:** Cost parity between SiC and high-voltage Si has been the EV/industrial power electronics bottleneck. A 40% reduction in kerf loss at 8-inch + first 14-inch crystal moves that timeline forward. For power EEs designing SiC converters: expect substrate pricing to continue declining through 2027–2028 as these processes mature into volume.

**6. 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ Ferroelectric Sets Polarization Record at 230 μC/cm² with 4×10⁹ Endurance**

New research published in *Advanced Materials* (2026) demonstrates 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ ferroelectric semiconductor crystals with ultrahigh polarization of 230 μC/cm² — ten to one hundred times larger than most 2D ferroelectric materials — combined with ultralong endurance of 4×10⁹ cycles. The material was grown via an oxygen-self-supply synthesis route. Separately, CuInP₂S₆-based ferroelectric materials continue advancing for neuromorphic computing applications, exploiting the layered van der Waals structure for continuous-weight synaptic behavior.

> **Why it matters:** High polarization + high endurance = the two requirements for embedded non-volatile memory competing with Flash. 230 μC/cm² far exceeds HZO (~30 μC/cm²) used in current [[Ferroelectric Field-Effect Transistors]]. If integration into BEOL processes can be demonstrated, this opens a path to post-NAND storage in sub-10nm nodes.

## Deep Dive: HBM4 Architecture and the AI Memory Wall

**What it is:** High Bandwidth Memory 4 (HBM4) is the next-generation stacked DRAM standard designed to break through the "memory wall" — the growing gap between GPU compute bandwidth and DRAM data delivery rate. HBM4 is currently in competitive development between SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron; HBM capacity is sold out through 2026 with a projected $100B TAM by 2028.

**5 Technical Points to Understand:**

- **Bus width: 2,048 bits** — HBM4 targets a 2,048-bit bus width per stack (vs 1,024-bit in HBM3), effectively doubling per-stack bandwidth without increasing frequency. Current H100 uses 6 HBM3 stacks × 1,024 bits = 6,144-bit aggregate bus.
- **Bandwidth: ~2 TB/s per stack targeted** — HBM3 delivers ~819 GB/s per stack; HBM4 targets ~2 TB/s via wider bus + higher data rates (>6.4 Gbps/pin). Full B200 SXM configuration (6 stacks) = >12 TB/s aggregate.
- **TSV die stacking** — HBM stacks 8–16 DRAM dies vertically via Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs). Each TSV is ~5 μm diameter, ~50 μm pitch. The package substrate routes thousands of TSVs between the logic base die and DRAM.
- **Thermal density problem** — stacking 8+ dies concentrates thermal load; HBM4 dies operate at ~85°C junction max. Power density >1 W/mm² at the base die requires careful co-design of the interposer thermal path. Microbump pitch shrinks to ~25 μm in HBM4.
- **Memory wall math** — A100: 312 TFLOP/s (BF16) / 2 TB/s bandwidth = 156 ops/byte. Transformer attention scales O(n²) but memory bandwidth scales O(n). The wall appears when the arithmetic intensity of an operation falls below this ratio — which happens in attention layers with large batch sizes.

**Calculation to work through:** A transformer with 70B parameters at BF16 requires 140 GB of weight storage. At HBM3's 3.35 TB/s (H100 aggregate), fully streaming those weights once takes 140GB / 3,350 GB/s ≈ 42 ms. At HBM4's ~12 TB/s projection, the same operation takes 140GB / 12,000 GB/s ≈ 11.7 ms.
