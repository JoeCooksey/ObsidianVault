# EE Daily — June 19, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

---

## Top Stories

**1. EPFL Demonstrates Chip-Scale Femtosecond Laser — 1.05 nJ Pulses at 147 fs on Erbium-Doped Si3N4**
Researchers at EPFL led by Prof. Tobias Kippenberg published in *Nature* (June 3, 2026) the first integrated ultrafast laser rivaling tabletop femtosecond systems: 1.05 nJ pulse energy in 147 fs pulses from a Mamyshev oscillator architecture on an erbium-doped [[silicon nitride]] chip. The 42 cm cavity folds onto a chip roughly the size of a match head, and the fabrication process yields over 1,000 individual laser cavities per chip. The design uses two spectrally offset bandpass filters flanking a nonlinear waveguide — a configuration the integrated photonics community had largely overlooked for two decades.

> **Why it matters:** Chip-scale femtosecond sources have been called a "holy grail of integrated photonics" because existing on-chip mode-locked lasers top out at ~10-50 pJ pulse energies — one to two orders of magnitude below what applications like optical coherence tomography, dual-comb spectroscopy, and optical atomic clocks require. At 1.05 nJ, this Mamyshev oscillator closes that gap entirely. The Mamyshev architecture is self-starting and tolerant of dispersion variations, unlike conventional soliton mode-locking which requires precise anomalous dispersion engineering. For RF and photonics engineers, the immediate consequence is that fs-precision optical frequency combs, currently requiring $50k+ tabletop Ti:sapphire or fiber lasers, could become a $100 photonic chip. The >1,000 cavities/chip yield also suggests wafer-scale testing and binning — a semiconductor-style manufacturing flow for ultrafast lasers.

**2. Monash University Builds First Integrated Valleytronic Optoelectronic Circuit**
Monash University researchers demonstrated the first on-chip programmable valley optoelectronic nanocircuit, published in *Nature Photonics* (May 25, 2026). The device generates, steers, and reads valley-polarized photoluminescence from atomically thin [[transition metal dichalcogenide]] (TMD) layers integrated with engineered [[metasurface]] nanostructures — all on a single chip at room temperature. The team encoded and processed two separate images simultaneously using the valley degree of freedom as the information carrier, demonstrating multiplexed optical information processing without additional hardware.

> **Why it matters:** Valleytronics encodes information in the crystal momentum valley index (K vs. K') of 2D materials — a degree of freedom orthogonal to charge and spin. Previous demonstrations required cryogenic temperatures or bulky off-chip circular polarization optics. This work integrates the entire generate-route-detect chain on-chip at 300 K using a stacking approach that avoids the lattice-matching constraints of epitaxial growth on photonic structures. For anyone working on [[optical interconnects]] or photonic computing, this is a proof-of-concept for a third multiplexing dimension beyond wavelength and polarization. The room-temperature operation makes it immediately relevant to near-term optical communication and sensing architectures.

**3. Coherent Secures $50M CHIPS Act Funding to Quadruple 6-inch InP Wafer Production**
Coherent Corp. signed a CHIPS Act letter of intent on June 16, 2026 for $50 million in direct federal funding to expand its 6-inch [[indium phosphide]] (InP) semiconductor fab in Sherman, Texas. The expansion will double manufacturing floor space and quadruple wafer production capacity for InP-based optical transceivers, lasers, and photodetectors used in AI datacenter interconnects. An additional ~$20M comes from the Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund and local economic development. NVIDIA co-hosted the groundbreaking, underscoring that optical I/O is now the bandwidth bottleneck for GPU-scale AI clusters. The project will create over 1,000 jobs including 550+ engineering and advanced manufacturing roles.

> **Why it matters:** InP is the only III-V material system that can monolithically integrate lasers, modulators, and photodetectors at 1310/1550 nm — the wavelengths used in 800G and 1.6T pluggable transceivers. Current 6-inch InP wafer supply is capacity-constrained globally, with only a handful of fabs (Coherent, II-VI legacy, HRL, a few in Japan) operating at this diameter. Quadrupling capacity at the world's leading InP fab directly addresses the optical transceiver supply bottleneck that is already limiting AI cluster scale-out. For power and signal integrity engineers: every 800G-DR8 module contains 8 InP EMLs (electro-absorption modulated lasers) operating at ~50 GBaud PAM-4 — the InP supply chain scales linearly with AI interconnect bandwidth growth.

**4. SiC JFETs and Bidirectional GaN Enable Microsecond Solid-State Circuit Breakers for 800V DC Grids**
At PCIM 2026, Infineon's Peter Wawer presented the CoolSiC JFET family for solid-state circuit breakers (SSCBs) targeting 800V DC architectures in AI data centers and more-electric aircraft. onsemi's complementary SiC JFET portfolio offers devices from 650V to 1700V V_DS with R_DS(on) as low as 4 mohm at less than half the die area of competing technologies. In parallel, 650V monolithic bidirectional GaN HEMTs have been demonstrated in SSCB topologies, enabling bidirectional fault current interruption in a single die — eliminating the need for anti-series MOSFET pairs used in conventional designs.

> **Why it matters:** The shift from 48V to 800V DC power distribution in hyperscale data centers (driven by [[SiC]]-based server power supplies and 800V bus bars) creates a fault current problem: at 800V, arc flash energy in mechanical breakers exceeds safe limits, and fault currents must be interrupted in <10 us to protect downstream GaN or SiC power stages. SiC JFETs are normally-on devices — they fail to the conducting state, which is actually desirable in an SSCB: the breaker conducts with near-zero gate drive power during normal operation and only requires active drive to open. The 4 mohm R_DS(on) at 1200V means conduction losses of ~16W at 2000A — far below the thermal budget of a rack-level breaker. Bidirectional GaN eliminates the 2x device count of anti-series topologies, halving both conduction loss and PCB footprint.

**5. Nanometer-Scale Thickness Tuning of RuO2 Reveals Polarization-Driven Work Function Control in Metals**
University of Minnesota researchers published findings (June 6, 2026) showing that the electronic behavior of epitaxial ruthenium dioxide (RuO2) films changes dramatically with nanometer-scale thickness variations. At approximately 4 nm thickness — roughly the width of a DNA strand — the surface work function becomes tunable through atomic polarization at the film-substrate interface. This challenges the conventional assumption that metals are electronically rigid, demonstrating that interface-engineered polarization gradients in a metallic oxide can serve as a controllable knob for electronic properties.

> **Why it matters:** Work function engineering is foundational to transistor threshold voltage setting — every CMOS process node uses metal gate work function tuning to set V_th for NMOS and PMOS. Current approaches rely on alloy composition (TiN/TiAl stacks) or dipole interlayers, both of which become increasingly difficult to control at sub-2 nm gate lengths where the metal gate itself is only 3-5 nm thick. If polarization-driven work function modulation in RuO2 (or related metallic oxides) can deliver continuous V_th tuning over a useful range (say, 200-400 mV) via thickness control alone, it offers a new path for [[gate-all-around]] transistor integration at the 18A/A14 nodes. RuO2 is already under investigation as a low-resistivity interconnect metal and barrier layer — dual-use as a tunable work function gate metal would simplify the BEOL/MOL material stack.

**6. 2D Ferroelectric and Antiferroelectric Materials for Neuromorphic Computing (Advanced Functional Materials 2026)**
A review published in *Advanced Functional Materials* (2026) surveys two-dimensional van der Waals ferroelectric and antiferroelectric materials with atomic-scale thickness for neuromorphic device architectures. The paper covers ferroelectric tunnel junctions (FTJs), ferroelectric field-effect transistors (FeFETs), and memristive synaptic devices built from 2D ferroelectrics including alpha-In2Se3, CuInP2S6, and bilayer/few-layer h-BN with sliding ferroelectricity. Demonstrated switching endurance exceeds 10^9 cycles in some 2D FTJ configurations, with ON/OFF conductance ratios >10^4 — sufficient for multi-level synaptic weight storage.

> **Why it matters:** Neuromorphic hardware requires analog-tunable, non-volatile conductance elements with high endurance and low switching energy. Conventional HfO2-based [[FeRAM]] and FeFET devices suffer from wake-up/fatigue effects and depolarization below ~5 nm thickness. 2D ferroelectrics sidestep the thickness scaling problem because ferroelectricity in materials like alpha-In2Se3 persists down to monolayer thickness (~1 nm) — there is no critical thickness. The sliding ferroelectricity mechanism in bilayer h-BN is particularly elegant: polarization switching occurs via interlayer translation rather than ion displacement, eliminating the ionic fatigue mechanism entirely. For neuromorphic chip designers, 10^9 endurance at >10^4 ON/OFF ratio in a sub-nm-thick device is a combination no bulk ferroelectric can match.

## Daily Deep Dive: Chip-Scale Mamyshev Oscillators — Ultrafast Lasers on Integrated Photonic Platforms

**What it is:** The Mamyshev oscillator is a mode-locked laser architecture that uses two spectrally offset bandpass filters separated by a nonlinear gain medium to generate ultrashort pulses with high pulse energy. Unlike conventional soliton mode-locked lasers (which balance anomalous dispersion against self-phase modulation), the Mamyshev oscillator operates in the normal dispersion regime and relies on spectral broadening via self-phase modulation followed by spectral filtering to reshape the pulse each round trip. EPFL's June 2026 demonstration on erbium-doped silicon nitride is the first implementation on a photonic integrated circuit, achieving pulse energies and durations competitive with bulk fiber Mamyshev oscillators. This topic sits at the intersection of [[nonlinear optics]], [[integrated photonics]], and [[optical frequency combs]] — essential knowledge for anyone working on precision metrology, LiDAR, or next-generation optical clocks.

**5 Technical Bullets:**

- **Mamyshev Principle:** A CW signal enters a nonlinear waveguide where self-phase modulation (SPM) broadens the spectrum. A bandpass filter offset from the input center wavelength selects newly generated spectral content, suppressing the CW background. Two such stages in a ring cavity create a pulse-cleaning mechanism: only pulses with sufficient peak power to generate the required spectral broadening survive — CW lasing is suppressed by the filter offset. This is fundamentally different from saturable-absorber mode-locking and avoids the Q-switching instabilities that plague semiconductor saturable absorbers at high pulse energies.

- **EPFL Implementation Details:** The chip uses erbium-doped Si3N4 waveguides (Er:Si3N4) as the gain and nonlinear medium. Erbium provides gain at 1550 nm (C-band) with a broad emission bandwidth (~40 nm) supporting sub-200 fs pulses. The 42 cm cavity length corresponds to a free spectral range of ~500 MHz (repetition rate). The two bandpass filters are likely implemented as coupled microring resonators or Bragg grating reflectors integrated on the same chip — details in the *Nature* paper. Silicon nitride's low propagation loss (~0.1 dB/m in state-of-the-art platforms) and moderate Kerr nonlinearity (n2 ~ 2.5 x 10^-19 m^2/W) are sufficient for SPM-based spectral broadening at the mW-level intracavity powers available from Er:Si3N4.

- **Pulse Energy Scaling:** Conventional on-chip soliton mode-locked lasers are limited to ~10-50 pJ because the soliton energy scales as E_soliton = (2 * |beta_2| * A_eff) / (gamma * tau), where beta_2 is group velocity dispersion, A_eff is effective mode area, gamma is the nonlinear coefficient, and tau is pulse duration. For typical Si3N4 waveguides (A_eff ~ 1 um^2, gamma ~ 1 W^-1 m^-1), this yields single-digit pJ. The Mamyshev oscillator breaks this limit because it operates in normal dispersion — pulses are chirped and temporally broad inside the cavity, reducing peak power and avoiding nonlinear instabilities, then can be externally compressed.

- **Comparison to Fiber Mamyshev Oscillators:** Bulk fiber Mamyshev oscillators (demonstrated since ~2017) achieve 10-100 nJ pulse energies at 100 fs-1 ps durations, but require meters of fiber, discrete filter components, and free-space coupling. The EPFL chip achieves 1.05 nJ — within one order of magnitude — in a footprint reduction of >1000x. The trade-off is repetition rate: fiber versions run at 10-50 MHz; the chip version at ~500 MHz. For frequency comb applications, higher rep rate is actually preferred (wider comb line spacing simplifies detection).

- **Applications Enabled by nJ-Level On-Chip Pulses:** (1) Dual-comb spectroscopy with two chip-scale Mamyshev oscillators replacing paired fiber frequency combs. (2) Supercontinuum generation: 1 nJ pulses launched into a dispersion-engineered Si3N4 waveguide can generate octave-spanning spectra for f-2f self-referencing — the key step in optical frequency synthesis. (3) Two-photon microscopy: 147 fs pulses at 1550 nm, frequency-doubled to 775 nm, provide sufficient peak power (~7 kW) for nonlinear fluorescence excitation. (4) Chip-scale optical parametric oscillators pumped by the Mamyshev source.

**Concrete Calculation to Work Through:**
*Estimate the nonlinear phase shift required for Mamyshev oscillator operation:*
- Intracavity average power: P_avg ~ 500 mW (estimated from 1.05 nJ at ~500 MHz rep rate)
- Peak power of chirped intracavity pulse (assume 10x temporal stretching): P_peak ~ P_avg / (duty_cycle) ~ 1.05 nJ / (1.47 ps chirped) ~ 714 W
- Nonlinear coefficient of Er:Si3N4: gamma ~ 1 W^-1 m^-1
- Nonlinear waveguide length per stage: L_NL ~ 0.1 m (estimate for half the 0.42 m cavity)
- Nonlinear phase shift: phi_NL = gamma * P_peak * L_NL = 1 * 714 * 0.1 = **71.4 rad**
- This large phi_NL generates massive spectral broadening — consistent with the Mamyshev mechanism requiring strong SPM to bridge the spectral offset between the two filters. Compare to soliton lasers where phi_NL per round trip is constrained to ~pi/2.
