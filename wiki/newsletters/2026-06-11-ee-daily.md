# EE Daily — June 11, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. Semiconductor Stocks Rebound on Oracle AI Demand Signal**

After the June 5 sell-off triggered by Broadcom's cautious AI chip outlook and a deepening HBM supply crisis, chip stocks reversed sharply on June 11: NVDA, AMD, DELL, and SMCI all moved higher. The catalyst was Oracle's reaffirmed AI infrastructure expansion plans — larger data center buildouts require more high-bandwidth memory stacks and GPU compute nodes per rack. AMD's 12-month gain now stands at +114%, reaching ~35.5× its 2027 earnings estimate, while Cathie Wood's ARK shifted from AMD toward NVIDIA, reflecting a thesis that NVIDIA's vertical stack (Vera CPU + Blackwell GPU + NVLink + CUDA) is harder to displace than AMD's software ecosystem.

> **Why it matters:** The June 5 sell-off tested the thesis that AI semiconductor demand is durable. Oracle's signal — not a startup's, a $500B+ enterprise cloud operator's — restored it. For EEs evaluating which GPU architecture to target (CUDA vs ROCm vs custom ASIC), NVIDIA's platform stickiness remains the dominant factor in toolchain selection.

**2. TSMC CEO Warns Global Chip Capacity Will Lag AI Demand for Several Years**

TSMC reported a 2026 revenue growth outlook exceeding 30% driven by AI demand, but CEO C.C. Wei publicly cautioned that global semiconductor manufacturing capacity cannot keep pace with market requirements for the foreseeable future. TSMC is actively building capacity in Arizona (N4P, N2 GAA), Japan (N12FFC+), and Germany (SiC/automotive), but lead times on advanced EUV tools (ASML High-NA at ~€400M/unit) and cleanroom construction constrain ramp rates. The company stated it would "like to" raise prices on advanced nodes but is managing customer relationships carefully during the buildout phase.

> **Why it matters:** For EE product teams: the TSMC capacity warning is a tape-out scheduling signal. If you're targeting N3E or N2 for an ASIC, your queue position is now a strategic variable. The 15% N3 price hike forecast and extended lead times mean design-win economics need to be modeled at higher wafer costs than 2024 baselines.

**3. AMD +114% Year-Over-Year: MI300X/MI325X Data Center GPU Margin Expansion**

AMD's stock has quietly outperformed NVIDIA on a 12-month percentage basis (+114% vs ~50%), driven by MI300X and MI325X server GPU traction at Microsoft Azure, Meta, and Oracle. The MI300X ships with 192 GB HBM3 in a monolithic package (8× HBM3 stacks on a 4-chiplet base die via advanced packaging), achieving ~5.3 TB/s aggregate memory bandwidth — sufficient for single-GPU inference on 70B+ parameter models without offloading. AMD's ROCm 6.x software stack is now rated production-grade by major cloud operators.

> **Why it matters:** The MI300X architecture is the clearest proof that advanced packaging (silicon interposer + multi-chiplet HBM integration) can deliver competitive AI inference performance without monolithic die scaling. For EEs in power/thermal design: 750W TDP per card in a dual-slot OAM form factor requires liquid cooling at scale; air-cooled dual-socket density is the binding constraint.

**4. ASML Terafab Update: Musk Virtual Appearance, Tata India 300mm Partnership**

Elon Musk appeared virtually at an ASML event to discuss Terafab — a planned chipmaking facility backed by a SpaceX/Tesla joint venture — while ASML separately announced a strategic partnership with Tata Electronics for India's first 300mm semiconductor fab. Bank of America raised its ASML price target to €1,921 (from €1,850), and Barclays to €1,900, citing Q1 2026 sales of €8.8B (+31% YoY) and a 53% gross margin. ASML's order backlog includes potential EUV tool supply agreements with Apple and Intel for their planned captive fabs. The Terafab concept, if built, would be the first greenfield Tier-1 logic fab outside East Asia/Europe since the 1990s.

> **Why it matters:** Geopolitical semiconductor supply-chain diversification is accelerating. India's 300mm fab (with ASML EUV tooling) signals that 2030s logic manufacturing will have more geographic nodes, but ASML's monopoly on EUV (and near-monopoly on High-NA EUV) makes its order backlog the single tightest bottleneck in global chip supply. If you're modeling AI hardware availability, model ASML's EUV shipment schedule first.

**5. 2D Ferroelectric Semiconductors: Post-Moore Neuromorphic Frontiers**

A 2026 Advanced Materials paper reports synthesis of Fe₂Mo₃O₈ 2D single crystals with ferroelectric polarization up to 230 µC/cm² and endurance of 4 × 10⁹ cycles — 10–100× higher polarization than most 2D ferroelectrics and competitive with bulk PZT ceramics. A separate study from Advanced Functional Materials demonstrates CuInP₂S₆ (CIPS) 2D ferroelectric FeFETs implementing brain-inspired neuromorphic computing: potentiation/depression weight updates at sub-nanosecond switching speeds with non-volatile retention. The post-Moore framing: as conventional CMOS scaling slows, ferroelectric 2D materials enable in-memory compute architectures where logic and storage co-locate on a single atomic layer.

> **Why it matters:** For EEs tracking next-generation non-volatile memory: 2D ferroelectric FeFETs operate at lower switching voltages (1–3 V coercive field) than NAND flash and offer analog weight storage for neural network inference without the ADC/DAC overhead of SRAM CIM arrays. The Fe₂Mo₃O₈ endurance figure (4 × 10⁹) bridges the gap between experimental demonstrations and product-level write endurance requirements.

**6. GLP-1 Drug Boom Accelerating AI Chip Demand in Drug Discovery**

Eli Lilly's retatrutide (triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon receptor agonist) Phase 3 results — TRANSCEND-T2D-1, published in *The Lancet* — showed significant weight loss plus improvements in knee osteoarthritis, sleep apnea, and T2D. LLY's price target was raised to $1,350 (Jefferies). The connection to EE: Lilly's drug pipeline acceleration is driven by AI-accelerated molecular simulation on NVIDIA/AMD GPU clusters; each candidate requires petaflop-scale inference on protein folding + binding affinity models. The GLP-1 drug boom is one of the fastest-growing compute workloads in biopharmaceutical data centers.

> **Why it matters:** For EEs designing AI inference infrastructure for pharma: the workload profile is batch inference on large transformer models (AlphaFold, DiffDock), favoring HBM-dense GPU configurations over latency-optimized paths. Power density requirements in pharma AI clusters are converging with hyperscale training clusters — 50–80 kW per rack is the design target.

## 🔬 Daily Research Topic: GaN Cascode Architecture — Normally-On SiC JFET + Normally-Off GaN HEMT

**What it is:** A cascode configuration pairs a normally-on (depletion-mode) high-voltage SiC JFET with a normally-off (enhancement-mode) low-voltage GaN HEMT to produce a composite device that behaves as a normally-off switch — the standard requirement for power electronics — while inheriting the high-voltage blocking capability of SiC and the fast switching of GaN. This is the most actively contested IP territory in wide-bandgap power electronics in 2026.

**Technical specifics:**
- **Circuit topology:** JFET source connects to GaN drain; JFET gate connects to GaN source (not shown externally). Gate drive goes to the GaN gate. When GaN HEMT is off (V_GS = 0), JFET source is pulled negative, turning off the normally-on JFET.
- **Voltage sharing:** The SiC JFET blocks 600–1200 V; the GaN HEMT only blocks 20–30 V. During turn-off, the SiC JFET holds most of the bus voltage.
- **Switching speed:** Limited by JFET capacitance (C_GS, C_GD), but the GaN HEMT switches the low-side fast. Gate charge Q_g is dominated by the GaN device (~5–10 nC), giving very low switching losses at 1 MHz+.
- **Body diode issue:** SiC JFETs have no body diode; free-wheeling current flows through a parallel Si diode or the JFET channel itself (low R_DS(on) in the conduction direction).
- **Thermal design:** The SiC JFET runs hotter (higher losses at blocking) and the GaN HEMT runs cooler. Matched thermal paths and co-packaging (single TO-247 or QFN module) are the main package engineering challenge.

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore searches: `"GaN SiC cascode" power electronics`, `"normally-on JFET enhancement mode GaN"`
- [[Wide Bandgap Semiconductors]], [[SiC Power Devices]], [[GaN HEMT]]
- APEC 2026 conference papers on WBG cascode

**Concrete calculation to work through:**
A 1200 V GaN/SiC cascode switch drives a buck converter at 500 kHz with V_bus = 800 V and I_L = 10 A. Given: JFET C_oss = 100 pF, GaN Q_g = 8 nC, R_G = 5 Ω. Calculate: (1) capacitive switching loss P_sw = ½ × C_oss × V_bus² × f_sw; (2) gate drive loss P_g = Q_g × V_GS × f_sw; (3) compare to an equivalent all-silicon MOSFET (C_oss = 500 pF, Q_g = 40 nC). How much switching loss is saved per converter stage?
