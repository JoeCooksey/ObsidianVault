# EE Daily — June 02, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. SIA: Semiconductors Are 95% of AI Server Rack Value — Revenue Projected at $1.2T by 2028**

The Semiconductor Industry Association released a landmark report on June 1 finding that a leading AI server rack contains over 4,500 packaged chips, with semiconductors representing more than 95% of the rack's content value and more than 50% of total data center capital expenditure. Annual semiconductor revenue used in AI data centers is projected to reach $1.2 trillion by 2028 — nearly tenfold growth in four years, surpassing total 2025 global semiconductor sales across *all* end uses by more than 50%. The full chip stack is implicated: logic, memory (HBM), networking ASICs, power management ICs, and photonics.

> **Why it matters:** This is the clearest articulation yet of where the EE jobs and funding are going. If you're working anywhere in the semiconductor stack — from device physics to package design to power delivery — this report quantifies the demand curve you're riding. $1.2T/year by 2028 is not a niche opportunity.

**2. TSMC Plans 15% Price Hike on 3nm AI Chips in H2 2026; Hits $2.28T Market Cap**

TSMC is considering a 15% price increase on advanced 3nm process technology for the second half of 2026, with a potential further 10% hike in 2027, driven by AI chip demand exceeding available manufacturing capacity. TSM stock surged more than 5% on June 1 to a historic high, pushing market cap past $2.28 trillion. TSMC also announced a strategic collaboration with NVIDIA to integrate accelerated computing and AI into TSM's semiconductor design and manufacturing processes. Management raised the full-year 2026 revenue growth forecast to exceed 30%.

> **Why it matters:** Pricing power at this scale — double-digit hikes while customers still queue — is the economic signal that EUV capacity is the true bottleneck of the AI era. The NVIDIA collaboration accelerates co-optimization of chip architecture and process node simultaneously. Watch how the 15% hike flows through NVDA's gross margins in Q3 guidance.

**3. PsiQuantum "Omega" Photonic Chipset Targets Million-Qubit Utility-Scale Quantum**

PsiQuantum published details in *Nature* of "Omega," a silicon photonic quantum chipset designed on a 300mm foundry-compatible process, implementing the full technology stack for photonic qubits: generation, manipulation, transmission, and detection within a single integrated circuit. The company claims the process is capable of manufacturing million-qubit-scale systems. By leveraging silicon photonics infrastructure already deployed in data centers, PsiQuantum avoids building specialized fab capacity — a key differentiator from superconducting qubit approaches.

> **Why it matters:** Foundry-compatible silicon photonics is the only path to scaling quantum systems the same way classical chips scaled: through shared manufacturing infrastructure. If the optical loss figures and single-photon detector efficiency hold at volume, this is the architecture that closes the gap between lab demonstrations and utility-scale quantum advantage for chemistry and optimization.

**4. PCIM Expo 2026: SiC, GaN, and Data Center Power Trends Dominate**

PCIM Europe 2026 highlighted wide-bandgap (WBG) device reliability, MW-class converter design, and GaN/SiC solutions for data center power as lead themes. On the manufacturing side, Tiancheng Semiconductor (China) reported successful development of a 14-inch SiC single crystal — a shift from 12-inch adoption into early 14-inch commercialization. Chinese players now represent a meaningful fraction of 200mm SiC wafer production. APEC 2026 separately showcased record converter efficiencies and advanced capacitor technologies for high-frequency GaN circuits.

> **Why it matters:** The wafer size roadmap for SiC (150 mm → 200 mm → 300 mm eventual) directly determines device cost trajectory. Tiancheng's 14-inch announcement accelerates potential 300mm SiC production timelines, with downstream effects on EV inverter and data center PSU costs.

**5. 2D CuInP2S6 Ferroelectric Enables Brain-Inspired Neuromorphic Computing**

Researchers published in *Advanced Functional Materials* (2026) demonstrating copper indium thiophosphate (CuInP2S6, CIPS) van der Waals ferroelectric transistors with synaptic plasticity behavior enabling multi-state non-volatile memory. The devices show ferroelectric switching endurance of 10⁹ cycles and can mimic long-term potentiation/depression (LTP/LTD) for neuromorphic computing. A separate study in *Advanced Materials* reported a 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ single crystal with 230 µC/cm² polarization and ultralong endurance of 4×10⁹ cycles — 10–100× above 2D SOTA.

> **Why it matters:** CMOS-based neural networks require D/A converters and SRAM that burn significant power per multiply-accumulate. Ferroelectric 2D transistors that natively store analog weights with sub-fJ switching energy could change the efficiency equation for edge AI inference — a direct complement to the cloud-to-edge inference migration trend.

**6. Edge AI Inference Shifts from Cloud to Device at 26% CAGR**

Market analysis (Deloitte, Deloitte Insights 2026 Semiconductor Outlook) confirms that on-device AI — AI-enabled PCs, smartphones, and IoT — is growing at a compound annual growth rate exceeding 26%. A significant 2026 inflection point is the migration of AI inference from cloud data centers to edge devices. This redefines the power budget constraint from megawatt-scale data centers (where efficiency is measured in PUE and $/kWh) to milliwatt-scale edge devices (where efficiency is measured in TOPS/W and battery life).

> **Why it matters:** For EEs, this is a structural shift in design requirements. Cloud inference: focus on high-throughput throughput/power, high-bandwidth memory. Edge inference: focus on sparsity exploitation, analog in-memory compute, duty-cycle management, and strict thermal/power envelopes. Both markets are massive — and they need completely different EE skillsets.

## Daily Research Topic: Power Delivery Architecture for AI Servers — 48V Bus, HVDC, and High-Density VRM Design

**What it is:** Modern AI accelerator boards (H100/B200 class) consume 700–1,000W per GPU. Powering racks of these requires a fundamental rethink of server power architecture — from legacy 12V ATX bus to 48V/HVDC approaches and ultra-high-current voltage regulator modules (VRMs).

**5 specific technical concepts to master:**

- **48V Bus Adoption (Open Rack 3.0 / OCP):** Reduces I²R losses in the distribution path by 16× vs 12V (same power, 4× voltage → 4× less current → 16× less resistive loss). Meta, Google, and Microsoft data centers are standardizing on 48V rack-level distribution
- **Two-Stage Conversion:** 48V bus → intermediate (12V or direct) → point-of-load (PoL) VRM at 0.8–1.1V for the GPU die. Each conversion stage adds ~2–3% loss; co-design minimizes conversion stages
- **High-Frequency GaN VRMs:** GaN HEMTs enable switching at 1–3 MHz (vs 300–600 kHz for Si MOSFETs), shrinking filter inductors by 3–5× and enabling faster transient response for GPU voltage slew requirements (up to 1 A/ns)
- **Current Sharing in Multi-Phase VRMs:** GPU power delivery uses 8–16+ interleaved phases; equalize current sharing to within ±5% using droop compensation or active current share; each phase handles 20–30A at 1V out
- **Thermal Co-Design:** The PCB power path (GND/VCC planes, via arrays, copper pour) is part of the electrical design — resistance increases with temperature, creating thermal-electrical feedback. Model with thermal resistance R_θJA and use 2 oz copper pours for high-current layers

**Concrete calculation to work through:**

Design a 48V → 1V DC-DC converter at I_out = 300A (a representative GPU rail). Target switching frequency f_sw = 1 MHz, peak-to-peak inductor current ripple ΔI_L = 5% of I_out = 15A.

Using the buck converter inductor equation:

```
L = (V_in × D × (1 - D)) / (f_sw × ΔI_L)
D = V_out / V_in = 1/48 ≈ 0.0208
L = (48 × 0.0208 × 0.979) / (1×10⁶ × 15)
L ≈ 0.979 / 15×10⁶ ≈ 65 nH
```

That's 65 nH per phase at 1 MHz — compare this to the same design at 300 kHz: L ≈ 217 nH (3.3× larger). This is exactly why GaN enables smaller, higher-density VRMs. Now extend: at 300A total with 12 interleaved phases, each phase carries 25A. Calculate the required output capacitance to hold ΔV_out ≤ 5 mV during a 150A/µs load step.
