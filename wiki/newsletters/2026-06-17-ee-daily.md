# EE Daily — June 17, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. TSMC 2nm GAA Nanosheet Process Enters Volume Production — First Chips Shipping**
TSMC's N2 process node, using Gate-All-Around (GAA) nanosheet transistors, has entered volume production with first customer chips now shipping. N2 delivers ~15% speed improvement at the same power or 25–30% power reduction at the same speed vs. N3E, with a ~1.15× logic density improvement. Apple and NVIDIA are confirmed lead customers. The process uses a 4-sheet nanosheet stack with backside power delivery network (BSPDN) as an optional add-on (N2P variant).

> **Why it matters:** GAA replaces FinFET as the dominant transistor architecture below 3 nm. The nanosheet channel provides electrostatic control on all four sides, reducing subthreshold leakage by ~50% compared to FinFETs at equivalent gate length. For power IC designers, the key implication is that sub-0.5V operation becomes viable for digital logic — enabling lower VRM output rails and tighter voltage regulation tolerances.

**2. AMD Captures 46.2% Server CPU Market Share — Highest Ever**
Mercury Research data for Q1 2026 shows AMD's EPYC processors reached 46.2% unit share in the x86 server CPU market, the highest in company history. AMD's Venice (Zen 6, TSMC 2nm) enters full commercial production with the July 22 Advancing AI event as the launch catalyst. Intel's server share declined to 53.8%, the lowest since the Opteron era. AMD's data center revenue run rate is now ~$33B annualized.

> **Why it matters:** AMD's Venice is the first x86 HPC CPU on TSMC's 2nm GAA process. The architectural shift from Zen 5 (4nm) to Zen 6 (2nm) brings CCD-level chiplet redesign with increased L3 cache density and a new Infinity Fabric 4.0 interconnect targeting 100 GB/s per link. For system designers, Venice's power envelope (~400W TDP) means server motherboard VRM design must support sustained >33A per phase at 12V input.

**3. Samsung Begins HBM4 Mass Production — DRAM Shortage Drives DDR5 Prices Up 4×**
Samsung announced mass production of HBM4 memory using its 5th-generation 10nm-class DRAM process. HBM4 uses 16-Hi die stacking with a logic base die (replacing the traditional buffer die), enabling ~2.8 TB/s bandwidth per stack. Simultaneously, the HBM production ramp is creating severe DRAM supply reallocation — DDR5 module prices have increased approximately 4× since early 2025 as fab capacity shifts from commodity DRAM to high-margin HBM.

> **Why it matters:** The HBM4 logic base die is a fundamental architectural change: instead of a passive silicon interposer connecting DRAM dies to a buffer, the base die integrates compute-near-memory logic, error correction, and the PHY interface directly. This requires heterogeneous integration at the package level — DRAM dies on one process, logic die on another (potentially TSMC N5/N4), bonded via hybrid Cu-Cu thermocompression. The DDR5 price spike cascades into every server, edge device, and consumer PC using standard DRAM.

**4. Monash University Demonstrates Room-Temperature Valleytronic Chip (Nature Photonics)**
Published in *Nature Photonics* (June 2026), researchers at Monash University demonstrated a room-temperature valleytronic device using MoS₂ monolayers integrated with a silicon photonic circuit. The device exploits valley polarization — the selective population of inequivalent K and K' valleys in the Brillouin zone — to encode and process information using valley index rather than charge or spin. Previous demonstrations required cryogenic temperatures; this is the first room-temperature on-chip demonstration.

> **Why it matters:** Valleytronics offers a third degree of freedom for information encoding beyond charge (electronics) and spin (spintronics). The MoS₂ valley lifetime at room temperature was measured at >100 ps — sufficient for GHz-class switching if valley-to-photon transduction efficiency improves. The integration with standard silicon photonics (SiPh) waveguides suggests a path to CMOS-compatible valley-photonic hybrid processors. This is early-stage but architecturally significant.

**5. Record 10.2 W/mm GaN-on-Si HEMTs Demonstrated for 5G/6G Power Amplifiers**
Researchers reported GaN-on-Si high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) achieving a record power density of 10.2 W/mm at 28 GHz, published at the 2026 IEEE MTT-S International Microwave Symposium. The devices used an advanced field plate design with a 150 nm gate length on a 200 mm GaN-on-Si platform. Power-added efficiency (PAE) reached 62% at the P₃dB compression point.

> **Why it matters:** 10.2 W/mm on GaN-on-Si (not GaN-on-SiC) is significant because Si substrates are 5–10× cheaper than SiC. If thermal management can be solved (GaN-on-Si thermal conductivity is ~150 W/mK vs. ~490 W/mK for SiC), this enables cost-effective 5G/6G FR3 massive MIMO base station PAs. The 62% PAE at 28 GHz approaches GaN-on-SiC performance, closing the gap that has kept GaN-on-Si out of mmWave infrastructure.

**6. 2D Molecular Ferroelectric Achieves Sub-10 nm Thickness with Stable Polarization**
Published in *Advanced Materials* (2026), researchers demonstrated a molecular ferroelectric thin film maintaining stable switchable polarization down to sub-10 nm thickness — below the critical depolarization limit that plagues conventional perovskite ferroelectrics (BaTiO₃, PZT) at similar dimensions. The material uses hydrogen-bonded molecular crystals with an intrinsic polarization mechanism that does not rely on ionic displacement, avoiding the size-effect limitations of oxide ferroelectrics.

> **Why it matters:** Sub-10 nm ferroelectrics are the key enabler for next-generation [[FeFET]] nonvolatile memory scaling beyond the ~20 nm HZO (HfZrO₂) limit. Molecular ferroelectrics process at <200°C (vs. >400°C for HZO), making them back-end-of-line (BEOL) compatible. If endurance (>10¹⁰ cycles) and retention (>10 years at 85°C) can be demonstrated, this material class could displace HZO in embedded NVM for AI edge inference chips.

**7. Polariton-Based Neuromorphic Computing Demonstrated at Room Temperature**
Researchers demonstrated a room-temperature neuromorphic computing element using exciton-polariton condensates in an organic semiconductor microcavity. Polaritons — hybrid light-matter quasiparticles formed by strong coupling between photons and excitons — were used to implement nonlinear activation functions and synaptic weighting at optical speeds (~ps timescales). The system operates at room temperature in ambient conditions, unlike prior polariton demonstrations requiring cryogenic GaAs microcavities.

> **Why it matters:** Polariton neuromorphic computing combines the speed of photonics (THz bandwidth potential) with the nonlinearity of electronic excitations — without requiring electrical-to-optical conversion at each node. The room-temperature operation using organic materials (processable by spin-coating) removes the cryogenic barrier that made polaritonics a lab curiosity. Energy per MAC operation is projected at ~1 fJ — three orders of magnitude below CMOS digital MACs (~1 pJ). Watch for integration with silicon photonic platforms.

## Daily Research Topic: HBM4 Architecture, Packaging, and the DRAM Supply Crisis

**What it is:** HBM4 (High Bandwidth Memory, 4th generation) represents a fundamental architectural shift from HBM3E. Instead of passive TSV-connected DRAM die stacks on a buffer die, HBM4 introduces a logic base die that integrates the memory controller, PHY, ECC engine, and potentially compute-near-memory functions directly beneath the DRAM stack. Understanding the packaging, signaling, thermal, and supply-chain implications is essential for any EE working in AI hardware, memory systems, or advanced packaging.

**5 Technical Bullets:**
- **Logic Base Die Architecture:** HBM4 replaces the traditional buffer die with a logic die fabricated on an advanced logic process (TSMC N5 or N4). This die integrates the memory controller, 1024-bit wide I/O PHY, on-die ECC, and test/repair logic. The shift from a simple buffer to a full logic die means memory vendors (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) now need logic design teams and foundry partnerships — a structural industry change.
- **16-Hi Die Stacking and TSV Challenges:** HBM4 stacks 16 DRAM dies (vs. 12-Hi for HBM3E) using Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs). Each die is thinned to ~30 µm (total stack height ~480 µm + base die). TSV aspect ratio for 16-Hi stacking reaches ~10:1 (3 µm diameter, 30 µm depth), requiring sub-micron TSV-to-TSV pitch alignment across all 16 layers. Thermal resistance of the stack is proportional to the number of layers — at 16-Hi, junction-to-top thermal resistance exceeds 15°C/W per die without active cooling.
- **Bandwidth and Signaling:** HBM4 targets ~2.8 TB/s per stack using a 2048-bit interface (doubled from HBM3E's 1024-bit) at >6.4 Gbps per pin. The PHY must handle high-speed signaling through 16 layers of TSVs with characteristic impedance matching at each via transition. Signal integrity simulation requires 3D EM modeling of the full TSV-microbump-RDL stack — SPICE models are insufficient.
- **Hybrid Bonding vs. Thermocompression:** HBM3E used thermocompression bonding (TCB) with solder microbumps (~40 µm pitch). HBM4 is transitioning to Cu-Cu hybrid bonding at ~10 µm pitch, enabling higher I/O density and lower parasitic resistance/inductance per connection. Hybrid bonding requires <1 nm surface roughness (CMP) and <0.5 µm alignment accuracy — a 10× tighter spec than TCB.
- **DRAM Supply Crisis:** HBM production consumes 3× more wafer area per bit than standard DDR5 (due to TSV keep-out zones, test structures, and yield loss from stacking). As Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron shift capacity to HBM4 for NVIDIA/AMD AI GPU demand, commodity DDR5 supply contracts. DDR5-5600 module prices have risen ~4× since early 2025. This is not a transient shortage — it is a structural reallocation of the global DRAM supply chain toward AI.

**Concrete Calculation to Work Through:**
*Estimate the thermal resistance of a 16-Hi HBM4 stack:*
- Silicon thermal conductivity: k_Si = 148 W/(m·K)
- Each die: 30 µm thick, ~5.5 mm × 8 mm footprint → A = 44 mm² = 44 × 10⁻⁶ m²
- Thermal resistance per die: R_th = t / (k × A) = 30 × 10⁻⁶ / (148 × 44 × 10⁻⁶) = **0.0046 °C/W per die**
- 16 dies in series: R_stack = 16 × 0.0046 = **0.074 °C/W** (silicon only)
- Add bonding layer thermal resistance (~0.01 °C/W per interface × 15 interfaces) = 0.15 °C/W
- Total stack R_th ≈ **0.22 °C/W** — at 15 W per stack, ΔT_junction-to-top ≈ 3.3°C (manageable, but concentrated at bottom dies)
