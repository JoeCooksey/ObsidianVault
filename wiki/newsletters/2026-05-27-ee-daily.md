## Top Stories

**1. Broadcom AI Chip Revenue Hits $8.4B in Q1 FY2026 (+106% YoY) as Custom ASIC Displacement of GPUs Accelerates**

Broadcom reported $8.4 billion in AI semiconductor revenue for Q1 FY2026, a 106% year-over-year increase, with CEO Hock Tan citing a "line of sight to achieve AI revenue in excess of $100 billion in 2027" backed by a $73 billion disclosed backlog. The structural driver is hyperscaler-specific [[Custom AI ASICs]]: ASIC-based AI server shipments are projected to reach 27.8% of the total AI server market in 2026, growing 44.6% year-over-year, as Google (TPU), Meta (MTIA), and Microsoft (Maia) build silicon optimized for their specific inferencing and training workloads. [[NVIDIA]] still holds approximately 70% of the AI chip market but that share is projected to compress as XPU-to-workload matching improves inference efficiency far beyond general-purpose GPU throughput.

> **Why it matters:** The shift from GPU-centric to ASIC-centric AI compute is an interconnect problem as much as a logic problem. As each hyperscaler designs their own accelerator, the interface standards — [[NVLink]], [[PCIe]], [[CXL]], and increasingly optical I/O — become the battleground. EEs working on packaging, high-speed SerDes, and chiplet interconnects are at the center of this displacement.

**2. Moore4Power: Infineon Leads €91M EU Project for Heterogeneous Si/SiC/GaN Integration in Power Electronics**

Europe's Moore4Power initiative, officially launched May 20, 2026, is a Chips Joint Undertaking-funded project uniting 62 partners across 15 countries under Infineon Technologies AG. The three-year, €91 million program's technical core is *heterogeneous integration* — deploying silicon (Si), [[Silicon Carbide Power Electronics|silicon carbide (SiC)]], and [[Gallium Nitride Power Electronics|gallium nitride (GaN)]] within a single module, co-located with sensing, gate drive, and communications logic, with each semiconductor used where its material properties are most advantageous. Target efficiency goals include near-lossless bidirectional EV charging (>99% conversion efficiency), wind and solar converter improvements, and ≥30% reduction in railway propulsion switching losses. The project explicitly targets the next generation of modular power electronics beyond what monolithic WBG devices can achieve alone.

> **Why it matters:** Moore4Power is the clearest European signal yet that the WBG future is *heterogeneous*, not winner-take-all. A GaN FET's low on-resistance and fast switching wins in soft-switching converters; SiC's high breakdown voltage wins in hard-switching 1.2–3.3 kV bus applications. Integrating both in the same half-bridge — with a shared silicon gate-drive ASIC — demands careful co-design of dV/dt, common-source inductance, and Miller-plateau management across dissimilar gate thresholds.

**3. IBM Achieves 10× Speedup in Quantum Error Correction Decoding — One Year Ahead of Schedule**

IBM achieved a 10× speedup in [[Quantum Error Correction]] (QEC) decoding hardware, a milestone that arrived approximately one year earlier than the company's published quantum development roadmap had projected. The speedup applies to syndrome extraction and decoding latency — the time between a physical qubit measurement and the issuance of a corrective operation — which is the dominant bottleneck in real-time, below-surface-code-threshold computation. Concurrently, new protocols combining quantum [[QLDPC Codes|low-density parity-check (QLDPC)]] codes with concatenated Steane codes have been demonstrated that achieve both low space overhead (fewer physical qubits per logical qubit) and low time overhead, a combination previously thought to require separate code families. The IBM-Cisco partnership targeting networked distributed quantum infrastructure is on track for a 2030 operational target.

> **Why it matters:** The decoding bottleneck is fundamentally a real-time digital signal processing problem: a syndrome decoder must process measurement outcomes faster than qubits decohere (typically microseconds for superconducting transmons). Achieving 10× speedup in hardware decoding is equivalent to extending coherence time by 10× from the system's perspective. EEs with FPGA, ASIC, or high-speed digital background are directly applicable to this problem — syndrome decoders are increasingly implemented in custom digital logic rather than software.

**4. Google's Willow Quantum Echoes Algorithm Runs 13,000× Faster Than Classical Supercomputers**

Google demonstrated the first verifiable quantum advantage using the *Quantum Echoes* algorithm on its Willow processor — running an out-of-order time correlator algorithm 13,000 times faster than the best available classical supercomputer implementations. The result uses Willow's 105 physical qubits operating below the surface code error threshold, meaning logical error rate actually decreases as the system is scaled up: from a distance-3 to a distance-5 to a distance-7 surface code, each doubling of the code distance cut the logical error rate by approximately 2×. This contrasts with pre-threshold operation where scaling increases error. The algorithm is not yet industrially useful, but the threshold crossing is the key engineering milestone.

> **Why it matters:** "Below threshold" is the watershed event in quantum computing — it means additional physical qubits buy you exponentially better logical qubits rather than accumulating more errors. From an EE microwave engineering perspective, the Willow processor relies on superconducting transmon qubits coupled via tunable couplers at millikelvin temperatures, with readout fidelity driven by parametric amplifiers (JPAs) and careful 4–8 GHz coaxial chain design. The hardware is as much analog RF engineering as it is quantum physics.

**5. FMCW Silicon Photonics LiDAR: Tower Semiconductor + LightIC Collaborate; Voyant Carbon Family Enters Volume Production**

Two parallel developments are pushing [[Silicon Photonics|silicon photonics]]-based [[Frequency-Modulated Continuous Wave LiDAR|FMCW LiDAR]] toward commercial volume. First, Tower Semiconductor and LightIC Technologies announced a strategic collaboration leveraging Tower's mature silicon photonics process for LightIC's Lark™ long-range automotive LiDAR and FR60™ compact robotics LiDAR. Second, Voyant Photonics' Carbon family of [[Optical Phased Array|optical phased array (OPA)]]-based silicon photonics LiDAR is expected to begin volume production in Q2 2026. FMCW LiDAR offers a fundamental sensing advantage over [[Time-of-Flight LiDAR|time-of-flight (ToF)]] approaches: coherent detection provides simultaneous range *and* radial velocity in a single laser pulse, with photon-shot-noise-limited sensitivity that makes the system immune to interference from other LiDAR units operating at the same wavelength.

> **Why it matters:** FMCW on a silicon photonics chip collapses what was previously a rack of optical components — laser, chirp driver, 90° optical hybrid, balanced photodetectors, ADCs — into a sub-cm² die. The key EE challenges are the coherent receiver [[Signal-to-Noise Ratio|SNR]] chain (balanced detection for shot-noise rejection, TIA bandwidth, ADC ENOB vs linearity trade-off) and the frequency chirp linearity requirement: a non-linear chirp produces a broadened point-spread function equivalent to a blurred pixel. Chirp linearity must be controlled to <1% deviation over the full frequency excursion.

**6. Advanced Materials Review: 2D Ferroelectric Semiconductors Enable Non-Volatile Logic-in-Memory at Atomic Thickness**

A 2026 *Advanced Materials* review ("Ferroelectrics Hybrids: Harnessing Multifunctionality of 2D Semiconductors in the Post-Moore Era") consolidates the state of 2D ferroelectric semiconductors — materials that simultaneously exhibit ferroelectric polarization switching and semiconducting channel conductivity at the ultimate thickness limit of a few atomic layers. Key material: α-In₂Se₃ maintains robust, stable polarization down to single-digit atomic layer thicknesses where conventional perovskite ferroelectrics (HfO₂-ZrO₂, PZT) suffer from depolarization fields and catastrophic polarization collapse. [[Ferroelectric Field-Effect Transistors|Ferroelectric field-effect transistors (FeFETs)]] built on these 2D channels combine non-volatile data retention with steep subthreshold swing (potentially below the 60 mV/dec Boltzmann limit) and in-place logic-in-memory: the same device stores and computes simultaneously, eliminating the von Neumann memory wall. Van der Waals interfaces between layers suppress the dangling-bond trap states that plague conventional gate dielectric integration.

> **Why it matters:** For EEs tracking advanced CMOS scaling, the FeFET represents a credible path to logic-in-memory architectures that sidestep the bandwidth bottleneck dominating AI inference accelerators. The key engineering challenge is retention-endurance trade-off: the polarization switching cycle count in 2D FeFETs currently peaks around 10⁶–10⁷ cycles, well below the 10¹² target for SRAM replacement. Understanding the physics — domain nucleation kinetics, Merz's Law exponent, coercive field scaling with film thickness — is critical for anyone working in advanced memory or post-CMOS logic.

## Daily Research Topic: Quantum Error Correction — From Surface Codes to QLDPC

**Why now:** IBM's 10× decoding speedup and Google's below-threshold demonstration on Willow make this week the highest-density week of QEC news since the field was formalized. Whether you work in RF, digital design, cryogenic electronics, or control systems, QEC is the central engineering bottleneck between today's NISQ devices and fault-tolerant quantum computation.

**What it is:** Quantum error correction encodes one *logical* qubit into many *physical* qubits such that local errors — bit-flips (X), phase-flips (Z), or both (Y) — can be detected and corrected without measuring the qubit's quantum state directly. Detection uses *stabilizer measurements* (ancilla qubits that measure parity without collapsing the data qubit), producing a classical *syndrome* that a decoder maps to a correction operation.

**Specific technical concepts to understand:**

- **[[Surface Code]]**: The dominant near-term QEC code. Logical qubits are encoded on a 2D lattice of physical qubits; distance-d code requires d² physical qubits per logical qubit and can correct ⌊(d-1)/2⌋ errors. Error threshold ~1% per gate. Decoders: minimum-weight perfect matching (MWPM) on the syndrome graph.
- **[[QLDPC Codes]]**: Quantum low-density parity-check codes have sparse parity-check matrices. Asymptotically optimal: achieve constant encoding rate (k logical qubits per n physical qubits, k/n = const > 0) with distance growing as √n. IBM's 2024 Gross code is a [[Bivariate Bicycle Code]] with 72 physical qubits per logical qubit (vs ~1,000 for equivalent surface code). The space overhead reduction is the headline.
- **[[Concatenated Codes]]**: Steane [[7,1,3]] code nested within itself. Concatenation level l achieves error rate p^(2^l) with 7^l physical qubits per logical qubit. Threshold ~10⁻³. Advantage: transversal gates (fault-tolerant Clifford gates without ancilla magic state distillation).
- **Syndrome decoding hardware**: Real-time decoders must run faster than qubit decoherence (T₁ ~100 µs for transmons, T₂ ~200 µs). IBM's 10× speedup targets this loop: FPGA- or ASIC-based MWPM must complete a syndrome extraction round in ~1 µs including classical communication latency.
- **[[Logical Error Rate]] scaling**: Below threshold, logical error rate p_L ≈ (p/p_th)^⌊(d+1)/2⌋. Above threshold, adding more qubits makes things *worse*. The threshold crossing demonstrated on Willow (Google) is the empirical proof that the device operates in the favorable regime.

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore search terms: "surface code fault tolerant", "QLDPC threshold", "syndrome decoder FPGA", "bivariate bicycle code IBM"
- Primary wikilinks: [[Quantum Error Correction]], [[Surface Code]], [[QLDPC Codes]], [[Logical Error Rate]]
- Preskill's lecture notes Ch. 7 (quantum error correction) — freely available; covers stabilizer formalism from first principles in ~40 pages.

**Concrete calculation to work through:**

A distance-5 surface code has threshold p_th ≈ 0.01 (1%). If your physical gate error rate is p = 0.001 (0.1%):

1. Calculate the logical error rate per round: p_L ≈ A·(p/p_th)^3 where A ≈ 0.1 is an empirical prefactor.
2. How many rounds of syndrome extraction are needed to complete one logical T-gate via magic state distillation (hint: ~15d rounds for the leading-order protocol)?
3. What is the total logical error probability for one T-gate?
4. If your algorithm requires 10⁶ T-gates, what distance d is required to keep the total algorithm failure probability below 1%?

Work through this to understand why code distance scales only logarithmically with algorithm depth — a key insight for estimating quantum resource requirements.
