## Top Stories

**1. NVIDIA Vera CPU Enters Full Production — OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX First Adopters**

NVIDIA's Vera CPU (Grace successor, ARM Neoverse V3 core complex, CoWoS-L interposer with NVLink-C2C at 900 GB/s) has entered full production volume. OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX are confirmed early adopters. Separately, the RTX Spark "superchip" (20-core Grace + Blackwell RTX GPU on one package, 1 petaflop INT8 AI, 128 GB unified LPDDR5X) will ship in laptops from Microsoft, Dell, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, and MSI starting this fall. CNBC noted NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang describes this as "owning every layer of the AI stack."

> **Why it matters:** Vera in production signals that custom ARM silicon for HPC/AI inference is no longer an experiment — it's the data center architecture. For EEs designing rack-level power delivery, Vera changes the thermal envelope significantly: expect 900W TDP per chip at 48V bus, necessitating GaN-based VRMs and advanced liquid cooling. The RTX Spark's 1 PFLOPS in a laptop form factor is a forcing function for edge inference PCB layout and DDR5X power integrity.

**2. Renesas TP65B110HRU: First Depletion-Mode GaN Bidirectional Switch (APEC 2026)**

Renesas unveiled the TP65B110HRU at APEC 2026 — claimed first commercial bidirectional switch built on depletion-mode (d-mode) GaN technology. Rated 650V, 11mΩ Rds(on), targeting solar microinverters, AI data center power, and V2G on-board chargers. D-mode (normally-on) GaN avoids the p-GaN gate overhead of enhancement-mode devices, yielding lower Rds(on) for the same chip area, at the cost of requiring a negative gate supply (typically −5V) to hold the device off.

> **Why it matters:** Bidirectional power flow is the key enabling topology for V2G, battery energy storage, and solid-state transformers. Previously, engineers stacked two discrete e-mode GaN FETs back-to-back. A single monolithic d-mode bidirectional switch cuts gate driver complexity, parasitic inductance, and PCB area. The 11mΩ Rds(on) at 650V beats comparable e-mode stack configurations by ~30%. This is today's deep-dive topic — see below.

**3. QuiX Quantum FFCU: Feed-Forward Photonic QC at 150 ns Latency**

QuiX Quantum installed its Feed-Forward Control Unit (FFCU) on June 2, enabling real-time adaptive measurement operations inside a photonic quantum computing stack. The FFCU uses a dual-FPGA bus with a 32×32 routing matrix and processes measurement outcomes at deterministic 150-nanosecond latency — fast enough to use mid-circuit measurement results to condition subsequent gate operations in a photonic cluster-state computation. This is a requirement for fault-tolerant measurement-based quantum computing (MBQC).

> **Why it matters:** Photonic QC requires active feed-forward: measure one photon, use the result to adjust the next gate before the photon decoherence time (~nanoseconds). Getting this below 200ns with classical FPGA processing removes the primary engineering bottleneck for photonic qubit scale-up. EEs working on FPGA timing closure and low-latency optical interconnects should watch this space — the tools are classical RF/digital engineering applied to quantum control planes.

**4. κ-Ga₂O₃ Ferroelectricity Mechanism Revealed — In-Plane Sliding Like 2D Materials**

A 2026 npj Computational Materials study titled "Origin of suppressed ferroelectricity in κ-Ga₂O₃: interplay between polarization and lattice domain walls" resolves a long-standing puzzle: polarization reversal in κ-Ga₂O₃ occurs via in-plane Ga–O sublayer sliding and shear — the same mechanism as 2D sliding ferroelectrics such as h-BN and MoS₂. This explains why domain walls pin at low fields and why MOCVD-grown thin films show ferroelectric switching only below ~50 nm. The bandgap of κ-Ga₂O₃ is ~5.0 eV (UWBG), with a theoretical breakdown field of ~8 MV/cm.

> **Why it matters:** Ga₂O₃ is the leading UWBG candidate to displace SiC in medium-voltage (1–3.3 kV) power switches for traction and grid applications. Understanding the ferroelectric polarization mechanism matters for two reasons: (1) uncontrolled polarization switching in n-type UWBG FETs introduces threshold voltage instability; (2) intentional ferroelectric switching could enable non-volatile gate memories. This clarifies the design rules for MOCVD epitaxial stacks targeting stable, non-ferroelectric operation.

**5. Perovskite Catalyst Splits Water at Room Temperature — Hydrogen at Lower Energy Cost**

University of Birmingham researchers published a perovskite-based heterogeneous catalyst that drives water electrolysis at substantially lower overpotential than platinum-group-metal benchmarks. The LaCoO₃-δ perovskite with controlled oxygen vacancy concentration achieves a 280 mV overpotential at 10 mA/cm² in alkaline solution (pH 14), compared to ~320–370 mV for IrO₂. The catalyst is synthesized by sol-gel with a 700°C anneal and shows stable performance over 200 hours.

> **Why it matters:** [[Green Hydrogen]] production cost is dominated by electrolyzer efficiency (η = V_thermo / V_applied, where V_thermo = 1.23V and typical V_applied = 1.8–2.0V). A 280 mV OER overpotential would push V_applied toward 1.7V, raising electrolyzer efficiency from ~65% to ~72%. At GW-scale production, 7 percentage points of efficiency translates directly to LCOH (levelized cost of hydrogen). For power electronics engineers: this changes the power profile of the electrolyzer stack, reducing the reactive power draw during startup.

**6. Memristor Neuromorphic Platform — Multimodal Sensing + Unsupervised Learning on One Chip**

Researchers published a self-powered memristor-based neuromorphic platform that unifies multimodal sensing (pressure, temperature, light), spike encoding, and unsupervised Hebbian learning within a single analog circuit operating at ultralow power. The platform harvests ambient energy and uses resistive switching in HfO₂-based memristors as both memory and computing elements. Demonstrated edge-case classification of multi-sensor inputs without external power or cloud connectivity.

> **Why it matters:** This is the physical realization of the "sensor-compute fusion" architecture that IoT and edge-AI roadmaps have promised for a decade. The critical EE insight: memristive synaptic weights eliminate ADC/DAC overhead at the sensor-compute interface. For circuit designers, this opens the question: can HfO₂ memristors be co-integrated with CMOS sensing front-ends on standard 22nm or 28nm FDSOI processes without process modification? The paper suggests yes, using TiN/HfO₂/TiN stacks compatible with standard BEOL.

SK Hynix reached a $1 trillion market capitalization this week, joining Samsung and Micron as the AI memory demand reshapes semiconductor industry valuations. HBM3E (High Bandwidth Memory) demand from NVIDIA, AMD, and Google TPUs is outstripping supply, with prices holding premium over standard DDR5 by 4–5×. SK Hynix is the primary supplier of HBM for NVIDIA's Blackwell and upcoming Vera Rubin architectures.

> **Why it matters:** HBM pricing and supply is a direct input into AI accelerator BOM cost — and therefore into the economics of every cloud inference workload. For hardware engineers: HBM3E uses Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs) at 9 µm pitch with 64-layer DRAM stacking and a base logic die. Power density of an HBM stack is ~25W/stack at peak bandwidth (1.2 TB/s per stack). Thermal management of the package is a first-order design constraint for next-gen AI server PCBs.

## 🔬 Daily Deep Dive — D-Mode GaN Bidirectional Switches

**What it is:** A bidirectional power switch built from depletion-mode (normally-on) GaN HEMTs, where two devices share a back-to-back common-source or common-drain configuration to block voltage and conduct current in both directions. This topology eliminates the need for two discrete e-mode devices plus isolation circuitry.

**Technical bullets:**

- **Device physics:** D-mode GaN HEMTs have Vth ≈ −3V to −6V. The 2DEG channel is fully formed at zero gate bias (hence "normally on"). Turning off requires applying −5V (or more negative) to the gate, demanding a negative gate supply rail — the main practical complexity vs. e-mode.
- **Rds(on) advantage:** Without the p-GaN gate overhead (which in e-mode devices narrows the 2DEG under the gate and adds ~15% Rds(on) penalty), d-mode devices achieve 30–50% lower Rds(on) at equivalent voltage rating and die size. Renesas TP65B110HRU: 11mΩ at 650V.
- **Bidirectional blocking:** In common-source back-to-back configuration (sources tied together, drains facing out), each device blocks reverse voltage through its Vth. Net blocking voltage = single device's Vds_max. Common-drain (sources facing out) requires independent gate drives but allows higher blocking.
- **Switching loss:** E_off = ½ × Coss × VDS². For TP65B110HRU with Coss ≈ 40 pF at VDS = 650V: E_off ≈ ½ × 40×10⁻¹² × (650)² = **8.45 µJ** per switching event. At 100 kHz and 10A, P_sw ≈ 0.845W per switch — competitive with SiC MOSFETs.
- **Applications:** V2G on-board chargers (bidirectional OBC), battery energy storage DC-DC, solid-state transformers, solar microinverters with grid-tied operation in both power-flow directions.

**Concrete calculation — compare switching loss budget at 100 kHz:**

```
D-mode GaN (TP65B110HRU):  P_sw = f × E_off = 100kHz × 8.45µJ = 0.845W
Conduction loss at 10A:    P_cond = I² × Rds(on) = 100 × 0.011 = 1.1W
Total per device:           ~1.95W

Equivalent SiC MOSFET (650V, 16mΩ):  P_cond = 100 × 0.016 = 1.6W; P_sw ≈ 1.2W → ~2.8W
GaN advantage: ~30% lower total loss at 100 kHz
```
