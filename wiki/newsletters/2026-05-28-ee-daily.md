# EE Daily — May 28, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. AMD EPYC "Venice" Enters Production on TSMC 2nm — First 2nm HPC Processor in the World**

AMD announced on May 21, 2026 that its 6th Gen EPYC "Venice" server CPU has begun production ramp at TSMC's N2 (2nm-class) process node — the first high-performance computing product in the industry to reach volume production at this node. Venice uses gate-all-around nanosheet transistors, replacing the FinFET architecture used since 2014. AMD plans a follow-on 2nm part called "Verano" optimized for performance-per-dollar-per-watt. Initial production is in Taiwan; a US (TSMC Arizona) ramp follows. AMD's broader data center revenue hit $10.3B in Q1 2026, up 38% YoY, with data center revenue up 57%.

> **Why it matters:** The first 2nm production milestone confirms GAA nanosheet technology is manufacturable at volume. For EE students: study GAA vs. FinFET — nanosheet gate geometry, short-channel control, and threshold voltage tuning via nanosheet width. This is the dominant transistor architecture for the rest of the decade.

**2. Broadcom AI ASIC Revenue Doubles YoY — Custom Silicon Now 27.8% of AI Server Market**

Broadcom reported $8.4B in AI semiconductor revenue for Q1 FY2026 (ending February), up 106% year-over-year, with CEO Hock Tan stating "line of sight to achieve AI revenue from chips in excess of $100 billion in 2027." Custom ASIC-based AI server shipments are projected to reach 27.8% of the market in 2026, growing 44.6% YoY — nearly triple the 16.1% growth rate of merchant GPUs (primarily Nvidia). The shift is structural: hyperscalers (Google TPU, Meta MTIA, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia) are designing purpose-built inference and training silicon rather than buying commodity GPUs.

> **Why it matters:** The ASIC vs. GPU trade-off is an EE design decision: ASICs achieve 2-10× better performance-per-watt for fixed workloads but cost $50–500M to tape out. Study [[Digital Logic and Boolean Algebra]], systolic array architectures, and HBM-to-SRAM memory hierarchies — these are the core skills behind ASIC design.

**3. SK Hynix 2026 HBM Production Fully Sold Out — AI Memory Crunch Reaches Critical Level**

SK Hynix has sold out its entire 2026 HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) production allocation, with the global semiconductor industry projected to exceed $975B in sales in 2026. HBM3E is the dominant stack, providing 1.15 TB/s per package at 12-hi stacks. The memory bottleneck is now the binding constraint on AI training throughput: a single H100 GPU uses five 8-hi HBM3 stacks delivering ~3.35 TB/s aggregate bandwidth, and next-gen AI accelerators demand even higher. IDC projects data center semiconductor revenue alone hits $477B in 2026.

> **Why it matters:** [[HBM]] architecture — 3D stacking via TSV (through-silicon via) bonding, inter-die µbumps at 55 µm pitch, SerDes interface at 6.4 Gbps/pin × 1,024 pins — is one of the most technically demanding packaging problems in the industry. Understand TSV fabrication, thermo-mechanical stress, and power delivery to stacked dies.

**4. Ferroelectric κ-Ga₂O₃ MOCVD Framework: UWBG Meets Non-Volatile Memory**

A 2026 study in *Advanced Electronic Materials* established a quantitative temperature-pressure processing map for phase-pure orthorhombic (ε/κ) gallium oxide via MOCVD, identifying a narrow stability window at 560–590°C and 7–23 Torr. κ-Ga₂O₃ combines a 4.9 eV bandgap (UWBG) with intrinsic ferroelectricity — enabling non-volatile logic-in-memory in a single material. β-Ga₂O₃ has a Baliga Figure of Merit (BFOM) ~3,300, 3–10× larger than SiC and GaN, with a theoretical breakdown field of 8 MV/cm. Separately, China's Tiancheng Semiconductor successfully grew 14-inch SiC boules in March 2026, pushing large-area substrate availability.

> **Why it matters:** Ultra-wide bandgap (UWBG) semiconductors represent the next frontier beyond SiC and GaN for power electronics. Ga₂O₃ is commercially available on native 4-inch substrates now. Understand the tradeoffs: Ga₂O₃ has no p-type doping (bipolar device problem), poor thermal conductivity (~11 W/m·K vs SiC's 490), and depletion-mode only devices. These are the unsolved engineering problems — ripe for thesis work.

**5. NVIDIA Quantum-X Co-Packaged Optics: 100–400 Tbps at 3.5× Better Power Efficiency**

NVIDIA's Quantum-X and Spectrum-X photonic switches use co-packaged optics (CPO) for InfiniBand and Ethernet networks, achieving 100–400 Tbps aggregate bandwidth with 3.5× improvement in power efficiency and 10× better signal integrity versus conventional pluggable modules. TSMC's COUPE platform for CPO integration is entering pilot production in 2026. PsiQuantum separately demonstrated a 300mm silicon photonics process with waveguide-integrated photon-number-resolving detectors at 98.9% single-photon efficiency — the first feature-complete photonic quantum computing foundry platform.

> **Why it matters:** Silicon photonics integrates optical waveguides, modulators (MZI, ring), and photodetectors into CMOS-compatible silicon using 193nm DUV lithography. Key parameters: waveguide loss ~2 dB/cm, ring resonator Q ~10,000, Mach-Zehnder modulator bandwidth ~50 GHz. CPO eliminates the electrical-to-optical conversion penalty of pluggables. Study [[Silicon Photonics]] fundamentals — this is a key EE career path for 2026–2030.

**6. Bidirectional Graphene Neural Interface Published in Nature Communications**

A bidirectional neural interface based on graphene transistors was published in *Nature Communications* on May 28, 2026, demonstrating simultaneous recording and stimulation of neural activity with gate-referred noise <5 µV_rms and impedance <100 kΩ at 1 kHz. The graphene transistor operates in electrolyte-gated mode, enabling conformable, biocompatible electronics in direct contact with neural tissue.

> **Why it matters:** Graphene's ambipolar transport, ~10,000 cm²/V·s carrier mobility, and optical transparency (97.7%) make it uniquely suited for neural electronics. The design challenge: graphene has no bandgap (Dirac point), requiring electrolyte gating or substrate engineering to achieve transistor behavior. This work bridges EE, materials science, and neuroscience.

## Daily Deep Dive: TSMC N2 Gate-All-Around (GAA) Nanosheet Technology

**Why today:** AMD's Venice is the first product in production at N2, making this the most live and relevant node transition in semiconductor history right now.

**What it is:** TSMC N2 replaces FinFET (fin field-effect transistor) with gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistors, where the gate metal wraps the channel on all four sides instead of three. This restores short-channel control at sub-3nm geometries where FinFETs can no longer adequately suppress leakage.

**Technical specifics:**
- **Nanosheet geometry:** Stacked Si nanosheets, each ~5 nm thick × ~20 nm wide; 2–4 sheets per stack; gate wraps all surfaces
- **Effective gate length:** ~12 nm at N2 vs ~15 nm at N3E; DIBL (Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering) suppressed to <50 mV/V
- **Drive current:** ~15% increase over N3E FinFET at same Vdd; leakage ~3× lower
- **Vt tunability:** Nanosheet width controls Vt — wide sheet = lower Vt (faster, more leakage); narrow sheet = higher Vt (slower, less leakage). TSMC calls this "FinFlex" equivalent
- **Backside power delivery (BSPDN):** N2P (future) will route VDD/VSS on the wafer backside via buried power rails, eliminating routing congestion on the front-side

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore: search "gate-all-around nanosheet CMOS 2025 2026"
- IEEE Xplore: "TSMC N2 process transistor characterization"
- [[Semiconductor Device Fundamentals]] — start with MOSFET I-V and short-channel effects before attacking GAA
- [[Silicon Photonics]] — complementary CPO context

**Calculation to work through:**
A nanosheet transistor has W_sheet = 30 nm and 3 stacked sheets. Effective channel width W_eff = 3 × (2 × t_sheet + W_sheet) where t_sheet = 5 nm. Calculate W_eff. Then, if drive current density J = 1.5 mA/µm at N2, what is the drive current per stack? Compare to a FinFET with fin height 42 nm and fin width 6 nm at N3E where W_eff = 2 × H_fin = 84 nm. Which has higher W_eff per unit footprint?
