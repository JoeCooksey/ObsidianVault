# EE Daily — June 21, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

### 1. **SK Hynix Ships 12-Layer HBM4E Samples — Samsung Three Weeks Ahead**

SK Hynix began shipping 12-layer HBM4E samples on June 18, targeting Nvidia's Rubin Ultra and AMD Instinct MI500 platforms. Key specs: 16 Gbps per pin, 48 GB per package, >20% power efficiency improvement, and ~17% thermal resistance reduction versus HBM4. Samsung shipped its own 12-layer HBM4E samples on May 29, roughly three weeks earlier. SK Hynix holds 58% HBM market share in Q1 2026; Samsung and Micron each hold ~21%. Combined with LPDDR6, the Rubin generation targets >3 TB/s memory throughput per GPU.

> **Why it matters:** HBM4E is the memory generation that will define AI training performance for 2027–2028. The 16 Gbps pin speed and 48 GB per stack finally close the [[HBM4 Architecture and the AI Memory Wall|memory wall]] gap that's been throttling GPU utilization at scale. The Samsung–SK Hynix race guarantees dual-source supply and competitive pricing for hyperscalers.

### 2. **Intel 18A-P Enters Risk Production — 9% Perf Gain, Apple/Google Interest**

Announced at VLSI Symposium 2026 in Santa Clara, Intel's 18A-P process enters risk production with measured results on standard Arm core sub-blocks: up to 9% performance increase at iso-power, or 18% power reduction at iso-performance versus 18A. [[Backside Power Delivery Network|Backside power delivery]] reduces frontside routing area by ~11%. Intel reportedly has secured foundry interest from Apple and Google, and formed a strategic partnership with UMC for mature-node capacity.

> **Why it matters:** 18A-P is Intel Foundry's bid to reclaim process leadership from TSMC. The 9%/18% numbers on real silicon (not simulation) give credibility to the roadmap. If Apple or Google sign production contracts, Intel Foundry becomes a viable second source for leading-edge logic — the most consequential shift in the foundry market since TSMC's monopoly solidified.

### 3. **PsiQuantum Breaks Ground on $940M Utility-Scale Photonic Quantum Computer**

Construction began June 18 at Moreton Bay Central, Petrie, Queensland, Australia. The AU/QLD governments committed ~$940M (US$620M). The facility will house PsiQuantum's Asia-Pacific HQ and the world's first utility-scale, fault-tolerant [[Silicon Photonics|silicon-photonics]]-based quantum computer. Linde Engineering is building the cryoplant (delivery expected H2 2027). Operational target is 2029 — pushed from the original 2027 timeline.

> **Why it matters:** PsiQuantum's bet is that photonic qubits fabricated on standard 300 mm GlobalFoundries CMOS lines can scale to millions of qubits where trapped-ion and superconducting approaches cannot. A $940M government commitment is the largest single quantum infrastructure investment to date, and the 2029 target puts it on a collision course with IBM's and Google's superconducting roadmaps.

### 4. **Duke/IonQ Demonstrate First Tripartite Entanglement Across Remote Trapped-Ion Nodes**

Published June 20: Duke University and IonQ demonstrated the first distributed GHZ (Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger) state across a three-node quantum network. Three spatially separated modules (~2 m apart) linked by 3 m single-mode optical fibers, each containing a single ¹³⁸Ba⁺ ion in a four-rod Paul trap. Measured fidelity: 0.841–0.881 (84–88%). Entanglement rate: 0.095 s⁻¹. No local two-qubit gates or post-selection required. Violated the Mermin inequality, closing the detection loophole.

> **Why it matters:** This is the first detection-loophole-free tripartite entanglement of remote matter qubits — the minimum viable demonstration of a modular quantum network. The 84–88% fidelity is above the threshold needed for error-corrected protocols. It proves the trapped-ion + fiber link architecture can scale beyond two nodes, which is the fundamental open question for distributed quantum computing.

### 5. **Bosch Roseville SiC Fab Begins 200 mm Production — $1.9B Total Investment**

Bosch's Roseville, California facility begins [[Silicon Carbide Power Electronics|SiC]] semiconductor production on 200 mm wafers in 2026. Total investment: up to $1.9 billion, including $225 million in CHIPS Act funding. At full capacity, the fab could comprise >40% of all US-based SiC device manufacturing capacity. Key application: electrified mobility (traction inverters, onboard chargers). Up to 700 permanent manufacturing/engineering/R&D jobs.

> **Why it matters:** This is the first major European-headquartered SiC fab reaching production on US soil. The 200 mm transition is critical — [[Wolfspeed]] has struggled with 200 mm SiC yield, so Bosch proving volume production validates the wafer-size migration. With >40% of US SiC capacity, Bosch becomes a strategic domestic supplier for the EV power electronics supply chain.

### 6. **Monash Creates First On-Chip Programmable Valleytronic Nanocircuit**

Published in *Nature Photonics*: Monash University demonstrated the first single chip that can generate, route, and read valley-polarized light signals on one integrated device. The chip uses the valley degree of freedom (K vs K' valleys in 2D TMD materials) as a binary information carrier — an alternative to charge (electronics) or polarization (photonics). Applications include quantum computing interfaces, advanced imaging, and next-gen low-energy computing.

> **Why it matters:** Valleytronics has been theory-heavy and demo-poor. A fully integrated circuit that does generate + route + detect on one chip is the "transistor moment" for the field — it proves the concept is implementable, not just publishable. For EEs, this opens a new device-physics design space alongside charge, spin, and photon degrees of freedom.

### 7. **NVIDIA RTX Spark Superchip — 1 PFLOP AI in a Laptop Form Factor**

Unveiled at Computex and shipping fall 2026: the RTX Spark is a 70-billion-transistor SoC on TSMC 3 nm fusing two chiplets via NVLink-C2C — a 20-core Grace Arm CPU + Blackwell RTX GPU with 6,144 CUDA cores and 5th-gen Tensor Cores (FP4). Delivers up to 1 petaflop of AI compute with 128 GB unified LPDDR5X and 300 GB/s memory bandwidth. Three-generation roadmap: Spark (Blackwell) → Rubin (LPDDR6) → Rosa Feynman.

> **Why it matters:** 1 PFLOP in a laptop collapses the distinction between edge and cloud for inference workloads. The unified memory architecture (like Apple Silicon but with a full Blackwell GPU) means local fine-tuning and inference of 70B-parameter models without network round-trips. For EE applications: on-device [[Surrogate Modeling|surrogate models]], real-time digital twins, and SPICE-scale simulation become laptop-portable.

## 🔬 Daily Research Topic

### Distributed Quantum Networking via Photonic Interconnects

**What it is:** The architecture and protocols for entangling qubits across spatially separated quantum processors using photonic links — the "networking layer" that could make quantum computers modular and scalable beyond single-chip limits.

**Why now:** The Duke/IonQ result (June 20) is the first three-node GHZ state without post-selection, and PsiQuantum just broke ground on the first utility-scale photonic quantum computer. Two competing architectures are converging on the same goal from different directions.

**Technical bullet points:**
- **Trapped-ion + fiber approach (Duke/IonQ):** Single ¹³⁸Ba⁺ ions in Paul traps emit photons entangled with ion spin states; 3 m single-mode fiber links; 493 nm photon collection via high-NA lenses; Bell-state measurement at central node creates remote entanglement; measured fidelity 84–88%, rate 0.095 s⁻¹
- **Silicon photonics approach (PsiQuantum):** Qubits encoded in single photons on 300 mm CMOS wafers; Mach-Zehnder interferometers for gates; GBS (Gaussian Boson Sampling) as near-term computational primitive; fusion-based linear optical quantum computing (FBQC) for fault tolerance
- **Key engineering challenges:** Photon loss in fiber (~0.2 dB/km at 1550 nm telecom band, but trapped-ion photons at 493 nm need frequency conversion); detector efficiency (>98% needed for fault tolerance); quantum memory buffers for asynchronous entanglement; cryogenic infrastructure at scale
- **Competing modular approaches:** Superconducting microwave links (IBM, short-range only), neutral atom shuttling (Atom Computing), ion transport through junctions (Quantinuum QCCD)
- **Error correction implications:** Distributed GHZ states are the building blocks of surface codes spread across modules; the 84% fidelity is above the ~67% threshold for useful entanglement but below the ~99% needed for competitive error rates — closing this gap is the critical engineering challenge

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore: search "modular quantum computing photonic interconnect" and "trapped ion remote entanglement fidelity"
- The Duke/IonQ paper (arXiv, June 2026) — focus on the Methods section for trap RF drive design, fiber coupling optics, and photon detection chains
- PsiQuantum's FBQC architecture paper (arXiv 2021, Bartolucci et al.) for the alternative photonic-native approach
- Related wiki pages: [[Silicon Photonics]], [[Quantum Computing]]

**Calculation to work through:** Given a fiber link with 0.2 dB/km loss at 1550 nm and a frequency converter with 30% efficiency from 493 nm → 1550 nm, what is the maximum inter-node distance that maintains >50% two-photon coincidence rate? How does this compare to the 3 m links used in the Duke experiment, and what does it imply for rack-scale vs. campus-scale quantum networks?
