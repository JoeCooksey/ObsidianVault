# EE Daily — June 01, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. NVIDIA Enters the PC Processor Market at Computex Taipei**

Jensen Huang unveiled NVIDIA's first-ever PC processor at Computex 2026, framing it as a paradigm shift "as big as the reinvention of the phone into the smartphone." The chip integrates GPU, NPU, and general-purpose compute in a single SoC targeting AI-native PC workloads. NVDA shares rose 2.1% in pre-market trading while Intel fell more than 6% on the news — the sharpest Intel single-session drop in months.

> **Why it matters:** This is the first credible challenge to Intel's grip on the x86 PC CPU market from a GPU vendor. For EEs, the key design questions are: ISA licensing approach (x86 vs. ARM), what process node is used (likely TSMC N3E or N2), and how the NPU tile integrates with the GPU fabric for on-device AI inference. The [[Neural Processing Unit]] and [[System-on-Chip Architecture]] implications extend directly to [[Edge AI Inference]] deployment and [[Heterogeneous Integration]] packaging strategies.

**2. China's Tiancheng Semiconductor Demonstrates 14-inch SiC Single Crystal**

Tiancheng Semiconductor announced a successful 14-inch (350mm) SiC single crystal using proprietary growth equipment, marking China's exit from the "12-inch adoption phase" into early 14-inch commercialization. Multiple Chinese players are simultaneously scaling 200mm SiC wafer production, putting supply-chain pressure on US and EU SiC incumbents including Wolfspeed, STMicro, and Coherent.

> **Why it matters:** Wafer area scales as πr², so moving from 150mm to 350mm yields a ~440% area gain, dramatically compressing per-die cost for SiC power devices. The critical R&D challenge at larger diameters is maintaining low micropipe density (<0.1/cm²) and threading screw dislocation density where thermal gradients in the physical vapor transport (PVT) growth reactor become the limiting factor. Tracks directly with [[Wide Bandgap Semiconductors]] supply chain diversification and [[SiC Power MOSFET]] economics.

**3. PCIM 2026: GaN and SiC Converge at 800V Data Center Power**

PCIM Expo 2026 highlighted 800VDC rack power delivery as the defining battleground between GaN and SiC. AI data centers at 100–200 kW/rack now require power electronics operating above 800V at switching frequencies above 500 kHz — a regime where GaN's lower Qg and SiC's higher Vbr advantage overlap. IDTechEx projects GaN power electronics market growth through 2036, with data centers and EV fast-charging as dual growth engines.

> **Why it matters:** At 800V/100 kHz, GaN's figure-of-merit Qg×Rdson is 2–4× lower than equivalent SiC MOSFETs, directly reducing switching losses. But 1200V SiC devices dominate where overvoltage transients exceed GaN's 650V Vbr. EEs designing 800V data center PSUs should model dV/dt-induced common-mode EMI, PCB layout parasitic inductance (L_loop < 1 nH target), and thermal resistance through AlN DBC substrates. See [[GaN HEMT]] and [[SiC Power MOSFET]] for design references.

**4. 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ Ferroelectric: 230 µC/cm² Polarization, 4×10⁹ Endurance Cycles**

Advanced Materials published the oxygen-self-supply CVD synthesis of a 2D Fe₂Mo₃O₈ ferroelectric semiconductor single crystal achieving 230 µC/cm² remnant polarization — 10 to 100× higher than most 2D ferroelectrics — and endurance exceeding 4×10⁹ switching cycles. Complementary work demonstrated CuInP₂S₆-based neuromorphic FeFET devices targeting analog weight storage in AI inference hardware.

> **Why it matters:** Ferroelectric polarization density (Pr, µC/cm²) directly sets minimum write voltage and cell area for [[FeFET Memory]]. At 230 µC/cm², a 2D FeFET gate stack can be fabricated at sub-10nm channel lengths without the crystallinity and leakage problems plaguing HfO₂-based 3D ferroelectrics. This is the core materials frontier for [[Neuromorphic Computing]] hardware at the edge.

**5. PsiQuantum "Omega" Photonic Chipset Fabricated on 300mm Silicon Wafers at GlobalFoundries**

PsiQuantum revealed details of "Omega," a quantum photonic chipset manufactured at GlobalFoundries' silicon photonics fab in Malta, NY on full-size 300mm wafers. The chipset integrates spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) single-photon sources, superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs), and optical switches — all at wafer scale, as published in Nature.

> **Why it matters:** The central EE challenge in photonic quantum computing is co-fabricating room-temperature photonics with cryogenic SNSPDs on the same substrate. SNSPDs require sub-3K operation (Tc ~2–4K for NbN/NbTiN films); integration on 300mm wafers requires CTE-matched stack design, via engineering, and careful attention to waveguide propagation loss (<0.1 dB/cm) and coupler insertion loss (<1 dB) to maintain photon fidelity. See [[Silicon Photonics]], [[Superconducting Nanowire Single-Photon Detectors]], [[Quantum Computing Hardware]].

Deloitte estimates the AI chip market at $500B in 2026, on track toward $1.2T by 2028 — a nearly 10× increase in four years. HBM bandwidth remains a key bottleneck, with memory chips identified as the largest supply constraint in AI infrastructure build-out.

> **Why it matters:** AI infrastructure capital intensity at this scale drives demand for every layer of the semiconductor stack — EUV lithography (ASML), advanced packaging (CoWoS, HBM stacking), [[High Bandwidth Memory]], GaN/SiC power conversion in data center PSUs, and photonic interconnects for rack-to-rack bandwidth. This is the macro demand signal underlying every hardware story in this newsletter.

## 🔬 Daily Deep Dive: GaN Power Amplifiers for 6G FR3 — PAE, Load Pull, and Harmonic Termination

**What it is:** The 6G FR3 band (7–24 GHz) requires base station power amplifiers (PAs) achieving >50% power-added efficiency (PAE) at 5–10W average output. GaN HEMTs on SiC substrates are the enabling technology — Soitec/NTU reported record PAE >50% at FR3 frequencies in 2026. Understanding how to design, simulate, and measure these PAs is foundational for 6G hardware work.

**Technical bullets:**

- **PAE definition:** PAE = (Pout − Pin) / PDC. At a bias of VDD = 28V, IDC = 200mA, Pout = 3W, Pin = 0.3W: PAE = (3.0 − 0.3) / (28 × 0.2) = 2.7 / 5.6 = **48.2%**. Every 1 dB of added transducer gain reduces required Pin by 20%, directly improving PAE at fixed Pout and VDD.

- **Load-pull contours:** The optimal load Γopt for max PAE is never 50Ω. For a 0.25 µm GaN HEMT at 10 GHz, Γopt typically lies near |Γ|≈0.55, ∠150° on the Smith chart. Load-pull sweeps (active or passive tuner) map PAE contours across the Γ plane; the 1% PAE contour defines the impedance matching tolerance for the output network.

- **Harmonic termination (Class F):** Presenting a near-open at 2f₀ and near-short at 3f₀ at the drain shapes the drain voltage waveform toward a square wave, eliminating idealized overlap between drain current and voltage. Theoretical PAE → 100%; practical Class F GaN PAs at 10 GHz achieve 65–80%. Realizing harmonic terminations at 10+ GHz is the hard part given parasitic Cds and package inductance.

- **6G FR3 link budget:** Free-space path loss at 10 GHz over 500m: L = 20·log₁₀(4π·500·10×10⁹/3×10⁸) ≈ 126 dB. With a 32 dBi base station array and 10 dBi UE antenna, a required SNR of 20 dB at the receiver implies roughly +30 dBm (1W) per active element — confirming why 64–256 element mMIMO arrays are the baseline at FR3, not higher per-element power.

- **Thermal management:** GaN HEMT thermal resistance Rth ≈ 3–5°C/(W·mm) for GaN-on-SiC (vs. ~15–20 for GaN-on-Si). At 4W/mm power density, Tj = Tamb + 5 × 4 = 45°C above ambient — well within the 150°C reliability limit. SiC substrate thermal conductivity (κ ≈ 370 W/m·K) is the reason GaN-on-SiC dominates base station PAs while GaN-on-Si remains in lower-power consumer applications.

- IEEE Xplore: `"GaN HEMT" "FR3" "PAE" "load pull" 2025 2026` and `"Class F amplifier" GaN "harmonic termination" millimeter-wave`
- Wikilinks: [[GaN HEMT]], [[6G Wireless]], [[Power Amplifier Design]], [[Load Pull Measurement]], [[Class F Amplifier]]

Then: if improving the output match raises Pout to 3.0W at identical PDC and Pin, what is the new PAE? *(Answer 1: PAE₁ = (2.5−0.25)/(28×0.15) = 2.25/4.2 = 53.6%. Answer 2: PAE₂ = (3.0−0.25)/4.2 = 2.75/4.2 = 65.5%)*
