**1. Custom AI ASICs Capture 27.8% of AI Server Market — Broadcom Guides to $100B by 2027**

Broadcom reported $8.4 billion in AI semiconductor revenue for Q1 FY2026 (ending Feb 2026), a 106% year-over-year increase, with Q2 guidance at $10.7 billion and a disclosed $73 billion AI backlog. CEO Hock Tan stated the company has "line of sight to achieve AI revenue from chips in excess of $100 billion in 2027," underpinned by co-design programs with Google (TPUs), Meta (MTIA), and a new ~$11 billion order from Anthropic. Custom ASIC shipments are growing at 44.6% YoY vs. 16.1% for merchant GPUs; Broadcom and Marvell together now control ~95% of the hyperscaler ASIC co-design market.

> **Why it matters:** The ASIC architecture co-designed for one specific workload (transformer inference, retrieval, etc.) can achieve 3–10× better TOPS/W than a general-purpose GPU. For EEs: the co-design requirement means HBM memory interfaces (currently HBM3E, ~1.2 TB/s per stack), SerDes at 112–224 Gbaud, and co-packaged optics (CPO) are no longer "future tech" — they're the table stakes for competing in this market. The 2.5D/3D packaging stack (CoWoS, SoIC) and power delivery rail integrity at >100 kW/rack are the key engineering challenges right now.

**2. imec + Ghent: World's First Thin-Film LiNbO₃ Transfer-Printed onto Silicon Photonics — 70 GHz, 320 Gb/s**

imec and Ghent University demonstrated the world's first transfer-printing of thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) modulators onto a standard silicon photonics platform via micro-transfer printing (μTP). The resulting O-band modulator achieves 70 GHz electro-optic bandwidth and demonstrates a 320 Gb/s unamplified optical link over 2 km of standard single-mode fiber. In a simultaneous double world-first, the team also transfer-printed a lithium tantalate (LiTaO₃) modulator — a first for that material on any silicon PIC platform. The approach bypasses the expensive wafer-bonding process where ~95% of the LiNbO₃ crystal is wasted during substrate removal.

> **Why it matters:** Silicon Mach-Zehnder modulators (Si-MZMs) are limited to ~50 GHz bandwidth due to the plasma dispersion effect and carrier lifetime in silicon. TFLN achieves ~70+ GHz via the linear Pockels effect (no free carriers, no chirp, lower Vπ·L). Micro-transfer printing achieves this without the cost and yield penalty of full-wafer bonding. Path to >400 Gb/s per lane is now clearer, directly relevant to [[Silicon Photonics]] for AI data center interconnects and future [[6G]] front-haul.

**3. China's Tiancheng Announces 14-Inch SiC Crystal — 12→14-Inch Commercialization Phase Begins**

Tiancheng Semiconductor (China) announced successful growth of a 14-inch silicon carbide (SiC) single crystal with 30 mm effective thickness using proprietary Physical Vapor Transport (PVT) equipment. This marks the transition from China's "12-inch adoption phase" to the initial stage of 14-inch commercialization. The announcement is significant because global SiC leaders (Wolfspeed, Coherent, SiCrystal) have been working on 150 mm (6-inch) → 200 mm (8-inch) transitions for volume production; a 14-inch (350 mm) boule represents a significantly different wafer-cost curve.

> **Why it matters:** SiC wafer diameter directly drives die cost: a 200 mm wafer yields ~3.4× more die than a 150 mm wafer at the same die size. For 800V EV traction inverters using ~0.5 cm² SiC MOSFETs and onboard chargers, die cost is still the primary cost driver. The 14-inch growth requires solving macrostep bunching, basal plane dislocations (BPDs), and thermal gradient control at much larger scales. If Tiancheng achieves production quality, it shifts the SiC supply chain geopolitics significantly. Track: [[Silicon Carbide Power Electronics]], [[Wide Bandgap Semiconductors]].

**4. 2D Van der Waals Molecular Ferroelectric Shows Robust Ferroelectricity at 9 nm Thickness**

Two papers published in *Advanced Materials* in 2026 mark milestones in [[2D Ferroelectrics]]: (1) The first 2D van der Waals molecular ferroelectric is reported — coordination-bonded molecule-based monolayers held by weak vdW forces show robust room-temperature ferroelectricity in mechanically exfoliated flakes as thin as 9 nm, the first molecular 2D ferroelectric below 10 nm. (2) Brain-inspired CuInP₂S₆ (CIPS) 2D ferroelectric materials are demonstrated for neuromorphic computing, exploiting the voltage-controllable polarization switching for analog weight storage.

> **Why it matters:** Conventional hafnium zirconium oxide (HZO) ferroelectrics for FeFET applications lose reliable switching below ~5–7 nm due to surface depolarization. A vdW-bonded 2D molecular ferroelectric can scale to sub-10 nm while retaining Ec ~10–50 MV/cm switching fields. For neuromorphic and logic-in-memory applications, the ability to encode multi-level analog states in a ferroelectric layer as thin as 9 nm opens post-CMOS integration paths. Also: ferroelectricity + superconductivity coexisting in MoTe₂ suggests reconfigurable qubit primitives — a connection point to [[Quantum Computing]] hardware.

## 🔬 Daily Research Topic: Thin-Film Lithium Niobate (TFLN) Electro-Optic Modulators

**What it is:** LiNbO₃ is a ferroelectric crystal with a large Pockels coefficient (r₃₃ ≈ 30 pm/V). By thinning single-crystal LiNbO₃ to 200–600 nm and forming a ridge waveguide, the optical mode overlaps strongly with the applied RF electric field — enabling electro-optic modulation via index change Δn = −½ n³ r₃₃ E with no free-carrier injection and no chirp.

**Key technical points:**
- **Bandwidth physics:** The RC bandwidth limit of Si-MZMs is ~50 GHz (carrier lifetime ~ns). TFLN Mach-Zehnder modulators achieve >100 GHz bandwidth because the Pockels effect is instantaneous (~femtoseconds); the limit is the velocity mismatch between the RF traveling wave and the optical wave (group indices nRF ~ 2.1 vs. n_opt ~ 2.25 at telecom wavelengths)
- **Vπ·L product:** TFLN achieves Vπ·L ≈ 2–4 V·cm vs. 15–20 V·cm for Si-MZM, reducing the required drive voltage from ~3 V to ~0.5 V at 1 cm device length — critical for direct CMOS driver integration
- **Integration challenge:** Native LiNbO₃ substrates can't be processed in standard CMOS fabs. Micro-transfer printing (imec's approach) and wafer bonding (Luxtera, Luminous) both transfer pre-processed TFLN onto SiO₂/Si carriers post-fab
- **Polarization state:** TFLN modulators typically use the TE mode (E-field in-plane) for maximum r₃₃ overlap; TM mode uses r₁₃ ≈ 8 pm/V (lower). Waveguide cross-section AR (aspect ratio) determines TE/TM confinement
- **2026 state of the art:** Demonstrations at 1.6 Tb/s single-carrier (8-channel WDM at 200 Gb/s/ch), O-band links at 320 Gb/s/λ unamplified over 2 km (imec/Ghent), LiTaO₃ as a CMOS-compatible alternative (less reactive to hydrogen annealing)

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore search terms: `"thin-film lithium niobate" modulator bandwidth 2025 2026`, `"TFLN" electro-optic Pockels integration silicon`
- Key papers: Wang et al. *Nature* 2018 (100 GHz TFLN first demonstration); Xu et al. *Nature* 2020 (1 Tb/s TFLN comb); imec 2026 (μTP integration)
- [[Silicon Photonics]], [[Electro-Optic Modulator]], [[Photonic Integrated Circuit]]
- Textbook: Saleh & Teich *Fundamentals of Photonics* Ch. 18–19 (electro-optics and acousto-optics)

**Calculation to work through:**
A TFLN MZM has Vπ·L = 2.5 V·cm at λ = 1550 nm. If the device length is L = 5 mm, what is the required half-wave voltage Vπ? Now: the modulator is driven by a 50 Ω source with 1 V amplitude (200 mW). What phase shift Δφ is produced? Express in terms of π. (Use Δφ = π × (V/Vπ) at quadrature.)
