# EE Daily — June 05, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. NVIDIA RTX Spark Superchip Enters PC Market — 1 Petaflop on ARM**

NVIDIA's RTX Spark superchip combines a 20-core ARM-based Grace CPU with a Blackwell RTX GPU on a single SoC, delivering up to 1 petaflop of AI performance (INT8). Microsoft's Surface Laptop Ultra ships with 128 GB of unified LPDDR5X RAM. Dell, Lenovo, and HP are also adopting the platform this fall. The chip runs a 4 nm process node and targets the same workloads historically confined to cloud inference — local diffusion, RAG, and agent pipelines.

> **Why it matters:** This is NVIDIA's first direct move into the CPU-GPU integrated SoC space, directly challenging Apple Silicon's dominance in edge AI compute density. For EEs: the Grace-Blackwell NVLink-C2C interconnect at 900 GB/s eliminates the PCIe bandwidth bottleneck that has constrained discrete GPU designs. Pay attention to the power envelope — early reports put TDP at 60–80 W for the laptop variant, squeezing the thermal budget significantly.

**2. 14-Inch SiC Single Crystal Commercialization Begins — Tiancheng Semiconductor**

Tiancheng Semiconductor (China) announced successful development and early-stage commercialization of 14-inch silicon carbide single crystal boules, marking a transition from China's 12-inch SiC adoption phase. The 14-inch format increases usable die area by ~36% vs. 12-inch, targeting cost reduction in SiC MOSFET and Schottky diode production for EV inverters and industrial power supplies. The move follows global pressure to reduce SiC device costs below $0.10/A at 1200 V.

> **Why it matters:** Larger SiC substrates directly reduce $/die. The dominant barrier to [[SiC Power Devices]] adoption in consumer EVs is cost, not performance — a 36% area gain per boule meaningfully shifts economics. Watch for implications on Wolfspeed and STMicroelectronics pricing: Chinese substrate suppliers at 14-inch will accelerate the commodity transition of SiC wafers. The next bottleneck becomes epitaxial layer growth uniformity at scale.

**3. Light-Powered Photonic Chip Demonstrated for AI and Quantum Acceleration**

Researchers (ScienceDaily, June 1, 2026) demonstrated a new light-powered chip architecture that performs matrix multiplication using photons rather than electrons, targeting both AI inference and quantum state manipulation. The chip operates at room temperature — a critical advantage over cryogenic quantum systems. The approach leverages interference patterns in silicon waveguides to perform analog multiply-accumulate operations at the speed of light with sub-pJ/MAC energy consumption.

> **Why it matters:** Digital matrix multiply is the bottleneck for transformer inference. Photonic matrix multipliers offer O(1) energy per operation regardless of matrix size and nanosecond latency. The room-temperature operation removes the $10M+ dilution refrigerator cost barrier. For EE research focus: study [[Silicon Photonics]] modulator linearity, photodetector shot noise floor, and analog-to-digital conversion as the key bottlenecks that must be solved before this replaces GPU clusters.

**4. 800V EV Power Architecture Migrating to AI Data Centers**

IDTechEx (June 3, 2026) published analysis showing that 800 V bus topologies pioneered in EV powertrains — using SiC MOSFETs for main inverter stages — are being directly adapted for 800 VDC AI data center power distribution. The architecture eliminates one transformer stage vs. 48 V bus designs, cuts copper losses, and enables >98% efficiency from utility input to GPU VRM. Data center power density is projected to double by 2028, making the higher-voltage bus essential.

> **Why it matters:** This is the convergence of EV and data center power electronics that [[800V EV Architecture]] predicts. SiC switching at 800 V and 100+ kHz enables smaller magnetics and higher power density. The EE opportunity: GaN-based totem-pole PFC at 800 V input → SiC LLC resonant converter → 48 V bus → GaN VRMs for GPU rails. Understanding this full power conversion stack is becoming a core competency for data center power engineers.

**5. Ga₂O₃ UWBG HEMT Research Advances High-Voltage Power Switching**

Published research (PMC, June 2026) demonstrates gallium oxide (β-Ga₂O₃) HEMT (High Electron Mobility Transistor) devices achieving breakdown voltages in the 3–8 kV range, exploiting Ga₂O₃'s 4.8 eV bandgap and theoretical critical field of 8 MV/cm — approximately 4× that of SiC. The 2DEG density in Ga₂O₃-based heterojunctions is being engineered through MOCVD growth of (AlGa)₂O₃/Ga₂O₃ interface structures. Current on-resistance remains the primary challenge: RDS(on) × A figures are still 3–5× worse than SiC at equivalent voltages.

> **Why it matters:** If on-resistance can be closed, Ga₂O₃ offers a Baliga Figure of Merit (BFOM) of ~3,400 MW·cm⁻² vs. ~340 for SiC and ~160 for GaN — a 10× potential advantage for ultra-high-voltage switching (3–15 kV) in grid-scale power electronics and railway traction. This is today's device-physics research frontier, not a commercial product — but HEMT progress is moving faster than expected.

**6. PsiQuantum Omega Silicon Photonics Platform Targets Million-Qubit Scale**

PsiQuantum published Nature details on its "Omega" quantum photonic chipset, fabricated on a 300 mm GlobalFoundries silicon photonics line. The architecture uses photonic qubits encoded in single-photon polarization states, with error-corrected logical qubits implemented via fusion-based quantum computing. The process node allows integration of ~10⁶ photonic components per chip. No operational quantum advantage demonstrated yet — the paper focuses on fabrication yield and component-level fidelity metrics.

> **Why it matters:** Fabricating quantum hardware on a commercial 300 mm CMOS-compatible silicon photonics process is the key scalability move. Traditional superconducting qubit approaches require dilution refrigerators; photonic qubits operate at room temperature. The EE connection: photonic qubit generation and detection requires low-jitter SPAD (single-photon avalanche diode) arrays, which are tightly coupled silicon photonics + standard CMOS design problems.

## 🔬 Daily Research Topic: Ultra-Wide Bandgap (UWBG) Semiconductors — Ga₂O₃ Power Device Design

**What it is:** β-Ga₂O₃ is the leading UWBG semiconductor candidate for next-generation high-voltage power switching. Its 4.8 eV bandgap enables critical electric field strengths of ~8 MV/cm, versus 3.5 MV/cm for SiC and 3.3 MV/cm for GaN. At these field strengths, extremely thin drift layers can support kilovolt-scale breakdown voltages, theoretically enabling ultra-compact, low-loss power switches for 3–15 kV applications like grid inverters, railway traction, and high-voltage motor drives.

**Technical deep-dive:**

- **Baliga Figure of Merit (BFOM):** BFOM = εμE_c³. For Ga₂O₃: ε ≈ 10ε₀, μ_n ≈ 200 cm²/Vs, E_c ≈ 8 MV/cm → BFOM ≈ 3,400 MW·cm⁻². Compare: GaN ~160, SiC ~340, Si ~1. This 10× advantage over SiC is the driving motivation for Ga₂O₃ research.
- **Current device types:** Fin-MESFETs, MOSFETs, and HEMTs on (AlGa)₂O₃/Ga₂O₃ heterojunctions. MOCVD and MBE growth on native Ga₂O₃ substrates. Native substrates are available (unlike GaN) from Tamura Corp. and Novel Crystal Technology.
- **Key limitation — thermal conductivity:** Ga₂O₃ has κ ≈ 0.1–0.2 W/(cm·K), approximately 10× lower than SiC (5 W/cm·K). Self-heating under high current density is the dominant reliability challenge. Current research focuses on diamond or SiC heat-spreading substrates bonded to the active layer.
- **Ferroelectricity in ε-Ga₂O₃:** The orthorhombic ε-phase exhibits spontaneous polarization (~6 µC/cm²), enabling gate-stack engineering for non-volatile memory applications — an unexpected multifunctionality in a power semiconductor.
- **Research-stage metrics (2026):** Best reported I_DS,max ≈ 500 mA/mm for HEMTs; V_BR > 8 kV for vertical MOSFETs on native substrates; specific RDS(on) ≈ 2–5 mΩ·cm² (vs. SiC targets of <1 mΩ·cm²).

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore search terms: `"gallium oxide" power device MOSFET breakdown 2025 OR 2026`, `"beta Ga2O3" HEMT 2DEG`
- Key authors: Masataka Higashiwaki (NICT, Japan), Uttam Singisetti (SUNY Buffalo), Avinash Patanè (Nottingham)
- [[UWBG Advanced Materials]] and [[SiC Power Devices]] wikilinks for context
- Review: *Advances in High-Voltage Power Electronics Using Ga₂O₃-Based HEMT* (PMC 2026)

**Concrete calculation to work through:**

A Ga₂O₃ vertical MOSFET must support 3.3 kV in off-state (same as a standard SiC module). Assuming one-sided abrupt junction with E_c = 8 MV/cm and N_D = 1 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ in the drift region:

1. Use V_BR = (ε × E_c²) / (2qN_D) to find the drift layer thickness W required for 3.3 kV
2. Compare W vs. a SiC device at the same N_D and E_c = 3.5 MV/cm — which is thinner?
3. Calculate specific on-resistance R_on,sp = W / (q × µ_n × N_D) for each — what's the ratio?

*Answer check: Ga₂O₃ drift layer should be ~2× thinner than SiC for same breakdown, but higher mobility offset partially by lower µ — net R_on,sp advantage depends on which term dominates.*
