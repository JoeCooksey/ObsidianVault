# EE Daily — June 18, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. Intel 18A-P Enters Risk Production — 9% Perf Gain, Drop-In Upgrade from 18A**
Intel announced at the 2026 IEEE/JSAP Symposium on VLSI Technology & Circuits that its 18A-P process node has entered risk production. The performance-optimized variant delivers a 9% clock speed improvement at iso-power, or 18% power reduction at iso-frequency versus standard 18A. Critically, 18A-P uses identical design rules to 18A, making it a drop-in upgrade with no mask or layout rework. Thermal conductivity improved 50% and thermal resistance dropped 20-40% through materials and design innovations. Xeon "Diamond Rapids" is the first confirmed product, slated for 2027.

> **Why it matters:** Same-design-rule process upgrades are rare and extremely valuable for foundry customers — they eliminate the NRE cost of a full respin while delivering meaningful PPA gains. The 18% power reduction at iso-frequency directly impacts server and edge inference power budgets. The thermal improvements address the long-standing criticism of Intel's [[RibbonFET]] GAA transistors running hot relative to TSMC N2. If Intel can hold this timeline, 18A-P becomes a credible alternative to TSMC A16 for high-performance compute — the first real competitive node from Intel Foundry in years.

**2. NVIDIA and TSMC Deploy AI Across Semiconductor Fabs — cuLitho Cuts Lithography Costs Up to 50%**
Announced at GTC Taipei (June 1, 2026), NVIDIA's CUDA-X libraries and cuLitho computational lithography platform are now deployed in TSMC production fabs. cuLitho delivers 20-50% cost or cycle-time improvement over CPU-based computational lithography by accelerating inverse lithography technology (ILT) mask optimization on GPU clusters. TSMC is also using NVIDIA Metropolis and TAO Toolkit for automated defect inspection with vision AI, detecting nanometer-scale defects while reducing labeling and retraining overhead.

> **Why it matters:** Computational lithography is the bottleneck for sub-2nm mask generation — a single EUV layer can require >100 hours of CPU compute for full-chip ILT optimization. GPU acceleration doesn't just save cost; it changes what's computationally feasible. Full-chip curvilinear mask optimization, previously impractical, becomes routine. For process engineers, this means tighter OPC margins and better pattern fidelity at the 18A/A16 nodes. The defect inspection piece is equally important — as feature sizes shrink below 10 nm, human-labeled defect datasets become unreliable, and AI-based classification becomes the only viable path.

**3. QuiX Quantum Installs 150 ns Feed-Forward Control Unit for Photonic Quantum Computing**
QuiX Quantum installed its Feed-Forward Control Unit (FFCU) on June 4, 2026 — a hardware component that converts single-photon measurement signals into control actions on photonic integrated circuits with a deterministic latency of approximately 150 nanoseconds. The FFCU enables real-time adaptive operations within QuiX's universal photonic quantum computing stack, automating hardware reconfigurations that were previously manual. QuiX is delivering a complete universal photonic quantum computer to the German Aerospace Center's Quantum Computing Initiative (DLR QCI) in Ulm.

> **Why it matters:** Feed-forward is the critical missing piece for [[measurement-based quantum computing]] on photonic platforms. Unlike superconducting qubits where gate operations are deterministic, linear-optical quantum computing relies on probabilistic photon-photon interactions (via the KLM protocol) that require mid-circuit measurement and conditional gate application. The 150 ns latency sets the control bandwidth at ~6.7 MHz — tight for photon generation rates in the tens of MHz range, but sufficient for current source brightness. This is the first commercially deployed FFCU for photonic QC and moves the architecture from proof-of-concept to engineering prototype.

**4. Novel Crystal Technology Ships 150 mm Beta-Ga2O3 Substrates — UWBG Power Devices Scale Up**
Novel Crystal Technology (Japan) began sample shipments of 150 mm (6-inch) beta-phase gallium oxide (beta-Ga2O3) substrates in March 2026, marking the transition from lab-scale to industrial wafer sizes. Separately, Nagoya University and spinout NU-Rei presented six advances in Ga2O3 epitaxial growth, centered on a new High-Density Oxygen Radical Source (HD-ORS) that doubles atomic oxygen density during thin-film growth compared to conventional RF plasma sources. Beta-Ga2O3 has a bandgap of ~4.9 eV and a Baliga figure of merit (BFOM) of ~3,300 — 3-10x higher than [[SiC]] and [[GaN]].

> **Why it matters:** The substrate bottleneck has been the primary obstacle to Ga2O3 commercialization. At 150 mm, beta-Ga2O3 wafers can enter existing 150/200 mm fab lines without requiring new tooling. The BFOM advantage over SiC (BFOM ~340) means Ga2O3 devices can theoretically achieve the same blocking voltage with 10x lower on-resistance — or equivalently, the same R_on at 10x higher voltage. The HD-ORS advance addresses the growth-rate limitation: conventional Ga2O3 MBE growth rates are ~1 um/hr, insufficient for thick drift layers needed in >1 kV devices. Doubling the oxygen radical density could push growth rates toward the ~5 um/hr threshold needed for production-viable 10+ um drift layers.

**5. Imec Achieves First Thin-Film LiNbO3 Integration on Silicon Photonics via Micro-Transfer Printing**
Researchers at imec and Ghent University demonstrated the first heterogeneous integration of thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) modulators on imec's standard silicon photonics platform using micro-transfer printing. In parallel, they achieved the first-ever integration of lithium tantalate (LiTaO3) modulators on a silicon PIC. Micro-transfer printing enables selective die-level transfer of pre-fabricated LiNbO3 and LiTaO3 devices onto SiPh wafers, bypassing the costly wafer bonding approach that wastes most of the donor material and requires extensive post-bonding processing.

> **Why it matters:** LiNbO3 has an electro-optic coefficient (r33 ~31 pm/V) roughly 30x larger than silicon's plasma dispersion effect, enabling Mach-Zehnder modulators with V_pi * L products below 2 V*cm — meaning sub-1V drive voltages at practical device lengths. Current SiPh modulators require ~2V drive and suffer from free-carrier-induced optical loss. The micro-transfer printing approach is the breakthrough: it is CMOS-back-end compatible, uses <5% of the donor LiNbO3 material (vs. >95% waste in bonding), and can place devices selectively — only where needed on the SiPh die. This opens the path to co-integrated Si/LiNbO3 transceivers for 800G and 1.6T [[data center interconnects]].

**6. Quandela Selected for DARPA Quantum Benchmarking — Hybrid Spin-Optical Architecture**
Quandela was selected on June 16, 2026 for Stage A of DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (QBI) to assess its Spin-Optical Quantum Computing (SPOQC) architecture. SPOQC merges linear-optical photonic qubits with semiconductor spin qubits, using photons for long-range entanglement distribution and spin qubits for local deterministic gates. The hybrid approach aims to reduce the optical component count by orders of magnitude compared to pure linear-optical schemes while enabling error correction via non-local optical links between spin-qubit nodes.

> **Why it matters:** Pure linear-optical QC (the KLM/fusion approach) has a resource overhead problem: generating a single logical qubit requires thousands of single-photon sources and detectors due to the probabilistic nature of photon-photon gates. SPOQC sidesteps this by using deterministic spin-qubit gates locally and only using photons for inter-node entanglement — similar to the distributed QC architecture proposed by Monroe and others for trapped ions. DARPA's QBI program is the U.S. government's attempt to impose hardware-agnostic benchmarks on quantum platforms; selection signals that the hybrid photonic-spin approach is taken seriously alongside superconducting, trapped-ion, and neutral-atom modalities.

**7. 2D Ferroelectric Hybrids for Post-Moore Multifunctional Devices (Advanced Materials 2026)**
A comprehensive study published in *Advanced Materials* (2026) proposes hybrid systems combining ferroelectric materials, 2D semiconductors, and functional molecular switches as a platform for simultaneous high integration density and multifunctional performance in the post-Moore era. Separately, researchers demonstrated electrical control of magnetism in a van der Waals heterostructure combining 2D ferroelectric and ferromagnetic layers, achieving non-volatile, reversible interferroic magnetoelectric coupling at room temperature.

> **Why it matters:** The ferroelectric/2D semiconductor interface is where three scaling crises converge: transistor scaling (FeFET), memory scaling (FeRAM/FTJ), and interconnect scaling (magnetoelectric spin-orbit logic). The molecular switch layer adds a fourth degree of freedom — optically or chemically switchable states that can modulate the ferroelectric polarization in situ. The magnetoelectric coupling demonstration is particularly significant: non-volatile electrical control of magnetism without current flow eliminates the Joule heating problem in [[MRAM]] write operations. If the switching energy can reach the ~1 aJ/bit regime, this could enable beyond-CMOS logic architectures based on [[magnetoelectric spin-orbit]] (MESO) devices.

## Daily Research Topic: Thin-Film Lithium Niobate (TFLN) Photonic Integration

**What it is:** Thin-film lithium niobate on insulator (LNOI) has emerged as a transformative material platform for integrated photonics, offering high electro-optic modulation efficiency, low optical loss, and broadband transparency from 400 nm to 5 um. The recent imec demonstration of micro-transfer-printed TFLN on silicon photonics marks a turning point: TFLN devices can now be co-integrated with mature SiPh ecosystems without abandoning existing foundry infrastructure. Understanding the material physics, device design, and integration trade-offs is essential for any EE working on optical interconnects, RF photonics, or quantum photonic circuits.

**5 Technical Bullets:**

- **Electro-Optic Effect in LiNbO3:** The Pockels effect in LiNbO3 provides a linear change in refractive index with applied electric field: Delta_n = -(1/2) * n_e^3 * r33 * E, where n_e = 2.14 (extraordinary index at 1550 nm), r33 = 31 pm/V. For a 1 um electrode gap, an applied voltage of 1V produces E = 10^6 V/m and Delta_n ~ 1.5 x 10^-4. This is ~30x larger than the plasma dispersion effect in silicon at equivalent drive voltage, and critically, it is a pure phase modulation — no excess optical loss, unlike silicon carrier-depletion modulators.

- **TFLN Waveguide Loss and Fabrication:** State-of-the-art TFLN ridge waveguides achieve propagation losses of 0.03-0.05 dB/cm at 1550 nm — approaching the material absorption limit. Fabrication uses Ar+ ion milling of 300-600 nm thick LiNbO3 films bonded to SiO2/Si substrates. The etch sidewall angle (~60-70 degrees) and roughness (~1 nm RMS) are critical for low-loss single-mode operation. Film thickness uniformity across a wafer must be held to +/-5 nm for consistent V_pi performance.

- **Modulator Architectures — MZM and Ring:** TFLN Mach-Zehnder modulators have demonstrated V_pi * L < 2 V*cm with 3 dB bandwidths exceeding 100 GHz — sufficient for 200+ GBaud PAM-4 signaling. Microring modulators offer smaller footprint (~50 um radius) but are limited by the photon lifetime-bandwidth trade-off: a ring with Q = 10^5 at 1550 nm has a photon lifetime of ~80 ps, limiting the electro-optic bandwidth to ~2 GHz. Traveling-wave electrode design on MZMs with velocity-matched CPW transmission lines is the preferred approach for >100 GHz operation.

- **Integration Approaches — Bonding vs. Transfer Printing vs. Monolithic:** Wafer bonding (imec/UCSB approach pre-2026) wastes >95% of the LiNbO3 donor wafer and requires post-bonding thinning and processing. Micro-transfer printing (imec 2026 breakthrough) transfers pre-fabricated TFLN devices selectively onto SiPh wafers — <5% material waste, back-end compatible, and allows heterogeneous co-integration with III-V lasers and Ge photodetectors on the same SiPh die. Monolithic growth of LiNbO3 on Si remains impractical due to lattice and thermal expansion mismatch.

- **Applications Beyond Telecom:** TFLN's transparency window (400 nm - 5 um) and high nonlinear coefficient (d33 ~ 27 pm/V) make it ideal for quantum photonic circuits (spontaneous parametric down-conversion for entangled photon pair generation), microwave photonics (broadband analog optical links with SFDR > 120 dB*Hz^(2/3)), and frequency comb generation via chi-2 and chi-3 cascaded nonlinearities in dispersion-engineered microring resonators.

- imec SiPh platform documentation and TFLN integration white paper (2026)

**Concrete Calculation to Work Through:**
*Calculate the V_pi of a TFLN Mach-Zehnder modulator:*
- Pockels coefficient: r33 = 31 pm/V = 31 x 10^-12 m/V
- Extraordinary refractive index: n_e = 2.14 at 1550 nm
- Electrode gap: g = 4 um (typical for CPW on TFLN)
- Modulator arm length: L = 10 mm
- Overlap integral (field-optical mode): Gamma ~ 0.5
- V_pi = (lambda * g) / (n_e^3 * r33 * Gamma * L)
- V_pi = (1.55 x 10^-6 * 4 x 10^-6) / (2.14^3 * 31 x 10^-12 * 0.5 * 10 x 10^-3)
- V_pi = 6.2 x 10^-12 / (9.80 * 31 x 10^-12 * 5 x 10^-3)
- V_pi = 6.2 x 10^-12 / (1.519 x 10^-12)
- V_pi ~ **4.1 V** (for 10 mm length, 4 um gap)
- V_pi * L = 4.1 V * 10 mm = **4.1 V*cm** — reduce gap to 2 um or increase Gamma with optimized waveguide geometry to reach the <2 V*cm state of the art.
