**1. SpaceX Rings the Nasdaq Opening Bell in the Largest IPO in History — Terafab Implications**

SpaceX (ticker: SPCX) began trading today at a $1.75 trillion valuation after raising $75 billion — the largest IPO in market history. Shares priced at $135 and opened at approximately $175, up 30%. Elon Musk rang the opening bell virtually from Starbase, Texas. The EE angle: a portion of IPO proceeds is expected to fund the first phase of Terafab, the $55–119B SpaceX/Tesla/xAI joint semiconductor fab that would be ASML's largest customer — ASML surged 5% today on the news.

> **Why it matters:** Terafab represents a step-change in EUV lithography demand. ASML can currently ship ~60 High-NA EUV systems per year; Terafab alone could absorb a significant fraction. If capitalized, this creates a multi-year EUV supply bottleneck and pushes ASML's 2030 revenue guidance toward the top of analyst ranges. Watch ASML quarterly order intake as the leading indicator.

**2. NVIDIA cuLitho + TSMC: AI-Enhanced Semiconductor Fabrication Achieves 20–50% Efficiency Gain**

NVIDIA and TSMC have expanded their AI-in-fab partnership to cover computational lithography (cuLitho on NVIDIA GPUs), defect inspection, and scheduling optimization across TSMC's advanced node fabs. cuLitho delivers 20–50% improvement in cost effectiveness or cycle time versus CPU-based OPC (optical proximity correction) workflows. TSMC reported 30% year-on-year revenue growth in May 2026 and guided for continued >30% growth, with 15% price increases on 3nm process planned for H2 2026.

> **Why it matters:** Computational lithography (OPC, SMO — source mask optimization) is one of the most compute-intensive steps in chip design. GPU-accelerated OPC at production scale means EUV mask generation cycles that previously took days can run overnight. This is a direct path to faster tape-out → higher wafer throughput at the same fab capacity, compressing the time from tapeout to validated silicon.

**3. Sub-10nm 2D Molecular Ferroelectric Demonstrated — van der Waals Limits of Ferroelectricity**

Published in *Advanced Materials* (2026), researchers demonstrated robust ferroelectricity in the molecular compound 2,2,3,3-TFPS at sub-10nm thickness — the first molecular 2D van der Waals ferroelectric to show switchable polarization at this scale. Separately, 2D vdW ferroelectric/antiferroelectric materials (CuInP₂S₆, In₂Se₃, Fe₂Mo₃O₈) are emerging as a platform for neuromorphic memory and logic-in-memory architectures, offering switchable polarization states with atomic-scale thickness control.

> **Why it matters:** Conventional HfO₂-based FeFETs hit scaling limits around 5–7nm due to depolarization fields and grain-boundary leakage. 2D ferroelectrics decouple thickness from ferroelectric stability — polarization persists at 1–2 unit cell thickness where bulk films would go paraelectric. This opens a path to sub-1nm equivalent oxide thickness (EOT) FeFETs, in-memory compute at the transistor level, and neuromorphic devices that store analog weights without DRAM's volatility or Flash's wear.

**4. PCIM Expo 2026: SiC and GaN Define the Data Center Power Architecture**

PCIM Europe 2026 highlighted the bifurcation between SiC and GaN in the data center power stack: 800VDC bus architectures with SiC in the AC/DC front-end (1200V class, double-sided cooling modules) and GaN in the DC/DC final-stage regulation (48V→12V, 10–100A, GaN FETs with switching frequencies >1 MHz). Wolfspeed and STMicro presented 1200V SiC high-temperature module platforms; GaN device scaling toward 650V for telecom rack power is progressing. IDTechEx forecasts GaN power electronics market growth as SiC/GaN application areas increasingly overlap.

> **Why it matters:** The 800V data center bus is the key architectural decision for AI GPU clusters — it cuts I²R losses by ~4× versus 48V, enabling higher density racks without liquid cooling or heavier copper busbars. EEs designing power systems for AI infrastructure need to understand the full topology: PFC rectifier (SiC) → 800V bus → LLC/CLLC resonant converter (SiC or GaN) → point-of-load (GaN). Each stage's frequency, efficiency, and thermal constraints are distinct.

**5. ZnBr₂ Flow Battery: Sodium Sulfamate as Bromine Scavenger Boosts Energy Density and Cycle Life**

Researchers demonstrated that sodium sulfamate (Na₂SO₃NH₂) as a bromine complexation agent in zinc-bromine (ZnBr₂) flow batteries reduces free corrosive Br₂ levels, improving energy density, cycle life, and corrosion resistance for large-scale stationary energy storage. ZnBr₂ flow batteries have theoretical energy density ~67 Wh/kg (2× vanadium redox), but free bromine corrodes components and creates safety risks.

> **Why it matters:** Flow batteries are the leading candidate for 4–12 hour grid storage (wind/solar shifting), where lithium-ion's cost/cycle-life becomes impractical. Bromine scavenging is the key chemistry bottleneck for ZnBr₂ commercialization — solving it would put a low-cost, non-flammable, earth-abundant battery in direct competition with VRFB (vanadium redox). For EEs designing grid-tied power electronics, flow battery interfaces require bidirectional converters with wide-input-range MPPT-like control.

**6. Memristor Platform Enables Environment-Driven Autonomous Neuromorphic Computation**

A new memristor-based platform demonstrates how environmental input signals (temperature, humidity, chemical gradients) can directly drive analog computation without a host CPU, pointing toward autonomous, ultralow-power neuromorphic sensor-compute systems. The platform exploits memristive state-dependent conductance modulation (STDP-like plasticity) for in-situ pattern recognition.

> **Why it matters:** Current edge AI requires a microcontroller or DSP to sample sensors, run inference, and actuate — each step burning power. A true neuromorphic approach collapses sense→compute into the same device, enabling always-on monitoring at nanowatt budgets. Relevant for implantables, structural health monitors, and any IoT application where battery replacement is infeasible.

## 🎯 Daily Research Topic: Ferroelectric Field-Effect Transistors (FeFETs) — Polarization Physics and Embedded NVM Design

**What it is:** A FeFET replaces the SiO₂ gate dielectric of a conventional MOSFET with a ferroelectric layer (typically HfO₂ doped with Zr, Si, or Y — called HZO). The remnant polarization of the ferroelectric modulates the channel threshold voltage (VT), creating a non-volatile memory state without a floating gate. FeFETs are the leading candidate for embedded NVM in logic processes (MCUs, AI edge chips) because HZO integrates natively into CMOS at <1nm EOT-equivalent.

**5 Technical Bullets:**

- **Polarization switching:** HZO in the orthorhombic phase exhibits remnant polarization Pr ≈ 10–30 µC/cm² and coercive field Ec ≈ 1–2 MV/cm. Applying a gate pulse V_write = Ec × d_FE switches Pr direction, shifting VT by ΔVT = 2Pr/Cox — for Cox = 20 nF/cm² and Pr = 15 µC/cm², ΔVT ≈ 1.5V (adequate for 2-bit multi-level cell).

- **Depolarization field:** The semiconductor channel creates a depolarization field Edep = −Pr/(ε₀ε_FE) × (d_semiconductor/d_FE) that opposes the stored polarization state. This is the dominant retention failure mechanism — the field drives the ferroelectric back toward zero polarization. Minimizing d_semiconductor (thin body FETs: FDSOI, nanosheets) and maximizing d_FE reduces Edep.

- **Wake-up and fatigue:** Fresh HZO is often pinched (paraelectric-like). Cycling 10³–10⁴ write pulses activates the full polarization window (wake-up) by redistributing oxygen vacancies. Beyond ~10⁸–10⁹ cycles, polarization degrades (fatigue). This bi-phasic behavior requires endurance characterization distinct from charge-trap Flash.

- **2D ferroelectrics advantage:** CuInP₂S₆ (~4 µC/cm², in-plane switching), In₂Se₃ (switchable out-of-plane, room temperature), and Fe₂Mo₃O₈ (230 µC/cm² — one of the highest reported in 2D) overcome HZO's grain-boundary and thickness-scaling limits. Sub-5nm 2D FeFETs are fabricated directly on h-BN substrates with near-ideal subthreshold swing.

- **Applications in AI edge:** FeFETs enable in-SRAM compute (FeSRAM), non-volatile LUT replacement in FPGAs, and synaptic weight storage for on-chip ML inference — eliminating off-chip DRAM latency and energy. Intel's FeMFET (via GlobalFoundries) and TSMC's FeFET research programs are the leading industrial efforts.

**Where to Start:**
- IEEE Xplore: `"hafnium oxide ferroelectric MOSFET" AND "retention"`, `"HZO FeFET NVM 2025 OR 2026"`, `"2D ferroelectric FET CuInP2S6"`
- Wikilinks: [[Ferroelectric Memory]], [[Non-Volatile Memory]], [[2D Materials for Electronics]], [[Neuromorphic Computing]]
- Start with: Böscke et al. 2011 (HfO₂ ferroelectricity discovery, foundational), then search for 2024–2026 IEEE IEDM and Symposium on VLSI proceedings for state-of-the-art.

**Calculation to Work Through:**
Given a FeFET with HZO ferroelectric layer d_FE = 10 nm, remnant polarization Pr = 15 µC/cm², permittivity ε_FE = 30, and gate oxide capacitance Cox = 20 nF/cm²:
1. Calculate the memory window ΔVT = 2Pr/Cox
2. Calculate the depolarization field Edep = Pr/(ε₀ × ε_FE) in MV/cm
3. For a 10-year retention target, which parameter — increasing Pr or decreasing Edep — gives more leverage? (Hint: consider how each scales with film thickness.)
