## Top Stories

**1. NVIDIA RTX Spark Superchip Launches PC Market Disruption at Computex**

NVIDIA unveiled the RTX Spark "superchip" at Computex 2026 in Taipei — an ARM-based SoC combining a 20-core Grace CPU (N1X/N1 ISA) with a Blackwell RTX GPU in a single package via NVLink-C2C interconnect at 900 GB/s bandwidth. The chip delivers up to 1 petaflop of AI performance and will debut in laptops from Microsoft (Surface Laptop Ultra, 128 GB unified memory), Dell, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, and MSI starting fall 2026. Intel stock fell on the announcement; Qualcomm countered with the Snapdragon C platform targeting sub-$600 Windows AI laptops.

> **Why it matters:** RTX Spark eliminates the PCIe bottleneck between CPU and GPU by integrating them on-package with NVLink-C2C — 900 GB/s vs. PCIe 5.0's ~128 GB/s. For EE engineers, the unified memory architecture (no discrete DRAM boundary between CPU and GPU address spaces) mirrors Apple M-series but adds discrete GPU-class compute. This architecture shifts edge AI inference from cloud-dependent to truly on-device — 1 PFLOP at sub-100W is a step-function change for embedded AI workloads.

**2. Broadcom AI Chip Q3 Outlook Misses Estimates; Stock Plunges 15%, Sector Follows**

Broadcom (AVGO) reported Q2 FY2026 revenue of $22.19 billion vs. $22.27 billion consensus; EPS of $2.44 (adj.) vs. $2.40 estimate. The critical miss: Q3 AI chip revenue guidance of $16.0 billion vs. analyst consensus of $17.2 billion, and CEO Hock Tan declined to raise the full-year $100 billion AI ASIC target. Shares fell 15% in after-hours on June 3, dragging Micron (−6%), Marvell (−5%), ARM Holdings (−6%), and the VanEck SMH semiconductor ETF (−3%).

> **Why it matters:** Broadcom's custom AI ASIC division (producing Google TPUv5, Meta MTIA, Apple Intelligence chips) is the benchmark for non-NVIDIA AI silicon. A $1.2B quarterly guidance shortfall — with maintained full-year targets rather than a raise — signals either hyperscaler capex timing shifts or demand absorption at 3nm capacity. The selloff hit broad-line fabless players regardless of NVDA exposure, confirming market sensitivity to AI demand forecasts at the ASIC level. Design-to-revenue cycles for custom ASICs run 18–36 months, so any miss traces to customer roadmap decisions made in 2023–2024.

**4. Valleytronics Chip Achieves Room-Temperature Valley Polarization**

Researchers published breakthrough valleytronics results in *ScienceDaily* (June 1, 2026): a chip exploiting the valley degree of freedom in 2D transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs — MoS₂/WSe₂) achieves stable valley polarization at room temperature (300 K). This eliminates the cryogenic requirement (typically <4 K) that has kept valley-based computing confined to physics labs, and opens a path to ultra-low-power logic, quantum memory, and light-based information processing compatible with CMOS fabrication.

> **Why it matters:** In 2D TMDs, electrons occupy two inequivalent valleys (K and K') in momentum space — a binary degree of freedom analogous to spin but more robust to thermal decoherence. Room-temperature polarization means the valley state survives for computation timescales without liquid helium cooling. This is a foundational prerequisite for [[Valleytronics]]-based logic-in-memory or photonic qubits on silicon-compatible platforms. The intersection with silicon photonics makes this especially relevant for optical interconnect engineers.

**5. Gallium Oxide ε(κ)-Phase Ferroelectric MOCVD Window Mapped**

A 2026 study (*Advanced Electronic Materials*, Kung et al.) identified the precise MOCVD growth window for phase-pure ε(κ)-Ga₂O₃ — an intrinsically ferroelectric polymorph of gallium oxide: temperature 560–590°C, pressure 7–23 Torr. This narrow stability window enables controlled growth of a ferroelectric UWBG material with a ~4.9 eV bandgap via industry-compatible deposition. Applications target non-volatile memory elements and reconfigurable power switching in UWBG ICs — combining switching and storage in a single material system.

> **Why it matters:** β-Ga₂O₃ already holds a Baliga FOM of ~3,300 — 3–10× SiC and GaN — for power transistors. Adding ferroelectric functionality in the ε(κ) phase means a single material could serve as both a power transistor and a non-volatile logic element (memristor). This maps to neuromorphic power management and adaptive gate bias circuits. The MOCVD temperature-pressure map is the practical enablement step: without a reproducible growth recipe, the physics is academic. This paper provides that recipe.

## Daily Deep Dive: Custom AI ASICs — Architecture, Tape-out Flow, and Broadcom vs. Google TPU Design

**What it is:** Custom AI accelerator chips (XPUs) designed to order for hyperscaler workloads — distinct from general-purpose GPUs. Broadcom is the leading ASIC design partner for Google (TPUv5), Meta (MTIA), and Apple; Marvell serves Amazon (Trainium/Inferentia support). The systolic array at their core is a fixed dataflow architecture optimized for matrix multiplies (the dominant operation in transformer inference and training).

**Why now:** Broadcom's Q3 AI ASIC guidance miss today sent the sector down 15%. Understanding *why* custom ASICs exist — and what constrains their revenue — requires understanding the design-to-production timeline: 18–36 months from customer tape-out order to revenue recognition. Any earnings miss today reflects hyperscaler decisions made in 2023–2024.

**5 Key Technical Points:**

- **Systolic array mechanics:** An N×N systolic array processes N² MAC operations per cycle with O(N) data movement — data flows between neighboring PEs rather than hitting global memory each cycle. Google TPUv4 MXU: 128×128 bfloat16 → 32,768 MACs/cycle at 940 MHz = 61.5 TFLOPS per MXU before memory bound
- **Process node and packaging:** TPUv5 targets TSMC N3B/N4 (3–4nm); advanced packaging via CoWoS-L adds HBM3e at 3.0 TB/s bandwidth — 24× GDDR6 and 3× PCIe 5.0. Memory bandwidth is the binding constraint at inference time, not compute
- **Interconnect fabric:** Google ICI (Inter-chip Interconnect) runs at 1.6 Tbps per chip with custom SerDes, enabling 256-chip TPU pods without NVLink licensing fees. This is the architectural moat vs. Broadcom-designed chips using standard SerDes IP
- **Power delivery:** A 100-chip TPU pod consumes 100–200 kW; 48V direct-to-chip (DTC) bus with on-board [[GaN Power Electronics]] buck arrays reduces I²R losses ~4× vs. 12V distribution
- **NRE and tape-out economics:** A 3nm ASIC tape-out costs $30–50M in NRE; only hyperscalers at 100k+ unit volumes justify this. Multi-project wafer (MPW) shuttles reduce prototyping cost to $500K–2M but don't reduce production NRE

**Where to start:**
- IEEE Xplore: "google tensor processing unit architecture" | "custom ASIC systolic array dataflow" | "VLSI design tape-out flow"
- [[GPU vs TPU Trade-offs]] | [[CUDA Ecosystem Flywheel]] | [[AI Five-Layer Cake]]
- Sze, Chen, Yang, Emer: *Efficient Processing of Deep Neural Networks* (MIT Press 2020, free PDF)
- Patterson & Hennessy: *Computer Architecture* (Ch. 7: Domain-Specific Architectures)

**Concrete calculation to work through:**

Google TPUv4 MXU: 128×128 int8 operations per cycle at 940 MHz.
```
Peak TOPS = 2 × 128² × 940×10⁶ = 30.8 TOPS per MXU
Power per MXU ≈ 40W (estimated)
Efficiency = 30.8 TOPS / 40W = 770 GOPS/W
```
Compare to NVIDIA H100 SXM: 3,958 TOPS (INT8, dense) at 700W = 5.65 TOPS/W.

**Question to work through:** Why is the H100 efficiency (~5.65 TOPS/W) much lower than the theoretical TPU efficiency (~770 GOPS/W), despite H100 being on a newer process node (4nm vs. 7nm for TPUv4)?

*(Answer involves: programmability overhead in CUDA cores vs. fixed dataflow in systolic arrays; SRAM vs. HBM access energy; cache hierarchy costs; and the fact that programmable GPUs must support non-matrix operations that add idle cycles)*
