## Top Stories

**1. AI Chip Stocks Surge on US-Iran Peace Deal — NVDA +2%, AMD +4%**

Global markets rallied after the US and Iran reached a framework agreement on June 14–15 to end fighting and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which had been blockaded for over three months. Oil fell more than $4/barrel on the news. AI chip stocks led the risk-on move: AMD surged more than 4% and NVIDIA gained over 2% in pre-market trading, extending a rebound that began June 11 when Oracle announced $70 billion in data center spending. The PHLX semiconductor index had lost 10% on June 5 after Broadcom's Q3 AI revenue guide missed estimates — today's action partially recovers that gap.

> **Why it matters:** Geopolitical risk has been the primary macro headwind for semiconductor capex decisions in 2026. A reopened Strait of Hormuz normalizes energy costs and supply chain confidence, directly benefiting fab expansion timelines for TSMC, Samsung, and Intel Foundry. Watch for capex guidance revisions upward in Q2 earnings calls.

**2. Google "Icefish" AI Chip: TSMC 1.4nm + Samsung — Volume 2028**

Alphabet is co-developing its next-generation AI accelerator chip, codenamed "Icefish," with TSMC manufacturing the main compute engine at its forthcoming 1.4nm process node, while Samsung handles ancillary chiplets. Volume production is targeted for 2028. The chip represents Google's most aggressive custom silicon bet to date, designed to power its next-generation AI training and inference workloads. TSMC's 1.4nm node (expected commercial readiness 2027–2028) uses gate-all-around nanosheet transistors, reducing leakage current by ~20% vs N2.

> **Why it matters:** At 1.4nm, interconnect RC delay and power-delivery density become the dominant design challenges — not transistor density. Custom ASIC designers at this node must co-optimize signal integrity (coupling caps at <5 nm pitch), on-chip voltage regulators, and clock distribution simultaneously. The Icefish design team is effectively writing the playbook for 1.4nm system design.

**3. α-In₂Se₃ Reconfigurable Vertical Schottky Photodiodes — 2D Ferroelectric Neuromorphic Vision (Advanced Materials 2026)**

Researchers published results (Advanced Materials 2026, DOI: 10.1002/adma.202521243) demonstrating reconfigurable vertical Schottky photodiodes based on α-In₂Se₃, a room-temperature 2D ferroelectric semiconductor. The ferroelectric polarization state (switchable without thermal annealing) modulates the built-in field at the Schottky interface, enabling context-aware perceptual precision: the device can selectively amplify or suppress photocurrent based on its prior polarization history — mimicking retinal adaptation. Demonstrated non-volatile photocurrent retention >10⁴ s at room temperature.

> **Why it matters:** α-In₂Se₃ combines in-plane and out-of-plane ferroelectric polarization at the monolayer limit (~0.6 nm), making it one of the few 2D materials viable for room-temperature FeFET and photodetector integration. The vertical Schottky architecture avoids the contact resistance penalty of lateral 2D devices — a longstanding fabrication challenge. This is a prototype for event-driven vision sensors that don't need analog-to-digital conversion, which saves orders of magnitude in power at the edge.

**4. SIA/Deloitte: Semiconductors = 95% of AI Server Rack Value — $1.2T by 2028**

A joint report from the Semiconductor Industry Association and Deloitte estimates that annual revenue from chips deployed in AI data centers could reach over $1.2 trillion by 2028, a nearly tenfold increase over four years. The report quantifies semiconductors as representing approximately 95% of a fully loaded AI server rack's cost, spanning GPUs, HBM, networking ASICs, power management ICs, and PCIe switches. The bottom of the semiconductor stack — power delivery, decoupling capacitance, thermal management — is flagged as the constraint most likely to limit AI server density scaling in 2026–2028.

> **Why it matters:** Power delivery is the unsexy chokepoint. A single 4U AI server rack with 8× H200 GPUs draws ~10 kW and requires milliohm-class bus bars, 48V-to-point-of-load conversion with <1% efficiency loss per stage, and bulk/decoupling capacitor stacks that must deliver 1–10 A transients in <100 ns. This is a power electronics problem as much as a semiconductor one.

**5. PsiQuantum Omega: Silicon Photonics for Million-Qubit Quantum Computing**

PsiQuantum published details of its "Omega" quantum photonic chipset in Nature, fabricated at GlobalFoundries' 300mm silicon photonics fab. The chip implements photonic qubit generation, routing, and detection in a foundry-compatible process, targeting the million-qubit scale required for fault-tolerant quantum error correction using surface codes. Key metrics: single-photon detector efficiency >90%, photon loss per crossing <0.01 dB, integration density of ~10⁴ photonic components per mm².

> **Why it matters:** The photonic qubit approach avoids the millikelvin cryogenic requirements of superconducting qubits — but demands extraordinary photon source purity (Hong-Ou-Mandel visibility >99.9%) and near-lossless routing. A 300mm fab run means PsiQuantum can leverage existing CMOS process infrastructure without a greenfield fab — the scaling path is realistic in ways that, say, trapped-ion scaling is not.

**6. QuiX Quantum FFCU: 150 ns Photonic Feed-Forward Unlocks Adaptive Quantum Operations**

QuiX Quantum installed its Feed-Forward Control Unit (FFCU) on June 2, 2026, enabling real-time adaptive quantum operations within photonic quantum computing stacks. The FFCU measures an ancillary photon and classically conditions the next gate operation in <150 ns — below the coherence lifetime of subsequent photons. This enables measurement-based quantum computing (MBQC) where entangled cluster states are consumed by adaptive single-qubit measurements, with the feed-forward correcting for teleportation byproducts in real time.

> **Why it matters:** Feed-forward latency is the bottleneck in photonic MBQC. At 150 ns, the FFCU operates within the ~ns-to-µs coherence window of stored photons in delay lines. The key EE challenge: the classical control electronics must perform fast single-photon detection, threshold discrimination, and gate command generation in a loop tight enough that the photon hasn't leaked or dephased. It's a mixed-signal real-time control problem at the quantum-classical interface.

**7. WBG Power Electronics: 2026 Market Inflection for SiC in EVs and Data Centers**

IDTechEx and MDPI reviews released in June 2026 confirm wide-bandgap semiconductors have crossed the cost-performance threshold for mass adoption. SiC MOSFET on-resistance continues to fall with 150mm (6-inch) and 200mm (8-inch) wafer adoption; GaN-on-Si is capturing the <650V mid-power segment for server power supplies and EV onboard chargers. The 800V EV powertrain architecture standardization (now adopted by GM, Hyundai, Porsche, and others) has become the primary demand driver for SiC modules rated 1200 V/300 A+.

> **Why it matters:** The SiC module design challenge at 800V: switching transients of 20–50 V/ns expose parasitic inductances that generate ringing; common-source inductance (L_CS) of even 1–2 nH in a TO-247 package adds tens of volts of dynamic overshoot at 100+ A. Kelvin-source packages and gate-drive return pins directly to the source bond wire reduce L_CS to <0.5 nH and are now mandatory for 800V automotive designs.

## 🔬 Daily Deep Dive: 2D Ferroelectric Semiconductors — α-In₂Se₃ and FeFET Device Physics

**What it is:** α-In₂Se₃ is a van der Waals layered semiconductor (bandgap ~1.4 eV) with spontaneous out-of-plane and in-plane ferroelectric polarization stable at room temperature down to monolayer thickness. Unlike conventional ferroelectrics (PZT, HZO), it is also a semiconductor — enabling a single-material FeFET without a separate ferroelectric/channel stack.

**Why study it now:** The Advanced Materials 2026 Schottky photodiode paper, plus the ferroelectric hybrids review (DOI: 10.1002/adma.202517269), represent a convergence of materials maturity: α-In₂Se₃ CVD growth on SiO₂ is now reproducible enough for device-level studies. Understanding the polarization switching mechanism is essential before these devices appear in commercial neuromorphic chips.

**Technical bullet points:**
- **Polarization switching:** α-In₂Se₃ polarization reversal is driven by In-atom displacement within a Se-In-Se-In-Se quintuple layer. Coercive field ~5–10 MV/cm for few-layer films, reducing to ~1–3 MV/cm for thicker (>10 nm) films — still manageable with standard back-gate biases of ±5–10 V.
- **FeFET architecture:** In a 2D FeFET using α-In₂Se₃ as both the channel and the gate dielectric (self-gating), the ferroelectric polarization modulates the channel charge density — eliminating the depolarization field problem that plagues HZO-on-silicon FeFETs where interfacial dead layers reduce effective coercive field.
- **Retention and fatigue:** Room-temperature polarization retention >10⁴ s demonstrated in vertical Schottky structures; fatigue onset (polarization degradation) observed after ~10⁶ switching cycles — comparable to HZO but without the crystallization anneal requirement.
- **Photodetector sensitivity modulation:** In the vertical Schottky structure (metal / α-In₂Se₃ / n-Si), the ferroelectric polarization changes the Schottky barrier height by ~50–100 meV, modulating photocurrent gain by 2–3 orders of magnitude between polarization states.
- **Bandgap engineering:** Strain engineering or alloying with GaSe tunes the bandgap from ~1.2–2.0 eV, covering visible through near-IR for photodetection — key for multispectral neuromorphic sensors.
