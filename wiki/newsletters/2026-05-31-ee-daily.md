# EE Daily — May 31, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. Custom AI ASICs Cross 27.8% of AI Server Market — Broadcom's $100B Backlog Signals Structural Shift**

ASIC-based AI server shipments are projected to reach 27.8% of the total AI server market in 2026, with custom ASIC shipments growing 44.6% year-over-year — nearly triple the 16.1% growth rate for merchant GPUs. Broadcom reported $8.4 billion in AI semiconductor revenue for Q1 FY2026, a 106% year-over-year increase, and guided to $10.7 billion for Q2. CEO Hock Tan cited a disclosed $73 billion AI backlog and "line of sight to exceed $100 billion in AI revenue from chips in 2027."

> **Why it matters:** Every major hyperscaler now designs its own silicon. The merchant GPU model (NVIDIA) coexists with an increasingly large ASIC tier (Google TPUs, Meta MTIA, Microsoft Maia, ByteDance custom). For EEs, this means tapeout experience at advanced nodes (N3/N2), packaging expertise (CoWoS, SoIC), and high-speed SerDes/die-to-die interconnect are among the highest-leverage skills in the industry right now.

**2. Samsung Ships HBM4E Samples — Memory Topology Becomes the AI Economy's Limiting Reagent**

Samsung Electronics shipped HBM4E engineering samples to major AI hardware customers in May 2026, widening its lead over SK Hynix and Micron in AI memory. Simultaneously, Analog Devices (ADI) acquired Empower Semiconductor, an AI rack power delivery specialist, for $1.5 billion — targeting the voltage-regulator bottleneck in high-density AI compute racks where HBM stacks require tight, low-inductance power delivery at sub-microsecond transient response.

> **Why it matters:** HBM4E pushes to 1.2 TB/s bandwidth per stack via 16-layer stacking and μ-bump pitch scaling to ~40 μm. The power delivery problem is non-trivial: a single GB100-class SXM module draws 700–1,000 W with di/dt transients exceeding 1 A/ns. The ADI acquisition signals that power-integrity EEs, not just memory designers, are a key bottleneck in the AI infrastructure buildout.

**3. NVIDIA Commits $6.5B to Silicon Photonics — Spectrum-X Co-Packaged Optics Switches Launched**

NVIDIA has invested at least $6.5 billion in photonics technology companies since March 2026, and unveiled Spectrum-X and Quantum-X co-packaged optics (CPO) networking switches designed to connect millions of GPUs across AI factory sites. The Spectrum-X CPO Ethernet switches deliver terabit-scale fabric bandwidth while targeting a 2–5× reduction in power-per-bit vs. pluggable QSFP modules.

> **Why it matters:** Pluggable optics (QSFP-DD800) consume ~15–20 W per port and face bandwidth density limits due to PCB routing. Co-packaged optics place the silicon photonics modulator/detector array directly on the switch ASIC package, cutting laser-to-fiber path length, reducing SerDes pre-emphasis requirements, and enabling 800G/1.6T per-lane rates. The thermal co-design challenge — managing III-V laser heat and CMOS logic heat in a single package — is an active EE research problem.

**4. SiC Manufacturing Breakthroughs: 8-Inch Laser Lift-Off at 40 μm and 14-Inch Crystal Growth**

Two SiC manufacturing advances in May 2026 push the material cost and scalability limits. Zhongwei Precision Instrument reported the world's first mass-production of 8-inch SiC substrates via laser lift-off with total material loss in the 40 μm range — disrupting the industry's standard 60–80 μm kerf loss and potentially reducing substrate cost by 30–40%. Tiancheng Semiconductor successfully developed a 14-inch SiC single crystal boule, marking China's transition from the "12-inch adoption phase" to early 14-inch commercialization.

> **Why it matters:** SiC substrate cost (~$400–800 per 8-inch wafer) remains the primary barrier to SiC power device adoption below 800V. Laser lift-off reduces crystal waste, and 14-inch diameter cuts per-die substrate cost by ~36% vs 8-inch (area ratio = (14/8)² ≈ 3.06× more die per boule). These are supply-chain developments with direct implications for EV inverter and industrial drive cost trajectories.

**5. Soitec / NTU Singapore GaN-on-SiC at MWC 2026 — Record PAE Demonstrated for 6G FR3 Handsets**

At Mobile World Congress 2026, Soitec and Nanyang Technological University Singapore reported results of their joint four-year 6G research program using GaN devices on Soitec's epitaxial GaN-on-silicon substrates. The team demonstrated record power-added efficiency (PAE) at the low supply voltages required for handset battery applications in the FR3 (7–24 GHz) and mmWave frequency bands — a critical milestone because base-station GaN (typically 28–50 V) PAE >50% is routine, but handset-class GaN at 3–5 V supply with low quiescent current is a fundamentally harder design point.

> **Why it matters:** 6G FR3 (upper mid-band) requires power amplifiers that deliver both high linearity (to meet EVM specs for 1024-QAM) and high efficiency at low battery voltages. GaN-on-Si is cheaper than GaN-on-SiC but has inferior thermal conductivity (Si: 150 W/m·K vs SiC: 490 W/m·K). Soitec's approach uses engineered epitaxial substrates to bridge this gap. Search IEEE Xplore: "GaN handset power amplifier FR3 efficiency 2025 2026."

**6. β-Ga₂O₃ UWBG Power Devices — 2DEG Channel Demonstrated in β-(AlₓGa₁₋ₓ)₂O₃ Heterostructures**

Recent research demonstrated two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) channel confinement in β-(AlₓGa₁₋ₓ)₂O₃/β-Ga₂O₃ heterostructures — analogous to the AlGaN/GaN HEMT architecture — opening a viable path to high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) in ultra-wide bandgap material. β-Ga₂O₃ has a bandgap of 4.9 eV (vs 3.4 eV GaN, 3.3 eV SiC), a theoretical breakdown field of 8 MV/cm, and a Baliga figure of merit approximately 3–10× larger than SiC or GaN.

> **Why it matters:** The holy grail of ultra-wide bandgap (UWBG) is reaching breakdown voltages >10 kV with switching speeds beyond SiC. Ga₂O₃'s challenge has been n-type doping only (no viable p-type), which restricts device topologies. The 2DEG demonstration bypasses the need for p-type by using the polarization-induced sheet charge as the channel — the same trick that made GaN HEMTs practical. Follow: [[Wide Bandgap Semiconductors]], [[Gallium Oxide Power Electronics]].

**7. Photonic Inc. Raises $180M CAD — Fault-Tolerant Silicon-Spin Quantum Computing Accelerated**

Photonic Inc. closed a $180M CAD financing round in January 2026, with participation from Royal Bank of Canada and TELUS, to accelerate its fault-tolerant quantum architecture. Separately, Nature published results demonstrating a monolithic silicon-photonics platform achieving 99.98% state preparation/measurement fidelity, 99.22% two-qubit fusion fidelity, and 99.72% chip-to-chip qubit interconnect fidelity — all in dual-rail photonic qubit encoding.

> **Why it matters:** Fault-tolerant quantum computing requires physical qubit error rates <10⁻³ with millions of physical qubits per logical qubit (surface code threshold). The photonic path (flying qubits, room-temperature operation, photon loss as the primary error channel) competes with superconducting (cold, fast gates) and ion trap (high fidelity, slow). The 99.98% SPAM fidelity and 99.72% chip-to-chip interconnect represent genuine benchmarks, not marketing metrics.

## 🔬 Daily Research Topic: Ultra-Wide Bandgap β-Ga₂O₃ Power Devices

**What it is:** Gallium oxide (β-Ga₂O₃) is the leading ultra-wide bandgap (UWBG, Eg > 4 eV) semiconductor for next-generation power electronics. Its bandgap of 4.9 eV places it a full category above SiC (3.3 eV) and GaN (3.4 eV), promising converter operation at voltages and switching speeds beyond either material.

**5 Technical Bullets:**

1. **Baliga FOM advantage:** The Baliga figure of merit for power switching — ε·μ·E_c³ — evaluates to ~3,400 for β-Ga₂O₃ vs ~340 for SiC and ~870 for GaN. This means Ga₂O₃ could, in principle, support a 10 kV, 1 mΩ·cm² specific on-resistance device that SiC cannot reach.

2. **The p-type problem:** β-Ga₂O₃ has no viable p-type dopant. All commercially relevant devices are n-type (Sn, Si, Ge doping, ND up to ~10¹⁹ cm⁻³). This rules out BJTs, MOSFETs with inversion channels, and complementary logic — JFET and depletion-mode MOSFET architectures dominate.

3. **Thermal conductivity mismatch:** κ(β-Ga₂O₃) ≈ 13–27 W/m·K — far below SiC (490 W/m·K) and GaN (130 W/m·K). Self-heating under high-power operation is the material's Achilles heel. Flip-chip bonding to diamond or AlN substrates is being researched to extract heat from the back side.

4. **2DEG heterostructure breakthrough:** β-(Al₀.₂₃Ga₀.₇₇)₂O₃/β-Ga₂O₃ heterostructures produce a polarization-induced 2DEG channel with sheet charge densities ~5–10 × 10¹² cm⁻² and electron mobility ~180 cm²/V·s at room temperature — enabling a Ga₂O₃ equivalent of the GaN HEMT. HRL Laboratories' T3L GaN-on-SiC process reaching MRL-6 provides a roadmap analog.

5. **Current device landscape (2026):** Commercialized devices exist only in the research stage (no foundry production). POWER4GATE and other EU projects are targeting vertical Schottky barrier diodes (SBDs) at 1–3 kV. The highest demonstrated SBD breakdown is >8 kV (ARL, 2024) with specific on-resistance of ~2.4 mΩ·cm².

**Where to Start:**
- IEEE Xplore: `"gallium oxide power device 2025 2026"`, `"beta Ga2O3 MOSFET breakdown"`, `"UWBG heterostructure 2DEG"`
- Wikilinks: [[Gallium Oxide Power Electronics]], [[Wide Bandgap Semiconductors]], [[Semiconductor Device Fundamentals]]
- Key group: Air Force Research Lab (AFRL), Cornell Wide Bandgap Laboratory, Ohio State WBG group

**Concrete Calculation:** What on-resistance can a β-Ga₂O₃ drift layer achieve for a 3,300 V device?

Ron,sp = 4 · VBR² / (ε · μ · E_c³)

Using: VBR = 3,300 V, ε = 10 · 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m, μ = 200 cm²/V·s = 0.02 m²/V·s, E_c = 8 MV/cm = 8×10⁸ V/m

Ron,sp = 4 × (3300)² / (10 × 8.85×10⁻¹² × 0.02 × (8×10⁸)³) ≈ 0.08 mΩ·cm²

Compare: SiC 3.3 kV device theoretical Ron,sp ≈ 1.0 mΩ·cm². Ga₂O₃ is theoretically 12× better — if the thermal problem can be solved.
