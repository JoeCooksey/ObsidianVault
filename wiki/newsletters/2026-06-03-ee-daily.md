# EE Daily — June 03, 2026

*Daily briefing on electrical engineering news, breakthroughs, and one topic worth a deep dive.*

## Top Stories

**1. NVIDIA RTX Spark "Superchip" Invades the PC Market at Computex 2026**

NVIDIA unveiled the RTX Spark at Computex Taipei, combining a 20-core ARM-based Grace CPU with a Blackwell RTX GPU in a single package delivering up to 1 petaflop of AI performance and 128 GB unified memory. Laptop designs from Microsoft (Surface Laptop Ultra), Dell, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, and MSI are shipping on this silicon. Separately, Jensen Huang confirmed NVIDIA's Vera Rubin CPU is now in full production, with early-access customers including OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX.

> **Why it matters:** This is NVIDIA expanding its total addressable market beyond data centers into the PC ecosystem—a direct attack on Intel and AMD's core business. For EEs designing power delivery and thermal management into thin-and-light laptops, Blackwell's power envelope (rumored 55–75 W TDP) in this form factor is a design constraint worth tracking. The ARM-based Grace CPU also signals that x86's dominance in consumer laptops may finally crack under AI workload pressure.

**2. NVIDIA and TSMC Integrate AI Across the Full Fab Stack**

NVIDIA and TSMC announced an expanded partnership bringing AI and accelerated computing into semiconductor manufacturing workflows. The collaboration covers computational lithography (replacing rule-based OPC with neural nets), transistor simulation, wafer defect inspection using vision models, and factory floor scheduling optimization. TSMC's N2 GAA lines are among the first to deploy these AI-driven tools at volume.

> **Why it matters:** Computational lithography is one of the most compute-intensive steps in chip manufacturing—NVIDIA's cuLitho has already demonstrated 40× speedup over CPU-based OPC. Bringing AI into defect inspection and scheduling closes the loop: faster design iterations, tighter yield management, and lower cost-per-wafer. For process engineers, this represents a shift from deterministic rule sets to probabilistic, model-driven process control.

**3. Microsoft Demonstrates 10,000-Year Data Storage in Borosilicate Glass**

Microsoft Research reported a laser-based storage technique using borosilicate glass that achieves unprecedented write speeds for glass storage while guaranteeing data integrity for an estimated 10,000-year lifetime. The technique exploits phase modifications induced by femtosecond laser pulses to encode binary data in the glass bulk, with readout via polarized light microscopy.

> **Why it matters:** Magnetic and flash storage degrade in decades; tape in 30–50 years. Glass storage is chemically inert, radiation-hardened, and thermally stable to >600°C. For archival applications—regulatory compliance, genomic databases, long-duration space missions—this closes a genuine gap. The write-speed breakthrough is the engineering unlock: prior glass storage was prohibitively slow for bulk data migration.

**4. DEEPX and AAEON Sign 3-Year Global Mass Production Agreement for Physical AI NPUs**

DEEPX Inc. and AAEON Technology announced a strategic mass-production cooperation agreement at Computex 2026 to integrate DEEPX's Neural Processing Units into AAEON's embedded and industrial computing product lines. DEEPX's NPUs target always-on inference at sub-5 W TDP, suitable for robotics, smart factories, and autonomous vehicles operating at the network edge without cloud connectivity.

> **Why it matters:** Edge inference at low power is where most real-world AI deployments actually live. Cloud-dependent AI fails in latency-sensitive, connectivity-challenged, or privacy-constrained environments. Sub-5 W NPUs capable of running production-grade vision models represent the inflection point where physical AI (robotics, industrial vision, autonomous machinery) becomes economically viable at scale. Watch interconnect specs: DEEPX claims MIPI CSI-2 and PCIe Gen3 interfaces for drop-in integration.

**5. Valleytronics Breakthrough: Room-Temperature Valley Control in Ultra-Thin 2D Semiconductors**

Researchers demonstrated room-temperature control of the valley degree of freedom in ultra-thin semiconductors by stacking the 2D material on a patterned metasurface, reshaping the potential landscape beneath the layer without chemical modification. The approach achieves stable valley polarization at 300 K, removing the primary obstacle (cryogenic cooling) to practical valleytronic devices.

> **Why it matters:** Valleytronics uses the crystal momentum valley index (K vs. K' in hexagonal Brillouin zones) as an additional information carrier alongside charge and spin. In MoS₂ and WSe₂, spin-orbit coupling locks spin to valley at ~150 meV splitting—orders of magnitude above thermal noise at room temperature—but intervalley scattering via phonons has historically destroyed coherence. This metasurface suppression of scattering pathways is the engineering key. If durable, it enables logic elements with no moving charge: zero dynamic power dissipation from charge transport.

**6. Brain-Inspired CuInP₂S₆ Ferroelectric for Neuromorphic Computing**

A new study in Advanced Functional Materials demonstrates that CuInP₂S₆ (CIPS), a 2D van der Waals ferroelectric, can implement synaptic weight updates with multilevel polarization states, enabling neuromorphic (brain-like) computing in a single-material device. CIPS exhibits polarization switching down to 6 V/nm with retention exceeding 10⁴ seconds at room temperature.

> **Why it matters:** Neuromorphic hardware promises orders-of-magnitude improvements in energy efficiency over von Neumann architectures for inference tasks. The challenge has been finding a material that combines non-volatile memory, analog programmability (multilevel states), and CMOS-compatible deposition. CIPS, grown by chemical vapor transport and stackable with graphene or MoS₂, hits all three. The 6 V/nm switching field remains high for back-end-of-line integration—expect process engineering research here next.

**7. 300 mm GaN Wafers Enable Acceleration of EV Electrification**

Industry reports confirm 300 mm GaN-on-silicon wafer production is ramping, with projected cost-per-wafer reductions of 40–60% versus current 200 mm production. The larger format enables more aggressive EV onboard charger (OBC) and DC-DC converter designs targeting 800 V bus architectures, where GaN's breakdown field (3.3 MV/cm) and switching speed (sub-10 ns at 650 V) are unmatched by silicon.

> **Why it matters:** GaN's value proposition in EVs is switching at 800 V bus voltages with minimal switching losses—enabling bidirectional OBCs at 11+ kW in packages that fit a shoebox. Moving from 200 mm to 300 mm fab lines is not a materials breakthrough but a manufacturing economics unlock: 2.25× more die per run, fewer edge effects, and the ability to share capacity with existing silicon CMOS fabs. Watch Infineon, STMicro, and ROHM for first-to-market claims on 300 mm GaN OBC modules.

## 🔬 Daily Research Topic — Valleytronics: Valley Degree of Freedom in 2D TMDs

**What it is:** Transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) like MoS₂ and WSe₂ have hexagonal crystal symmetry with two inequivalent energy minima (valleys) at the K and K' points of the Brillouin zone. These valleys can carry information independently of charge—a "valley qubit" or "valleyon"—enabling logic with zero charge transport and, theoretically, zero dynamic power.

**5 Technical Bullets:**
- **Valley splitting:** Spin-orbit coupling (SOC) in TMD monolayers lifts spin degeneracy by 150–450 meV at K/K'. In WSe₂, valence band splitting ≈ 450 meV; in MoS₂ ≈ 150 meV. Both ≫ kT at 300 K (26 meV), so thermal scrambling is not the fundamental limit.
- **Valley polarization via optical pumping:** σ+ circularly polarized light excites the K valley selectively (optical selection rules from C₃ᵥ symmetry); σ- excites K'. Degree of polarization ρ = (I_K - I_K') / (I_K + I_K') has reached >90% at low temperature, ~30–50% at room temperature before today's metasurface result.
- **Intervalley scattering mechanisms:** The enemy of valley coherence is short-range disorder (atomic defects, grain boundaries) and zone-edge phonons (intervalley LA/TA scattering). The K–K' separation in reciprocal space (~1.7 Å⁻¹ in MoS₂) means only large-momentum phonons cause scattering—metasurface potential engineering suppresses these phonon modes by modifying the local strain field.
- **Valley Hall effect:** Applied in-plane E-field drives K-valley carriers to one edge of the device and K'-valley carriers to the other—a transverse voltage with no net charge current (zero Hall coefficient). This is the basis for proposed valley-Hall logic gates.
- **Berry curvature and anomalous velocity:** Each valley has opposite Berry curvature Ω(k) ≈ ±D²Δ/(2(ℏ²k²/m + Δ²))^(3/2), where Δ is the gap and D is the coupling constant. This gives an anomalous transverse velocity v_⊥ = (e/ℏ) E × Ω(k), the microscopic origin of the valley Hall effect.

- IEEE Xplore: `"valleytronics" "MoS2" OR "WSe2" "room temperature" 2025 2026`
- IEEE Xplore: `"valley Hall effect" "transition metal dichalcogenide" experimental`
- Start reading: Xiao et al., PRL 2012 ("Valley-Contrasting Physics in Graphene") — the foundational paper
- [[Silicon Photonics]], [[Quantum Computing]], [[2D Materials (concept)]]

**Concrete Calculation to Work Through:**
For monolayer MoS₂ (Δ = 0.9 eV gap, effective mass m* ≈ 0.48 mₑ, D ≈ 7.9 eV·Å):
Estimate the Berry curvature magnitude Ω at the K point, and from it calculate the anomalous Hall conductivity σ_xy for a 1 µm × 1 µm device at full valley polarization. Compare to the minimum detectable voltage (Johnson noise limit) for a 1 MΩ load at room temperature in a 1 Hz bandwidth.
