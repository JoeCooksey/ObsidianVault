### Screwworm Outbreak Spreading in Texas and New Mexico — First US Cases Since 1960s

**What happened:** USDA confirmed six cases of New World screwworm in domestic animals (four cattle, one goat, one dog) across Texas and New Mexico as of June 9, the first US cases since the parasite was eradicated in the 1960s; the CDC activated a Level 3 emergency response on June 11, and Governor Abbott issued a disaster proclamation.

**Who is affected:** Livestock ranchers in the Southwest, pet owners, the US beef supply chain.

**What's contested:** Whether the federal response is adequate — Texas Agriculture Commissioner Sid Miller has publicly criticized USDA, noting the screwworm advanced 1,100 miles from southern Mexico to Texas despite intervention. Internal debates exist about whether the outbreak is larger than confirmed counts suggest.

**What's still unknown:** Full geographic extent. Whether human cases will emerge (none reported locally so far). Whether containment via sterile fly dispersal will succeed.

**Coverage spread:** Fox News covered multiple angles including the cartel-route vector and beef price impacts. CDC and USDA issued official updates. **BLINDSPOT:** Left-leaning outlets appear less prominently engaged on this story, though CDC/government sources fill the factual void.
