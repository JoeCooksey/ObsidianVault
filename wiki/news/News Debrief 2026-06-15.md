### US-Iran Framework Deal — Strait of Hormuz Set to Reopen

**What happened:** The United States and Iran reached a framework agreement on June 14–15 to end fighting and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which had been blockaded since late February when the US and Israel began military operations against Iran. A signing ceremony is scheduled for Friday in Switzerland, with Qatar as a key intermediary. Both sides agreed to lift their dueling naval blockades; Iran will halt uranium enrichment above specified limits under the deal's terms.

**Who is affected:** The ~21 million barrels of oil per day that transit the Strait — roughly 20% of global oil supply — would resume shipping. Global energy markets, oil-importing nations (especially Asia), and commodity traders are immediately affected. Israel's security situation remains unresolved, with the deal silent on Iranian ballistic missile programs.

**What's contested:** Whether Iran's enriched uranium stockpile (estimated at weapons-grade quantities sufficient for multiple warheads) will be verifiably removed. Whether Israel, which is not a party to the deal, will accept the outcome. Whether the deal is legally binding given the absence of Congressional approval.

**What's still unknown:** The exact verification mechanism for Iranian compliance. Russia's and China's roles in enforcement. Timeline for full naval de-escalation. Whether Iranian proxy groups (Hezbollah, Houthis) are covered.

**Coverage spread:** Reuters, AP, NPR, Bloomberg all reported factually on the deal framework. Left-leaning outlets (Guardian, NYT) emphasized the fragility of the deal and unresolved questions about Iran's uranium stockpile. Right-leaning outlets (Fox, WSJ, NY Post) framed it as a Trump foreign policy achievement. No major outlet was notably absent from coverage. ### ~10% of US Employers Plan to Drop Weight-Loss Drug Coverage in 2027

**What happened:** A Business Group on Health survey found that approximately 10% of US employers currently covering GLP-1 weight-loss medications (Eli Lilly's Zepbound, Novo Nordisk's Wegovy) plan to discontinue that coverage in 2027, citing rapidly expanding utilization and cost pressures. Reuters reported the finding on or around June 12. Eli Lilly's stock fell approximately 1% on the news. Separately, CVS Health announced earlier in June that it would add Zepbound as a co-preferred option starting October 1 — a countervailing development.

**Who is affected:** An estimated several million US employees currently receiving employer-covered GLP-1 prescriptions; Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk's US revenue forecasts; the broader obesity drug market, which analysts project at $100B+ by 2030; out-of-pocket drug affordability for patients who would lose coverage.

**What's contested:** Whether employer pullback will materially dent overall GLP-1 sales (given government programs like Medicaid and Medicare Part D expansion for obesity), or whether it signals a broader backlash against GLP-1 coverage costs. Eli Lilly's direct-to-employer model (launched March 2026) is positioned as a counter-argument.

**What's still unknown:** What fraction of the remaining ~90% of employers covering these drugs may quietly reduce dosing limits or impose prior-authorization requirements. Full market impact on Lilly and Novo Nordisk 2027 revenue guidance.

**Coverage spread:** Reuters reported the Business Group on Health data; TipRanks covered the stock impact. Business/financial press (WSJ, Bloomberg) covered it as an investment story.
