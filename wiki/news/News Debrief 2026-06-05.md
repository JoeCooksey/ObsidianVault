**What's still unknown:** Whether the Kazakhstan uranium-storage proposal constitutes a viable off-ramp; the current enrichment level of Iran's stockpile; whether third-party mediation (Oman, Qatar) remains active.

**What happened:** The Senate voted along party lines to pass a Republican bill funding immigration enforcement agencies through the end of President Trump's term, after weeks of delay tied to an unrelated $1.776 billion anti-weaponization settlement fund that was dropped after GOP pushback. Separately, Trump signed an executive order reclassifying approximately 8,000 GS-15-level federal workers into a new category that permits termination without standard civil-service protections.

**Who is affected:** Federal employees in the reclassified GS-15 category; migrants subject to enforcement; taxpayers funding the enforcement bill.

**What's contested:** Whether the Schedule F expansion is a legitimate reorganization of the executive branch or a politicization of the civil service. Republicans frame it as restoring accountability; Democrats and federal employee unions characterize it as enabling politically motivated firings.

**What's still unknown:** The total cost of the immigration enforcement bill; whether court challenges to Schedule F will succeed; the timeline for implementation of the new civil-service rules.

**Coverage spread:** CNN, CNBC, NPR cover immigration bill and Schedule F. WSJ editorial board framed Schedule F as overdue accountability reform. NYT and NPR emphasize civil service protection concerns. No major BLINDSPOT — both sides covered by their preferred outlets.

## Technology

**Bipartisan "Great American AI Act" Discussion Draft Released in House**

**What happened:** Representatives Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA), joined by four additional co-sponsors across both parties, released a 269-page discussion draft of the Great American AI Act on June 4, 2026. Key provisions: a three-year preemption of state AI development laws (states retain use-regulation authority), formal codification of the Center for AI Standards and Innovation with $100M/year authorized for FY2027–2029, and an extension of the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 through 2035. The draft is open for stakeholder comment before formal introduction.

**Who is affected:** Technology companies developing AI models, state legislators who have passed or planned AI bills, federal agencies that would administer the new framework.

**What's contested:** The state preemption provision is the central dispute — tech industry groups support federal preemption to avoid a patchwork of 50 state rules; civil liberties organizations and some state AGs argue states should retain broader authority over AI impacts within their borders.

**What's still unknown:** Whether the bill will advance past discussion-draft stage; how the three-year preemption sunset would interact with state laws passed before enactment; details of enforcement mechanisms.

**Coverage spread:** Roll Call, Bloomberg Law, FedScoop, Axios, Broadband Breakfast all covered it. Right-leaning coverage from Bloomberg Government noted "debate and tensions." Left-leaning outlets have not yet significantly covered it. BLINDSPOT: limited coverage in NPR, Guardian, NYT as of June 5.

## Science & Health

**GLP-1 Drugs Linked to Addiction Reduction in 600,000-Veteran VA Study**

**What happened:** A study of more than 600,000 US veterans suggests that GLP-1 receptor agonists — including semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy) and tirzepatide — may reduce addictive behaviors including alcohol use disorder and opioid use, not merely manage weight and blood sugar. The mechanism under investigation involves GLP-1 receptor activity in dopamine-reward circuits of the brain, which are distinct from the metabolic pathways targeted for diabetes and obesity.

**Who is affected:** The approximately 3 million Americans enrolled in medication-assisted addiction treatment; payers and insurers currently covering or denying GLP-1 coverage.

**What's contested:** Causality vs. correlation — the VA study is observational. Researchers note that patients prescribed semaglutide may already have better clinical engagement habits, which could independently reduce addiction rates.

**What's still unknown:** Optimal GLP-1 dosing for addiction applications vs. metabolic applications; long-term effects; whether FDA would require a new indication approval or whether off-label use will dominate.

**Coverage spread:** ScienceDaily covered the study results. Mainstream health outlets (WebMD, STAT News) have reported on the broader GLP-1 addiction research trend. No significant left-right framing divergence.
