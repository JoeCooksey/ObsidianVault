### NASA Detects Kelvin Waves Signaling Potential Super El Nino in 2026

**What happened**: NOAA officially declared El Nino on June 11 after NASA's Sentinel-6 satellite detected massive Kelvin waves (warm water pulses hundreds of miles wide) crossing the equatorial Pacific toward South America, with NOAA forecasting a 63% chance this becomes a "very strong" or Super El Nino -- potentially one of the largest since 1950.

**Who is affected**: Global populations facing altered weather patterns including heat waves, flooding, and drought; agricultural producers; hurricane-season planners; and climate researchers tracking temperature records (a Super El Nino virtually guarantees 2027 breaks the global heat record).

**What's contested**: The intensity and trajectory remain uncertain. Conditions in the western Pacific on June 8 resembled the exceptionally strong 1997 El Nino, but eastern Pacific warm conditions in 2026 have lagged behind that benchmark, with fewer Kelvin waves built up by the same date. Some meteorologists urge caution against premature "super" labeling.

**What's still unknown**: Whether El Nino will reach historic Super intensity, the precise regional weather impacts for the coming winter, and the magnitude of the global temperature spike expected in late 2026 and 2027.

**Coverage spread**: NASA/JPL, NOAA, ScienceDaily, CNN, Fox Weather, Severe Weather Europe, Smithsonian. Fox Weather reported the story straightforwardly without politicizing it. Left-leaning outlets connected it to climate change trends. No BLINDSPOT -- broad coverage across the spectrum.
