## World

### Ebola Outbreak in DR Congo Declared International Emergency, Cases Top 837

**What happened**: The WHO reported 837 confirmed Ebola cases (Bundibugyo strain) and 196 confirmed deaths in northeastern DR Congo as of June 15, with the outbreak also spreading into Uganda; the WHO declared it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on May 17.

**Who is affected**: Populations across Ituri, North Kivu, and South Kivu provinces in DR Congo, neighboring Uganda, and international health systems preparing response capacity.

**What's contested**: The WHO acknowledges community deaths are going unreported, meaning confirmed case counts understate the true spread. MSF and the WHO differ on the adequacy of the current response timeline. There is no approved vaccine or specific treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, though candidates are being tested.

**What's still unknown**: The actual case count given undetected community transmission; whether vaccine candidates will receive emergency authorization in time to affect this outbreak; and how armed conflict in the region will continue to impede containment efforts.

**Coverage spread**: Covered by CBS News, UN News, WHO, ECDC, MSF, and Wikipedia tracking pages. Notably underreported in US partisan media on both sides — neither Fox News nor major US left-leaning outlets have featured prominent ongoing coverage. BLINDSPOT: Minimal sustained US domestic coverage despite PHEIC declaration.

## Science & Health

### Ozone-Protecting Chemicals Found to Be Spreading "Forever Chemical" Pollution Globally

**What happened**: A study published June 9 found that refrigerants and anesthetic gases introduced to protect the ozone layer have generated over 335,000 tonnes of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), a persistent "forever chemical," now detected in rainwater and Arctic ice worldwide.

**Who is affected**: Global populations exposed to TFA through drinking water and precipitation, chemical manufacturers, and regulators overseeing the Montreal Protocol's substitute chemicals; related research from April found industrial feedstock emissions are delaying ozone recovery by approximately seven years.

**What's contested**: Whether the TFA levels detected pose an immediate health risk to humans (the study documents environmental presence but health thresholds remain debated), and whether the Montreal Protocol's framework adequately accounts for downstream chemical byproducts of approved substitutes.

**What's still unknown**: The long-term human health effects of chronic low-level TFA exposure; whether regulatory bodies will expand the Montreal Protocol to address these byproducts; and how quickly TFA concentrations are increasing in drinking water supplies.

**Coverage spread**: Covered by ScienceDaily, The Cool Down, Nature Communications, and Lancaster University press materials. Limited mainstream media pickup. BLINDSPOT: Neither left nor right partisan outlets have given this significant coverage despite its implications for environmental and public health policy.
