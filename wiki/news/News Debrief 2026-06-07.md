## Science & Health

**PEPFAR Funding Cuts Endanger HIV Patients in South Africa and Mozambique**

**What happened:** Health care workers in South Africa and Mozambique report that cancellation and redirection of U.S. PEPFAR funding under the Trump administration has resulted in reduced HIV testing, diagnosis, and preventive treatment, with providers attributing deaths directly to the funding gap. A modeling study in PMC estimates a $4.5 billion decrease in PEPFAR country funding over five years. South Africa — the country with the world's highest HIV burden — has not been included in replacement funding plans.

**Who is affected:** Estimated tens of millions of HIV-positive patients across sub-Saharan Africa who depend on PEPFAR-funded antiretroviral programs. Healthcare workers at PEPFAR-supported clinics who have been laid off.

**What's contested:** The Trump administration frames the cuts as ending wasteful foreign spending and redirecting funds to domestic priorities. Public health advocates and former PEPFAR officials argue the cuts will produce a humanitarian crisis that will ultimately cost more in emergency response than the programs saved.

**What's still unknown:** The full downstream mortality impact; whether donor countries (EU, UK, Gates Foundation) can fill the gap; whether any Congressional intervention is forthcoming.

**Coverage spread:** NPR (center-left), PBS NewsHour (center-left), Healthbeat (center health-focused), The Leaflet (left). BLINDSPOT: Fox News and NY Post have not prominently covered the mortality impacts; WSJ coverage focused on budget policy rather than humanitarian consequences.
