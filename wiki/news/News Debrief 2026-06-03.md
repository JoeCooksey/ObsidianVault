## Science & Health

**AI Analysis of CT Scans Links Thymus Health to Cancer Survival and Longevity**

**What happened:** Researchers at Mass General Brigham used AI to analyze CT scans from tens of thousands of adults, finding that people with healthier, more active thymuses had substantially lower risks of cancer, heart disease, and other age-related conditions, and lived longer. The thymus, typically regarded as relevant only in early childhood immune development, appears to remain functionally significant in adults.

**Who is affected:** Adults in middle and older age for whom thymic health may be a new predictive biomarker; cancer and immunology researchers; radiologists integrating AI tools.

**What's contested:** Causality vs. correlation: a healthier thymus may be a marker of overall immune fitness rather than an independent causal variable. The study design (CT scan analysis) does not establish mechanistic causality.

**What's still unknown:** Whether thymic health is modifiable (via diet, exercise, immune-boosting interventions); which CT scan features specifically the AI model weighted; whether the findings replicate across diverse populations.

**Coverage spread:** ScienceDaily, SciTechDaily (primary coverage). Major wire services and broadcast outlets did not prominently feature. No strong left/right framing — primarily a science-communication story. Minimal political framing.
