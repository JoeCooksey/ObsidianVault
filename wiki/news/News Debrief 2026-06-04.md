## US

### Trump Executive Order Strips Job Protections from 8,000 Senior Federal Employees

**What happened:** President Trump signed an executive order on June 3, 2026 reclassifying approximately 8,000 GS-15-level federal employees into a new "Schedule Policy Career" at-will category — an expansion of Schedule F from his first term. Affected employees can now be dismissed without cause. Nearly all 8,000 are at the top tier of the civil service: policy office leaders, chiefs of staff, regional directors, program managers, senior public affairs officers, and grant overseers.

**Who is affected:** Eight thousand senior career civil servants across federal agencies; the broader federal workforce (which may perceive a chilling effect on candid policy advice); unions representing federal employees; future administrations, which could face a more politically compliant bureaucracy.

**What's contested:** Critics — including federal employee unions — argue the order politicizes the career civil service by removing merit-based protections, violates the 1978 Civil Service Reform Act and Administrative Procedures Act, and may deter honest policy advice. The White House fact sheet framed the order as "increasing accountability in the federal workforce." The final scope (8,000) is smaller than the original OPM estimate of 50,000 positions.

**What's still unknown:** Whether courts will block the order (multiple lawsuits pending from federal unions); how quickly agencies will begin using at-will authority; long-term effect on civil service recruitment and retention of senior expertise.

**Coverage spread:** NPR, Washington Post, CNN, Time, Government Executive — center and center-left outlets framed the story around job protection concerns and politicization risk. White House published its own fact sheet framing it as accountability reform. ### Oral Drug Activating Skeletal Muscle Metabolism Shows Promise for Obesity and Diabetes

**What happened:** Researchers at Karolinska Institutet and Stockholm University published findings in *Cell* (reported June 3, 2026) on an experimental oral tablet that treats obesity and type 2 diabetes by activating metabolism in skeletal muscle — a mechanism distinct from GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic, Wegovy). Animal models showed fat burning and blood sugar lowering without appetite suppression or muscle loss; early clinical safety data is positive.

**Who is affected:** An estimated 890 million people globally with obesity; type 2 diabetes patients; people experiencing muscle loss as a side effect of GLP-1 therapy; pharmaceutical competitors to Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk in the obesity/diabetes market.

**What's contested:** The published data represents pre-clinical and early Phase 1 results — human efficacy at scale is not established. No developer or commercial timeline has been publicly disclosed. Whether skeletal muscle activation alone produces clinically meaningful long-term weight loss comparable to GLP-1 drugs (20–28% body weight loss in trials) remains unknown.

**What's still unknown:** Identity of the drug developer; Phase 2/3 trial timeline; whether the mechanism will be additive with GLP-1 drugs in combination therapy at scale; long-term safety profile in humans.

**Coverage spread:** ScienceDaily, Fox News Health, MedicalNewsToday, SciTechDaily — broadly covered across the political spectrum in factual science-news framing with no significant political divide. Fox Health framed it positively as an "Ozempic alternative" with muscle preservation benefits.
