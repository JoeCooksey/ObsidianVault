**What happened:** The EEOC (Equal Employment Opportunity Commission) under the Trump administration is seeking to end annual employer workforce demographic data reporting (a requirement in place since 1966), has already voted 2-1 to rescind Biden-era harassment enforcement guidance (January 22, 2026), and announced it will no longer investigate or litigate claims based solely on disparate impact. On May 14, 2026 the EEOC submitted a proposed rule to rescind employer annual EEO-1 data reporting requirements. The underlying laws prohibiting disparate-impact discrimination have not been repealed.

**Who is affected:** Employees who experience workplace harassment or discrimination based on protected characteristics; employers subject to EEO-1 reporting; civil rights organizations that use the demographic data for research and advocacy.

**What's contested:** Whether removing enforcement guidance and data reporting requirements constitutes a substantive rollback of civil rights protections, or whether it eliminates DEI-motivated regulatory overreach; whether the EEOC's new commissioner voting structure appropriately shifts power.

**What's still unknown:** Whether courts will invalidate any of the rule changes; how employers will adjust internal practices in the absence of enforcement; the long-term impact on workforce diversity data availability.

**Coverage spread:** NPR and MPR News provided the most detailed coverage of the enforcement changes. Left-leaning outlets (Guardian, NYT) framed as erosion of civil rights protections. WSJ and business press focused on compliance relief for employers. Fox News coverage was limited. ## Science & Health

**Ebola Bundibugyo Outbreak in DRC and Uganda Declared WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern**

**What happened:** On May 17, 2026 the WHO Director-General declared the Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo virus in the Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC). As of June 2, the DRC Ministry of Health reports 321 confirmed cases, 48 confirmed deaths, and 116 suspected cases under investigation. Ituri Province has 299 confirmed cases across 15 health zones; North Kivu has 19 cases; Uganda has 15 confirmed cases including one death. Bundibugyo virus is one of five Ebola species for which there is currently no approved vaccine or specific treatment, unlike the Zaire strain for which rVSV-ZEBOV (Ervebo) exists.

**Who is affected:** Residents of affected provinces in northeastern DRC and border regions of Uganda; healthcare workers; regional travel networks; global health response organizations (MSF, WHO, CDC).

**What's contested:** Whether existing candidate vaccines (some in early trials) can be deployed at speed before the outbreak expands; adequacy of international funding response given the PHEIC designation; cross-border containment given active conflict in eastern DRC.

**What's still unknown:** Whether Bundibugyo-specific vaccine candidates can be authorized for emergency use; true case count given testing access limitations in conflict zones; mortality rate as the outbreak evolves.

**Coverage spread:** WHO, CDC, ECDC, NPR, MSF/Doctors Without Borders provided detailed coverage. BBC and AP carried wire reports. Fox News and NY Post have minimal coverage of this story. BLINDSPOT: Right-leaning US media largely absent from this story — significant given the PHEIC designation and US global health implications.
