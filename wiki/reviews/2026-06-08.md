## Card 1 · Never Eat Alone

**Q:** What is the Follow-Up Formula?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Follow up within 24 hours of meeting someone. Reference a specific detail from your conversation (shows you listened). Give something (article, intro, resource) before asking for anything. The formula: quick + specific + give first. Most people never follow up — doing so once puts you in a tiny minority.

## Card 2 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What is Housel's thesis on Financial Freedom vs Wealth?

> [!tip]- Answer
> The highest dividend money pays is autonomy — the ability to do what you want, when you want, with who you want. Not possessions. Housel argues: save without a specific goal, because savings is optionality. It keeps options open when the unexpected happens, and that freedom is the real prize.

## Card 3 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is P/PC Balance?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Production (P) vs Production Capability (PC). You can maximize short-term output by killing the goose that lays golden eggs, but you lose the source. True effectiveness requires maintaining the capability (relationships, health, skills, tools) that produces results — not just maximizing today's output.

## Card 4 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is the Two-Minute Rule, and why does it work?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Any new habit should start as something completable in under two minutes. 'Read before bed' becomes 'open the book.' You're not building the habit — you're building the ritual of showing up. Once you've started, continuation is easy. The entry is the entire barrier.

## Card 5 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** How does Isaacson reframe Leonardo's legendary procrastination?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Leonardo's procrastination was often productive incubation — gathering more data, more observations, more connections. The Mona Lisa took 16 years. The distinction: some delays are evasion; some are incubation. Knowing the difference in your own work is a metacognitive skill. Don't mistake gathering for avoiding.

## Card 6 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What does 'taste as a skill' mean, according to Isaacson's portrait of Jobs?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Jobs believed taste was cultivated through exposure to great art, music, design, and poetry — not inherited. He could walk away from a product and return days later with a clear verdict. Aesthetic judgment is a trainable discipline, not a gift. It requires sustained exposure and the courage to trust your judgment.

## Card 7 · How to Win Friends

**Q:** What is Carnegie's foundational principle about human motivation?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Every person's deepest desire is to feel important. Not just liked — important. All influence flows from genuinely meeting this need. The error most people make: they focus on themselves. Carnegie's inversion: become genuinely interested in others, and influence follows naturally.

## Card 8 · The Subtle Art

**Q:** What is the 'Feedback Loop from Hell,' and what does it reveal about pursuing happiness?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Feeling anxious → feeling anxious about feeling anxious → feeling worse about feeling worse. The pursuit of positive experience is itself a negative experience; accepting negative experience is itself a positive one. Manson's inversion: stop trying to feel good all the time. That pursuit is the trap.
