## Card 1 · Laws of Human Nature

**Q:** What is the Law of Self-Sabotage?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Our life circumstances are largely the product of our own character and worldview — not external events. People who feel like victims create victim situations through their choices and interpretations. Those who see opportunities create them. Change the deep story you tell about your life, and the circumstances gradually follow.

## Card 2 · How to Win Friends

**Q:** How does Carnegie's 'praise every improvement' principle connect to behavioral science?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Praise every improvement immediately and specifically. The signal must reach the person in the moment — a week later it loses power. This is operant conditioning: reinforce the specific behavior you want to see more of, right when it happens. The timing of the reward defines what behavior it reinforces.

## Card 3 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What is the First Rule of Compounding?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Don't interrupt it. The biggest financial mistakes are not picking wrong stocks — they're selling at the bottom, taking on too much debt, or otherwise leaving the game before compounding does its work. Good investing is less about brilliant picks and more about never making the catastrophic mistake.

## Card 4 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What are the four renewal dimensions of Habit 7 (Sharpen the Saw)?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Physical (exercise, nutrition, stress management), Mental (reading, writing, planning), Social/Emotional (service, empathy, synergy), Spiritual (value clarification, meditation, study). Neglect any one dimension and all four degrade. The Upward Spiral: renewal in one dimension feeds the others.

## Card 5 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What is the A-Player Principle?

> [!tip]- Answer
> B-players hire C-players (to avoid being shown up). A-players hire A-players. One B-player infects a team — others tolerate lower standards, and the bar drops. Jobs maintained A-player density through radical honesty about performance. He believed this was respect, not cruelty.

## Card 6 · The Subtle Art

**Q:** What is Manson's core argument about values and problems?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Better values produce better problems. Problems never disappear — they upgrade. Choosing which problems you're willing to have (and work through) is more important than trying to eliminate problems. 'What are you willing to struggle for?' is a better life question than 'What do you want?'

## Card 7 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is identity-based habit formation?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Every action is a vote for the person you are becoming. Rather than 'I want to run a marathon,' say 'I am a runner' and act accordingly. Identity-based habits are durable because they're self-confirming; goal-based habits collapse after the goal is hit.

## Card 8 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** What is Sfumato, and what is its philosophical meaning beyond painting?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Sfumato (Italian: 'gone up in smoke') — Leonardo's painting technique of blurring edges, avoiding hard outlines, letting forms emerge from shadow. The Mona Lisa's smile is sfumato — it shifts as you look. Philosophically: the willingness to live in uncertainty without forcing premature resolution. Hold contradictions simultaneously.
