## Card 1 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What are Housel's 'Untouchables'?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Five things never worth trading for money: reputation, freedom, family, friends, happiness. These are the intrinsic values that money is supposed to serve — not the other way around. When money is used to acquire these things it often destroys them. Housel argues knowing your 'enough' is the critical financial skill.

## Card 2 · Never Eat Alone

**Q:** What is Ferrazzi's core philosophy about networking?

> [!tip]- Answer
> The currency of real networking is not greed but generosity. Give before you need. The most connected people don't 'network' — they build genuine relationships by helping others without expectation of return. Transactional networking fails because people can smell the transaction.

## Card 3 · How to Win Friends

## Card 4 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is the Maturity Continuum, and why does most self-help stop too early?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Dependence → Independence → Interdependence. Most self-help targets Independence ('I can do it'). But the highest leverage is Interdependence ('we can do it together') — results achievable in collaboration exceed anything an individual can produce alone.

## Card 5 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is identity-based habit formation?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Every action is a vote for the person you are becoming. Rather than 'I want to run a marathon,' say 'I am a runner' and act accordingly. Identity-based habits are durable because they're self-confirming; goal-based habits collapse after the goal is hit.

## Card 6 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** What is Sfumato, and what is its philosophical meaning beyond painting?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Sfumato (Italian: 'gone up in smoke') — Leonardo's painting technique of blurring edges, avoiding hard outlines, letting forms emerge from shadow. The Mona Lisa's smile is sfumato — it shifts as you look. Philosophically: the willingness to live in uncertainty without forcing premature resolution. Hold contradictions simultaneously.

## Card 7 · The Subtle Art

**Q:** What is Manson's Law of Avoidance?

> [!tip]- Answer
> The more you try to avoid something, the more it defines you. If you try to avoid failure, failure defines your life — every decision is made to avoid it. If you try to avoid embarrassment, embarrassment defines your life. The thing you avoid always wins. Engage with it instead.

## Card 8 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What was Jobs's philosophy on arts and technology, and where did it come from?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 'Technology alone is not enough — it's technology married with liberal arts and humanities that yields the results that make our hearts sing.' The calligraphy class at Reed College → Mac's beautiful typography → the aesthetic revolution in computing. Aesthetic sensitivity was an engineering requirement at Apple.
