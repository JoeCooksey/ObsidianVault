## Card 1 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What are the Four Laws of Behavior Change?

> [!tip]- Answer
> (1) Make it Obvious — design the cue. (2) Make it Attractive — pair it with something desired. (3) Make it Easy — reduce friction. (4) Make it Satisfying — reward immediately. To break a bad habit, invert all four: invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying.

## Card 3 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** How did Leonardo synthesize arts and sciences, and what does the Mona Lisa require?

> [!tip]- Answer
> He believed you could not paint a great human figure without understanding anatomy, optics, geometry, and fluid dynamics — and vice versa. The Mona Lisa required: anatomy of facial muscles, how light hits curved surfaces, perspective geometry, and the psychology of emotion. His flying machines required bird anatomy, aerodynamics, and materials science.

## Card 5 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** Why is Buffett's compounding story the central lesson of The Psychology of Money?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 99% of Buffett's wealth came after his 60th birthday. He started investing at 10 and never stopped. His returns are not extraordinary — his duration is. The lesson: time in the game beats rate of return. Compounding is counterintuitive because the big numbers only appear at the very end.

## Card 6 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** Describe the four quadrants of the Time Management Matrix.

> [!tip]- Answer
> Q1: Urgent + Important = crises, deadlines (manage it). Q2: Not Urgent + Important = planning, relationships, health (invest here). Q3: Urgent + Not Important = most interruptions (minimize). Q4: Not Urgent + Not Important = trivia (eliminate). High performers deliberately build Q2 time — it prevents Q1 crises.

## Card 7 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What is Apple's Vertical Integration philosophy, and what does it trade off?

> [!tip]- Answer
> End-to-end control of hardware + software + retail is essential to a perfect, cohesive experience. Opposite of the Gates/Microsoft 'open systems' model. Vindicated by iTunes → iPod → iPhone → App Store flywheel. Trade-off: closed systems lock users in but deliver coherence Windows cannot match.

## Card 8 · The Subtle Art

**Q:** What is the 'Feedback Loop from Hell,' and what does it reveal about pursuing happiness?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Feeling anxious → feeling anxious about feeling anxious → feeling worse about feeling worse. The pursuit of positive experience is itself a negative experience; accepting negative experience is itself a positive one. Manson's inversion: stop trying to feel good all the time. That pursuit is the trap.
