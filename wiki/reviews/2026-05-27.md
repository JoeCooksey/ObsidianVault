## Card 1 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What does 1.01^365 equal, and what does it prove about habits?

> [!tip]- Answer
> ≈37.78. Improving 1% every day for a year makes you 37× better. Conversely, 0.99^365 ≈ 0.03 — you nearly disappear. Tiny daily choices compound dramatically in both directions. This is why systems beat goals: goals end; systems keep compounding.

## Card 2 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What are the four steps of the Habit Loop, in order?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Cue → Craving → Response → Reward. All habits follow this neurological cycle. Remove any step and the habit won't form. To build a habit: make the cue obvious, the craving attractive, the response easy, the reward satisfying. To break one: invert each.

## Card 3 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is the Maturity Continuum, and why does most self-help stop too early?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Dependence → Independence → Interdependence. Most self-help targets Independence ('I can do it'). But the highest leverage is Interdependence ('we can do it together') — results achievable in collaboration exceed anything an individual can produce alone.

## Card 4 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is the difference between Circle of Concern and Circle of Influence?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Circle of Concern = everything you worry about. Circle of Influence = what you can actually affect. Proactive people focus energy on their Circle of Influence (which grows as a result). Reactive people drain energy on their Circle of Concern (which stays the same or shrinks).

## Card 5 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What is the Reality Distortion Field, and what were its costs?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Jobs's ability to make the impossible seem expected — he could convince any team that 'can't do' was actually achievable. Named after Star Trek by Bud Tribble. It compressed timelines and forced breakthroughs (Mac, iPhone, iPad). Cost: delayed cancer surgery, damaged relationships, created a culture of fear. The RDF was inseparable from his greatness.

## Card 6 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What is Jobs's principle of Simplicity by Subtraction?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Simplicity is not removing decoration — it's deeply understanding complexity and eliminating everything extraneous. The iPod had one button. Focus means saying no to a hundred good ideas to say yes to one great one. Radical subtraction often requires more engineering, not less.

## Card 7 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** What is Isaacson's core argument about what made Leonardo exceptional?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Not talent — curiosity. Leonardo had no formal education beyond basic reading, writing, and math. His 7,200+ surviving notebook pages show relentless, methodical questioning: 'Describe the tongue of the woodpecker,' 'Observe the goose's foot.' Curiosity was his practice, not just his personality.

## Card 8 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** What is Sfumato, and what is its philosophical meaning beyond painting?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Sfumato (Italian: 'gone up in smoke') — Leonardo's painting technique of blurring edges, avoiding hard outlines, letting forms emerge from shadow. The Mona Lisa's smile is sfumato — it shifts as you look. Philosophically: the willingness to live in uncertainty without forcing premature resolution. Hold contradictions simultaneously.
