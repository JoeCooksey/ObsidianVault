## Card 1 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is the Goldilocks Rule, and why is boredom the real enemy of habits?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Peak motivation occurs when a task is at the edge of your current ability — not too easy (boring), not too hard (discouraging). Boredom, not failure, is the biggest threat to long-term habits. The most successful people show up even when the work feels tedious.

## Card 2 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What does Covey mean by Empathic Listening, and what blocks it?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Listening with intent to understand, not to reply. Requires reflecting both content AND feeling before responding. Four autobiographical blocks: evaluating (agreeing/disagreeing), probing (asking from your frame), advising (giving solutions), interpreting (projecting your experience). Most people listen through one of these filters instead of truly hearing.

## Card 3 · How to Win Friends

**Q:** What does Carnegie mean by 'give someone a reputation to live up to'?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Treat people as who they could be, not who they are. Tell someone they're known for their integrity, and they'll work to maintain it. This is identity-based influence — you shape behavior by shaping self-concept. Most people will rise to meet a stated belief about who they are.

## Card 4 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What is the Man in the Car Paradox?

> [!tip]- Answer
> People buy fancy cars expecting admiration. But observers admire themselves in the car — they imagine how they'd look driving it — not the driver. Status signals are projective. The wealth paradox: things we buy to signal success rarely generate the admiration we imagine. We want respect, not cars.

## Card 5 · Laws of Human Nature

**Q:** What is the Law of Envy, and why is it rarely acknowledged?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Envy is one of the most common and least admitted emotions. It disguises itself as criticism ('I'm just being honest'), advice ('for your own good'), or negativity. People rarely say 'I'm envious' — they rationalize it. Detecting envy (in others and yourself) is social intelligence because it reveals what someone truly values.

## Card 6 · Never Eat Alone

**Q:** What is the Follow-Up Formula?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Follow up within 24 hours of meeting someone. Reference a specific detail from your conversation (shows you listened). Give something (article, intro, resource) before asking for anything. The formula: quick + specific + give first. Most people never follow up — doing so once puts you in a tiny minority.

## Card 7 · The Subtle Art

**Q:** What is Manson's argument about certainty and growth?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Certainty is the enemy of growth. If you're sure you're right, you can't learn. The willingness to say 'I might be wrong' — and mean it — is not weakness, it's the prerequisite for improvement. Dogmatism is comfortable but terminal. Growth requires tolerating the discomfort of uncertainty.

## Card 8 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** How does Isaacson reframe Leonardo's legendary procrastination?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Leonardo's procrastination was often productive incubation — gathering more data, more observations, more connections. The Mona Lisa took 16 years. The distinction: some delays are evasion; some are incubation. Knowing the difference in your own work is a metacognitive skill. Don't mistake gathering for avoiding.
