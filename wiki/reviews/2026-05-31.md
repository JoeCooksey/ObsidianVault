## Card 1 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What are the Four Laws of Behavior Change?

> [!tip]- Answer
> (1) Make it Obvious — design the cue. (2) Make it Attractive — pair it with something desired. (3) Make it Easy — reduce friction. (4) Make it Satisfying — reward immediately. To break a bad habit, invert all four: invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying.

## Card 2 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is identity-based habit formation?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Every action is a vote for the person you are becoming. Rather than 'I want to run a marathon,' say 'I am a runner' and act accordingly. Identity-based habits are durable because they're self-confirming; goal-based habits collapse after the goal is hit.

## Card 3 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is the Maturity Continuum, and why does most self-help stop too early?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Dependence → Independence → Interdependence. Most self-help targets Independence ('I can do it'). But the highest leverage is Interdependence ('we can do it together') — results achievable in collaboration exceed anything an individual can produce alone.

## Card 4 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** Describe the four quadrants of the Time Management Matrix.

> [!tip]- Answer
> Q1: Urgent + Important = crises, deadlines (manage it). Q2: Not Urgent + Important = planning, relationships, health (invest here). Q3: Urgent + Not Important = most interruptions (minimize). Q4: Not Urgent + Not Important = trivia (eliminate). High performers deliberately build Q2 time — it prevents Q1 crises.

## Card 5 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** Why is Buffett's compounding story the central lesson of The Psychology of Money?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 99% of Buffett's wealth came after his 60th birthday. He started investing at 10 and never stopped. His returns are not extraordinary — his duration is. The lesson: time in the game beats rate of return. Compounding is counterintuitive because the big numbers only appear at the very end.

## Card 6 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What are Tail Events, and what do they mean for investing and career?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 1% of events drive 90% of outcomes. Most of the S&P's gains come from a handful of extraordinary years. Most of Amazon's revenue from a handful of products. Implication: expect most experiments to fail, don't quit the game before the tail event hits, and size your exposure so failure is survivable.

## Card 7 · Never Eat Alone

**Q:** What is Ferrazzi's core philosophy about networking?

> [!tip]- Answer
> The currency of real networking is not greed but generosity. Give before you need. The most connected people don't 'network' — they build genuine relationships by helping others without expectation of return. Transactional networking fails because people can smell the transaction.

## Card 8 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** What is Sfumato, and what is its philosophical meaning beyond painting?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Sfumato (Italian: 'gone up in smoke') — Leonardo's painting technique of blurring edges, avoiding hard outlines, letting forms emerge from shadow. The Mona Lisa's smile is sfumato — it shifts as you look. Philosophically: the willingness to live in uncertainty without forcing premature resolution. Hold contradictions simultaneously.
