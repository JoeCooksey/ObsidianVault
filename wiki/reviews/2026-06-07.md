## Card 1 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is the Goldilocks Rule, and why is boredom the real enemy of habits?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Peak motivation occurs when a task is at the edge of your current ability — not too easy (boring), not too hard (discouraging). Boredom, not failure, is the biggest threat to long-term habits. The most successful people show up even when the work feels tedious.

## Card 2 · How to Win Friends

**Q:** What does Carnegie mean by 'give someone a reputation to live up to'?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Treat people as who they could be, not who they are. Tell someone they're known for their integrity, and they'll work to maintain it. This is identity-based influence — you shape behavior by shaping self-concept. Most people will rise to meet a stated belief about who they are.

## Card 3 · Never Eat Alone

**Q:** What is the Pinging System?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Lightweight, ongoing contact that keeps relationships alive. A short email, article share, or congratulation note. Frequency beats intensity — a monthly ping beats a yearly dinner. The goal is to stay present without being a burden. Ping before you need something; never ping only when you need something.

## Card 4 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What is the Man in the Car Paradox?

> [!tip]- Answer
> People buy fancy cars expecting admiration. But observers admire themselves in the car — they imagine how they'd look driving it — not the driver. Status signals are projective. The wealth paradox: things we buy to signal success rarely generate the admiration we imagine. We want respect, not cars.

## Card 5 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What does Covey mean by Empathic Listening, and what blocks it?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Listening with intent to understand, not to reply. Requires reflecting both content AND feeling before responding. Four autobiographical blocks: evaluating (agreeing/disagreeing), probing (asking from your frame), advising (giving solutions), interpreting (projecting your experience). Most people listen through one of these filters instead of truly hearing.

## Card 6 · Laws of Human Nature

**Q:** What is Greene's Law of Irrationality, and why is it the foundation for all other laws?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Emotions color everything we perceive and reason, often without our awareness. We believe we're being rational, but emotion is the dominant force. Mastering others starts with mastering yourself — recognizing where your emotions are distorting your thinking. You can't read others accurately if you can't read yourself.

## Card 7 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** How did Leonardo train himself to observe?

> [!tip]- Answer
> He trained himself to see what others looked past: how water spirals when poured, how light diffuses through an eyelid, how lips form a smile. Spent four years dissecting corpses far beyond any contemporary artist's need. Would stare at a painting for hours to make one tiny change. Observation was an active, deliberate discipline — not passive looking.

## Card 8 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What is Apple's Vertical Integration philosophy, and what does it trade off?

> [!tip]- Answer
> End-to-end control of hardware + software + retail is essential to a perfect, cohesive experience. Opposite of the Gates/Microsoft 'open systems' model. Vindicated by iTunes → iPod → iPhone → App Store flywheel. Trade-off: closed systems lock users in but deliver coherence Windows cannot match.
