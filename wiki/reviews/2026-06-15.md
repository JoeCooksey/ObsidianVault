## Card 1 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is Habit Stacking, and what is the formula?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 'After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT].' You borrow the neural trigger of an existing habit to launch a new one. Example: 'After I pour my morning coffee, I will write in my journal for one minute.' The existing habit provides the cue automatically.

## Card 2 · How to Win Friends

**Q:** What is Carnegie's distinction between appreciation and flattery?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Appreciation is sincere, specific, and earned — it's true. Flattery is manufactured to get something — it's detectable. Sincere appreciation is one of the most powerful tools in human relations. The test: would you say it if you weren't trying to get anything? If yes, it's appreciation.

## Card 3 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What are Tail Events, and what do they mean for investing and career?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 1% of events drive 90% of outcomes. Most of the S&P's gains come from a handful of extraordinary years. Most of Amazon's revenue from a handful of products. Implication: expect most experiments to fail, don't quit the game before the tail event hits, and size your exposure so failure is survivable.

## Card 4 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is 'Win-Win or No Deal,' and when does it apply?

> [!tip]- Answer
> If two parties can't find a mutually beneficial solution, the best option is to agree to disagree rather than force a deal. No deal is better than a Win-Lose deal that breeds resentment. It applies to ongoing relationships — not one-time transactions — where trust compounds over time.

## Card 5 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** How did Leonardo train himself to observe?

> [!tip]- Answer
> He trained himself to see what others looked past: how water spirals when poured, how light diffuses through an eyelid, how lips form a smile. Spent four years dissecting corpses far beyond any contemporary artist's need. Would stare at a painting for hours to make one tiny change. Observation was an active, deliberate discipline — not passive looking.

## Card 6 · Laws of Human Nature

**Q:** What is the Law of Role-playing?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Everyone wears a social mask — a persona crafted for public consumption. People rarely reveal their real motivations. Learn to read what's beneath the mask: body language, microexpressions, behavioral patterns under pressure, how someone acts when they think no one is watching. Take the presentation as a starting point, not the truth.

## Card 7 · Never Eat Alone

**Q:** What is the Pinging System?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Lightweight, ongoing contact that keeps relationships alive. A short email, article share, or congratulation note. Frequency beats intensity — a monthly ping beats a yearly dinner. The goal is to stay present without being a burden. Ping before you need something; never ping only when you need something.

## Card 8 · The Subtle Art

**Q:** What does Manson mean by 'you are always choosing,' and how does it relate to responsibility?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Even if you didn't cause a problem, you are responsible for how you respond to it. Responsibility is not blame — it's recognition that you always have a choice in how you engage with your circumstances. Blaming others is comfortable but leaves you powerless. Taking responsibility returns agency to you.
