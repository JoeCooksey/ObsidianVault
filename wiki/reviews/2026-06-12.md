## Card 1 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What are the four renewal dimensions of Habit 7 (Sharpen the Saw)?

> [!tip]- Answer

## Card 2 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What is the First Rule of Compounding?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Don't interrupt it. The biggest financial mistakes are not picking wrong stocks — they're selling at the bottom, taking on too much debt, or otherwise leaving the game before compounding does its work. Good investing is less about brilliant picks and more about never making the catastrophic mistake.

## Card 4 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** How did Leonardo's 'outsider' status contribute to his creativity?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Born illegitimate, left-handed, gay, self-taught — he had no guild loyalty, no institutional allegiance, no disciplinary blinders. He approached every domain fresh, with no received wisdom to defer to. Isaacson's argument: the greatest cross-domain thinkers often sit outside the institutions that would otherwise constrain them.

## Card 5 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What are the Four Laws of Behavior Change?

> [!tip]- Answer
> (1) Make it Obvious — design the cue. (2) Make it Attractive — pair it with something desired. (3) Make it Easy — reduce friction. (4) Make it Satisfying — reward immediately. To break a bad habit, invert all four: invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying.

## Card 6 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What is the A-Player Principle?

> [!tip]- Answer
> B-players hire C-players (to avoid being shown up). A-players hire A-players. One B-player infects a team — others tolerate lower standards, and the bar drops. Jobs maintained A-player density through radical honesty about performance. He believed this was respect, not cruelty.

## Card 7 · How to Win Friends

**Q:** How does Carnegie's 'praise every improvement' principle connect to behavioral science?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Praise every improvement immediately and specifically. The signal must reach the person in the moment — a week later it loses power. This is operant conditioning: reinforce the specific behavior you want to see more of, right when it happens. The timing of the reward defines what behavior it reinforces.

## Card 8 · The Subtle Art

**Q:** What is Manson's core argument about values and problems?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Better values produce better problems. Problems never disappear — they upgrade. Choosing which problems you're willing to have (and work through) is more important than trying to eliminate problems. 'What are you willing to struggle for?' is a better life question than 'What do you want?'
