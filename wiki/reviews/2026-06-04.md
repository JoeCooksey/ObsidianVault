## Card 1 · Atomic Habits

**Q:** What is Habit Stacking, and what is the formula?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 'After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT].' You borrow the neural trigger of an existing habit to launch a new one. Example: 'After I pour my morning coffee, I will write in my journal for one minute.' The existing habit provides the cue automatically.

## Card 2 · How to Win Friends

**Q:** What is Carnegie's foundational principle about human motivation?

> [!tip]- Answer

## Card 3 · Leonardo da Vinci

**Q:** How did Leonardo synthesize arts and sciences, and what does the Mona Lisa require?

> [!tip]- Answer
> He believed you could not paint a great human figure without understanding anatomy, optics, geometry, and fluid dynamics — and vice versa. The Mona Lisa required: anatomy of facial muscles, how light hits curved surfaces, perspective geometry, and the psychology of emotion. His flying machines required bird anatomy, aerodynamics, and materials science.

## Card 4 · Never Eat Alone

**Q:** What is a Relationship Action Plan (RAP)?

> [!tip]- Answer

## Card 5 · Psychology of Money

**Q:** What are Tail Events, and what do they mean for investing and career?

> [!tip]- Answer
> 1% of events drive 90% of outcomes. Most of the S&P's gains come from a handful of extraordinary years. Most of Amazon's revenue from a handful of products. Implication: expect most experiments to fail, don't quit the game before the tail event hits, and size your exposure so failure is survivable.

## Card 6 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is the Maturity Continuum, and why does most self-help stop too early?

> [!tip]- Answer
> Dependence → Independence → Interdependence. Most self-help targets Independence ('I can do it'). But the highest leverage is Interdependence ('we can do it together') — results achievable in collaboration exceed anything an individual can produce alone.

## Card 7 · Steve Jobs

**Q:** What was Jobs's philosophy on arts and technology, and where did it come from?

The calligraphy class at Reed College → Mac's beautiful typography → the aesthetic revolution in computing. Aesthetic sensitivity was an engineering requirement at Apple.

## Card 8 · The 7 Habits

**Q:** What is 'Win-Win or No Deal,' and when does it apply?

> [!tip]- Answer
> If two parties can't find a mutually beneficial solution, the best option is to agree to disagree rather than force a deal. No deal is better than a Win-Lose deal that breeds resentment. It applies to ongoing relationships — not one-time transactions — where trust compounds over time.
