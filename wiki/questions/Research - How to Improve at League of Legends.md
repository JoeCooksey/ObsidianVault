# Research - How to Improve at League of Legends

## Overview

Two layers answered separately: **how to practice** (the research layer) and **what to get good at** (the skill layer). The research layer delivers the surprise: the best-controlled study finds **deliberate practice has a null effect on rank while plain volume of play predicts it** — so the guide-orthodoxy of drills and structured review only helps at the margin, never at the cost of games played. The skill layer is near-unanimous across guides: one role, 2–3 champions, laning fundamentals → wave management → macro/objectives → vision, with tilt control as the biggest rank-limiter below Diamond.

## Key Findings

1. **Volume first, structure second.** A pre-registered study of 411 LoL players found deliberate practice predicted rank at r = -.01 (null) while non-deliberate hours of ordinary play predicted it at r = .26 (Source: [[Martončik 2024 — Psychological Predictors of Esports Success]], high confidence). Riot API data on 913 expert players converges from a different method: Challenger players' signature is **consistent daily volume — more games/day, fewer zero days, low variability** — not exotic training (Source: [[Bennett 2024 — Practice Behaviours of Expert LoL Players]], medium — preprint, correlational). Reconciliation: ranked play already contains most of what deliberate practice adds (matchmade difficulty, fast feedback, forced variety). → [[LoL Improvement Practice System]]
2. **One role, 2–3 champions.** Top NA players average ~33 games per champion; benchmark = 30+ games at ≥50% win rate per pool champion. Shrinking decision load frees attention for macro. Champion-hopping with the meta resets learning every patch. (Sources: [[Dignitas — LoL Ranked Climbing and Tilt Guides]], [[Hotspawn — How to Get Good at LoL]])
3. **Laning is the trainable floor.** Target ~80 CS by 10 minutes (pros 8–10 CS/min, low elo 5–6); track CS@10/CS@15 — the same metrics pro analysts use. Trades follow archetype templates, and wave state is part of every trade calculation. Practice Tool drills exist for every weakness. (Source: [[Boosteria — LoL CSing Fundamentals]]) → [[Laning Fundamentals (LoL)]]
4. **Wave management is the most controllable macro lever.** Four states — freeze, slow push, crash, bounce — each mapping to a purpose (deny, build a timing window, reset, safety). Every roam, recall, and objective setup is downstream of wave state. → [[Wave Management (LoL)]]
5. **Macro = "what is the most valuable thing I can be doing right now?"** asked every ~20 seconds, over five pillars (information, time, space, resources, conversion). **Objectives > KDA** is the hardest low-elo lesson; the 60-30-10 rule (push 60 s before objective spawn, sweep vision at 30 s, position at 10 s) operationalizes setup. (Sources: [[Boosteria — LoL Macro Play Guide]], [[Hotspawn — How to Get Good at LoL]]) → [[LoL Macro Play]]
6. **Vision is a team-wide job and denial counts double.** Phase-based ward maps, control ward on nearly every back, Oracle Lens to clear. Minimum viable habit: 3+ wards placed, 2+ cleared per game. (Source: [[WeCoach — Vision Control Ward Guide]]) → [[Vision Control (LoL)]]
7. **Tilt is the biggest rank-limiter below Diamond.** Countermeasures are structural, not willpower: 3–5 game sets, stop-loss at 2 straight losses, chat muted, grade decisions not LP, sleep/hydration on schedule. (Source: [[Dignitas — LoL Ranked Climbing and Tilt Guides]]) → [[Tilt (Esports)]]
8. **The session template** that survives the evidence: 5–10 min warm-up → loading-screen game plan (both teams' win conditions) → one quantifiable focus goal → 3–5 game set → ~10 min self-only VOD review ("was the play a good idea?" before "did I execute it?") → stop. → [[LoL Improvement Practice System]]

## Contradictions

- **Guides vs research on practice structure.** Every guide prescribes drills, goals, and review ([[Dignitas — LoL Ranked Climbing and Tilt Guides]], [[Hotspawn — How to Get Good at LoL]], [[Boosteria — LoL CSing Fundamentals]]); the registered report finds structured practice doesn't predict rank ([[Martončik 2024 — Psychological Predictors of Esports Success]]). Resolution: the measurement is self-report and cross-sectional, so structure may still help at the margin — but the burden of proof flipped. Drills must never displace games.
- **Consistency: cause or effect?** Bennett 2024's Challenger consistency pattern is correlational — top players may play daily because they're already good (or full-time). Treat "mirror the Challenger schedule" as a reasonable bet, not proven causation.

## Open Questions

- Role-specific improvement paths (jungle pathing, support roaming) — sources covered laner fundamentals; jungle macro is its own literature.
- Whether coaching/VOD review shows measurable rank gains in any controlled study — nothing found either way.
- Current-patch specifics (item builds, meta champions) — deliberately excluded; they expire. The filed material is system-level.
- Optimal session length / games-per-day dose before quality degrades — Bennett shows consistency matters, not the ceiling.
