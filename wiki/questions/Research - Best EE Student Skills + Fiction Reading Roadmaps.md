# Research: Best EE Student Skills + Fiction Reading Roadmaps

## Overview
Two questions, one answer. **(1) The best skills for a college EE student** rank in a clear order — math fluency first, then circuit intuition, one programming language, lab tools, a specialty, and communication. **(2) "Fiction books to learn these skills"** has an honest catch: *no novel teaches you a Laplace transform.* What hard science fiction trains is the **engineering mind** — constraint-driven problem solving, systems thinking, ethics, and motivation. So the deliverable is a real skill roadmap **plus** a fiction roadmap that runs underneath it as the mindset layer.

## Key Findings

### Skills
- **Math is the gatekeeper, not just a prereq.** EE *is* Fourier/Laplace/phasors; weak math fluency is the wall students hit at Circuits and Signals. Treat calculus as a professional tool. (Source: [[Research - First-Year ASU EE Skills]])
- **The core technical stack is settled:** circuit analysis & design, signal processing, and programming (Python + C/C++, with MATLAB upper-division). Named in every 2026 skills survey. (Source: [[Research.com — Most Valuable EE Degree Skills]])
- **Lab-tool fluency separates candidates** in early internship interviews: multimeter → breadboard → oscilloscope, plus LTspice and Git. Most students learn these late; self-teaching them early is visible advantage. (Source: [[Research - First-Year ASU EE Skills]])
- **Communication is ranked the #1 skill by employers** — ahead of any specific technical tool; >90% prioritize teamwork. Control systems shows ~69% YoY job growth. (Source: [[Siemens — 7 In-Demand EE Skills]])
- Full ranked tiers (S → C, all four years, including the 2026 AI/WBG frontier) → [[EE Student Skill Stack (Ranked)]].

### Fiction
- **Fiction builds the mindset, not the math.** Be honest about this: the skills come from textbooks and labs. Fiction supplies disposition + motivation, and many practicing engineers trace their careers to a book. (Source: [[Public Books — 16 Books That Make You Want to Be an Engineer]])
- **[[Andy Weir]] is the on-ramp** — *The Martian* and *Project Hail Mary* model constraint-driven problem solving chapter by chapter; the plot *is* engineering.
- **The map runs:** constraint-solving (Weir) → systems thinking (*Dune*, *Seveneves*) → first-principles/build-from-scraps (*Rocket Boys*, *Flight of the Phoenix*) → strategy (*Ender's Game*, *Three-Body Problem*) → ethics (*Cat's Cradle*, *Frankenstein*, *I, Robot*). Full map + reading order → [[Fiction Roadmap for EE Skills (Sci-Fi to Skill Map)]].
