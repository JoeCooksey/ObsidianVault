# Research: Habits That Impact Testosterone

## Overview
A review of the modifiable habits that raise or lower testosterone in men, weighted by effect size and evidence quality. The dominant theme: in a healthy young man, **removing suppressors** (fat, poor sleep, alcohol, stress) matters far more than chasing "boosters" — the HPG axis already runs near its ceiling, so the big wins come from reversing deficits. See the two action lists: [[Testosterone-Boosting Habits to Include]] and [[Testosterone-Lowering Habits to Avoid]]. Mechanism backbone in [[Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals]].

## ✅ Beneficial habits to INCLUDE (best → corrective)
1. **Lose excess body fat** — biggest lever; BMI 30→25 ≈ +13–15% T (Source: [[Weight Loss and Testosterone Review 2024]])
2. **Sleep 7–9 h** — reverses the −10–15% sleep-loss penalty (Source: [[Leproult Van Cauter Sleep Restriction RCT 2011]])
3. **Resistance training** — acute spike + body-comp benefit; little chronic resting-T change in active adults (Source: [[Exercise and Testosterone Meta-analyses]])
4. **Eat adequate dietary fat (~≥20–35% kcal)** — avoid the low-fat T drop (Source: [[Low-Fat Diet Testosterone Meta-analysis 2021]])
5. **Eat enough protein + total calories** — protects the HPG axis from low-energy shutdown
6. **Correct zinc / vitamin-D / magnesium deficiencies** — restores T only if deficient (Source: [[Micronutrients and Testosterone]])
7. **Manage chronic stress** — removes cortisol suppression (Source: [[Cortisol and Testosterone Suppression]])

## ❌ Damaging habits to AVOID (worst → least)
1. **Excess body fat / sedentary obesity** — large, self-reinforcing; causal (Source: [[Weight Loss and Testosterone Review 2024]])
2. **Chronic short sleep / untreated sleep apnea** — −10–15% (Source: [[Leproult Van Cauter Sleep Restriction RCT 2011]])
3. **Heavy / chronic alcohol** — −7% (moderate) up to −50% (chronic heavy) (Source: [[Alcohol and Testosterone Review 2023]])
4. **Chronic psychological stress** — cortisol suppresses GnRH/LH + Leydig cells (Source: [[Cortisol and Testosterone Suppression]])
5. **Endocrine disruptors — plastics, phthalates, BPA** — negatively associated with T; direct Leydig-cell harm (Source: [[Endocrine Disruptors and Testosterone]])
6. **Very-low-fat dieting** — total-T SMD −0.38 (Source: [[Low-Fat Diet Testosterone Meta-analysis 2021]])
7. **Endurance overtraining without fueling** — exercise-hypogonadal male condition (Source: [[Exercise and Testosterone Meta-analyses]])
8. **Smoking/nicotine** — net harmful despite mixed cross-sectional T data (Source: [[Smoking Cannabis and Testosterone]])
9. **Cannabis** — slight decrease; harms sperm (Source: [[Smoking Cannabis and Testosterone]])

## Contradictions
- **Smoking**: cross-sections show *higher* measured T in smokers, yet smoking harms long-run reproductive health and T. The acute/measured signal contradicts the long-run health verdict — net call is "avoid" (Source: [[Smoking Cannabis and Testosterone]]).
- **Exercise**: popular framing ("lifting boosts testosterone") is contradicted by meta-analyses showing **negligible chronic resting-T change** in active adults — the real benefit is indirect via body composition (Source: [[Exercise and Testosterone Meta-analyses]]).
- **Vitamin D**: strong associational link but a null RCT in non-deficient men — association ≠ causation (Source: [[Micronutrients and Testosterone]]).

## Open Questions
- Magnitude of habit effects in **already-healthy young men specifically** (most data is in deficient/older/obese cohorts) — likely smaller than headline numbers.
- Real-world causal magnitude of **endocrine disruptors** in humans (controlled dosing is unethical).
- **"Testosterone-boosting" supplements** (ashwagandha, fenugreek, tribulus, D-aspartic acid) not deep-dived — most show weak/no effect in healthy men; flagged for a future pass.
- Effect of **dietary sugar / refined carbs** on acute T — touched on (insulin/weight pathway) but not isolated here.
