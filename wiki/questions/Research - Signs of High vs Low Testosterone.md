# Research: Signs of High vs Low Testosterone

## Overview
The signs of testosterone imbalance differ by sex and direction. In **men**, low T shows up as sexual + non-specific symptoms (most of which are unreliable), while symptomatic high T is almost always from steroids/TRT and — via a feedback paradox — produces some of the *same* signs as deficiency. In **women**, the clinically important problem is **high** T (hyperandrogenism, usually PCOS), which masculinizes; "low T in women" is not an established syndrome. The throughline: **symptoms tell you when to test, not what your level is — only morning bloodwork confirms it.**

## Men — at a glance

| Domain | Low testosterone | High testosterone (usually exogenous) |
|---|---|---|
| Sexual | ↓libido, ED, ↓morning erections, infertility | ↑libido, but infertility + ↓sperm |
| Testes | small/shrinking | **shrinking** (axis suppressed) |
| Breast | gynecomastia | gynecomastia (aromatized to estrogen) |
| Hair/skin | ↓body & facial hair | acne, oily skin, faster balding |
| Body | ↓muscle, ↑fat | rapid muscle gain, fluid retention |
| Mood | low mood, irritable, brain fog | aggression, mood swings, insomnia |
| Blood/CV | anemia | **polycythemia**, ↑BP, CV risk |
| Bone | osteoporosis (chronic) | — |

Note the overlap: **shrinking testes, infertility, and gynecomastia appear at both extremes** — because exogenous testosterone shuts down the body's own production ([[Testosterone Physiology Fundamentals|HPG axis]]). Detail: [[Low Testosterone Signs and Symptoms (Men)]], [[High Testosterone Signs (Men)]].

## Women — at a glance
Low T in women is not a recognized diagnosis; the signs that matter are **high** T:
- **Mild:** hirsutism (the most reliable sign), acne, scalp hair thinning, irregular periods, reduced fertility.
- **Virilizing (severe / abrupt = red flag):** clitoromegaly, voice deepening, ↑muscle, breast atrophy.
- **Threshold:** testosterone **> 5 nmol/L (≈144 ng/dL)** → rule out an androgen-producing tumor. Most common cause overall: **PCOS**. Detail: [[Testosterone Signs in Women (High and Low)]].

## Key Findings
- **Symptoms diagnose nothing on their own.** Diagnosis requires symptoms/signs *plus* unequivocally low (or high) blood levels (Source: [[Endocrine Society 2018 — Testosterone Therapy Guideline]], high).
- **Only the three sexual symptoms** (low libido, fewer morning erections, ED) track reliably with low T in men; fatigue/mood/concentration are non-specific and mislead (Source: [[Specific vs Non-Specific Symptoms of Low Testosterone]], high).
- **Test correctly:** fasting **morning** total T, **confirmed on a second morning**; add free T if borderline or SHBG is altered. Normal adult-male range ≈ **300–1,000 ng/dL** (Source: [[Endocrine Society 2018 — Testosterone Therapy Guideline]], high).
- **Symptomatic high T in men ≈ exogenous T / anabolic steroids**, not a natural state; watch for acne + muscle gain + polycythemia alongside shrinking testes (medium).
- **In women, hirsutism is the most effective single sign of androgen excess**; abrupt virilization with male-range testosterone means rule out a tumor (Source: [[Hirschberg 2023 — Hyperandrogenism in Postmenopausal Women]], high).

## Contradictions
- **"Low T in women" as a treatable syndrome.** Wellness marketing promotes it; the endocrine evidence does not — no defined syndrome, and androgen/DHEA therapy shows no consistent benefit in normal women (per PubMed, Wierman 2022, DOI [10.1210/clinem/dgac130](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgac130)). The narrow exception is **HSDD after menopause** (medium). The high-quality endocrine position wins here.
- **High T = pure virility.** In reality symptomatic high T (steroids/TRT) carries infertility, testicular atrophy, gynecomastia, polycythemia, and cardiovascular risk — the body's homeostasis penalizes the override.

## Open Questions
- Joe's own context (a young man) isn't a stated concern — this is filed as general reference, not a personal flag.
- Exact lab thresholds vary by assay and lab; "free T" formulas disagree at the margins.
- The male high-T symptom list rests on TRT/AAS and patient-education literature, not controlled studies of constitutionally high-T men (medium confidence).
- Borderline/age-related "low-normal" T and whether mild symptoms justify treatment remains genuinely contested in the guidelines.
