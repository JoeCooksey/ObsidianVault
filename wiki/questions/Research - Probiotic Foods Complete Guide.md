## 8 Key Findings

1. **Milk kefir is the single most strain-diverse probiotic food** — up to 61 distinct bacterial and yeast strains vs 5–7 in most yogurts or supplements. The yeast component (Kluyveromyces marxianus) provides antifungal properties and B vitamins not replicable by bacterial supplements alone.

2. **Natto stands alone for cardiovascular + bone benefits** — *Bacillus subtilis* var. natto produces MK-7 (Vitamin K2) and nattokinase found in no other food in meaningful concentrations. Natto consumption in Japan is directly correlated with dramatically lower CVD mortality. MK-7 deficiency is endemic in Western diets. The spore-forming bacteria also survive stomach acid at near-100%.

3. **Strain specificity beats CFU count** — *L. rhamnosus* GG at 10 billion for antibiotic-associated diarrhea outperforms any random 100-billion-CFU generic blend. The evidence for most probiotic benefits is strain-specific. Condition-matched strains are what makes clinical studies work.

4. **Kimchi is a built-in synbiotic** — the garlic in kimchi contains inulin (a prebiotic) that feeds the L. plantarum probiotics fermenting the same kimchi. No other common probiotic food naturally combines probiotic organism + prebiotic substrate in the same package. Anti-cancer compounds (sulforaphane + capsaicin) add a layer beyond gut health.

5. **Pasteurization is the critical factor for commercial probiotic foods** — sauerkraut and kimchi sold in most supermarkets are pasteurized (legally required for extended shelf life), which kills all live cultures. The only valid purchase criterion is "raw" or "refrigerated" or "live cultures" on the label. This explains why commercial sauerkraut has almost zero probiotic benefit.

6. **The gut-brain axis is the most exciting frontier in probiotics** (2025 status) — three mechanisms are now established: (1) vagus nerve direct neural signaling from gut bacteria, (2) 95% of serotonin synthesized in the gut by bacteria, (3) short-chain fatty acids from fermentation cross the blood-brain barrier. Psychobiotics (mental health-targeted probiotic strains) are ~3–5 years from clear clinical protocols, but daily fermented food intake is the accessible current intervention.

7. **Miso must be added to cooled food** — heat above 115°F destroys probiotics. The widespread practice of adding miso to boiling soup kills all the live cultures; maximum benefit requires adding to cooled/warm dishes. The fermentation-derived amino acid profile and antioxidants survive heat, but the probiotic benefit does not.

8. **DIY probiotic food is dramatically cheaper and higher quality than supplements** — milk kefir costs ~$0.30/cup vs $4 store-bought; sauerkraut costs ~$0.50/jar vs $8; yogurt costs ~$0.40/cup vs $2. All three are fresher, higher in live cultures, and can be customized. One-time equipment cost ~$47 → lifetime of free high-quality probiotics. Supplements remain useful for condition-specific strain targeting (e.g., *B. longum* 35624 for IBS).
