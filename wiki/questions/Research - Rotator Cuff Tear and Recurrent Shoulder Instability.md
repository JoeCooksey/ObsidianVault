# Research: Rotator Cuff Tear and Recurrent Shoulder Instability (What to Do)

> [!warning] This is research, not medical advice. A shoulder that repeatedly "pops out" should be evaluated in person by an **orthopedic shoulder surgeon or sports-medicine physician**, ideally with an **MRI (MR-arthrogram)** and, if bone loss is suspected, a **CT**. The right fix depends on exactly which structure is damaged — which only imaging + exam can establish.

## Overview
A shoulder that "tore once and pops out ever since" is almost certainly **chronic shoulder instability**, usually from a torn labrum (a [[Bankart Lesion]]) and stretched capsule — **not the rotator cuff itself**. The rotator cuff causes pain/weakness; the labrum/capsule is the "doorstop" that, once torn, lets the joint keep sliding out. (The two can coexist, especially over age 40–60.) The popping won't reliably stop on its own because a torn labrum doesn't reseal in the right position. (Source: [[Shoulder Instability vs Rotator Cuff Tear]])

## Key Findings
- **"Pops out" = instability, not a cuff tear.** Bankart (labral) lesions are present in ~90% of traumatic anterior dislocations; concurrent rotator cuff tears occur in ~25.7%, mostly in older patients. (Source: [[Shoulder Instability vs Rotator Cuff Tear]])
- **Strengthening helps symptoms but can't re-attach a torn labrum.** In young/active people with a traumatic Bankart lesion, conservative care leaves a **~53–67% re-dislocation rate** vs **~8–10% with arthroscopic repair**. (Sources: [[Hu et al 2023 — Bankart vs Conservative First-Time Dislocation Meta-analysis]], [[Hurley et al 2020 — Bankart vs Conservative Meta-analysis]])
- **There IS a durable long-term fix — surgical stabilization.** [[Arthroscopic Bankart Repair]] re-attaches the labrum (best for little/no bone loss). [[Latarjet Procedure]] (coracoid bone block) is for significant glenoid bone loss, failed repairs, or high-risk patients — long term it holds **~90% stable vs ~64%** for Bankart at 15 years. (Source: [[Delgado et al 2025 — Bankart vs Latarjet Long-Term Follow-up]])
- **Waiting makes surgery work worse.** Each additional dislocation damages more labrum and bone; operating after 2+ dislocations carries **~3.8× the failure odds** vs fixing it after the first. (Source: [[Gasparini et al 2015 — Predictors of Recurrence After Arthroscopic Stabilization]])
- **For a degenerative cuff (different problem):** repair is reasonable when symptoms persist and the tear is repairable; small/partial/irreparable tears often do well with PT. For cuff *pain*, a supervised exercise course is no better than a single advice session, and steroid shots give no long-term benefit. (Sources: [[Narvani et al 2020 — Degenerative Rotator Cuff Tear Repair or Not]], [[GRASP Trial 2021 — Exercise vs Advice for Rotator Cuff Disorders]])

## A practical path (general framework)
1. **Get the right diagnosis.** See a shoulder/sports-med doctor; get an **MRI/MR-arthrogram** to see if it's the labrum, capsule, cuff, or bone. This step decides everything.
2. **Try focused rehab (~6–12 weeks)** — rotator cuff + scapular + proprioception work, plus avoiding the abduction/external-rotation "danger position." This is enough for many (especially atraumatic/multidirectional or low-demand shoulders). (Source: [[Shoulder Instability Conservative Rehab]])
3. **If it keeps popping out despite rehab** — particularly if you're young/active with a labral tear on MRI — that's the signal to discuss **stabilization surgery** rather than repeating PT forever. This is the "long-term fix" being asked about.
4. **Bankart vs Latarjet** is chosen from measured **bone loss**, activity level, and laxity (ISIS score, glenoid-track assessment). Little bone loss → Bankart; significant bone loss / failed repair / high recurrence risk → Latarjet. (Source: [[Shoulder Instability Recurrence Risk Factors]])

## Contradictions
- **Surgery vs conservative for a first dislocation** is genuinely debated *as a default*: surgery slashes recurrence, but long-term function is similar and not everyone re-dislocates, so it isn't mandatory for all (Source: [[Hu et al 2023 — Bankart vs Conservative First-Time Dislocation Meta-analysis]]). However, for an **already-recurrent** shoulder (the situation described here), the case for stabilization is much stronger than for a one-time dislocation.
- The large **UK TASH-D** cohort found higher real-world re-dislocation even after surgery (~20%) than tightly-controlled trials, but it was underpowered/confounded — direction (younger = worse) still held.

## Open Questions
- **Which structure is actually torn in this shoulder?** Unanswerable without imaging — labrum vs cuff vs bone changes the whole plan.
- **How much glenoid bone loss is present?** Determines Bankart vs Latarjet; needs CT.
- **Age, activity level, and hyperlaxity** of the person asking — these set recurrence risk and weren't specified.
- Biologics/PRP for cuff or labral healing: not established as a long-term fix in current evidence.
