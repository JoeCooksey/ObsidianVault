# Research: Summer of Freshman Year Projects (What to Work On)

## Overview

Joe asked what **clear projects or things to work on during the summer of his freshman year** of college. The vault already holds the supporting playbooks ([[EE Freshman Portfolio Strategy]], [[Python EE Project Ladder]], [[Compounding Daily Projects Tier List]], [[Research - Summer Part-Time Job Guide Livermore College Student]]); this run adds a focused, ranked answer backed by fresh 2024–2025 career sources. The deliverable is [[Freshman Summer Project Plan (Tier List)]]. **Bottom line: build one real power-electronics project end-to-end, lock a fall ASU FURI research spot, and publish both on GitHub** — then backfill with a paid job and getting ahead in math.

## Key Findings

1. **A freshman without an internship is not behind — self-directed work is the realistic high-value path.** Multiple career-services sources frame projects, research, and skill-building as full substitutes for a freshman internship that is genuinely hard to land (Source: [[Resume Without a Summer Internship — Ohio State ECS]], [[15 Ways to Make Summer Meaningful — JHU]]). **medium confidence.**
2. **One finished end-to-end project beats many half-built ones.** The universal portfolio signal is **quality over quantity — 3–5 strong pieces total**, each shown as problem → design → prototype → measured outcome with plots (Source: [[6 Inspiring EE Projects — NJIT]], NYU Tandon portfolio guidance). **high confidence** (consensus across sources).
3. **The single highest-fit EE artifact for Joe is a power converter / regulated bench supply** — design → LTspice → breadboard → bench measurement. It maps straight onto his WBG/power-electronics track and is buildable in a summer (Source: [[6 Inspiring EE Projects — NJIT]]; reinforces the Deep-Work S-tier in [[Research - Long-Term Compounding Daily Projects]]). **medium confidence.**
4. **Faculty research beats a generic internship for a freshman — and Joe's school funds it.** ASU FURI is open to freshmen from their **2nd semester**, paying $1,500/semester + $400 supplies; the bottleneck is securing a faculty mentor (Source: [[ASU FURI — Fulton Undergraduate Research Initiative]]). The summer move is to cold-email power-electronics professors and submit for fall. **high confidence.**
5. **Go deep on ONE tool, not five shallow ones.** Pick KiCad, Arduino/STM32 embedded, or Python `control`, and produce a portfolio piece with it (Source: [[15 Ways to Make Summer Meaningful — JHU]]). **medium confidence.**
6. **A paid job — ideally STEM tutoring — still belongs in the mix.** It signals responsibility and funds the summer; non-engineering work counts too (Source: [[Resume Without a Summer Internship — Ohio State ECS]]; tactics in [[Research - Summer Part-Time Job Guide Livermore College Student]]). **medium confidence.**
7. **Public building is the multiplier.** A GitHub log turns project work into skill practice + public artifact + career evidence simultaneously — *permissionless leverage* (Source: [[Compounding Daily Projects Tier List]]). **medium confidence.**
8. **Low-effort compounders run in parallel:** automated index-fund DCA ([[Index Fund Investing]]), fitness/sleep ([[Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol]]), and 2–3 books. These cost almost no focus and pay off for years.

## Contradictions

- **Internship vs. research vs. projects.** Generic advice says "get an internship," but freshman-specific sources agree internships are hard at this stage and that **research (FURI/REU) + self-driven projects** are the realistic high-value path. Resolved in favor of the freshman-specific view — it matches the decision Joe actually faces. (Source: [[Resume Without a Summer Internship — Ohio State ECS]] vs. generic resume guides.)
- **Volume vs. depth of projects.** Some lists push many projects/certs; hiring-side sources favor 3–5 deep pieces and one finished end-to-end build. Resolved toward depth.
