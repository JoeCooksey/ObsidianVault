# Research: Mimicking Ice Cream for Bears (Honey Custard)

## Overview
**"Ice Cream for Bears"** is a St. Louis "farm-to-cone" brand, not a treat for actual bears. Its whole identity is a **3-ingredient honey custard**: grass-fed milk + raw unfiltered honey + egg yolks, with no gums, stabilizers, or refined sugar. Mimicking it = making a **French-style honey custard ice cream** at home.

## Key Findings
- Base ingredients: **grass-fed/grass-grazed organic milk, raw unfiltered honey (only sweetener), organic free-range egg yolks** (Source: [[Ice Cream for Bears (official site)]]; Source: [[Feast Magazine — Ice Cream for Bears]]).
- **Egg yolks + honey replace gums/stabilizers** as natural emulsifiers — that's how they keep a clean label.
- Home clone ratio: **3 yolks : ½ cup honey : 1 cup milk : 2 cups cream** + salt + vanilla; cook to a custard, chill 6–24 h, churn (Source: [[The Cook's Treat — Vanilla Honey Custard Ice Cream]]).
- **Honey is hygroscopic** → lowers freezing point → the pint stays **scoopable from the freezer**; it also keeps the custard thinner and ~1.25–1.5× as sweet as sugar.
- Full method, ratios, and flavor riffs: [[Honey-Sweetened Custard Ice Cream]].
- A [[Ninja CREAMi]] can stand in for a churn (freeze the chilled base solid, then spin).

- Search noise conflates three different "ice cream for bears": (1) **the St. Louis brand** ← the actual target; (2) bear-*shaped/themed* human desserts (Edy's/Dreyer's); (3) frozen gummy-bear snacks. None are enrichment food for live bears.
