# Research: Best Way to Develop Problem-Solving Skills

## Overview

The cognitive science is clear and slightly counterintuitive: there is **no general, content-free "problem-solving skill"** you can train in the abstract and use everywhere. Better problem solving = **deep, organized domain knowledge** + a **deliberate process** (understand → plan → struggle → look back) + **metacognition**. You build it by solving many real problems in real domains and reflecting on their deep structure — not with brain-training games. The actionable routine lives in [[Problem-Solving Skill-Building Protocol]].

## Key Findings

- **Expertise is organized knowledge, not raw reasoning.** Masters win by recognizing patterns from thousands of stored schemas; novices fall back on weak general methods (means–end analysis) that overload working memory. (Source: [[Expert–Novice Differences in Problem Solving]], [[Maries & Singh 2023 — Physics Problem-Solving Review]]) — **high**
- **Domain knowledge is the dominant factor.** Tricot & Sweller: domain-specific knowledge is possibly *the only* factor determining acquired cognitive performance. Generic critical-thinking courses don't transfer. (Source: [[Neelen & Kirschner 2018 — Domain-Specific Knowledge 1, Generic Skills 0]]) — **high**
- **But a transferable *process* helps — on top of domain knowledge.** [[Pólya's Problem-Solving Method|Pólya's four steps]] and ~67 heuristics (work backwards, solve a simpler problem, analogy, look back) raise your hit rate within domains you know. (Source: [[Polya 1945 — How to Solve It]]) — **high**
- **Far transfer is rare; brain training fails.** An 87-study meta-analysis found working-memory/brain training gives near transfer only — **no transfer to fluid intelligence or general reasoning**. There is no mental muscle to train. (Source: [[Near vs Far Transfer]], [[Melby-Lervag Redick Hulme 2016 — WM Training No Far Transfer]]) — **high**
- **Comparing examples builds the little transfer that's available.** Two analogous examples beat one; comparison strips surface features and exposes the deep schema (Gick & Holyoak 1983; Gentner 2003). (Source: [[Analogical Encoding (Comparing Cases)]]) — **high**
- **Match the method to your level.** Novices: study **worked examples** first (cuts cognitive load). Once you have footing: **struggle with the problem first**, then check (productive failure → deeper learning). (Source: [[Worked Examples and Productive Failure]], [[Teaching Discipline-Based Problem Solving (CBE-LSE 2022)]]) — **high**
- **Metacognition is a force multiplier.** Planning before, monitoring during, and a post-mortem after each problem predicts performance *even controlling for IQ*; it must be practiced explicitly. (Source: [[Metacognition in Problem Solving]]) — **high**
- **Struggle is supposed to feel hard.** Productive struggle and [[Desirable Difficulties|desirable difficulties]] feel worse but build durable, transferable skill. (Source: [[Teaching Discipline-Based Problem Solving (CBE-LSE 2022)]]) — **high**

## Contradictions

- **Strong domain-specificity (Kirschner/Sweller) vs. value of general heuristics (Pólya, metacognition research).** Resolution: domain knowledge dominates (~the necessary base), and process/metacognition add real value *on top of it* — they are multipliers, not substitutes. Both camps agree generic skill taught *without* domain content doesn't transfer. (See [[Domain-Specific Knowledge vs General Problem-Solving Skill]].)
- **Worked examples vs. productive failure** look opposed but are reconciled by the **expertise-reversal effect**: examples for novices, struggle-first for the more advanced.

## Open Questions

- How much does the *process* (Pólya/metacognition) transfer across very different domains for an individual, vs. needing re-learning in each? Evidence says "some, but limited."
- Optimal struggle timebox before consulting a solution — varies by learner and problem; not pinned down.
- Best concrete tooling for a self-learner to get fast feedback in math/EE (vs. a coach in classroom studies). Filed to [[Problem-Solving Skill-Building Protocol]] as a practical gap.
