# Research - Food Sourcing Guide (Organic, Grass-Fed, Wild-Caught)

## Overview
A complete, money-aware guide to **how to source food**: when the organic / grass-fed / pasture-raised / wild-caught premium is worth paying, which fruits and vegetables to buy based on pesticide testing (and the one self-test that personalizes it), and the best carb, protein, and fat sources to actually put in the cart. The unifying theme: **most sourcing premiums buy you *reduced exposure or better welfare*, not "more nutritious" food — so spend selectively on the few upgrades that matter and don't waste money on the rest.**

## Key Findings

### The 3 sourcing levers that actually matter (everything else is noise)
1. **Produce → buy the [[EWG Shopper's Guide to Pesticides in Produce (2025)|Dirty Dozen]] organic, the Clean Fifteen conventional.** Organic's defensible benefit is **~30% lower pesticide residue + lower cadmium**, *not* better nutrition or health outcomes (Source: [[Organic Nutrition Evidence (Stanford 2012 + Newcastle 2014)]]).
2. **Animal → [[Animal Product Sourcing (Grass-Fed, Pasture-Raised, Wild-Caught)|pasture-raised eggs + small wild fish]] are the high-ROI upgrades.** Grass-fed beef is marginal.
3. **Oils → buy genuine extra-virgin olive oil** (harvest-dated, single origin — fraud is common) and avoid all trans fats.

### Which fruits & vegetables to buy (the "test" answer)
- The test is **already done for you**: USDA tests ~47,000 produce samples; EWG ranks them. 95% of Dirty Dozen samples carried residue; ~60% of Clean Fifteen had none (Source: [[EWG Shopper's Guide to Pesticides in Produce (2025)]]).
- **Buy organic:** spinach, strawberries, kale/collard/mustard greens, grapes, peaches, cherries, nectarines, pears, apples, blackberries, blueberries, potatoes.
- **Buy conventional:** pineapple, sweet corn, avocado, papaya, onion, sweet peas, asparagus, cabbage, watermelon, cauliflower, banana, mango, carrot, mushroom, kiwi.
- **The personal test:** wear a **CGM for 2–4 weeks** to see which carbs spike *you* — glycemic response varies hugely between healthy people (Source: [[CGM in Healthy Populations (Metabolism 2023 + CGM-HYPE)]]). Mail-in IgG "food sensitivity" panels are **not validated** — use an elimination diet instead (Source: [[CGM Self-Testing for Food Response]]).

### Carb / protein / fat sources to buy
Full cart list in [[Macronutrient Sourcing Guide (What to Buy)]]:
- **Carbs:** oats, lentils/beans (bulk = cheapest), potatoes (organic), rice, **frozen organic berries**, Clean-Fifteen fruit conventional.
- **Protein:** **pasture-raised eggs + canned sardines/anchovies** (best value upgrades), salmon, Greek yogurt/cottage cheese, lean meats, legumes. Get omega-3 from **fatty fish, not beef**.
- **Fat:** **real EVOO**, avocado (conventional), bulk nuts/seeds, fatty fish, whole eggs.

### Grass-fed beef — the most over-hyped upgrade
2–5× more omega-3 sounds big but the absolute amount is **tiny** (~0.015 g vs 0.003 g per 3 oz; you'd need ~100× the beef to match one salmon). Buy it for taste/ethics, **not** health (Source: [[Grass-Fed vs Grain-Fed Beef (Healthline + PMC Review)]]).

### Egg & fish label decoder
- Eggs: **pasture-raised > free-range > cage-free > caged.** "Pasture-raised" has **no legal USDA definition** — it only means something with **Certified Humane** certification (Source: [[Egg and Fish Sourcing Labels (GoodRx + FDA)]]).
- Fish: **"small + wild" is the cheat code** — sardines, anchovies, wild salmon win on mercury *and* omega-3 *and* price. Avoid shark/swordfish/king mackerel/tilefish (high mercury).

## Contradictions
- **Is organic more nutritious?** [[Organic Nutrition Evidence (Stanford 2012 + Newcastle 2014)|Stanford 2012]] says no meaningful difference; [[Organic Nutrition Evidence (Stanford 2012 + Newcastle 2014)|Newcastle 2014]] found higher antioxidants/lower cadmium. **Resolution:** they *agree* on lower pesticide/heavy-metal exposure; they disagree on antioxidant magnitude, and neither shows a real-world health-outcome benefit. So sell organic as *exposure reduction*, not nutrition.
- **Mercury: wild vs farmed.** Some sources say wild has *more* mercury (bigger predators); others say farmed has less. **Resolution:** it's about **size, not wild/farmed** — small fish (wild or not) are low-mercury; large predators are high. "Small + wild" resolves both.
- **EWG's 2025 list ordering.** An early search summary claimed green beans/peppers topped 2025; two authoritative sources confirm the actual order is **spinach #1, strawberries #2** under the new toxicity-weighted methodology. The green-beans claim was a summary error.

## Open Questions
- **Long-term health outcomes of organic eating** are still unstudied (no RCT) — the case rests on exposure reduction, which is plausible but unquantified for an individual.
- **CGM benefit in healthy non-diabetics** is unproven beyond short-term behavior change — optimization, not medicine.
- **EWG methodology** is advocacy-driven; a regulator-grade toxicity-weighted ranking would be more authoritative. Absolute residue levels remain below EPA limits.
- Did not cover **regenerative/MSC/Fair-Trade certifications**, raw vs pasteurized dairy, or **budget-vs-organic tradeoffs by region/season** — possible follow-ups.
