# Research: The Cortisol Masterclass (Increase and Reduce)

## Overview
The "cortisol masterclass" is the **Huberman Lab episode "How to Control Your Cortisol & Overcome Burnout"** (Aug 2025). Its central, counterintuitive message: **cortisol is not bad — the goal is to fix its *rhythm* (high in the morning, low at night), not to minimize it.** So Joe's two asks map exactly onto the two halves of the rhythm: **increase = raise/time the morning peak; reduce = lower the evening/chronic level.**

## Key Findings
- **Cortisol's job is timing, not toxicity.** A steep morning rise + low evening trough = healthy energy, focus, mood, sleep. Most "cortisol problems" are a *flattened or shifted* rhythm (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]).
- **The Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR)** raises cortisol **50–156% in 30–45 min after waking**, triggered by the wake transition itself; a **robust CAR is healthy, a blunted CAR signals chronic stress/burnout** (Source: [[Stalder et al 2025 — Cortisol Awakening Response (Endocrine Reviews)]]).
- **To raise/time morning cortisol** (top levers): morning **sunlight within 30–60 min**, **consistent wake time**, **morning exercise**, **hydration + electrolytes**, and **delaying caffeine 60–90 min** so it doesn't blunt the natural peak (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]). → [[Ways to Raise Morning Cortisol (Tier List)]]
- **To reduce cortisol** (best-evidenced): protect **sleep + cut evening light**; **yoga/qigong, moderate intensity, 30–60 min, >3×/wk** (yoga ranked #1, SMD −0.59; **HIIT raised cortisol**) (Source: [[Li Huang Zhu 2025 — Exercise for Cortisol Network Meta-analysis]]); **ashwagandha 300–600 mg in the evening** (~11–32% cortisol drop, 15 RCTs) (Source: [[Bachour et al 2025 — Ashwagandha Cortisol Meta-analysis]]); **omega-3 2.5 g/day** (~19% lower stress-cortisol); slow breathing/NSDR; sauna; rhodiola. → [[Ways to Reduce Cortisol (Tier List)]]
- **Burnout has two opposite cortisol signatures:** early-phase = *high* morning cortisol (use NSDR, lower stress); late-phase = *flat/depleted* (rebuild the peak with light/exercise/timed caffeine) — so the right move depends on which way your rhythm is broken (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]).
- **Clinical extremes are different.** Chronically high = Cushing syndrome; chronically low = Addison's/adrenal insufficiency (**life-threatening untreated**) — both need a doctor, not lifestyle tweaks (Source: [[Cortisol — Cleveland Clinic]]).

## Contradictions
- **Ashwagandha's effect on *felt* stress is contested.** It reliably lowers the cortisol *number* across RCTs, but one 2025 meta-analysis found **no significant change in perceived stress** while others found a real reduction. Resolution: trust the biomarker effect; treat the subjective benefit as likely-but-variable (Source: [[Bachour et al 2025 — Ashwagandha Cortisol Meta-analysis]]).
- **"Lower cortisol" is not universally correct advice.** For a *flat* rhythm (late-phase burnout) the fix is to *raise* the morning peak. Generic "reduce your cortisol" content ignores this (Source: [[Huberman — How to Control Your Cortisol and Overcome Burnout]]).

## Open Questions
- Does deliberate **cold exposure** meaningfully raise cortisol, or mainly adrenaline/dopamine? Huberman leaves this less settled.
- The exercise meta-analysis studied people *in psychological distress*; effect sizes in already-calm individuals are unknown.
- Most "natural ways to raise cortisol" content targets sub-clinical low energy; there is little evidence that lifestyle meaningfully raises cortisol in true adrenal insufficiency (which requires replacement therapy).
