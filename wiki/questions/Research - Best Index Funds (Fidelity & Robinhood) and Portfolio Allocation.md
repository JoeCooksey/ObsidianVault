# Research: Best Index Funds (Fidelity & Robinhood) and Portfolio Allocation

> [!note] Educational research, not personalized financial advice. Confidence labels follow the vault's research program.

## Overview

Two questions: (1) which index funds to buy on Fidelity and Robinhood, and (2) how to split a portfolio between index funds and individual stocks. The decisive broker fact: **Fidelity offers index mutual funds (including 0.00% ZERO funds); Robinhood offers only ETFs.** Both let you index for free with different tickers. For the index-vs-stocks split, the evidence-backed answer is a [[Core-Satellite Portfolio]]: ~80–90%+ index core, ≤10–20% individual stocks.

## Key Findings

### Best funds on Fidelity (mutual funds)
- **Core (pick one):** FZROX (Total US, **0.00%**) or FXAIX (S&P 500, 0.015%) or FSKAX (Total US, 0.015%) (Source: [[Fidelity Index Funds Lineup (Fidelity.com)]]).
- FXAIX vs FSKAX is a near-tie — same 0.015% ER, near-identical returns; FSKAX adds mid/small caps (Source: [[White Coat Investor — FSKAX vs FXAIX]]).
- **International:** FZILX (0.00%) or FTIHX (0.060%). **Bonds (later):** FXNAX (0.025%).
- **ZERO funds catch:** 0.00% but not portable in-kind — leaving Fidelity means selling (taxable in a brokerage account; harmless in a Roth IRA). Perfect inside a Fidelity Roth IRA (Source: [[Fidelity Index Funds Lineup (Fidelity.com)]]).

### Best funds on Robinhood (ETFs only)
- Robinhood has **no mutual funds** — only ETFs (Source: [[NerdWallet — Robinhood Review 2026]]).
- **Core:** VOO (S&P 500, **0.03%**) or VTI (Total US, 0.03%). IVV ties VOO at 0.03%; SPY's 0.095% is worse for buy-and-hold (Source: [[NerdWallet — Top S&P 500 ETFs 2026]]).
- **International:** VXUS (~0.05%). **Bonds:** BND. All fully portable between brokers.

### Index funds vs individual stocks — the split
- **Core-satellite:** ~**80–90%+** in broad index funds, **≤10–20%** in individual stocks, sized so a total satellite loss doesn't change your trajectory (Source: [[Core-Satellite Portfolio]]).
- Why the cap is everything: ~90% of pros lose to the index long-term; the *typical* stock underperforms T-bills; retail active trading costs several %/yr — the core never plays that game and satellite losses are bounded ([[Index Fund Tier List]], [[Skewness of Stock Returns]]).
- For a beginner/young investor, **100% index funds is a completely valid answer.** Individual stocks are optional — a learning/engagement allocation, not a requirement.

### Stock/bond split by age
- ~19 years old → **~90–100% stocks, ~0–10% bonds**; matches what target-date funds actually hold for the young (Source: [[Asset Allocation by Age (Optimized Portfolio)]]).
- International = 20–40% of the stock sleeve. Add bonds gradually starting in your 40s ([[Asset Allocation by Age]]).

## Recommended Allocation (young, aggressive — illustrative)

```
ROTH IRA at Fidelity (the core — set-and-forget)
  ~70–80%  FZROX or FXAIX      US total market / S&P 500
  ~20–30%  FZILX                International
   0%      bonds                (add FXNAX in your 40s)

OPTIONAL satellite (≤10–20% of total, e.g. a Robinhood account)
  Individual stocks / tilts (QQQM, SCHD)
  Capped by flow; benchmarked annually vs VTI
```

This is the vault's existing [[Core-Satellite Portfolio]] applied to the specific brokers.

## Contradictions
- **"Best" core fund:** WCI marginally prefers FSKAX (total market) for diversification; NerdWallet treats VOO/FXAIX (S&P 500) as equally fine. Resolution: differences are noise — total-market vs S&P 500 returns are nearly identical; pick whichever the broker makes convenient (confidence: high).
