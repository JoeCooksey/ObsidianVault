# Research: Cooking Best-Tasting Scrambled Eggs and Ground Beef

## Overview
Two foods, **two opposite heat philosophies**. Great scrambled eggs come from cooking *gently and below browning temperature* to keep protein from over-tightening. Great ground beef comes from cooking *hot and dry, above 300°F*, to maximize the [[Maillard Reaction]]. Get the temperature philosophy right and everything else is detail.

## The one big idea
- **Eggs:** the enemy is overcooked protein. Slow it down (low heat, fat, gentle stirring, salt timing) and **pull early** — see [[Scrambled Egg Science]].
- **Beef:** the enemy is steaming instead of browning. Keep the surface dry and hot (hot pan, don't crowd, don't stir early, drain grease, baking soda) — see [[Ground Beef Browning Science]].

## Key Findings — Scrambled Eggs
- **Best-tasting style = soft French/Ramsay scramble:** cold pan + butter, no pre-whisk, **on-and-off the heat** every 30–60 s, finish with cold crème fraîche off-heat. (Source: [[Christopher Kimball — Creamiest Scrambled Eggs]]; [Gordon Ramsay/MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-make-the-perfect-scrambled-eggs-with-gordon-ramsay))
- **Fat coats proteins** → slower coagulation, creamier, overcook-resistant. Higher-fat dairy = creamier. (Source: [[Christopher Kimball — Creamiest Scrambled Eggs]])
- **Water → fluffier; dairy → creamier** — two different goals, ~1 Tbsp liquid/egg. (Source: [Food Republic](https://www.foodrepublic.com/1592835/water-versus-milk-fluffiest-scrambled-eggs/))
- **Carryover is real:** pull while glossy/underdone; cold crème fraîche stops the cooking instantly.
- **Kenji's starch hack** lets you go fast over higher heat and stay creamy. (Source: [[J. Kenji López-Alt]])

## Key Findings — Ground Beef
- **Maillard needs ~300°F+ and a dry surface.** Water (released moisture) caps the surface at 212°F → steaming. (Source: [[Ground Beef Browning Science]])
- **Don't crowd (~1 lb/10-in pan); preheat hot; leave undisturbed 3–4 min** to build crust before breaking up. (Source: [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2039950/avoid-ground-beef-mistakes/))
- **80/20–85/15 chuck** is the flavor/browning sweet spot; **drain pooled grease**.
- **Baking soda (¼ tsp/12 oz, rest 10–15 min)** raises pH → faster/deeper crust + less water loss. The single biggest trick. (Source: [[ATK — Baking Soda for Ground Beef]])
- **Bloom spices in the rendered fat** and **deglaze the fond** for the most flavor. (Source: [RawSpiceBar](https://rawspicebar.com/blogs/spices-101/best-ground-beef-seasoning-12-easy-blends-for-tacos-bowls-and-skillets))

## Pairings (the "stuff that goes well" ask)
- **Eggs:** cheese/herbs added at the end; sides of buttered sourdough, avocado, smoked salmon, bacon, salsa → [[Scrambled Egg Pairings and Add-Ins]].
- **Beef:** taco/bolognese/burger blends; serve with tortillas+guac+pico, pasta+parmesan, or mash → [[Ground Beef Seasoning and Pairings]].

## Key Concepts
- [[Maillard Reaction]] — the browning chemistry; the goal for beef, the enemy for eggs.
- [[Scrambled Egg Science]] / [[Ground Beef Browning Science]] — the two opposite heat philosophies.

## Contradictions
- **Salt timing for eggs:** Kenji/Keller salt *before* (softens protein, best if rested ~15 min); Ramsay salts *at the end* (prevents weeping). Testing shows mid-to-late salting most reliably prevents weeping; both extremes work if timed right. (See [[Scrambled Egg Science]].)
