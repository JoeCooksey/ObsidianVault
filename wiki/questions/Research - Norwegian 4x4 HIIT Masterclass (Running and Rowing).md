# Research: Norwegian 4x4 HIIT Masterclass (Running and Rowing)

## Overview
The Norwegian 4x4 is the most-studied HIIT protocol for raising VO2 max — **four 4-minute intervals at 85–95% HRmax, 3-minute active recoveries, ≈35–40 min total, 2×/week.** Developed at NTNU by [[Jan Helgerud]] & Jan Hoff and clinically validated by [[Ulrik Wisløff]]'s CERG, it delivers ~7–13% VO2 max gains in 8 weeks (≈2× moderate continuous training) and carries a longevity signal in a 5-year RCT. It works on any whole-body cardio modality; this masterclass covers **running** and **rowing**. Full hub: [[Norwegian 4x4 Interval Protocol]].

## The Protocol at a Glance
| Segment | Time | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 6–10 min | easy → ~70% HRmax |
| 4 × work | 4 min each | 85–95% HRmax, build rep 1→4 |
| 3 × recovery | 3 min each | active, ~60–70% HRmax |
| Cool-down | 3–5 min | easy |

## Key Findings
- **Format beats intensity.** Time above ~90% VO2 max drives gains; 4-min "long" intervals bank far more of it than 30-s "short" ones (~5.5 vs ~3.4 min above 90%), even when short reps are run 5% faster. The 4-min length is the point. (Source: [[Long vs Short Intervals — Time Above 90% VO2max 2025]])
- **The "95% HRmax" target is approximate.** Real telemetry peaks at ~93% HRmax in interval 4, and it takes ~1.3 min just to reach 85% in interval 1 — so start firm, don't ease in. ~80% of work time lands in-band; interval 1 contributes least. (Source: [[Edwardsen 2020 — Heart Rate Response to 4x4 Protocol]])
- **Pace for the 4th rep.** Target ~88–90% effort on rep 1 and climb 1–2% each rep; if you can't hold even output across all four, you started too hot. (Source: [[Norwegian 4x4 — PureGym Practical Guide]])
- **2×/week is the dose.** 1× maintains, 3× is the ceiling for the already-fit; more causes overreaching without benefit. 36–48 h between sessions. (Source: [[Myworkout — The Norwegian 4x4 Protocol]])
- **Big effect size.** ~7–13% VO2 max in 8 wk vs ~4–6% for MICT; replicated in obese (+10%/6 wk) and coronary-disease patients. (Source: [[Myworkout — The Norwegian 4x4 Protocol]])
- **It may extend life.** 5-yr RCT in 1,567 adults aged 70–77: HIIT arm showed a 2.9 pp absolute mortality reduction vs MICT and larger VO2 max gains — VO2 max is a top predictor of all-cause mortality. (Source: [[Generation 100 Study — HIIT and Mortality 2020]])

### Running execution → [[4x4 Intervals for Running]]
- Work pace = **harder than 5K pace**, repeatable ×4. Commit to pace immediately (HR lags 60–90 s).
- **Treadmill:** drive intensity with speed + **≥5% incline**; pre-set work/recovery speeds. Easiest way to learn pacing.
- **Outdoor:** flat track for splits, or a steady hill (jog down to recover) for lower impact.
- Recovery = easy jog / brisk walk, keep moving.

### Rowing execution → [[4x4 Intervals for Rowing]]
- Target by **watts / 500m split**, not just HR: ≈ **2K pace + 2–5 s/500m**.
- Work **28–32 spm**, recovery **18–20 spm**; power per stroke > rate.
- **Drag factor ~120–130** (damper ~3–5). If you can't hold 28–32 spm, drag is too high.
- Program Concept2 monitor: Intervals: Time → 4:00 / 3:00 ×4. Hold technique on rep 4. Lower-impact alternative to running 4x4.

## Contradictions
- **Short vs long intervals.** Joe's existing [[VO2 Max Interval Training]] page (cycling-leaning) says 30/15-s short intervals accrue *more* time-in-zone; the 2025 running study finds long intervals superior. Resolution: modality + rep count + structure interact. For the Norwegian 4x4 specifically, 4-min reps are evidence-supported. (Sources agree this is format-dependent.)
- **Intensity ceiling:** developers say "85–95% HRmax" / "up to 95%," but measured peaks sit ~93%. Treat 95% as a target you approach on the last rep, not a number to hit every rep.
- **Rest length:** Norwegian standard = 3 min; some erg variants use 2 min. Use 3 min to stay protocol-true.

## Open Questions
- Optimal block periodization for a runner *and* rower alternating modalities (combined cardiac load vs muscular specificity)?
- How does 4x4 interact with Joe's cycling training load — can erg 4x4 substitute on running rest days without overreaching?
- Transfer of elite-runner interval findings (small-n studies) to recreational athletes is only approximate.
- Long-term mortality benefit is borderline-significant (healthy-volunteer bias in Generation 100) — strong VO2 max evidence, softer survival evidence.
