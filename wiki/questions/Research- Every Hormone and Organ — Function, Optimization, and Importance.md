# Research: Every Hormone and Organ — Function, Optimization, and Importance

## Overview

A full sweep of the body's hormones and organs, what each does, how to optimize it, and how to rank them by importance. Two master reference pages hold the detail: [[Human Hormones Master Reference and Ranking]] (~40 hormones) and [[Human Organs Master Reference and Ranking]] (~20 major organs + expendable ones). The biggest finding: **importance is ranked differently for the two systems** — organs by survivability, hormones by physiological criticality — and the *optimization* levers collapse to the same short list for both.

## Key Findings

- The body makes **50+ hormones**; ~40 are clinically major. They are produced not only by classic glands but by **fat, gut, kidney, and liver**, which are major endocrine organs in their own right (Source: [[Cleveland Clinic — Hormones Overview]]).
- The control hierarchy is **hypothalamus → pituitary → target gland** — the "master controllers" sit in the brain (Source: [[Cleveland Clinic — Hormones Overview]]).
- There are exactly **five vital organs**: brain, heart, lungs, liver, kidneys. Everything else is survivable to lose or remove (Source: [[UF Physiology — Organs You Can Live Without]]).
- **Organs have a hard ranking criterion** (does failure kill you, can you live without it); **hormones do not** — almost any single hormone can be replaced medically, so their ranking reflects centrality, not lethality.
- **The brain outranks the heart** despite the heart killing faster, because the heart is replaceable (transplant/mechanical) and the brain is not — it is the seat of identity.
- **A handful of lifestyle levers move most hormones at once**: sleep, resistance + cardio exercise, protein + fiber, light/circadian timing, stress control (Source: [[Healthline — Balance Hormones Naturally]]). The same inputs (plus "don't smoke / limit alcohol") protect every vital organ.

## Importance ranking (the short answer)

**Organs (by survivability):**
1. Brain → 2. Heart → 3. Lungs → 4. Liver → 5. Kidneys *(S/A-tier, "the vital five")*
6. Pancreas, intestines, stomach, skin, bladder *(B-tier, adaptable)*
7. Spleen, gallbladder, appendix, tonsils *(C-tier, expendable)*

**Hormones (by survival criticality):**
1. Insulin → 2. Cortisol → 3. Thyroid (T3/T4) → 4. Aldosterone → 5. ADH *(S-tier)*
Then GH/IGF-1, glucagon, PTH, adrenaline, melatonin *(A-tier)*; sex hormones, leptin/ghrelin, EPO *(B-tier)*.

> [!note] Two different meanings of "important"
> For **survival**, the S-tiers above win. For **optimization payoff** in a healthy person, the practical order is closer to: insulin sensitivity → cortisol rhythm → thyroid → sex hormones → growth hormone → melatonin, and brain/heart/metabolic-organ health via exercise. The user asked for both function and optimization, so both framings are filed.

## Contradictions
- Source counts of "vital organs" vary between **5 and 6** — some lists add the **small intestine** as a sixth (you cannot survive total loss of it without TPN). The 5-organ list (brain/heart/lungs/liver/kidneys) is the consensus; the small intestine is a defensible sixth. Resolved in favor of the 5-organ consensus, with the intestine noted in B-tier.

## Open Questions
- **Effect sizes are unverified.** The optimization levers are directionally solid but drawn from a medium-confidence aggregator ([[Healthline — Balance Hormones Naturally]]). Magnitudes (e.g., exactly how much exercise raises insulin sensitivity or GH) need primary PubMed/Cochrane confirmation.
- **Neurotransmitter vs hormone boundary** — dopamine and serotonin act in both roles; this page treats the hormone roles only.
- **No per-hormone optimization deep-dives** were created this session beyond the existing Cortisol/Dopamine/Testosterone guides (page budget). Candidates for future autoresearch: a Thyroid Complete Guide, Insulin Sensitivity Complete Guide, Growth Hormone & IGF-1 Guide.
