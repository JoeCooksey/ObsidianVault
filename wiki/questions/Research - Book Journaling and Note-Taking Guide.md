# Research: Book Journaling and Note-Taking Guide

## Overview
The full guide to journaling and taking notes on books, in three parts: **what to write, the guiding questions to answer, and what to do with the notes afterward.** The core finding: effective book note-taking is **three separate activities separated in time** — annotate while reading, journal after, and *process into linked atomic notes weeks later* — and the value lives almost entirely in that last, most-skipped step. Complements the vault's existing [[Reading Retention Methods]] (techniques) and [[Reading Application Framework]] (turning ideas into action) by adding the missing middle: the *anatomy of a book note* and the *processing pipeline*.

## Key Findings

- **Separate capture from processing.** The single highest-leverage rule. [[Ryan Holiday]] waits 2–3 weeks before transcribing notes to cards — *"waiting helps you separate the wheat from the chaff"* (Source: [[The Notecard System - Ryan Holiday]]). (confidence: high)
- **Read with a pen — marking is active reading.** [[Mortimer Adler|Adler's]] toolkit (underline, margin line, sparing stars, numbered points, page cross-references, marginal notes) is the during-reading layer; "marking up a book is not mutilation but love" (Source: [[How to Mark a Book - Mortimer Adler]]). (confidence: medium — primary essay unreachable, devices are standard knowledge)
- **What to write per book**: thesis in one sentence, summary in your own words, 3–7 key ideas with *why*, quotes with page numbers, evidence/pushback, the ONE action, and connections to other notes (Source: reading-journal guides + [[Book Note-Taking System]]). (confidence: high)
- **Notes must be in your own words, atomic, and linked.** [[Sönke Ahrens|Ahrens's]] pipeline (fleeting → literature → permanent → linked) makes rephrasing the test of understanding and linking the source of recall; "writing notes IS thinking" (Source: [[How to Take Smart Notes - Sonke Ahrens]]). (confidence: high)
- **The two canonical systems are the same idea in two media**: digital [[Niklas Luhmann|Zettelkasten]] (linked notes) and analog commonplace notecards (4×6 cards, theme in the corner). Both: read → annotate → wait → rephrase one idea per unit → connect → reuse. (confidence: high)
- **What to do with notes**: review on a spaced schedule (Day 1 → week → month → year) with active recall, then **apply** (one implementation intention), **create** (assemble notes into writing), or **teach** (Source: [[Reading Retention Methods]], Readwise, [[Reading Application Framework]]). (confidence: high)
- **The four universal questions** ([[Mortimer Adler|Adler]]) anchor reflection: What is it about? What's said in detail? Is it true? *What of it?* (confidence: high)

## Contradictions
- **Process immediately vs. wait weeks**: Ahrens converts literature notes relatively promptly while [[Ryan Holiday|Holiday]] deliberately waits 2–3 weeks. Resolution: the *delay is the filter* for what's worth keeping — favor waiting for trade books; for technical material where you'll act soon, process sooner.
- **Quotes vs. own words**: commonplace books prize verbatim quotes; the Zettelkasten insists on rephrasing. Resolution: keep a few quotes verbatim (with page #), but the *idea* note must be in your own words to count as understood.
