# Research: Improving at Top Lane (League of Legends)

## Overview

The role-specific layer on top of [[Research - How to Improve at League of Legends]]. The general research established the engine (volume-first practice, one role, 2–3 champions, skill ladder laning → wave → macro → vision); this run answers **what is different about top lane** and which skills carry the most weight there. Short answer: top is the most isolated lane — longest lane, fewest escape routes, melee-vs-melee — so the climb runs through **trading, jungle tracking, wave management, and Teleport**, in roughly that order of daily relevance.

## Key Findings

1. **Isolation is the defining property of the role.** Top is the longest lane with the fewest escape routes, and most matchups are melee-vs-melee. Consequences: one lost trade can zone you off the wave for the rest of laning (trading > CS in importance), and ganks are more punishing than in any other lane (jungle tracking licenses everything). (Sources: [[Boosteria — Trading in Lane Guide]], [[Boosteria — Jungle Pathing Basics]])
2. **Wave management is still the #1 separator** — EloAscend calls it the single highest-impact skill from Gold to Platinum. The trainable habit: read the wave state every 30 seconds and consciously decide (freeze / slow push / crash). Already covered in depth at [[Wave Management (LoL)]]; top lane is where it pays the most because the lane is long and TP exists. (Source: [[EloAscend — Top Lane Guide 2026]])
3. **Trading is a system, not mechanics** — three stances (aggressive / defensive / cheater) chosen from wave state + jungle info, and a reliability-ordered window hierarchy: **cooldowns > last-hit punishes > level spikes (2/3/6) > item timings**. Spacing tools: tethering (hit once at the edge of your range, step out of theirs) and minion-aggro management. A won trade must immediately convert to a crash, freeze, or slow push or it's wasted. → [[Trading Stances and Windows (LoL)]]
4. **Jungle tracking is inference from observables, not guessing**: late enemy-laner arrival reveals the start side; jungler CS ≈ 4 per camp (~12 CS at 2:45 = level-3 gank window open); ward river/tri-brush at 3:00 every game; prolonged jungler absence while you push without vision = assume the gank. → [[Jungle Tracking for Laners]]
5. **Teleport is the role's macro identity** — a global tempo lever, not an escape hatch. Rule: TP only when it fixes your wave or turns a *guaranteed* numbers advantage; 3-question check (fight already even? arrival flips numbers? absence punishable?). Signature pattern: crash → recall → TP back to hold a freeze. → [[Teleport Usage (Top Lane)]]
6. **Split pushing is about forced responses, not towers** — push a side lane to make 2+ enemies answer, creating a numbers advantage elsewhere; the 1-3-1 forces two-man responses on both sides. Hard anti-trigger: never split while Baron/Elder is up. → [[Split Pushing (LoL)]]
7. **Build the champion pool by archetype, not tier list**: blind-pick tank/juggernaut + comfort carry + pocket counter, 30–50 games at ≥50% WR per champion before adding another. Tier lists for the *same patch* contradict each other (Darius S+ vs D — [[Patch 26.x Top Lane Tier Lists (Aggregate)]]), so archetype fit and mastery beat tier chasing. → [[Top Lane Champion Pool]]
8. **The general-research engine still drives the climb**: volume of play predicts rank, deliberate-practice structure adds only marginal value ([[Martončik 2024 — Psychological Predictors of Esports Success]]). Use the top-lane skills above as the *focus goals* inside the volume-first [[LoL Improvement Practice System]] — one per session (e.g. "ward at 3:00 every game", "convert every won trade").

## Practical Session Template (top lane)

- Loading screen: matchup plan — who wins level 1/2/6, freeze or shove, TP usage plan
- In game, one focus goal from: 3:00 ward · stance discipline · trade conversion · TP 3-question check · wave read every 30 s
- VOD review (~10 min, per [[Boosteria — Top Lane Fundamentals Guide]]): first three waves, first recall timing, two lost fights, side-lane decisions

## Contradictions

- **Darius, patch 26.12**: stat aggregators (METAsrc, u.gg) place him S+; one editorial list places him D after bruiser nerfs. Stat sites are more credible for placements (live win/pick/ban data), but the durable conclusion is that tier lists are noisy — pool decisions should ride on archetype + mastery. ([[Patch 26.x Top Lane Tier Lists (Aggregate)]])
- **Source independence is weak**: three of five sources are Boosteria (a boosting/coaching service), and EloAscend is also coaching-affiliated. The frameworks agree across both vendors, which helps, but everything here is practitioner consensus (medium confidence), not studied evidence — unlike the general-research layer, which has peer-reviewed anchors.

## Open Questions

- No peer-reviewed work on role-specific improvement (the Martončik/Bennett studies don't split by role) — the volume-vs-structure finding is assumed to transfer to top lane but isn't tested there.
- Matchup-specific preparation (the ~50 common top matchups) was out of scope — candidate next run: how high-elo tops actually prepare matchups (cheat sheets vs reps).
- Jungle CS-counting numbers (~4 CS/camp) came via search aggregate, medium confidence — verify against current-patch camp values.
- Tier snapshot expires with patch 26.12; the champion-pool page deliberately routes around this.
