# Research: How to Make Kefir

## Overview

Milk kefir is a fermented dairy drink made by culturing whole milk with **kefir grains** — a living symbiotic colony of bacteria and yeast that ferments milk in ~24 hours and is **reusable forever**. Making it is genuinely easy: grains + milk in a glass jar, room temp, one day, strain, repeat. The two decisions that matter are **where to get good grains** (a one-time choice) and **dialing in time/ratio so it isn't too sour**.

## The 60-Second Recipe

1. **1 Tbsp live grains + 2 cups whole milk** in a glass jar.
2. Cover with cloth/coffee filter + rubber band.
3. Sit at **room temp (65–75 °F) for ~24 hours**, out of direct sun.
4. **Strain** out the grains (plastic strainer); the liquid is your kefir.
5. Drop the grains into **fresh milk** to start the next batch.
6. (Optional) **Second ferment** the strained kefir 6–12 h with fruit/vanilla to mellow and flavor it.

## Key Findings

- **Ratio is the master dial:** ~**1 Tbsp grains per 1–2 cups milk** (1:10–1:15). More grains = faster/sourer; fewer = milder. Too sour is the #1 complaint — fix with fewer grains, shorter ferment, or a fridge second-ferment (Source: [[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]]).
- **Glass only, no prolonged metal, whole non-UHT dairy milk** gives the healthiest grains and thickest result (Source: [[Cultured.guru — How to Make Fermented Milk Kefir]]).
- **One purchase = lifetime supply.** Grains multiply, so you never buy twice if you care for them. They survive months in the fridge and years dried/frozen (Source: [[Yemoos — Milk Kefir FAQ]]).
- **Live > dried.** Live grains ferment immediately; dried need 3–10+ days of rehydration and have a less diverse microbiome (Source: [[Yemoos — Milk Kefir FAQ]]).
- **Microbiology:** grains carry 24–56+ bacterial OTUs (LAB-dominant) plus yeasts; finished kefir can hold ~300 species and the unique exopolysaccharide **kefiran**. Health benefits are well-shown in animals/in-vitro but **human clinical evidence is still limited** (Source: [[Frontiers-PMC — Microbiota of Kefir]]).

## Contradictions

- **Vendor potency claims vs. peer-reviewed caution:** Vendors ([[Cultures for Health — Milk Kefir Grains]]) market specific probiotic potency and health benefits; the peer-reviewed review ([[Frontiers-PMC — Microbiota of Kefir]]) stresses that **human evidence is limited**. The science is more credible — treat disease claims as promising-but-unproven, while accepting "diverse live probiotic, lower lactose than milk" as well-founded.

## Open Questions

- **How much does this actually help *Joe's* gut?** Human RCT evidence for specific outcomes (cholesterol, immunity, microbiome shifts) is thin — benefits are likely but not quantified.
- **Raw vs. pasteurized milk** trade-offs (flavor/safety) were not researched in depth.
- **Water kefir** (dairy-free, sugar-water based, different grains) is a separate culture not covered here.
