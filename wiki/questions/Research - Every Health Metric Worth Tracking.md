# Research: Every Health Metric Worth Tracking (Price, Frequency, Worth-It)

## Overview

A complete map of trackable health metrics — blood biomarkers, anthropometrics, wearable signals, fitness tests, and imaging — scored by **price, frequency, and worth-it**. The full ranked table lives in [[Health Metrics Master Tier List (Price, Frequency, Worth-It)]]. Bottom line: the highest-value metrics are also the cheapest, and the most expensive (whole-body MRI, epigenetic clocks) are the least worth it for healthy people.

## Key Findings

- **The best metrics are nearly free.** Home blood pressure, waist-to-height ratio, resting HR, and a wearable VO2-max estimate carry top-tier predictive power at ~$0 marginal cost (Source: [[2025 AHA-ACC Home Blood Pressure Guideline]], [[Waist-to-Height Ratio CVD 2025]], [[VO2 Max Mortality Evidence]]).
- **VO2 max is the single strongest mortality predictor** among trackable metrics — low fitness beats smoking and diabetes for risk; each +1 MET cuts mortality 13–15% (Source: [[VO2 Max Mortality Evidence]]).
- **ApoB has dethroned LDL-C** as the best blood biomarker for heart-attack risk; hs-CRP is now the ACC's preferred inflammation marker; Lp(a) is a one-time genetic test (Source: [[Empirical Health Best CVD Biomarkers 2025]]).
- **Waist-to-height ratio beats BMI** — the only anthropometric that survives adjustment for all classic risk factors. Keep waist <½ height (Source: [[Waist-to-Height Ratio CVD 2025]]).
- **Home BP predicts mortality better than office BP** and is highly cost-effective; 2025 AHA/ACC targets <120 systolic (Source: [[2025 AHA-ACC Home Blood Pressure Guideline]]).
- **One ~$200 annual blood membership** (Superpower 100+ markers; Function 160+) covers ~90% of actionable lab findings (Source: [[Blood Testing Memberships 2025]]).
- **Wearables: trust trends, not stages.** Sleep-vs-wake, RHR, and HRV trends are reliable; multi-stage sleep accuracy is only 50–80% (Oura ~79%, Apple Watch ~51%) (Source: [[Wearable Sleep and HRV Validation 2025]]).
- **CGM for healthy people = short experiment, not lifelong metric** — not routinely recommended; useful for 2-week n=1 learning (Source: [[CGM Non-Diabetic Review 2025]]).
- **Whole-body MRI is not worth it for average-risk adults** — ACR advises against; ~1.6% confirmed-cancer yield, high false-positive cascade (Source: [[Whole-Body MRI Screening 2025]]).
- **DEXA is the value pick for body composition** — $40–60, ~95% cheaper than MRI, tracks visceral fat, lean mass, and bone (Source: [[Health Screening Cost Guide 2026 — BodySpec]]).

## Worth-It Summary (by tier)

| Tier | Metrics |
|------|---------|
| **S** | Home BP, waist-to-height ratio, resting HR, VO2 max estimate, body weight, HbA1c/glucose |
| **A** | ApoB, Lp(a) (once), hs-CRP, fasting insulin, lipids, Vitamin D, ferritin, testosterone, CBC/CMP, thyroid, ~$200 lab membership, wearable sleep/HRV |
| **B** | DEXA, grip strength, omega-3 index, homocysteine, CAC score (40+), B12/folate, RBC magnesium, lab VO2 test, gait speed |
| **C** | Non-diabetic CGM, wearable sleep stages, epigenetic age, microbiome, telomere length |
| **D** | Whole-body MRI, broad genetic panels, consumer GDF-15/Klotho/IL-6 |

## Contradictions

- **Blood-test memberships:** vendors frame 100+ markers as essential, while NPR (Apr 2026) and Sherwood News call them partly **sales funnels** that drive over-testing and anxiety. More credible: a focused ~$200 panel beats reflexively chasing every marker (Source: [[Blood Testing Memberships 2025]]).
- **Wearable accuracy:** manufacturer-funded studies report higher accuracy than independent ones (e.g., Apple Watch sleep staging ~51% independently). Trust independent validations (Source: [[Wearable Sleep and HRV Validation 2025]]).
- **Whole-body MRI:** marketing positions it as longevity screening; radiology consensus (ACR) says it lacks life-extension evidence for average-risk adults (Source: [[Whole-Body MRI Screening 2025]]).

## Open Questions

- Long-term outcome data for non-diabetic CGM and epigenetic clocks remains thin — do interventions guided by them actually change hard endpoints?
- Wearable VO2-max *estimates* are convenient but their accuracy vs lab testing across fitness levels is not fully quantified here.
- Emerging aging biomarkers (GDF-15, Klotho, IL-6) lack validated individual action thresholds — left D-tier pending stronger evidence.
- Sex/age-specific worth-it scoring (e.g., female iron/ferritin priority, prostate PSA timing) is only partially covered; could be split into its own page.
