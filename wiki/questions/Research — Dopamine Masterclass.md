# Research: Dopamine Masterclass

## Overview
A full masterclass on dopamine: what it is, how it works, **exactly how much different healthy and unhealthy activities raise it**, and why every peak is paid for with a dip. The core insight: dopamine is the currency of **motivation/wanting**, not pleasure — and chasing peaks lowers your baseline, while slow earned inputs raise it. Start at [[Dopamine Complete Guide]]; the centerpiece quantitative table is [[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]].

## Key Findings
- **Dopamine = motivation, not pleasure.** It drives pursuit and craving; "liking" uses opioids/endocannabinoids (Source: [[Huberman Lab Episode 39 — Controlling Your Dopamine]]).
- **Activity multipliers above baseline** (directional): chocolate ~1.5×, sex ~2×, exercise ~2×, nicotine ~2.5×, cocaine ~2.5×, **amphetamine ~10×**; cold water ~2.5× **but sustained for hours with no crash** (Sources: [[Huberman Lab Episode 39 — Controlling Your Dopamine]], [[Sramek 2000 — Cold Water Immersion and Catecholamines]]). Roots trace to rat microdialysis in [[Di Chiara and Imperato 1988 — Drugs of Abuse and Dopamine]].
- **The multiplier isn't the whole story — the crash is.** Cold (2.5×) and nicotine (2.5×) peak alike, but cold is tonic/crash-free while nicotine spikes-and-crashes. **Speed + reliability of the spike drive addictiveness**, not peak height alone (Source: [[Dopamine Increases by Activity (Tier List)]]).
- **Pleasure has a price.** Every peak draws down the readily-releasable pool → baseline dips. Satisfaction = peak **relative to baseline**. Lembke's pleasure–pain seesaw says the same: pleasure is balanced by equal-and-opposite pain (Sources: [[Anna Lembke — Dopamine Nation]], [[Huberman Lab Episode 39 — Controlling Your Dopamine]]).
- **Chronic spikes → tolerance → dopamine deficit state** (receptor downregulation, anhedonia). This is the addiction mechanism and reverses with abstinence (Source: [[Dopamine Downregulation and Tolerance]]).
- **"Dopamine detox" is misnamed but the protocol works** — it's CBT/stimulus control, ~30 days off the problem behavior, not literal dopamine reduction (Source: [[Dopamine Detox (Evidence Review)]]).
- **Healthy stack**: morning sunlight, cold, exercise, music, meditation, connection, sleep, protein — all **raise the tonic floor without a crash** (Source: [[Healthy Dopamine Protocols]]).

## Contradictions
- **Multiplier framing varies.** Some sources phrase Huberman's data as "1.5×" (a 50% increase) and others as "150%," causing apparent disagreement. They describe the **same** underlying numbers; this wiki uses **fold-increase** (1.5× = +50%) consistently.
- **"Dopamine detox" — fad vs tool.** Harvard Health and critics call the viral version pseudoscientific; Sepah (its originator) and Lembke support the **underlying CBT/abstinence** practice. Resolution: the *chemical* claim is wrong, the *behavioral* protocol is sound (Source: [[Dopamine Detox (Evidence Review)]]).

## Open Questions
- **Human multipliers are imprecise.** Most exact fold-increase data are from rodent microdialysis or small human samples; precise, comparable human numbers per activity remain under-measured.
- **Cold-exposure dose-response** beyond the single 14 °C protocol (cold showers vs plunges vs duration) is not well quantified for dopamine.
- **Receptor-recovery timelines** during abstinence vary by substance/dose; "30 days" is a clinical heuristic, not an established constant.
