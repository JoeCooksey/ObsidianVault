# Research: Healthy vs Unhealthy Gut Signs

## Overview

All the signs of a healthy vs unhealthy gut, organized into three deliverable pages: the positive checklist ([[Signs of a Healthy Gut]]), the warning catalog ([[Signs of an Unhealthy Gut]]), and the doctor-now list ([[GI Red Flag Symptoms]]). Core insight: a healthy gut is mostly *silent*, the best free markers are stool form + transit time (not frequency), and systemic signs only point at the gut when they cluster with digestive signs.

## Key Findings

1. **Healthy = silent + regular.** Bristol type 3–4, easy passage without straining or urgency, brown with no blood/mucus, predictable personal rhythm, minimal post-meal bloating, varied diet tolerated. (Source: [[Signs of a Healthy Gut]])
2. **Frequency is the most overrated marker.** Anything 3×/day–3×/week is normal; frequency correlates poorly with transit time, and transit time explains more microbiome variation than either frequency or consistency. (Source: [[Blue Poo Transit Time Study (PREDICT 1)]])
3. **Transit time is the best free marker.** Median 28.7 h, healthy band ~14–58 h, measurable at home with blue dye/corn/sesame. Long transit independently associates with visceral fat and worse post-meal glucose/triglyceride responses. (Source: [[Blue Poo Transit Time Study (PREDICT 1)]])
4. **Unhealthy signs span four tiers of confidence:** digestive (bloating, gas, irregular stools, pain — high confidence, direct readout) → food-response (new intolerances, sugar cravings — medium-high) → systemic (fatigue, sleep, mood, brain fog, skin, weight, infections — medium, associative) → distal (migraines, autoimmune flares — medium-low). (Source: [[Signs of an Unhealthy Gut]])
5. **Dysbiosis shows up outside the abdomen** — mouth (bleeding gums, cavities), skin (acne, eczema), urogenital tract — because every body surface hosts its own linked microbiome. (Source: [[Cleveland Clinic Dysbiosis Overview]])
6. **Red flags are a separate category, not a severity level.** Blood in stool, vomiting blood, 10+ lb unexplained weight loss in 3 months, dysphagia, fever, nighttime symptoms, anemia, new symptoms after 50 — functional gut imbalance *cannot* produce these; they require workup. (Source: [[IFFGD GI Alarm Symptoms]])
7. **Triage rule:** no red flags → optimize diet/lifestyle, skip testing; red flags → doctor for calprotectin + celiac serology, not a consumer panel. (Sources: [[GI Red Flag Symptoms]], [[Gut Health Tests]])

## Contradictions

- **Popular advice fixates on frequency; the evidence says form + transit.** Consumer articles rank "regular daily bowel movement" as the top healthy sign; PREDICT 1 shows frequency is the weakest of the three stool metrics. Form and transit win. ([[Blue Poo Transit Time Study (PREDICT 1)]] is the more credible source — n>800, *Gut*/BMJ.)
- **Systemic-sign lists overreach.** Marketing-adjacent sources attribute nearly any symptom (anxiety, weight, skin) to the gut; clinical sources (Cleveland Clinic) label these "potentially linked." Filed at medium confidence with the clustering rule.

## Open Questions

- Causal direction for gut→mood and gut→skin remains unresolved — most evidence is associative and bidirectional.
- No validated "optimal transit time" exists — the ~14–58 h band comes from older marker studies, not outcome trials.
- Whether normalizing transit time (vs just symptoms) improves hard outcomes is untested.
- Sugar-craving ← microbiome causation in humans is still mostly extrapolated from animal work.
