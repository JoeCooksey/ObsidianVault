> **Fiscal quarter:** Q1 FY2027 (ended April 26, 2026)

### Headline Results

|--------|--------|----------|----------------------|-----------|

| Operating Margin | ~65%+ | — | ~60% | Expansion |

> **Note on EPS:** GAAP EPS ($2.39) came in above Non-GAAP EPS ($1.87) this quarter, an unusual situation. This suggests a significant one-time GAAP gain (likely a favorable tax item or investment gain) benefited GAAP results. The $4.5B H20 inventory charge taken in a prior quarter may have produced a tax benefit reversal. Analysts focus on Non-GAAP EPS ($1.87) as the operational measure.

**Key Segments:**
- Data Center: $75.2B (+92% YoY, +21% QoQ)
- Data Center Networking: $14.8B (+199% YoY) — record
- Hyperscale vs. ACIE: ~50/50 split (widening demand base)

### Free Cash Flow Check

- **OCF:** $50.3B (vs $27.4B in Q1 FY26 — +84% YoY)
- **CapEx:** ~$1.8B
- **FCF:** $48.5B (record)
- **FCF vs Net Income:** FCF of $48.5B far exceeds GAAP net income (~$19.4B implied by $2.39 EPS × 8.1B shares). This is expected: NVIDIA's business model generates massive OCF due to huge non-cash charges (D&A, SBC); the gap is structural and not a red flag.

### Guidance

- **Q2 FY2027 Revenue:** $91.0B ±2% (~+68% YoY)
- **Q2 FY2027 Gross Margin:** 74.9%/75.0% (GAAP/Non-GAAP) ±50 bps
- **Guidance direction:** **RAISED** — $91B Q2 guide significantly above prior consensus of ~$85B
- **Full-year:** No explicit guidance, but Q2 trajectory implies $300B+ annualized run rate

### Management Commentary

- **Blackwell demand insatiable:** GB200 NVL72 rack production on smooth cadence across all major hyperscalers; GB300 Ultra transitioning from sampling to production. Management cited no supply-side bottlenecks from their side.
- **China exclusion:** H20 export restrictions mean China revenue is zero in forward guidance. The previously disclosed $4.5B inventory charge remains as baseline. Management was measured on China — not expecting recovery in guidance assumptions.
- **Networking as emerging powerhouse:** InfiniBand, Spectrum-X Ethernet, and NVLink collectively hit $14.8B (+199% YoY), making networking a standalone mega-business.
- **Capital allocation:** Quarterly dividend raised 25× from $0.01 to $0.25/share. New $80B share repurchase authorization added on top of existing program. Q1 returned ~$20B to shareholders via buybacks + dividends — a record.
- **Red flags:** Gross margin trajectory is the key watchpoint — GAAP GM has compressed from ~78% to ~75% over the past year as Blackwell mix ramps (more complex, costlier to manufacture). Management guided 75% is the new floor, but execution must prove this.

### Thesis Check

NVIDIA's thesis — dominant AI infrastructure monopoly capturing the lion's share of the AI capex supercycle — is not just intact, it is being exceeded at every turn. $81.6B in a single quarter (85% YoY), $48.5B FCF, and a $91B Q2 guide demonstrate a business with no visible ceiling. The 50/50 hyperscale/enterprise demand split signals the addressable market is broadening, reducing concentration risk. **Sell signal to watch:** Non-GAAP gross margin sustained below 73% for two consecutive quarters would signal pricing power erosion or Blackwell cost issues; alternatively, a hard pause in hyperscaler AI capex spending (none signaled) would be the other trigger.

**Net Income:** Beat consensus expectations that had modeled near breakeven.

### Free Cash Flow Check

- **OCF:** Not separately cited; FCF reported directly as $1.4B
- **CapEx:** Part of the $25B+ full-year 2026 capex plan; Q1 CapEx was modest relative to full-year run-rate
- **FCF:** $1.4B (vs consensus estimate of **−$1.3B** — a $2.7B beat vs expectations)
- **FCF vs Net Income:** FCF of $1.4B tracking ahead of net income — consistent with strong operating leverage. **Management explicitly warned FCF will turn negative for Q2–Q4 2026** as $25B+ annual capex ramps up for factory expansion, AI infrastructure, Robotaxi, and Optimus programs.

### Guidance

- **Next quarter:** Tesla provides no formal quarterly revenue/EPS guidance
- **Full-year CapEx:** >$25B (significant acceleration; this is the dominant financial narrative)
- **FCF outlook:** Negative for the remainder of 2026 (management guided explicitly)
- **Guidance direction:** **LOWERED** on FCF; capex raised, implying significant near-term cash burn

### Management Commentary

- **Robotaxi as the North Star:** Tesla grew its Robotaxi fleet QoQ and plans to accelerate fleet expansion into additional geographies. Management reframed Tesla as an AI/autonomy company first. A research semiconductor fab is being ordered in Austin.
- **Energy storage record:** Energy Storage gross margin hit a record 39.5%, providing an increasingly important and high-margin revenue pillar. Management cited strong commercial and utility-scale deployments.
- **$25B capex pivot:** The company is simultaneously building out six new factories, AI infrastructure, a semiconductor fab, and solar manufacturing. Elon Musk characterized this as "planting seeds" for the 2027–2030 harvest. This is the biggest bull-vs-bear debate in the stock.
- **Capital allocation:** No dividend. All capital reinvested into growth programs. Elon Musk's compensation structure and focus on xAI were briefly raised in Q&A with evasive responses from management — a minor red flag on corporate governance.
- **Red flags:** (1) Musk distraction risk (running Tesla, SpaceX, xAI, X simultaneously). (2) Negative FCF for 3 quarters ahead could pressure the balance sheet. (3) Regulatory approval timelines for Robotaxi in new markets are outside Tesla's control.

### Thesis Check

Tesla's thesis has bifurcated into two co-existing narratives: the EV manufacturer thesis (maturing, low-margin, competitive) and the AI/autonomy/energy thesis (high-optionality, unproven). Q1 2026 results were encouragingly strong on margins (21.1% gross, +478 bps YoY) and FCF. However, management's explicit FCF-negative guidance for Q2–Q4 and the $25B capex plan mean investors must buy the autonomy vision, not just current results. **Sell signal to watch:** Any delay to Robotaxi commercial launch beyond Q1 2027, or if gross margin falls back below 18% (suggesting the margin recovery was temporary rather than structural).

- **FCF (H1 FY26):** ~$82.6B operating cash flow for first half; $36B returned via buybacks H1 alone
- **FCF vs Net Income:** FCF generation substantially exceeds net income — Apple's capital-light model means OCF >> GAAP net income by a significant margin. No red flag; this is structural.

### Guidance

- **Q3 FY2026 Revenue Growth:** +14% to +17% YoY

- **Red flags:** Memory cost inflation is the #1 near-term risk. R&D growing 33% vs. revenue growing 17% suggests Apple is in heavy investment mode — fine if AI monetization follows, concerning if not.

### Thesis Check

Apple's thesis — Services flywheel + iPhone upgrade cycle + emerging AI monetization platform — remains intact. Record March quarter iPhone revenue, Services at an all-time high of $31B, and 49.3% gross margin (Apple's highest in years) all confirm execution strength. The Q3 memory cost headwind is a real but temporary drag on margins; the longer question is whether Apple Intelligence meaningfully accelerates upgrade velocity and Services attach rates. **Sell signal to watch:** Services gross margin declining below 74% for two consecutive quarters (signals pricing pressure or cost structure shift); or iPhone revenue growth turning negative YoY (upgrade cycle exhaustion).

Meta's thesis — AI-powered advertising monopoly generating exceptional cash flows that fund a long-term platform bet on AI/AR — remains intact but is entering a high-stakes test phase. Revenue growth of 33%, FCF of $12.4B, and 41% operating margins confirm the core ad business is humming. The $125–145B capex guidance is the central debate: Zuckerberg is making a generational infrastructure bet, and if AI monetization materializes (through Llama, AI ads, Orion glasses), these returns could be extraordinary. **Sell signal to watch:** Revenue growth decelerating below 20% YoY while FCF continues to compress due to capex — that combination would suggest the infrastructure bet isn't translating to revenue.

### Thesis Check

Alphabet's thesis — Search monopoly + Google Cloud as an emerging #3 cloud leader + AI monetization across all surfaces — is strongly intact after Q1 2026. Revenue +22%, operating margin +200 bps to 36.1%, and Cloud growing 63% to $20B with a dramatic margin expansion to 32.9% are all powerful proof points. The AI-search cannibalisation fear appears overblown based on management's commentary. **Sell signal to watch:** Google Cloud growth decelerating below 35% YoY for two consecutive quarters (suggests losing AI share to AWS/Azure), or evidence that AI Overviews are materially suppressing search ad revenue per query.

## Key Takeaways

1. **The AI capex supercycle is accelerating, not plateauing.** NVIDIA, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, and TSMC all raised capital expenditure guidance simultaneously. Combined announced AI infrastructure spend across Joe's holdings for 2026 exceeds $600B. NVIDIA's Q2 revenue guide of $91B represents another +11% sequential leap. There is no sign of a pause in hyperscaler AI spending.

2. **Eli Lilly was the portfolio's standout performer.** Revenue +56%, EPS +170%, full-year guidance raised — LLY produced the single most impressive quarter of any holding. The GLP-1 category ($12.8B combined Mounjaro/Zepbound in one quarter) is becoming one of the largest drug franchises in pharmaceutical history, and Lilly is the clear category leader.

3. **Margin expansion is broad-based across the AI infrastructure stack.** TSMC hit a record 66.2% gross margin; Google Cloud expanded operating margins from 17.8% → 32.9% YoY; AMD's FCF margin hit a record 25%; Microsoft operating margins expanded to ~46%. The AI buildout is translating into margin improvement for producers (TSMC, AMD, NVDA) and increasingly for cloud platform operators.

4. **The three mega-capex debates to watch in Q2 2026:** (1) Will Meta's $125–145B capex plan show early AI monetization proof by Q3? (2) Can Alphabet's $180–190B capex maintain 63%+ Cloud growth? (3) Will Tesla's $25B capex show Robotaxi and Optimus commercial milestones — or will negative FCF trigger a confidence test? Each of these narratives will define their respective stocks' next 12 months.

5. **Memory costs are an emerging portfolio-wide risk.** Apple explicitly flagged 150–200 bps of gross margin headwind from memory cost increases in Q3. Meta cited higher component costs (especially memory) as a driver of the capex raise. Given that AMD, TSMC, and ASML all benefit from memory capex expansion, this is simultaneously a risk for end-device manufacturers (AAPL) and a tailwind for the semiconductor supply chain (TSM, ASML, AMD).
