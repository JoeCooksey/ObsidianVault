# Research: Best Activities When Bored

## Overview

Joe asked (via /autoresearch) what the best activities are while bored. The research answer reframes the question: boredom is a *diagnostic signal*, not a problem to suppress, so the "best activity" depends on what the signal is reporting — and the most popular response (phone scrolling) is the one response experimentally shown to make boredom worse. The practical output is the [[Boredom Activity Playbook]].

## Key Findings

1. **Boredom = a signal of attention and/or meaning deficits**, like pain. It alerts you that you're unable or unwilling to engage attention in meaningful activity. Neither good nor bad — what counts is the response. (Source: [[Westgate 2020 - Why Boredom Is Interesting]], confidence: high)
2. **Both too-easy and too-hard cause boredom** (U-shaped difficulty curve) — the empirical backbone of the [[Goldilocks Rule]]. Different causes need different fixes: raise demands, lower demands, or reframe meaning. (Source: [[Westgate 2020 - Why Boredom Is Interesting]], confidence: high)
3. **Scrolling makes boredom worse.** "Digital switching" — flipping between/within short videos — increased boredom across 7 experiments (~1,200 people); people reliably mispredict this. Watching one thing in full is more satisfying. (Source: [[Tam 2024 - Digital Switching Study]], confidence: high)
4. **Choose interesting over merely enjoyable.** Easy-enjoyable activities are "junk food": instant relief, but they build none of the cognitive resources that prevent future boredom. Demanding-but-interesting activities compound. (Source: [[Westgate 2020 - Why Boredom Is Interesting]], confidence: high; see [[Interest vs Enjoyment]])
5. **The phone-free walk is the best single default** — it restores depleted directed attention ([[Attention Restoration Theory]]: 30 min in nature improves attention-task performance; Berman 2008 + 2024 RCTs), hosts constructive mind-wandering, and gives crash-free dopamine. (confidence: high)
6. **Hobbies are independently protective**: hobby engagement predicts lower depression and higher life satisfaction across 16 countries (*Nature Medicine* 2023) and longitudinally in the U.S. Health & Retirement Study — via creative expression, cognitive stimulation, and social contact. (confidence: high)
7. **Boredom spikes impulsivity and reward sensitivity** — bored people will literally self-administer electric shocks (>90% in one study). This is why the bored brain reaches for the dopamine slot machine, and why pre-deciding a playbook matters. (Source: [[Westgate 2020 - Why Boredom Is Interesting]], confidence: high)
8. **Researchers' consensus shortlist**: call a friend, go for a walk, hit the gym, play an instrument — constructive responses named by boredom researchers themselves (Danckert, Westgate, Elpidorou via National Geographic). (confidence: medium — expert recommendation, not trial-tested as a bundle)

## Contradictions

- **Boredom → creativity is contested.** [[Mann and Cadman 2014 - Boredom and Creativity]] found boring tasks boosted subsequent idea generation; a 2016 study found boredom does not generate creativity (practice does) and sometimes impairs it. Resolution (Elpidorou): the benefit lives in the *response*, not the boredom — "I would praise you, not the boredom." Treat the creativity claim as medium-confidence; the mind-wandering-window claim is safer.
- Westgate notes attentional theories and meaning theories of boredom each fail alone; the MAC model's claim is that both are partially right and independent.

## Open Questions

- A 2025 *Communications Psychology* paper proposes boredom as deviation from a "cognitive homeostatic set point" (covers over-stimulation boredom — relevant to feeds) — fetch failed twice (Nature paywall redirect); worth a future pass.
- Tam's digital-switching samples skew to Canadian college students; age generalizability untested.
- Does the playbook's diagnose-then-act structure actually outperform "just go for a walk" in practice? No direct trial evidence; it's a synthesis-level construction.
