# Research: How Healthy Are Ninja CREAMis?

## Overview
A "Ninja CREAMi" isn't a food — it's an [[Ninja CREAMi|appliance]] that freezes a blended base and shaves it into ice cream. So health is **entirely a function of the recipe**, and the typical high-protein recipe is genuinely good: 20–40g protein, low-to-moderate sugar, and far fewer calories than store-bought ice cream.

## Key Findings
- The machine adds nothing nutritionally — it's a delivery system for your base (Source: [[Ninja CREAMi]]).
- A registered-dietitian protein base runs **~240 cal / 26g protein** plain, or **~393 cal / 38g protein / 21g sugar** loaded with toppings (Source: [[Maria Lucey — Ninja CREAMi Protein Ice Cream]]).
- Low-cal and keto builds hit **157–235 cal** for a serving-to-pint, vs **478 cal / 6.7g protein** for 1 cup of premium store vanilla (Source: [[Forks Over Knives — Ninja CREAMi Comparison]]).
- Homemade lets you **avoid the gums and artificial sweetener blends** in many commercial protein ice creams.
- Full health detail and caveats: [[Protein Ice Cream (Ninja CREAMi) Health Profile]].

## Contradictions
- Marketing frames CREAMi desserts as uniformly "healthy"; dietitians are more precise — **healthy is recipe-dependent**. A clean protein base is a great daily snack; a toppings-loaded, sugar-alcohol-heavy pint is closer to a normal dessert (and can cause GI upset).

## Open Questions
- Long-term effects of daily high-dose **sugar alcohols / allulose / monk fruit** at ice-cream quantities.
- Whether **casein vs whey vs plant** protein meaningfully changes satiety or just texture.
