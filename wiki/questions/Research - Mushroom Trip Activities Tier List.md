# Research: Mushroom Trip Activities Tier List

## Overview

Joe asked for a tier list of things to do while tripping on mushrooms. The answer splits into a research-backed core (music, inward focus, nature, support) and a community consensus layer (art, journaling, films), organized by the one variable that reorders everything: **dose**. Deliverable: [[Psilocybin Trip Activities Tier List]]. Framed as harm reduction throughout — psilocybin remains federally Schedule I ([[Psilocybin Legal Status (US)]], which this run cross-links; the vault's cultivation cluster now has a "during" companion).

## Key Findings

- **Dose is the master variable.** At medium/high doses the deepest-studied model involves no activities at all: couch, eyeshades, headphones, curated music, surrender — the Johns Hopkins protocol used in its depression trials (Source: [[Johns Hopkins Psilocybin Playlist Articles]]). The activity tier list properly applies to low–moderate doses.
- **Music is the most evidence-backed activity.** It intensifies emotion and mental imagery under psilocybin, acts as a "hidden therapist," and real-time playlist adjustment is a documented in-trip emotional-regulation tool. Hopkins built a phase-structured 7h40m classical playlist around the session arc (Sources: [[Kaelen 2018 - The Hidden Therapist (Music in Psychedelic Therapy)]], [[Dutton 2025 - Psychedelic Harm Reduction Scoping Review]]). Lyric-free and calm beats energetic and worded.
- **Nature earns S-tier with an asterisk.** Natural surroundings during the acute state amplify the well-documented lasting increase in nature relatedness (persists ≥2 years, correlates with well-being) — but the effect also appears indoors, so nature is enhancement, not requirement, and adds physical risk needing a sitter + familiar location (Source: [[Psychedelics and Nature Relatedness Studies (Imperial College)]]).
- **The peer-reviewed practice set matches community lore.** The 2025 scoping review's during-use practices — emotional support/sitters (16 studies), music (4), moving to calmer environments or nature (4) — plus journaling for integration, are exactly the community consensus menu (Source: [[Dutton 2025 - Psychedelic Harm Reduction Scoping Review]]).
- **The avoid-list is sharper than the do-list.** Driving (illegal everywhere, impairment outlasts the felt trip), climbing/swimming, crowds and clubs, phone/social media (state hijack — airplane mode), substance mixing (lithium genuinely dangerous), and any consequential decision (Sources: [[CISUR Safer Tripping Infosheet]], [[TripSafe Psilocybin Guide]]).
- **Contraindications are absolute, not tiered:** personal/family psychosis or schizophrenia history, heart conditions, lithium/MAOIs, current crisis (Sources: [[CISUR Safer Tripping Infosheet]], [[TripSafe Psilocybin Guide]]).

## Key Concepts

- [[Psilocybin Trip Activities Tier List]] — the deliverable: S (music / eyeshades-inward / safe nature) → A (meditation, journaling, trusted friends, walks) → B (art, calm films) → C (games, sex at light doses, mirrors) → F (driving, heights, crowds, phone, mixing)
- [[Set and Setting]] — the field's closest thing to a law; mindset + environment dominate outcomes
- [[Trip Sitter]] — most-cited safety practice; scripts: "You're safe, I'm here," never "Calm down"
- [[Psilocybin Session Protocol (Johns Hopkins)]] — the clinical answer to "what to do": nothing, inwardly

## Contradictions

- **Mirror-gazing**: [[CISUR Safer Tripping Infosheet]] (institutional) says avoid; [[Montclair Mirror-Gazing Psilocybin Study (2024)]] found predominantly positive self-perception outcomes in 89 Reddit accounts and calls the lore overstated. Montclair is self-selected data; CISUR is precautionary default. Filed at C-tier: permitted, unpredictable, unnecessary.
- **Nature as requirement vs enhancement**: community guides romanticize outdoor trips; Kettner 2019 shows context-dependence but clinical-room efficacy. Resolution: nature amplifies, indoors works, safety decides.

## Open Questions

- No controlled study compares trip *activities* head-to-head — the tier ranking is evidence-triangulation, not direct measurement.
- Hopkins primary articles were 403-blocked; playlist details reconstructed from excerpts (medium confidence).
- Does the Hopkins playlist outperform personalized playlists? (Kaelen's later work suggests personalization matters; not researched this run.)
- Optimal dose bands for each tier are folk-calibrated (community "1–5 g dried" ranges), not clinically standardized.
