# Research - Organic Frozen Vegetables & the Costco Stir-Fry Blend

## Overview
**Verdict: organic is low-priority for the Costco (Kirkland) Stir-Fry Vegetable Blend — skip it and buy conventional.** Organic is an *exposure-reduction* purchase, not a nutrition upgrade, and almost every vegetable in this specific blend is a low-residue crop that's further washed, blanched, and cooked before you eat it. Spend the organic budget on the Dirty Dozen (berries, leafy greens) instead.

## Key Findings

1. **Organic = lower pesticide intake, not "more nutritious."** An all-organic diet cuts urinary pesticide metabolites 60–95% within days (Source: [[Organic Diet Intervention Reduces Urinary Pesticides (Hyland 2019)]]). But nutrient content is essentially the same as conventional, and **no study links low-level conventional residues to disease** (Source: [[Organic Nutrition Evidence (Stanford 2012 + Newcastle 2014)]]). Conventional residues are typically well below EPA safety limits.

2. **The stir-fry blend is dominated by low-residue crops.** Ingredients: broccoli, sugar snap peas, green beans, carrots, mushrooms, red bell peppers, water chestnuts, onions. Of these, **onions (#5), sweet peas (#6), carrots (#13), and mushrooms (#14) are on the 2025 Clean Fifteen**; broccoli and water chestnuts are consistently low (Source: [[EWG Shopper's Guide to Pesticides in Produce (2025)]]). See ingredient-by-ingredient table in [[Kirkland Signature Stir-Fry Vegetable Blend]].

3. **Only 2 of 8 ingredients have any pesticide-priority history** — green beans and bell peppers. Neither made the **2025 Dirty Dozen** (which is spinach, strawberries, kale, grapes, peaches, cherries, nectarines, pears, apples, blackberries, blueberries, potatoes). So even the "worst" items in the blend are borderline at most.

4. **Freezing + cooking removes residue twice.** Commercial frozen veg is washed and **blanched before freezing**, and you then stir-fry/steam it — each step strips a meaningful fraction of surface pesticide (Source: [[Cooking Reduces Pesticide Residues — Boiling, Blanching, Stir-Frying (2022)]]). The blend you eat carries **less** residue than the raw-produce figures behind the EWG list.

5. **Eating the vegetables at all >> organic vs conventional.** The dominant health move is getting the produce in; don't let "couldn't find/afford organic" turn into "didn't eat the stir-fry."

## Decision
> [!tip] Buy the **conventional** Kirkland Stir-Fry blend without worry. If you want to spend on organic, redirect it to Dirty Dozen items — spinach, berries, grapes — bought **frozen organic** for the best value (see [[Organic vs Conventional — Worth-It Decision]]).

## Where organic *would* matter
- A blend built around **spinach, kale, or other leafy greens** (Dirty Dozen, and flash-freezing can trap residue in leaf tissue).
- If a **young child or pregnant person** eats it daily and you want to minimize cumulative organophosphate exposure as a precaution (mechanism plausible, outcome unproven).

## Contradictions
- An early 2025 search snippet listed **green beans and bell peppers among the most-contaminated** produce, but the **full 2025 Dirty Dozen does not include them**. Likely a conflation with EWG's older "Dirty Dozen Plus" or a prior-year methodology. Treated here as **borderline, not Dirty Dozen** — the full-list source is more credible than the summary snippet.

## Open Questions
- No lab assay of the **specific Kirkland blend** exists publicly — ingredient-level EWG mapping is the best available proxy.
- Long-term health effect of chronic low-level dietary pesticide exposure remains **unresolved** (biomarker data exists; disease-outcome RCTs do not).
