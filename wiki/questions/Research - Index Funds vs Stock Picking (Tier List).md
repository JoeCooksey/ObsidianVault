# Research: Index Funds vs Stock Picking (Tier List)

## Overview

Two deliverables: a fund-by-fund tier list ([[Index Fund Tier List]]) and the head-to-head comparison ([[Index Funds vs Stock Picking]]). The research surfaced a three-layer evidence stack against stock picking as a primary strategy — professional failure rates (SPIVA), return skewness (Bessembinder), and retail behavior costs (Barber & Odean) — plus the evidence-compatible compromise ([[Core-Satellite Portfolio]]).

## Key Findings

1. **The tier list's top is boring and crowded**: every broad cap-weighted fund at ≤0.04% ER (VTI, FSKAX, VOO, FXAIX, SWPPX, VT) is functionally interchangeable; pick by brokerage convenience (Source: [[Index Fund Comparison 2026 (FinanceWonk)]]).
2. **2025 was the 4th-worst year ever for active managers**: 79% of large-cap active funds lost to the S&P 500; ~92% lose over 20 years; no category has a majority of winners after 15 years (Source: [[SPIVA Scorecard — Active vs Index]]).
3. **The deep reason picking fails is skewness, not stupidity**: ~4% of stocks generated all net wealth creation since 1926; the median stock loses to T-bills. Indexing = guaranteed tail capture (Source: [[Bessembinder - Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills]]).
4. **Retail activity is self-harming**: ~3.6%/yr behavior gap; the most active traders lag buy-and-hold by ~7 pp/yr; stocks retail buys subsequently underperform stocks it sells (Source: [[Barber and Odean - Individual Investor Performance]]).
5. **Leveraged ETFs are categorically not investments**: TQQQ 5-yr CAGR 22.9% vs the ~48% naive 3× expectation — volatility decay; F-tier for holding.
6. **Thematic funds are the modern cautionary tale**: ARKK 5-yr annualized −14.67% (Feb 2026) while the market doubled; every ARK fund trails the S&P 500 at 3 and 5 years.
7. **The strongest pro-picking cases survive only at satellite size**: domain edge (Joe's semiconductor knowledge), tax-loss harvesting, learning, and the structural fact that an index can never outperform. Resolution = index core 80–90%+, capped picking satellite.
8. **Small-cap is the one soft spot in the pro-index case**: 70% of active small-cap funds beat the SmallCap 600 in 2024 — inefficient corners give active its best (inconsistent) odds.

## Contradictions

- **SPIVA methodology is contested**: a 2025 industry report challenges SPIVA's category assignments and survivorship handling (wealthmanagement.com). SPIVA's direction survives every independent replication (Morningstar Active/Passive Barometer agrees), so high confidence in the conclusion, medium in exact percentages.
- **"90% of pros fail" vs pro-picking commentators** (e.g., Charles Payne): the pro-picking case is framed around possibility and engagement, not base rates — these don't actually conflict; they answer different questions.
- Barber & Odean's absolute return numbers are from 1991–1996; the *gaps* replicate in modern data, the levels don't transfer. Marked accordingly.

## Open Questions

- Direct indexing (own all 500 stocks individually for per-lot tax-loss harvesting) — does it beat ETF + simplicity at Joe's account size? Not researched this session.
- Optimal satellite size: 80/20 is convention, not derived. Is there research on the max satellite that doesn't materially change outcome distributions?
- International allocation (0%? market-weight ~35%? home-bias-adjusted?) — perennial Bogleheads dispute, not settled here.
- Fidelity ZERO fund portability caveat filed at medium confidence — verify against Fidelity docs before acting on it.
